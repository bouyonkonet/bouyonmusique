Bouyon in Guadeloupe: the genre’s second home

By Smokiller · Published September 28, 2026 · Updated October 28, 2026

Guadeloupe is the second home of bouyon. The island shares Creole with Dominica, and its audience has adopted the sound to the point of creating its own branch: bouyon gwada.

  2. An audience that makes the hits

From jump up to bouyon gwada

At carnival and at parties, Guadeloupean audiences call bouyon jump up. Then the musicians of Gwada, in collaboration with Dominicans, hardened the sound: that is the birth of bouyon gwada, more electronic and rawer.

An audience that makes the hits

Guadeloupe’s bouyon DJs and pages play a decisive role: it is often their choices and their networks that turn a riddim into a carnival track, then relayed on to Martinique and Paris. See also bouyon in Paris.

Frequently asked questions

Where is bouyon popular?

Born in Dominica, bouyon is now central in Guadeloupe and Martinique, where audiences also call it jump up. It is present in France, Saint Lucia, Antigua, Saint Martin and the Caribbean diaspora, from New York to London, Toronto and Montreal.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica. The genre was born in Roseau in the late 1980s, when the band WCK, formed in 1988, fused cadence-lypso with jing ping and traditional drums such as the lapo kabwit. It remains the sound of Roseau carnival, Mas Domnik.

What is bouyon gwada?

Bouyon gwada, also called hardcore bouyon, is the branch of bouyon born from collaborations between musicians from Dominica and Guadeloupe. Harder, more electronic and rawer than the bouyon of live bands, it pushes drum machines, bass and vocals in Guadeloupean Creole. See bouyon gwada.

What is bouyon called in Guadeloupe?

In Guadeloupe, audiences call bouyon jump up, at carnival as at parties. The word also describes the way of dancing bouyon by jumping, which became the signature of gwada parties. The sound came from neighboring Dominica, carried by the shared Creole language and the back-and-forth of carnivals.

Which bouyon artists come from Guadeloupe?

Among the profiles this site follows: Miimii KDS, credited on "Pow Pow / Allo Doudou", certified gold by SNEP, TIITII NBA from Morne-à-l'Eau, whose "Kitty Kitty" passed 1.65 million plays, and Ricky Bishop, of Guadeloupean and Malagasy origin, creator of the New Bouyon concept. See the bouyon artists of Guadeloupe.

Is bouyon from Guadeloupe?

No, bouyon comes from Dominica, where WCK created it in Roseau in 1988. Guadeloupe did not invent it: it received it from neighboring Dominica, made it jump as jump up, then turned it into a full scene of its own, bouyon gwada. Dominica keeps the credit as creator, Guadeloupe the credit for giving bouyon one of its biggest scenes. See Gwada from Dominica.

How did bouyon reach Guadeloupe?

Through the shared Creole language, carnivals and tours. Dominican Creole and Guadeloupean Creole are mutually understood, and bouyon needed no translation. It grew in Guadeloupe through the tours of Dominican bands like WCK and Triple Kay, then collaborations between musicians of the two islands produced bouyon gwada. See the history of bouyon.

Which Guadeloupean bouyon track was certified gold?

"Pow Pow / Allo Doudou", by Niska, Litleboy LSBeats767 and Guadeloupe's Miimii KDS, was certified Single Or (gold single) by SNEP at 15 million stream equivalents. The name Litleboy LSBeats767 refers to Dominica's 767 dialing code, which makes the track a bridge between the two islands. See Miimii KDS.

Who is TIITII NBA?

TIITII NBA is a bouyon artist from Morne-à-l'Eau, in Guadeloupe. His name carries a program: NBA stands for New Bouyon Age, the new era of bouyon. His track "Kitty Kitty" passed 1.65 million plays according to Dominica News Online, and he runs bouyonews, the French-language bouyon media outlet that covers the genre's news for Guadeloupe, Martinique and France.

Which Guadeloupean label signed a Dominican artist?

More Sewo, a Guadeloupean label founded in 2007 by Arturo Bellot, signed BONNA, a Dominican bouyon artist from La Plaine. It shows that traffic between the two scenes now runs both ways: a Gwada label is investing in a talent from bouyon's island of origin, while Dominica brings the origin, the artists and the founding repertoire.

Are bouyon gwada and jump up the same thing?

Not quite. Jump up is the name the French Antilles give to bouyon from Dominica in general, and to the way of dancing it by jumping. Bouyon gwada is more specific: it is the hardcore branch born from collaborations between Dominica and Guadeloupe, with drum machines, heavy bass and vocals in Guadeloupean Creole. See the jump up page.

Which flag represents the bouyon gwada scene?

The local flag that Guadeloupeans call their own: green bands at the top and bottom, a red band in the middle and a yellow star. Bouyon Shattaz uses it in its designs about the gwada scene, because it is the sign that this scene recognizes itself, not an improvised or administrative symbol.

What is Ricky Bishop's New Bouyon?

New Bouyon is the concept created by Ricky Bishop, a rapper and sound engineer of Guadeloupean and Malagasy origin based in Bordeaux: bouyon mixed with trap and electronic music. He gave it shape in 2024 with the EPs #newbouyon and #sexybouyon. Not to be confused with TIITII NBA's New Bouyon Age. See Ricky Bishop.

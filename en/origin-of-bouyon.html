Origin of bouyon: Dominica first

By Smokiller · Published September 20, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Every bouyon road leads to Roseau, in Dominica. It is there, in the late 1980s, that Dominican musicians created a new genre from their own traditions. Knowing the origin of bouyon means understanding why Bouyon Shattaz repeats one simple phrase: bouyon was born in Dominica.

A unique Dominican context

Dominica is one of the rare Caribbean islands where Creole culture, the Kwéyòl language and traditional musics like jing ping and lapo kabwit stayed so alive. This richness, far from the industry circuits, gave Roseau’s musicians raw material that nobody else had.

1988, the year of creation

In 1988, WCK fuses cadence-lypso and jing ping with the technology of the time. Bouyon is born. The French Antilles, which share Creole with Dominica, adopt the sound very fast, then all of France. But the timeline is unambiguous: the creation is Dominican.

Why the origin is an economic issue

Today, a large part of the money of bouyon, certifications, distribution, big stages, is made in France and in the French Antilles. Defending the Dominican origin of bouyon is not nostalgia: it is a question of credits, rights and revenue for the island’s creators. It is the editorial mission of Bouyon Shattaz.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of the genre, born in Roseau in 1988 with the band WCK, then listen to the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, on Spotify and YouTube, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon. To hear the roots, add jing ping and the cadence-lypso of Exile One and the Grammacks. See the history of bouyon.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, a Caribbean island. Bouyon was born in Roseau in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band. The French Antilles, which share Creole with Dominica, adopted the sound very fast, then all of France, but the timeline is unambiguous: the creation is Dominican.

Who created bouyon?

The band WCK, short for Windward Caribbean Kulture, formed in Roseau in 1988, created bouyon by fusing cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. Other bands of the founding generation, like Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade, carried the genre in its early days.

Why is Dominica the birthplace of bouyon?

Because Dominica is one of the rare Caribbean islands where Creole culture, the Kwéyòl language and traditional musics like jing ping and lapo kabwit stayed so alive. Far from the industry circuits, this richness gave Roseau's musicians raw material that nobody else had.

Why defend bouyon's Dominican origin?

Because a large part of the money of bouyon, certifications, distribution and big stages, is made today in France and in the French Antilles. Defending the Dominican origin is not nostalgia: it is a question of credits, rights and revenue for the island's creators, and it is the editorial mission of Bouyon Shattaz.

What year was bouyon created?

In 1988. That year, the band WCK formed in Roseau and fused cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. That is why bouyon is said to be born in the late 1980s. In the 1990s, it becomes the dominant music of Dominica's carnival and village fetes.

Which music did bouyon grow out of?

Two Dominican parents. Jing ping, the traditional dance music led by the accordion, with the lapo kabwit, the boom-boom and the bamboo flute. And cadence-lypso, carried in the 1970s by Exile One and the Grammacks. Add bèlè, kwadril, chanté mas, mazurka and a little zouk. See the cadence-lypso page.

Was bouyon born in Guadeloupe or Martinique?

No, it was born in Dominica, the neighbouring island. Guadeloupe and Martinique, which share the Creole language with Dominica, adopted the sound very fast and call it jump up. Guadeloupe later developed its own branch, bouyon gwada, born from collaborations between musicians of the two islands. But the creation, in 1988 in Roseau, is Dominican.

How did bouyon reach France?

Through the French Antilles and their diaspora. After Guadeloupe and Martinique, bouyon reached France, where it lives a second life on social media through the riddims of Antillean producers. Songs earn certifications there, like Pow Pow / Allo Doudou, certified gold by the SNEP, and Paris holds a large diaspora. See bouyon in France.

Is Dominica the same as the Dominican Republic?

No, they are two different countries. Dominica, the birthplace of bouyon, is an English-speaking and Creole-speaking Caribbean island whose capital is Roseau; it shares Creole with its neighbours, Guadeloupe and Martinique. It is there that the band WCK created bouyon in 1988, and it is this Dominica that this site defends as the origin of the genre.

Why is Roseau the city of bouyon?

Because everything started there. The band WCK formed in Roseau, the capital of Dominica, in 1988. The city is also the centre of the island's carnival, Mas Domnik, where the audience tested, approved and carried the new music. Roseau still hosts the World Creole Music Festival every October. See Dominica Carnival.

Nensha: one singer, one documented single, and the Dj Blax pen

Published October 20, 2026

Nensha is a singer the platforms file between contemporary R&B and afro-pop, and whose public file is one of the thinnest on this site. Only one record can be checked line by line on a reference platform: “Comprends pas”, released on August 12, 2022 on Delta Records, classed as afro-pop by Shazam, and co-written by three names, Nensha herself, ZE L and Dj Blax. That last name is what brings this profile onto a bouyon site: Dj Blax is one of the composers this site follows from catalog to catalog, and his signature on the single of a Paris singer shows how far his writing network reaches. Four further titles are cited by one aggregator, with no opened platform page to confirm them one by one. This profile therefore states, from its introduction, what it is: an honest and short credits file, not a biography.

The frame: neither bouyon nor shatta, a credits file

The first thing to write about Nensha is negative, and it protects the reader: no source consulted presents her as a bouyon singer, nor as a shatta artist, nor as a French Caribbean musician. Shazam classes her single “Comprends pas” as afro-pop. Viberate, the only other opened document that describes her, presents her as a contemporary R&B musician based in Paris, France. This profile therefore files her exactly where the sources put her: a French R&B and afro-pop singer, one of whose records crosses the writing network this site documents.

That crossing fits in one credits line. The Shazam page of “Comprends pas” credits three songwriters: Dj Blax, ZE L and Nensha, the latter under the doubled form “Nensha Nensha” that the platform displays. Dj Blax is followed by this site on its Dj Blax page across the bouyon, shatta, rap and R&B catalogs where his signature appears. His presence in the credits of a 2022 Paris afro-pop single does not make it a bouyon record, and this profile will never say so: it makes it a documented crossing point between the scene our bouyon playlist follows and French urban pop, of the kind only credits can reveal.

Everything else in the file fits in four titles and one source. Viberate cites, among Nensha songs, “Tokyo” and “Coller”, dated 2023, “Evident”, dated 2022 like “Comprends pas”, and two repertoire titles, “Je t’aime encore” and “Rester fort”. None of those five mentions could be cross checked, during this research, by an opened page on Shazam, Deezer, Qobuz or YouTube, where searches mostly returned a cumbersome namesake, the song “Nensha” by the Swedish metal band Orbit Culture, which a full section below deals with. The reader therefore knows, from the frame onward, which parts of this profile rest on a reference platform and which rest on a single aggregator.

“Comprends pas”: the single documented line by line

The central, and almost only, record of the file is “Comprends pas”. Its Shazam page, opened for this profile, gives the full set of checkable facts: released on August 12, 2022 on Delta Records, as a single titled “Comprends pas - Single”, in French, genre afro-pop, tempo 186 BPM, and about 180 Shazam identifications counted when the page was opened. No album follows this single in the sources consulted, no alternative version is referenced, and no official video was found during the research: the record exists as a platform single, and this profile describes it at that level.

The tempo deserves a remark, because it is the only numbered musical data in the file. 186 BPM is, on paper, a speed zone close to the fast tempos of bouyon and shatta, and very far from the slow tempos of the R&B Viberate attaches the singer to. This profile is careful not to draw a style conclusion from it: a technical page BPM does not make a genre, the Shazam classification says afro-pop, and only listening would settle what the metadata does not say. But the figure at least explains why the record can surface, in platform feeds, in the neighbourhood of the fast records of the Caribbean scenes this site follows, and why a bouyon reader can cross this name without the singer belonging to that scene.

The writing credits are the richest part of the page. Three songwriters are named: Dj Blax, ZE L and Nensha. The singer is therefore a co-writer of her single, which places her in the position of an artist who writes, not a performer handed a finished record. ZE L, the second name, is documented in no other source opened for this profile: no artist page, no other credit found, and this site lends it neither an identity nor a catalog. Dj Blax, the third, is by contrast a name this site knows well, and the next section returns to what his signature is doing in these credits. Keep the essential point here: the only fully documented Nensha record is a three handed written record, one of whose hands is a composer of the bouyon and shatta network.

The writing room: Dj Blax, ZE L, and what the signature tells

To understand what the Dj Blax signature is doing on “Comprends pas”, recall how this site knows him. On our Dj Blax page, the credits gathered show him at work in several worlds at once: bouyon and shatta records from the French West Indies, French rap records where he is credited as Blax or Dj Blax, and R&B and afro-pop records like this one. That circulation is his documented mark: a composer who writes for Paris singers as for artists of the Caribbean scenes, without the platforms ever telling how those sessions are put together. The Nensha single is one small but clean piece of that puzzle: one date, one label, three songwriters.

The presence of ZE L in the same credits opens a second trail, which this profile states as a question and not as a fact. A name of two letters and an initial, ZE L, co-writer of a 2022 afro-pop record, recalls the shape of the workshop signatures of this generation of French speaking composers, who sign short, sometimes under several spellings, as Blax and Dj Blax do for one same network. But where the Dj Blax credits cross check from platform to platform and catalog to catalog, ZE L leaves, in the sources opened for this profile, no second trace. He therefore remains what the document says: a co-writer named ZE L, full stop. If a page one day documents him, this profile will be completed.

What, then, is “Comprends pas” worth in its singer path? The sources allow a narrow answer: it is her earliest title among those Viberate dates to 2022, level in year with “Evident”, and the only one the reference platforms document in full. It is therefore, for want of better, the entry point of the catalog: an afro-pop single in French, written with a composer of the bouyon network, released on Delta Records, a label about which the opened sources say nothing beyond its name on this page. Any wider story, about a strategy, a circle or an ambition, would be invented, and this site prefers a short and true file to a long and false portrait.

The rest of the catalog: four titles, one source

Beyond the documented single, the public Nensha catalog rests on one opened page only: that of Viberate, a music data aggregator whose artist texts are presentations generated from platform catalogs. That page gives two lists that must be kept apart. In the most popular songs, it lines up “Coller”, “Evident” and “Comprends pas”. In the latest releases, it cites “Tokyo” and “Coller” as 2023 songs, and “Evident” and “Comprends pas” as 2022 songs. And it mentions, in the singer repertoire, two further titles: “Je t’aime encore” and “Rester fort”.

What that page allows anyone to write, and no more: the known Nensha catalog holds at least six titles, spread over two release years, 2022 and 2023, all in French judging by their titles, and no album is cited. The 2023 titles, “Tokyo” and “Coller”, suggest continuity after the 2022 single, but no opened page gives their label, their exact date, their credits or their tempo. It would be easy to write that “Coller” continues the afro-pop vein of “Comprends pas”: this profile does not write it, because no opened document says so, and because the Viberate texts, written in general formulas about her voice and her style, do not amount to a listening.

That caution has a name, and this site applies it to all its thin profiles: a single source earns an attributed mention, never an established fact. When a second platform documents “Tokyo”, “Coller” or “Evident” with credits, those records will enter the main narrative of this profile with their dates and their writers. In the meantime, they stand here for what they are: titles cited by an aggregator, consistent with each other, and not enough to tell a musical evolution. A reader discovering Nensha through this page should therefore start with “Comprends pas”, the only record for which everything, from the date to the BPM, can be checked, then look for the others on the platforms, where their very presence remains to be confirmed page by page.

The name trap: Nensha is not Orbit Culture

Every search on Nensha starts with a misunderstanding that must be cleared, because it pollutes results and conversations alike. In September 2019, the Swedish metal band Orbit Culture released a single titled “Nensha”, the first extract from its album “Nija”, released in 2020, a song written by its singer Niklas Karlsson and since become one of the band known records. That “Nensha” has nothing to do with the singer of this profile: not the same country, not the same genre, not the same scene decade, and the word itself also circulates in Japanese culture, where nensha names an early twentieth century concept of psychic photography. Three uses of one word, three worlds that never touch.

The confusion is not just a search engine detail: it skews counters and recommendations. A listener typing Nensha on a platform lands first on the metal song, its videos and its lyrics, and the “Comprends pas” singer only appears when searching her title or her label. That relative invisibility also sheds light on the thinness of the file: an artist whose name is occupied, in the indexes, by a successful metal song mechanically leaves fewer readable traces, and the aggregators themselves have to disentangle two artists under one word. This profile keeps the practical lesson: to find the singer, search “Nensha Comprends pas” or “Nensha Delta Records”, never the name alone.

There is finally a platform glitch worth flagging, because it can mislead credits readers: the Shazam page of “Comprends pas” credits the songwriter under the doubled form “Nensha Nensha”, the name twice over, as happens in credit databases when the stage name and the writer name are entered in two fields. This profile keeps the simple form, Nensha, the artist name of the single, and flags the doubling so that nobody goes looking for a second person who does not exist in the document. In a file this thin, every line counts, and every line must be read for what it is: a credit database entry, with its strengths and its dross.

The limits of the file: what this profile does not know

End with the inventory of absences, which here is longer than the inventory of facts, and which this profile fully owns. No source consulted gives Nensha real name, nor an age, nor a birth town, nor an interview, nor a press portrait, nor a verified social account. The only location available comes from Viberate, which presents her as a Paris artist, and this profile cites it as an aggregator presentation, not as a civil fact. No official photograph is attached to the file, no concert is documented, no chart placing, no certification and no listening figure other than the roughly 180 Shazam identifications of “Comprends pas” was found.

Even the genre filing stays double and unsettled: afro-pop at Shazam for the documented single, contemporary R&B at Viberate for the artist. The two labels can coexist, a fast single inside a sung R&B repertoire, but no opened source allows anyone to describe the singer sound beyond those two words, and this profile holds to them. Likewise, the label Delta Records is documented only by its name on the single page: this site lends it neither a catalog, nor a country, nor a history, for want of an opened source describing it.

Why, then, a whole profile for six titles and one complete credits line? Because this site documents a scene through its credits as much as through its stars, and the Dj Blax signature on a 2022 Paris afro-pop single is a network fact of the same order as a carnival hit: it shows how French speaking composers circulate between bouyon, shatta, rap and sung pop, and how an almost unknown singer can find herself, for the length of one record, in the same credits as them. If Nensha publishes an album, a biography or complete pages for “Tokyo”, “Coller” and “Evident”, this profile will be completed and dated. In the meantime, listening starts and, in essence, comes down to “Comprends pas”, and the context is read on our Dj Blax page.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Nensha?

A French singer presented by Viberate as a contemporary R&B artist based in Paris. Her only record checked line by line on a reference platform is “Comprends pas”, classed as afro-pop by Shazam. No source consulted gives her real name, her age, a biography or an interview: this profile is a credits file, not a portrait.

What is the best documented Nensha record?

“Comprends pas”, released on August 12, 2022 on Delta Records, in French. Shazam classes it as afro-pop, gives it a tempo of 186 BPM and about 180 identifications when the page was opened. Its credited songwriters are Dj Blax, ZE L and Nensha herself.

Does Nensha sing bouyon?

No source consulted says so. Her documented single is classed as afro-pop by Shazam, and Viberate files her under contemporary R&B. She appears on this site because “Comprends pas” was co-written by Dj Blax, a composer this site follows across bouyon and shatta catalogs: it is a credits link, not a genre belonging.

Which other Nensha songs are known?

Viberate cites “Tokyo” and “Coller”, dated 2023, “Evident”, dated 2022, and two repertoire titles, “Je t’aime encore” and “Rester fort”. During this research, none of those titles could be cross checked on an opened reference platform page: they are therefore cited here as aggregator mentions, not as releases verified one by one.

Who are Dj Blax and ZE L in the Nensha file?

The two co-writers, with Nensha, of “Comprends pas”, according to the Shazam page of the record. Dj Blax is a composer documented by this site on its Dj Blax page, present in many bouyon, shatta, rap and R&B credits. ZE L, by contrast, appears in no other source opened for this profile: no identity, no other credit found.

Why is the name Nensha confusing?

Because it is shared with a famous record from another world: in September 2019, the Swedish metal band Orbit Culture released a single titled “Nensha”, taken from its album “Nija”. That record has nothing to do with the singer. To find her on the platforms, search “Nensha Comprends pas” or “Nensha Delta Records”, never the name alone.

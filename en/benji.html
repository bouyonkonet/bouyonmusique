Benji: the singer who turned Triple Kay toward bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Benji is a bouyon artist from Dominica, a singer with Triple Kay International. His real name is Wayne Benjamin, and sources present him as Mr Benji N2O or as Mr Benji N’O. His own band official site tells the fact that structures his whole career: when Benji arrives, with “Log On” and then with the title track “Sewo”, the Triple Kay repertoire swings toward the fast bouyon he was already known for, and he becomes a full member of the band in 2005. Twenty years on, the same man is named a Cultural Ambassador of Dominica. This profile gathers the verified facts about Benji, separates his name from his namesakes, and states plainly what the record does not hold.

Who Benji is: Wayne Benjamin, one name, two spellings

Benji real name is Wayne Benjamin. That full name is given by every serious source that covers him: the official site of TK International, the stage name of Triple Kay, Soca News, eMAGE DM and Writeups24. He is one of the band three frontline vocalists, with David “Sonic Tazzy” George and Lennan “Ticky” Matthew. In a nine member band founded by three friends from the community of Laudat, that vocal trio is the public face of the group, and Benji is the voice the sources describe through his raspy chants.

His full stage name calls for a precision few pages take the time to make. eMAGE DM and A Virtual Dominica write it Mr Benji N2O, with the figure 2 and the letter O. The official Triple Kay site and Soca News write it Mr Benji N’O, with an apostrophe. Both spellings circulate in published, checkable sources, and no source consulted for this profile explains which one the artist prefers today. This profile therefore prints both forms, attributed to their sources, rather than inventing a third one or silently correcting one with the other. It is a detail, but credit begins with the spelling of names.

Benji is not a singer who arrived in bouyon with the latest fashion. The official Triple Kay site presents him, at the moment he joins the band, as an artist already known for the bouyon genre, a fast paced, upbeat sound popular among the younger crowd. That prior reputation is the starting point of his public story: before Triple Kay, Benji already had a bouyon identity. The band did not manufacture him, it recruited him for what he brought, and the next section shows that the recruitment changed the sound of the band itself.

What the record does not hold must be stated in this first section too. No verified source consulted gives Benji date of birth, his home community or a solo album under his name alone. His documented public roles tie him to Roseau, where he sits on the city council according to the minister who announced his ambassadorship, and to the Fond Cole Development Committee. This profile does not turn those roles into an invented biography: it tells the artist through his records, his band and his published functions, which are already, as will be seen, considerable material.

Benji is not Benjai: the disambiguation this profile owes its readers

The name Benji is generic, and platforms mix it with everything that sounds like it. The most serious confusion, for a bouyon or soca reader, carries an almost identical name: Benjai. Benjai is a different soca artist, born Rodney Le Blanc in Trinidad and Tobago, known for his work with Bunji Garlin Asylum Band and with Machel Montano HD Family, according to his ItzCaribbean artist page. Benji from Dominica and Benjai from Trinidad are not the same person, do not share a full name, do not come from the same island and do not work in the same bands. This profile credits no Benjai record, award or fact to Benji, and none of Benji facts to Benjai.

Other artists called Benji exist far from the Caribbean, DJs, rappers and producers with no published link to bouyon, Dominica or Triple Kay. The rule of this page is therefore the same as the rule of the other generic name profiles on this site: only facts tied to Wayne Benjamin, Triple Kay singer, cultural ambassador appointed in 2021 and documented Flamboyant Awards winner in 2005, are kept. A track found under the name Benji alone, with no link to Triple Kay or to Dominica, does not enter this profile.

That discipline has a practical consequence for listening. The documented discography of Benji is, in the main, the discography of Triple Kay during his frontline years. The records listed in the structured data of this page are therefore presented for what they are: Triple Kay recordings, by the band in which Benji is a lead singer with Tazzy and Ticky, and not solo singles signed Benji. A reader looking for a solo Benji album will not find one here, because no verified source documents one. What that reader will find, record by record, is the band Benji helped transform, and that is where his voice is heard.

Disambiguation also protects the other artists in the band. Triple Kay is a nine musician collective, with a producer, Kendel “Killa” Laurent, who produces the band music according to its official site, and a chief lyricist, Jeoffrey “JJ Yout” Joseph. Crediting Benji alone with what belongs to the band would be the mirror image of crediting him with Benjai facts. His own documented role is precise, and large enough to need no borrowing: arriving with a sound, producing two hinge records, becoming a member, then carrying the raspy voice of the frontline trio through two decades on the road.

Log On and Sewo: the arrival that turned Triple Kay toward bouyon

The official Triple Kay site tells the arrival of Benji as a turning point, and it tells it in three beats. In the early 2000s, the band is known for a repertoire influenced by local cadence-lypso, Haitian compas and the zouk of the neighbouring French West Indies, heard on its debut album “Big Ting”, released in 2000, then on “Fanatik”, in 2003, with songs such as “Fanatik” and “Crevice”. It is into that landscape that Benji steps: he produces the single “Log On” and is its lead singer. The band site says, in so many words, that this introduction of Benji signalled the coming change in the band musical style.

Second beat, in 2004: Benji returns as a guest act and produces the upbeat title track of the album “Sewo”. Third beat, in 2005: he is a full member of the band, and his influence is clear, as the Triple Kay repertoire now reflects the local bouyon genre for which Benji was known, a fast paced sound popular among the younger crowd. David and Lennan follow the same movement, and the trio becomes what the site calls a dominant bouyon vocal performing trio. The albums that follow carry that mark: “All Out” in 2006, “Pressure” in 2007, “Colours” in 2008, “Allo” in 2009, “Hush Your Mouth” in 2011 and “Bubblin” in 2012.

The title “Sewo” deserves a stop, because it concentrates the swing. A Virtual Dominica reports that Triple Kay was named Band of the Year in 2004 and again in 2005, and that “Sewo” was awarded Song of the Year in 2005. The record produced by the guest Benji thus becomes, in the very year he joins the band for good, the band award winning title. Few recruitments leave such a readable trace: a producer singer arrives with a bouyon sound, the band adopts it, and the Dominican market immediately rewards the result. It is that moment that makes Benji more than a Triple Kay singer: he is one of its sound architects.

That swing belongs in the longer history of the genre. Triple Kay is among the bands that adopted and amplified the bouyon soca started by Dada in Dominica in 2003, according to the signed testimony of Smokiller published on this site: the style Dada started became popular when Triple Kay and other bands took it up in their own version of bouyon. The arrival of Benji, across 2003, 2004 and 2005, sits exactly in those welding years. The Laudat band, formed on cadence-lypso and compas, then becomes a bouyon machine, and the raspy voice of Benji is one of the signatures of that transformation. For the full context, read our history of bouyon.

The Triple Kay machine with Benji: road marches, anthems and twenty years on the road

After the swing, the documented discography of the band with Benji on the frontline forms the backbone of this profile. In 2008, Triple Kay won the People Road March with “Please”, according to A Virtual Dominica. The official site adds, for the following years, a run of records this profile lists in their published order: the compa number “Pou Yo” and “They Must Be Mad”, featuring Shadowflow, in 2013; “Nous Konnet Vive” in 2014, whose remix brings together the bouyon stars Nayee, Rah and Asa Bantan, and which was selected as the official theme song of Dominica carnival celebrations; then “Lock De Gate” in 2015, which won the band second People Road March title.

The run continues with the soca fusion “Waist”, with Mr Killa of Grenada and Trinidad Flippo, then “Nice Time” in 2016 and “F.T.M (Fete Till Morning)” the following year. In 2017, “Roll dat Boom Boom” took the Road March title and “Jose” became, within a month, the band second most popular YouTube upload, behind 2007 “Soos”. After the devastation of Hurricane Maria, the band released “Still Standing” in 2018, which became an anthem of resilience for the country. In 2019, “Heat” won what the official site presents as the world first ever People Bouyon Road March title. At the dawn of the new decade, “Toufe” is presented as one of the band most loved songs, and the single “Whistle” is described as topping several regional charts, in Antigua among others.

That list is not catalogue filler. It shows the function of a band like Triple Kay in the life of bouyon: supplying, season after season, the carnival records, the anthems of hard moments and the official themes of national celebrations. Benji holds his place in that machine as a frontline singer, alongside Tazzy high pitched falsettos and Lennan smooth vocals, in the official site description, which sets their three timbres against each other to explain the trio chemistry. The same site tells, with humour, of shirtless performances, Tazzy falls and Benji mixing up his lyrics mid song, the signs of a road band, not a studio product.

The road itself is documented at the same scale. The official site recalls that Triple Kay has played the World Creole Music Festival since its 2002 debut there and, from 2005, has never missed a festival performance. Soca News confirms that the band are crowd favourites at WCMF, with standout performances at the Antilliaanse Feesten in Hoogstraten, Belgium, in 2006 and 2010, and at the Terre de Blues Festival in Marie Galante in 2012, plus carnivals around the world. The band site adds Houston, Toronto, London Notting Hill Carnival, Miami, New York, Antigua, St. Thomas, St. Croix and St. Martin. For a bouyon artist, that geography is a biography: Benji has sung Dominica bouyon on the stages where the diaspora and the Caribbean meet.

Recognition: Best Male Artist 2005, St Kitts 2009, Cultural Ambassador in 2021

The first documented personal recognition arrives early. A Virtual Dominica reports that Wayne “Mr Benji N2O” Benjamin, the band lead vocalist, was recognised as Best Male Artist of the Year at the 2005 Flamboyant Awards. The year is the year of “Sewo”, Song of the Year, and of his arrival as a full member. The personal award and the band award tell the same moment: in 2005, Benji is no longer the guest who brings a sound, he is the award winning voice of the dominant band on the island.

The second trace is shorter, and this profile reports it with its limit. Writeups24, in its article on the ambassadorship, states that in 2009 Benji was the first runner up in a competition held in St Kitts and Nevis. The article does not name that competition, so this profile does not name it either. The fact kept is the one the source publishes: a runner up placing in a regional competition in 2009, a sign that the reputation of Benji already reached beyond Dominica alone by the end of the 2000s.

The institutional consecration arrives in January 2021. eMAGE DM, on January 27, 2021, announced that Wayne Benjamin, Mr Benji N2O, of the Triple Kay Band, was Dominica newest Cultural Ambassador, named by the Government of Dominica, with the appointment taking effect on February 1. Writeups24 confirmed the next day that Cabinet had approved the appointment of Wayne “Benji” Benjamin as a Cultural Ambassador of the Commonwealth of Dominica, effective February 1, 2021. eMAGE DM quotes Benji, humbled and elated, thinking of his grandmother and his mother, and set on representing his island and advancing its cultural art forms on the world stage. The outlet describes Triple Kay as an ensemble that has dominated the bouyon scene for over a decade, and Benji as its front man.

The appointment rewards more than a singer. Minister Edward Registe, quoted by eMAGE DM, stresses that Benjamin has at every opportunity promoted his country and its people, that he is engaged in mentorship activities focused on youth and the underprivileged, that he is deeply involved in community activism as part of the Fond Cole Development Committee and that he is a sitting member of the Roseau City Council. The official portrait is therefore double: a bouyon artist at the top of his band, and a community actor in the capital. The band itself was then honoured collectively, with the Commonwealth of Dominica Meritorious Service Award in 2022 and the Golden Drum Award in 2022, for its contribution to culture and promotion of the country worldwide, according to its official site. The ambassadorship of Benji and the awards of the band answer each other: Dominica honoured, a year apart, the man and the machine he helped build.

Why Benji matters, and how to hear him

Benji matters because he is the documented link between two states of Dominican bouyon. Before him, Triple Kay is an excellent cadence-lypso, compas and zouk band. With “Log On”, “Sewo” and his 2005 arrival, the band becomes a bouyon reference, chains road march titles, supplies the official carnival theme of 2014 and the post Maria resilience anthem, then sees its singer named a cultural ambassador. Few bouyon careers offer such a complete chain, from a record produced as a guest to a state function, by way of a Best Male Artist award and twenty years of festival.

Benji also matters for the method of this site. His name is generic, his stage suffix is written two ways, and his most dangerous catalogue neighbour is called Benjai, with an a and another island. A careless profile would have mixed all three. This profile chose the opposite: every fact tied to Wayne Benjamin, every spelling attributed, every record returned to Triple Kay when it belongs to Triple Kay. It is the same credit work Bouyon Shattaz carries for WCK, for Dada and for the current generation, from Reo to Smokiller.

Listening follows the order of the story. Start with “Log On”, the single that signals the change, then “Sewo”, the award winning record of the swing. Continue with “Please”, “Lock De Gate” and “Roll dat Boom Boom”, the road marches, then “Nous Konnet Vive”, the carnival theme, and “Still Standing”, the 2018 anthem. Finish with “Heat”, “Toufe” and “Whistle”, the recent period documented by the official site. Those records, Triple Kay recordings with Benji on the frontline, are listed in the structured data of this page and can be found in our bouyon playlist, alongside the rest of the Dominican scene.

The limits remain written here rather than hidden. No solo album by Benji is documented by the sources consulted, no official personal social account was confirmed during this research, and neither his date of birth nor his home community is printed here, for lack of a verified source. If Triple Kay or the artist publish those elements, this profile will be completed and dated. In the meantime, the record is already rich: a full name, a band, a dated sound turning point, award winning records across twenty years, and a cultural ambassadorship. For Dominica bouyon, Benji is not one more namesake. He is one of its founding voices of the second generation.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Benji, the bouyon artist?

Benji is the stage name of Wayne Benjamin, a bouyon singer from Dominica and one of the three frontline vocalists of Triple Kay International, with David “Sonic Tazzy” George and Lennan “Ticky” Matthew. Sources present him as Mr Benji N2O or Mr Benji N’O.

Are Benji and Benjai the same artist?

No. Benji is Wayne Benjamin, from Dominica, singer with Triple Kay. Benjai is a different soca artist, born Rodney Le Blanc in Trinidad and Tobago, linked to the Asylum Band and the HD Family. The names look alike, the two artists share neither island, band nor catalogue.

What did Benji change in the Triple Kay sound?

According to the band’s official site, Benji produced and sang “Log On”, then produced the “Sewo” title track in 2004 as a guest, before becoming a member in 2005. His influence swung the Triple Kay repertoire, which came from cadence-lypso, compas and zouk, toward the fast bouyon Benji was already known for.

When did Benji become a Cultural Ambassador of Dominica?

In January 2021. eMAGE DM announced on January 27, 2021, that Wayne Benjamin, Mr Benji N2O, had been named a Cultural Ambassador by the Government of Dominica, taking effect on February 1, 2021. Writeups24 confirmed the Cabinet approval the next day.

Which records should I hear to discover Benji?

Hear the Triple Kay recordings with Benji on the frontline: “Log On”, “Sewo”, “Please”, “Nous Konnet Vive”, “Lock De Gate”, “Still Standing”, “Heat”, “Toufe” and “Whistle”. “Sewo” was named Song of the Year in 2005, and “Heat” won the People Bouyon Road March in 2019. Find them in our bouyon playlist.

Why is his name written both N2O and N’O?

Because published sources use both spellings. eMAGE DM and A Virtual Dominica write Mr Benji N2O, while the official Triple Kay site and Soca News write Mr Benji N’O. No source consulted explains the difference, so this site prints both forms with their attribution, without inventing another one.

What awards has Benji received?

A Virtual Dominica reports that Wayne “Mr Benji N2O” Benjamin was recognised as Best Male Artist of the Year at the 2005 Flamboyant Awards, the year “Sewo” was Song of the Year. Writeups24 says he was first runner up in a competition held in St Kitts and Nevis in 2009, without naming it. In 2021, the Government of Dominica named him a Cultural Ambassador.

Where is Benji from?

Benji is an artist from Dominica. He sings in Triple Kay, a band founded by three friends from the Laudat community, and his documented public roles tie him to Roseau, where he sits on the City Council, and to the Fond Cole Development Committee, according to Minister Edward Registe quoted by eMAGE DM. No verified source publishes his community of birth.

Who are the other members of Triple Kay with Benji?

According to the band’s official site, Triple Kay has nine musicians. Benji forms the frontline vocal trio with David “Sonic Tazzy” George, with his high falsetto, and Lennan “Ticky” Matthew, with his soft voice. Kendel “Killa” Laurent produces the band’s music, and Jeoffrey “JJ Yout” Joseph is its main lyricist.

Which Road Marches did Benji win with Triple Kay?

With Benji on the frontline, Triple Kay won the People Road March in 2008 with “Please”, according to A Virtual Dominica, then a second People Road March in 2015 with “Lock De Gate” and the 2017 Road March with “Roll dat Boom Boom”, according to the official site. In 2019, “Heat” won what the band presents as the world’s first People Bouyon Road March.

Did Triple Kay create bouyon soca?

No. Bouyon soca was launched in Dominica in 2003 by Dada, according to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller published on this site. Triple Kay is one of the bands that adopted and popularised it in their own version of bouyon. Benji’s arrival in the band, between 2003 and 2005, falls exactly in those welding years.

Which Triple Kay albums does Benji sing on?

Benji sang “Log On” in the “Fanatik” era, produced the title track of “Sewo” as a guest in 2004, then became a full member in 2005. The following albums carry his mark: “All Out” in 2006, “Pressure” in 2007, “Colours” in 2008, “Allo” in 2009, “Hush Your Mouth” in 2011 and “Bubblin” in 2012. No solo Benji album is documented.

What is “Nous Konnet Vive” by Triple Kay?

“Nous Konnet Vive” is a 2014 Triple Kay track, chosen as the official theme of Dominica’s carnival celebrations, according to the band’s official site. Its remix brings together bouyon stars Nayee, Rah and Asa Bantan. It is one of the tracks where Benji can be heard on the frontline, in the national role the band plays season after season.

Does Triple Kay, Benji’s band, play the World Creole Music Festival?

Yes, it is a pillar of the festival: according to its official site, Triple Kay has played the World Creole Music Festival since 2002 and has not missed an edition since 2005. For 2026, this site’s TK International page places the band on Night 1 of the festival, Friday 23 October 2026, in Roseau.

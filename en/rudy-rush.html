Rudy Ru$h: Here for You and the Bronx crossover, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Rudy Ru$h is an artist born in Dominica and based in the United States, in the Bronx, where he runs his OffDaBlock Records banner. His public file rests almost entirely on his own press releases, and this profile reads them as such: in October 2024, he releases “Exodus”, a reggae and dancehall single with Junior Reid, filmed in Jamaica. In 2025, he pivots toward bouyon, with “Anywhere We Go” and then, in September 2026, “Here for You”, a crossover bouyon single written with Yasmine and Chioneso, produced by Ridge, and carried by an official video opened and verified for this profile. One part of the file remains confirmed by nothing outside his releases, including a World Creole Music Festival 2025 stage that the lineups consulted do not list: this profile flags it, instead of repeating it.

Who Rudy Ru$h is: one name, two spellings, one banner

The name first, because the sources write it two ways. The releases of 2026 and the music press write Rudy Ru$h, with a space. The official titles of the YouTube videos, the 2024 release and the single link page write RudyRu$h, with no space. Both forms point to the same artist: the 2026 release quotes “Exodus” in the catalog, and the “Exodus” video carries the joined form. This profile keeps the spaced form, the form of the most recent coverage, and flags the joined form as the video platform spelling.

The base identity is stable from one release to the next: an artist born in Dominica, based in the United States, a songwriter and producer. The 2024 texts place him in the Bronx, in New York, and present him as the CEO of OffDaBlock Records, his banner, whose website is given as offdabloc.com. The description of the official “Exodus” video credits OffDaBlocRecords and OneBlood Family, and the description of “Here for You” gives his social handle, iamrudyrush. The 2024 release printed a variant of that handle, iamrudyrush3, in its about line: this profile gives the form carried by the official video of 2026, and notes the variant instead of choosing in silence.

What the sources do not give, this profile does not invent. No legal name is published in the opened documents: no birth first name, no date, no childhood biography, unlike the file of an artist like TriSzy, whose press tells the schools. For Rudy Ru$h, the story starts directly with the music and the Bronx. The genres claimed by his releases draw the positioning: in 2024, dancehall, reggae, hip hop and pop; in 2026, bouyon, reggae, soca, AfroFusion, hip hop and dancehall. Bouyon only reaches the head of the list in 2026, and the whole documented path fits inside that shift.

A point of method, finally, because it structures this whole profile. The Rudy Ru$h releases travel through EIN Presswire, a distribution service where the text is supplied by the artist team, here Playbook MG, and carried as is by dozens of sites. When twenty sites publish the same paragraph, that is not twenty confirmations, it is one source repeated twenty times. This profile therefore treats each claim by its origin: verified when an independent source or an opened official page carries it, attributed to the release when it exists only there.

“Exodus” with Junior Reid: the reggae base of 2024

The first documented milestone of the catalog is a reggae record, not a bouyon record. On October 30, 2024, from the Bronx, the release announces “Exodus”, a RudyRu$h single featuring Junior Reid, presented as a fusion of reggae and dancehall with contemporary forms, on themes of freedom, unity and empowerment. Both artists are quoted in it: RudyRu$h speaks of an old creative connection and of an anthem to share, Junior Reid speaks of music as a bridge between cultures. The same text announces a double drop with a second single, “Casamigos”, featuring KELLV: no platform page for that second record was found during this research, and its existence therefore stays at the announcement stage.

On November 26, 2024, a second release announces the “Exodus” video, shot in Jamaica, with stops in Kingston and the Gully Trenches. The official video, opened for this profile on YouTube, carries the title “Exodus - RudyRu$h x Junior Reid” and a description speaking of an artistic journey through the trenches of Jamaica, credited to OffDaBlocRecords and OneBlood Family. The video therefore exists as a public object, with both names in the title: it is, with “Here for You”, one of the two recordings of this profile verified by opening an official source.

The same November release reports a listening party where Sizzla, I Wayne and several members of the Marley family are said to have joined the two artists on stage. No independent source opened during this research confirms that evening, and this profile therefore files it among the claims of the release, like the quotes it contains. The distinction may seem severe, but it is the price of a clean file: in a scene where credit is the central issue, repeating a team claim as an established fact would be exactly the fault this site corrects in others.

What “Exodus” establishes solidly, without the ornaments, is already enough to place the artist. A Bronx singer of Dominican birth who obtains, from 2024, a feature and a joint video with Junior Reid, a reggae figure known for “One Blood” and for his past in Black Uhuru, according to the presentation of the release itself. The record has no claimed sound link with bouyon: it belongs to the reggae and dancehall base of the artist. Its importance for this site is genealogical: it is from that base, and from that New York base, that the bouyon pivot of the following year will start.

The turn of 2025: “Anywhere We Go” and the bouyon wave

The pivot is dated by a music press source independent of the releases. In October 2026, 13th Street Promotions, which follows the artist, writes that he caused a scene by releasing “Anywhere We Go” in 2025, and that he has taken his talent to various stages since. The 2026 release files the same record in his catalog, alongside “Bouyon Fete” and “Exodus”. No platform page for “Anywhere We Go” was opened during this research: neither Shazam, nor Apple Music, nor Spotify delivered an artist or song page for Rudy Ru$h despite repeated searches. The record is therefore documented as a press and release fact, and it stays out of the verified track list of this page.

“Bouyon Fete” is in the same case, thinner still. The title appears only in the catalog line of the 2026 release: no article, no video, no page carried it during this research. Its name states the program, a bouyon party, but a name is not a proof of listening, and this profile stops there: the record is flagged as declared by the artist team, waiting for a platform page that would let it be dated, credited and heard.

The context of this pivot, though, can be read without the releases. In the middle of the 2020s, Dominican bouyon is going through a phase of export by the diaspora: artists based in the United States take the sound of Roseau and rewire it for American club audiences, while the wave of the island, Signal Band, Trilla G, Shelly, Reo, pushes in the other direction. The music press, moreover, groups the same names spontaneously: the 13th Street Promotions text on “Why Should I?”, a record by Redpunchdrip, Strosey and Devon produced by Xavvoknockin, opens by filing TriSzy, Rudy Ru$h, Khallion and Reo among the artists waving the flag for Dominica. That is not a Rudy Ru$h record, and this profile does not attribute it to him: it is a proof of neighbourhood, the trace that the diaspora press already counts him in the bouyon group before his 2026 single.

What this pivot does not say must also be measured. No source consulted tells of a musical childhood in Dominica, a training in the bands of the island or a passage through the Roseau carnival. The documented path is that of a diaspora artist adopting bouyon from New York, as a catalog positioning and a stage language, in the same years when the genre is, according to the testimony gathered by this site from Smokiller for bouyon soca, an affair more than twenty years old: bouyon soca was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica, then popularized by Triple Kay. Our bouyon soca page holds that history, of which the diaspora version of Rudy Ru$h is a late chapter.

“Here for You”: the making of a crossover single

The central record of the file is “Here for You”, whose official video was announced on September 9, 2026 by a release dated from New York. The text gives a complete making, and the description of the official video, opened for this profile, confirms it point by point. The record is written by Rudy Ru$h, Yasmine and Chioneso, and Chioneso, a Brooklyn based artist according to 13th Street Promotions, sings on it as a feature. The production is signed by Ridge, the Dominican producer whose role in the current wave this site documents on his Ridge page: that credit alone plugs the single into the workshop of the island, and not into an American club producer.

The intention of the record is phrased by the artist in the release, and this profile quotes it as an intention, not as a result. “Here for You” speaks of real relationships, of two people letting go, dancing together and reassuring each other that they are present for one another. During the writing, the team says it took the playful dancefloor energy of the “Thong Song” by Sisqó as a starting point, to rework it through a modern bouyon and crossover lens. In his words carried by the release, the artist describes the record as the meeting point between the high energy bouyon sound of his island roots and the crossover influences of his American experience. That is the exact formula of the positioning: neither Roseau bouyon transposed as is, nor American pop in disguise, but an in between, owned and claimed.

The video gives the positioning its image. The official YouTube video, titled “Here for You - RudyRu$h ft. Chioneso”, is directed by ReemGrafix, credited as ReemGrafixStudios in the description, with MultiArtistry Entertainment on casting and co-direction. 13th Street Promotions places the action in a club, with the two artists among the dancers. The release insists on the casting: Caribbean women of different body types, heights and complexions, to celebrate confidence, individuality and natural beauty, and to let a wider audience feel represented. The description of the video names four dancers by their handles and states that the record is available on all streaming platforms, from Spotify to Apple Music and YouTube Music. No page of those platforms was found during this research, all the same, and the counters of the video were not recorded: this profile publishes no figure it has not read.

The life of the record outside the video is reported by the release, and attributed as such. Over the Labor Day weekend, in early September in the United States, Rudy Ru$h is said to have performed at several venues, where “Here for You” is said to have had patrons wining and jumping. In October 2026, 13th Street Promotions adds an aim: the artist wants to take things to another level for Carnival 2027, while the bouyon wave keeps growing. Those are the only two stage and agenda milestones of the file, and they are enough to read the strategy: a late summer single, a club video, a diaspora press that follows, and a carnival target. The sequel will tell whether the record enters the mixes and the playlists like those of our bouyon playlist, or whether it stays an object of releases.

What the releases claim, and what is not verified

Now to the claims this profile cannot take on as its own, because a profile that does not sort its sources is worth no more than a copied release. The presentation text of 2026 claims that in 2025, Rudy Ru$h performed at Dominica World Creole Music Festival, alongside Vybz Kartel and other major Caribbean names. That is a heavy claim, the WCMF is the national stage of the island, and it appears in no other document opened during this research. The complete 2025 festival guide consulted for this profile, which lists the artists night by night, on October 24, 25 and 26, 2025 at Windsor Park in Roseau, does not contain his name. This profile therefore writes exactly this: the WCMF 2025 performance is claimed by the artist team, and not confirmed by the lineups consulted. If a poster, an official program or a report documents it one day, the page will be corrected and dated.

The same sorting applies to the collaborations. The 2026 text lists collaborations with Capella Grey, Junior Reid, Spice, Mavado and Faith Evans. Only one is documented by opened sources: Junior Reid, with the single and the video of “Exodus”, announced by two releases and present on official video. For Capella Grey, Spice, Mavado and Faith Evans, no song page, no video and no independent article was found during this research. Those names therefore stay as claims of the press file, printed here as such, and no corresponding record enters the verified list of this page.

The sorting also applies to the words of genre. The 2026 release presents the artist as an emerging voice of Caribbean music with global crossover potential: that is a promotion formula, attributed here to his team, and not a ranking this site would take up. Likewise, the themes lent to “Exodus”, freedom, unity, empowerment, are those of the release of exit, and the reading of the video confirms at least the frame, Jamaica, Kingston and the trenches, which the official description names. Everywhere this profile could verify, it did: two official videos opened, their descriptions read, the credits cross checked between the release, the music press and the descriptions of the videos themselves.

This sorting work takes nothing away from the interest of the file, it specifies it. A Dominican artist of the diaspora who, in two years, moves from a major reggae feature to a bouyon single produced by Ridge, with a club video designed for representation, is a real and dated fact of the scene, independently of the unverified claims around it. It is even a textbook case of the way bouyon travels in 2026: through artists based outside, producers of the island, releases that travel faster than the platform pages, and a diaspora press that groups the names before the catalogs follow.

Limits of the file and state of the catalog

The verified state of the catalog fits in two records, and this profile owns that as its main conclusion. “Exodus”, with Junior Reid, released in October 2024, video in November 2024, verified by opening the official video. “Here for You”, with Chioneso, official video announced in September 2026, written by Rudy Ru$h, Yasmine and Chioneso, produced by Ridge, verified by opening the official video and cross checking its description. Everything else in the quoted catalog, “Anywhere We Go”, “Bouyon Fete”, the announced “Casamigos” with KELLV, rests on press or release texts, with no opened platform page. The track list in the structured data of this page therefore contains only those two recordings, and it will grow on the day verifiable pages appear.

The absences are as clear as the presences. No Shazam, Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer or Qobuz artist page was found despite repeated searches under both spellings of the name: that is the most telling absence of the file, because it prevents the fine dating of the releases, the reading of the full credits and the measuring of the listening. No official link is therefore carried in the structured data, the list of verified external links is empty, and this profile says so instead of filling it with unopened addresses. No legal name, no birth date, no long form interview was published in the opened sources: the public character starts at his banner, OffDaBlock Records, and at his social handle.

The comparison with the rest of the wave helps to place what this file is and is not. Where a TriSzy offers a complete artist page, dated song pages and published lyrics, the Rudy Ru$h file offers two solid video objects and a layer of releases to sort. Both profiles are published in the same wave of this site because both artists hold the same function in the scene of 2026: Dominican diaspora voices taking bouyon as an export language, one from a documented rap catalog, the other from a reggae base and a New York release machine. Their contexts are read on our World Creole Music Festival and bouyon playlist pages.

Listening, to finish, starts with the two verified objects: the “Here for You” video for the crossover bouyon of 2026, and the “Exodus” video for the reggae base of 2024 and the meeting with Junior Reid. Then it waits for the sequel the artist announces himself: a level to reach for Carnival 2027. If “Anywhere We Go” and “Bouyon Fete” receive platform pages, if the festival performance receives a document, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page of this site, and the claims will pass, with the pieces in support, from the release to the catalog.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Rudy Ru$h?

Rudy Ru$h, also written RudyRu$h on the official videos, is an artist born in Dominica and based in the United States, in the Bronx according to his 2024 releases. He presents himself as a songwriter and producer, and as the CEO of his banner OffDaBlock Records. No legal name is published in the sources opened for this profile: his public identity starts at his artist name and at his social handle, iamrudyrush, given in the description of the “Here for You” video.

What is the Rudy Ru$h bouyon record?

“Here for You”, featuring Chioneso, whose official video was announced on September 9, 2026. The record is written by Rudy Ru$h, Yasmine and Chioneso, and produced by Ridge. The release presents it as a record of romance and bouyon energy for the dancefloor, inspired by real relationships, and the description of the official video confirms the writing credits, the direction by ReemGrafix and the casting by MultiArtistry Entertainment.

Which Rudy Ru$h records are verified by an opened official source?

Only two. “Exodus”, with Junior Reid, released in October 2024 with a video shot in Jamaica and announced in November 2024, verified by opening the official video. And “Here for You”, with Chioneso, verified by opening its official video. The records “Anywhere We Go”, quoted by the press for 2025, “Bouyon Fete” and “Casamigos”, quoted by the releases, have no platform page found and do not figure in the verified list.

Did Rudy Ru$h perform at the World Creole Music Festival 2025?

His team claims so: the presentation text of 2026 says he performed at Dominica World Creole Music Festival in 2025, alongside Vybz Kartel. No independent document opened during this research confirms it, and the complete 2025 festival guide consulted for this profile, which lists the artists of October 24, 25 and 26, 2025 in Roseau, does not contain his name. The performance therefore stays an unverified claim to date.

Which artists has Rudy Ru$h collaborated with?

One collaboration is documented by opened sources: Junior Reid, on “Exodus” in 2024, single and video. Chioneso sings with him on “Here for You” in 2026, and Yasmine co-writes that record. The presentation text of 2026 also quotes Capella Grey, Spice, Mavado and Faith Evans among his collaborations, but no corresponding record was found on the platforms or in the independent press during this research: those names are reported as claims of his team.

Why is this Rudy Ru$h profile shorter than others?

Because his public file is thin and of a single nature. It rests almost entirely on releases distributed by his team via EIN Presswire, carried as is by many sites, which does not multiply the sources. No artist page was found on Shazam, Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer or Qobuz, and only two records could be verified by opening an official source. This profile therefore publishes what is established, attributes what is not, and flags its own limits, rather than filling the gaps.

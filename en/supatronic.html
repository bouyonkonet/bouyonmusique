Supatronic

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Supatronic is a name that enters this site through a single title, and that title carries the genre in its name: “Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon”, a single released on July 15, 2026 on 12690212 Records DK, running 2 minutes 14, with a writer and composer credit to Justin Felipe Harris on his Deezer artist page. The same page lists four further singles from 2026 and one featured title, and none of them states a bouyon tie. This profile is therefore deliberately short: no biography, no interview and no origin were found in the opened sources, and this site assigns Supatronic no island without a source that names it.

One bouyon single among the 2026 releases

The public file on Supatronic so far fits into a single primary page opened during this research: his Deezer artist page. It lists five singles, all released in 2026 on 12690212 Records DK and all credited to the same writer and composer, Justin Felipe Harris. In chronological order: “Pilot (Bajan Styling)” on May 11, 2026 (2 minutes 09), “Guh Long Down” on May 16, 2026 (2 minutes 18), “Jab Jab Army” on May 21, 2026 (3 minutes 05), “Capital P” on June 8, 2026 (2 minutes 45) and “Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon” on July 15, 2026 (2 minutes 14). That last title, the most recent of the five, is the one that carries the word bouyon and the name of a bouyon riddim, Havana Riddim, in its title itself.

The same page adds one featured title: “Active”, by Sabali, with Young star9ine and Supatronic, released on June 25, 2026 on 12790987 Records DK, running 3 minutes 10, with a writing credit shared between Lumiere Kejan Clarke, Keimaj Jakeim Clarke and Justin Felipe Harris. That featured title counts in Supatronic visible discography, but it states no bouyon tie on the opened page: it is therefore named here, in the text, and it does not enter the structured data of this page. On that page, only one title out of six verifies the bouyon link, and this profile rests on that single title alone.

What the title says, and what it does not say

“Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon” says two things at once. It names a riddim, Havana Riddim, following the practice of the riddim circuit where one instrumental can carry several voices, and it attaches the bouyon label to the title, readable by platforms, DJs and the audience that searches for the genre by name, as in our bouyon playlist. The rest of the page says nothing more about genre: not “Capital P”, not “Jab Jab Army”, not “Guh Long Down”, not “Pilot (Bajan Styling)” and not “Active” carries a bouyon mention, a soca mention or any other genre label in the opened listing. This profile therefore does not present them as bouyon titles.

Two title words call for the same caution. “Havana” is part of the riddim name, not a stated origin, and “Bajan Styling”, in the title of “Pilot”, is a circuit signal, a styling nod inside a title, not proof of origin. None of the opened listings names a country, an island or a town for Supatronic, gives a birthplace or a home base, or publishes any biography. Turning a title word into a passport would be exactly the kind of deduction this site refuses: the origin stays blank, and the only verified genre fact stays the word bouyon in the title of the single of July 15, 2026.

The limits of the file, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they are the most useful information it can give about Supatronic today. One Deezer artist page, five singles from 2026, one featured title, a single writer and composer credit repeated across every solo single, two distribution label references, and no biography, no interview, no official account confirmed beyond that page and no named origin: that is the whole verifiable file on October 20, 2026. Searches on the other platforms during this session returned no other primary page for this artist under this name.

If Supatronic publishes a biography, an official page or a new title that states its genre and its origin, this page will be completed and dated, like every page on this site, for example in the tracking of new bouyon in 2026. In the meantime, the verified listening fits into one title: “Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon”, 2 minutes 14, released on July 15, 2026 on 12690212 Records DK. One title, one named riddim, one credit, and an origin left open: that is little, and it is exactly what the file contains.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Supatronic?

Supatronic is the artist of five singles released in 2026 on 12690212 Records DK, listed on his Deezer artist page, all written and composed by Justin Felipe Harris. Only one of those titles states a bouyon tie: “Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon”. No public biography was found during this research.

Which Supatronic title is the verified bouyon one?

“Ouu La La (Havana Riddim) Bouyon”, a single released on July 15, 2026 on 12690212 Records DK, running 2 minutes 14, written and composed by Justin Felipe Harris. It is the only title whose opened primary page states bouyon, in its title itself, and the only title entered in the structured data of this page.

Where is Supatronic from?

His origin is named in none of the sources opened during this research. The word Bajan in the separate title “Pilot (Bajan Styling)” is a styling signal, not proof of origin, and the name Havana Riddim is the name of a riddim, not a place of origin. This site therefore assigns Supatronic no island.

Who is Justin Felipe Harris?

Justin Felipe Harris is the name credited as writer and composer on each of the five Supatronic singles listed on Deezer, and as a co writer of the Sabali title “Active”. No opened source states the exact link between that name and the artist name Supatronic: this profile quotes the credit as it stands, without drawing a biographical conclusion from it.

Which other titles appear on the Supatronic Deezer page?

Four further singles from 2026, all on 12690212 Records DK and all credited to Justin Felipe Harris: “Pilot (Bajan Styling)” on May 11, “Guh Long Down” on May 16, “Jab Jab Army” on May 21 and “Capital P” on June 8, plus one featured title, “Active” by Sabali with Young star9ine and Supatronic, on June 25, 2026. None of those titles states a bouyon tie on the opened page.

Why is this profile so short?

Because the verifiable file is short: one opened artist page, one title that states bouyon, and no documented biography or origin. This site prefers a short and exact profile to a portrait filled with deductions. It will grow if the artist publishes more primary sources.

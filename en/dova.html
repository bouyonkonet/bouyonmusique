Dova: Earthquake and the Ti Vilaj stage, bouyon, Roseau

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Dova is a name bouyon listeners know by ear before they know it from texts. Her track “Earthquake” sits inside Smokiller Bouyon Music 2026 Mix, her collaborations with Kenny G, Pudaz and Gwada G travel through the big mixes of the scene, and her name appears on the official list of artists confirmed for Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026, at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau. For the rest, the public record is thin, and this profile says so plainly: no verified biography, no confirmed real name, no confirmed official page. What follows gathers only what can be checked, track by track and list by list.

Who Dova is: what the record allows us to say, and what it does not

Dova is a voice of Dominican bouyon from the mix generation. Unlike the founding bands such as WCK, whose history is documented album by album, or the new wave artists who publish full biographies, Dova has, at the time this profile was written, no verifiable public biography. Research for this page across streaming platforms, YouTube and the Caribbean press returned no real name, no home village, no label and no confirmed official artist page.

That documentary silence does not mean an absence of career. It says something about how part of bouyon still travels: through DJ mixes, through riddims shared between artists, through festival lists, long before press releases and official pages. Dova name appears in exactly those three places, and that is where this profile looks for it, adding nothing that is not found there.

A note on method is therefore needed from the start. When a track is named here, it is named because it appears on a published list: the chapter list of the Smokiller mix for Bouyon Shattaz, or the tracklists of mixes released as podcasts. When a stage is named, it is named because a name appears on an official list reported by the press. Everything else, age, path, influences, personal life, stays off this page, waiting for sources.

Publishing a profile this openly incomplete may surprise. It follows the rule of this site: document the scene as it is, with its lit areas and its shadows, rather than only covering artists who are already well archived. Dova is part of the Dominican sound of 2026. The minimum is to record that correctly, and to record just as clearly everything that is still missing.

“Earthquake”: the track that places Dova in the reference bouyon mix of 2026

The most recent document carrying Dova name is the Bouyon Music 2026 Mix Vol. 1, a 41 minute nonstop mix made by Smokiller for Bouyon Shattaz and published on YouTube. The mix description presents DOVA among the best new Dominica bouyon songs of 2026, alongside Trilla-G, J Lion, Tipsy, Khallion and Reo. In the timed chapter list, DOVA “EARTHQUAKE” arrives at 30:48, between OG Park and Rum Lawd “Whale Fu See” and Slyme Hoyss “Back and Forth”.

That place carries a precise meaning. A mix of this kind works as a photograph of the season: it keeps the tracks the DJ judges able to hold the road, the fete and the warm up, to use the words of the description itself. Appearing there, with a solo track and not only as a collaborator, places Dova in the core of the artists the scene itself is putting forward for 2026, not in a long tail of names.

The chosen title, “Earthquake”, belongs to the usual vocabulary of party bouyon: an image of shaking, of ground moving under the dance, built to be understood at once by a carnival audience. This profile holds no published credit for the track: no producer, writer or label was confirmed in the sources opened for this page. The track is therefore cited for what it is, a recording in circulation inside the reference mix of the season, with no credits invented around it.

To hear this moment of the path, the most direct entry point remains our bouyon playlist, which follows the Dominican new releases kept by Bouyon Shattaz, season after season.

The documented collaborations: Kenny G, Pudaz and Gwada G

Before “Earthquake”, Dova name appears in the tracklists of two major reference mixes of the bouyon and soca scene. The first is Sewo Jam 2023-24, presented by Digga D as a homage to Dominica, where the pride of bouyon reigns supreme. Its track listing holds, at position 22, “Kenny G feat. Pudaz and Dova” for the title transcribed as “Push Ur Hand In Your”. The same track returns in the DJ Close Connections mix Soca Passport, devoted to bouyon and the French takeover, as “Kenny G, Dova and Pudaz, Push Your Hand In Your”, and in the Dbreakaway Show of 21 March 2024, in the short form “Pudaz and Dova”.

Those spelling and name order variants are typical of circulation by mix: one recording travels from DJ to DJ, and each list transcribes it in its own way. This profile therefore keeps the title in the fuller form “Push Your Hand In Your”, noting that published lists also write “Push Ur Hand In Your”. None of the sources consulted gives an official release date, label or production credit for this track, so none is stated here.

The second documented track is “What Name”, credited to “Kenny G, Gwada G and Dova” in that same Soca Passport mix. The company matters: Kenny G and Gwada G are, like Dova, names confirmed for the 2026 edition of Ti Vilaj Kweyol, which draws a small circle of Dominican artists who record together, travel in the same mixes and meet again on the same Creole week stages. That is, to date, the best documented collective context around Dova.

Two collaborations and one solo track: Dova verifiable discography stands, for now, at three recordings. That is little, and it is exactly why they are listed here with care, in the discography section of this page schema, rather than inflated into a catalogue the sources do not confirm.

Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026: the official confirmation, in Roseau

The strongest news fact in the record is a booking. According to the list reported by Dominica Weekly from the Government of Dominica official post, Dova appears among the first twenty artists confirmed for the fifth edition of Ti Vilaj Kweyol, the Creole village held at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau from 19 to 22 October 2026, under the theme Tout Biten Domnik, everything Dominican. Her name stands there alongside Shelly and the Signal Band, Ridge, TK International, Extasy, Pudaz, Reo, Unstoppable Shax and Trixx.

Ti Vilaj Kweyol is not just any stage. It is the cultural gathering of Dominica Independence celebrations, in the days just before the World Creole Music Festival, with live music, bouyon, quadrille and other folk performances, cultural demonstrations and more than ninety vendors. Being kept on the first official list, the list of twenty names, and not in a later addition, places Dova inside the national cultural programme, on the same footing as established bands of the island.

By 18 October 2026, an update had carried the total to twenty nine confirmed artists, including the addition of Kenny G, but the day by day schedule had not yet been published. This profile therefore gives neither the day nor the time of Dova performance, since no official source had given them at the time of writing. As soon as an official programme publishes them, this page will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

That booking also sheds light on the rest of the record. An artist can be absent from the databases and present on the national stage: the two findings do not contradict each other, they describe two different circuits, the digital archive circuit and the Dominican stage circuit itself. Our history of bouyon page tells how that stage was built, from the founding bands to the mix generations.

How bouyon travels when the archive does not follow: the mix circuit

Dova case illustrates a central working of bouyon today. A large part of Dominica new music does not pass first through albums or press campaigns: it passes through mixes. A DJ such as Smokiller, Digga D or DJ Close Connections assembles the season, track by track, and that tracklist becomes, in practice, the most complete public document on who is recording what, with whom, at a given moment.

That circuit has its strengths: it is fast, it stays close to the fetes and the carnival road, and it gives a chance to voices the industry has not yet formatted. It also has its blind spots: spellings vary, production credits disappear, dates get lost, and an artist can chain together tracks heard by thousands of listeners without leaving a single biographical page behind. Dova is, today, exactly in that situation.

Bouyon Shattaz documents this circuit from inside, since the mix carrying “Earthquake” is a house mix, made by Smokiller. That position gives this profile a particular responsibility: not to turn scene proximity into unverifiable claims. What the mix proves, it proves: the track exists, it is in circulation, it was kept to represent the new Dominican bouyon of 2026. What it does not prove, civil identity, origin, path, this profile does not guess.

If what follows gives Dova a better documented presence, official pages, credited releases, interviews, this page will grow with the record. In the meantime, it plays the role of an honest marker: one name, three verified tracks, one confirmed national stage, and a clear list of what remains to be confirmed.

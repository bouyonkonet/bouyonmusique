A band, not just a name on a track

Signal Band belongs to the tradition of Dominica live formations, the bands that make the island party music and hold a whole stage in front of an audience that came to dance. In a genre born with bands, that form matters: bouyon is not only a matter of producers and solo voices, it is also a matter of ensemble, collective energy and stage presence.

The Signal Band name now travels beyond the island fetes thanks to its tracks with Shelly. That collaboration is the clearest doorway for a new audience: an identified singer, a band behind him, and two songs that found their audience online. That is often how bouyon travels now, through one shared track, then through curiosity about the names carrying it.

Presenting Signal Band on BouyonMusique.com therefore corrects a frequent platform reflex: remembering only the voice at the front. Behind Where Did You Come From and What YOU Say there is a Dominican band, with its manager, its audience and its own place in the calendar of the scene. This profile makes that credit visible for listeners in Guadeloupe, Martinique, France and the wider Caribbean.

Two tracks that carried the name

Where Did You Come From, with Shelly, shows 334K views on the popular shelf of Smokiller YouTube channel. What YOU Say, also with Shelly, shows 283K views on the same shelf. Those figures are the ones recorded in Bouyon Shattaz coverage: they do not say everything about a career, but they show that both tracks moved beyond the local fete circuit to find a lasting online audience.

Both songs also sit in the Bouyon Shattaz ROAD MIX playlists. That presence matters for listening: inside a selection, a Signal Band track is measured against the other Dominican sounds of the moment, and the band gains listeners who were not looking for its name at first. That is the very principle of a scene playlist: moving tracks between audiences.

To hear these songs in context, go to our bouyon playlist page, then come back to this profile to place the band. And to discover the singer of these two collaborations, read the profile of Shelly, whose solo path completes the band story.

Jerlani Robinson: contracts in the open

Signal Band manager is Jerlani Robinson. He has spoken publicly in the first episode of Riddim Talk, the Bouyon Pulse format, about contracts, royalties and protecting artists intellectual property. In a scene where many agreements are still made by word of mouth, speaking on those subjects with the microphone open is a gesture that counts.

Those themes are not abstract for a band like Signal Band. A track that circulates online, enters playlists and is played on the island biggest stage raises practical questions: who is credited, who signed what, and how income returns to the musicians. A manager who asks those questions publicly helps his whole band, and also the artists listening who recognise the same situation.

Signal Band is also in the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic audit cohort, with a verified contact route. The band profile therefore sits at the crossing of the two stories Bouyon Shattaz tells in 2026: a stage story, with the festival, and a structure story, with rights. The two move together, or the party benefits people other than those who make it.

WCMF 2026: Night 3, with Shelly

Per published WCMF 2026 guides, Shelly and Signal Band play Night 3 of the festival, on Sunday October 25, 2026. The World Creole Music Festival is the great meeting point of Dominica Creole music, and its final night is a powerful moment: the weekend audience is there, the other Caribbean scenes are watching, and a Dominican band plays at home, in front of its most demanding audience.

Bouyon Shattaz named Signal Band among the five Dominican acts of its WCMF Countdown, alongside Reo, Asa Bantan, TK International and Midnight Groovers. That editorial selection states the band place in the 2026 landscape: neither a legend of the past nor a simple bill name, but a present act the outlet follows closely and puts forward for the festival.

To prepare for that night, read our World Creole Music Festival page and the profile of Shelly. The evening of Sunday October 25 will bring together, on one bill, the singer and the band of their trending tracks: it is their shared moment, built track by track, and it is played at home, in Dominica.

Understanding bouyon before the name

To read the story of Signal Band properly, bouyon first has to be put back at its source. The genre was created in Dominica, and its full story is told on our history of bouyon page. That reminder matters, because bouyon now travels through Guadeloupe, Martinique, France and the diaspora, sometimes far from the island that created it. Keeping the names of Dominican artists at the centre of the story keeps the map of the genre readable for new listeners.

Bouyon is band music, street music and party music as much as it is listening music. A track can live in a playlist in the morning, in a fete in the evening and on a big stage the following month. That is why a profile like this one is not limited to a list of songs: it is also about the stage, the audience and the place the island holds inside its own creation.

This page therefore follows a simple rule: verified facts only, titles attributed when they are a matter of reputation, and no invented numbers. When something is not confirmed, it is not written. That discipline is part of the work of Bouyon Shattaz, which documents the Dominican scene for French-speaking listeners as well as for the island's own English-speaking audience.

Rights, the other stage

Behind the party sits a question the Dominican scene is now asking more loudly: who owns the music, who signs the contracts and who is paid when a track travels? A song can fill a room and circulate online without its creators being properly protected. That is exactly the ground of the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic, an audit carried out with artists and bands on the island to check contact routes, credits and the protection of their work.

Signal Band is part of that Rights Clinic audit cohort, with a verified contact route. The detail may sound administrative, but it changes everything that follows: an artist who can be reached, who is correctly credited and who is well advised can turn one strong season into a lasting career. Bouyon Shattaz argues this point across its whole network strategy: Dominica created bouyon, so its artists should also benefit from its value.

The subject also returns often in public conversations around the scene, around contracts, royalties and intellectual property. Putting those questions on the same level as releases and concerts is a way of recognising that Creole music deserves the same professional standards as any other music industry.

Finally, the event that brings all of these worlds together remains the World Creole Music Festival, the WCMF, three nights in Roseau that place Dominica's Creole music in front of an audience from across the Caribbean. Frequently asked questions

What are Signal Band's best-known songs?

The two tracks highlighted in Bouyon Shattaz coverage are “Where Did You Come From” and “What YOU Say”, with Shelly, at 334K and 283K views on the popular shelf of Smokiller's YouTube channel. Add “Bye to de Old Me”, with Shelly, released as a single in 2025, and the album “The Electric Room” (2024).

Where is Signal Band from?

Signal Band is a bouyon band from Dominica. It belongs to the island's tradition of live bands, the groups that make its party music and hold a whole stage in front of a crowd that came to dance. In a genre born with bands, from WCK in Roseau in 1988 to today's groups, that collective form remains central.

What is Signal Band's album?

“The Electric Room”, released in 2024 with Shelly, includes “Mic Check 12345678”, “Playground”, “Yes Baby”, “My Cake”, “Math Not Mathing”, also with Trilla-G, “Passing” and “In the Morning”. According to Black Island Girl's report, “Electric Room” won album of the year at the Dominica Music Awards on January 3, 2026.

What awards has Signal Band won?

According to Black Island Girl, Signal Band won six awards at the second Dominica Music Awards, on January 3, 2026, including band of the year, album of the year for “Electric Room” and video of the year for “Bye to the Old Me”. In 2026, “Shelly and Signall Band”, as the name was printed, is also among the bouyon artist of the year finalists at the Caribbean Music Awards.

Who produced Bye to de Old Me?

According to production notes published by Sokah2Soca and picked up by Island Vybe, the version performed by Shelly, Nice and Pudaz was composed and produced by Dernel "Dernz" Green, with additional riddim programming by Fr3EzE and live bass by Giovannie "Gio" Green. The song also exists as a Signal Band single with Shelly, released in 2025.

Who has Signal Band collaborated with?

Beyond its tracks with Shelly, Signal Band recorded “We Ready” (2023) and “Math Not Mathing” (2024) with Trilla-G, a “Stronger” remix with Nice, released on January 22, 2024, and “Make Her Bawl” with DJ Taffy and Trixx, listed among Dominica's trending bouyon and soca songs in late April 2025. See DJ Taffy.

What is Signal Band's Stronger?

“Stronger (feat. Shelly & Nice) [Remix]” is a Signal Band single released on January 22, 2024 on Signal Band Inc., according to its Shazam listing. That version was a finalist for collaboration of the year at the 2025 Dominica Music Awards, and the band's catalogue makes it one of its reference tracks of the period. Carnival mixes credit it as “Stronger Remix”.

When does Signal Band play WCMF 2026?

According to published festival guides, Shelly and Signal Band play Night 3 of WCMF 2026, on Sunday October 25, 2026, the closing night. A few days earlier, the official Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026 programme also places Shelly and Signal Band as the closing act of the grand finale, on Thursday October 22 at 7 pm, at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau.

Who manages Signal Band?

The band's manager is Jerlani Robinson. He spoke publicly in the first episode of Riddim Talk, the Bouyon Pulse format, about contracts, royalties and protecting artists' intellectual property. In a scene where many deals are still made verbally, those questions directly concern a band whose songs circulate online.

Where can I listen to Signal Band?

“Where Did You Come From” and “What YOU Say” sit in Bouyon Shattaz's ROAD MIX playlists, on Spotify and YouTube, where the band's songs measure up against the other Dominican sounds of the moment and reach listeners who were not looking for its name.

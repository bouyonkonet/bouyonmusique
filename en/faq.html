Definition and origin

What is bouyon music?

Bouyon is a music genre born in Dominica in the late 1980s. It mixes jing ping and cadence-lypso with traditional dances like bele, quadrille and chante mas, mazurka and zouk, all driven by keyboards and fast electronic rhythms. Its name comes from Antillean Creole, where bouyon means a soup: a mix of everything. The full guide: what is bouyon.

Where does bouyon come from?

Bouyon comes from Roseau, Dominica, where it was created in the late 1980s. In a Caribbean dominated by zouk, Dominican musicians looked for a sound of their own, built on jing ping, lapo kabwit and cadence-lypso. The French Antilles, which share Creole with Dominica, adopted it quickly, and then France did too. See the origin of bouyon.

Who created bouyon?

Bouyon was created in Dominica by the band WCK (Windward Caribbean Kulture), formed in 1988 in Roseau. Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade are also among the original groups of the genre. More details on the history of bouyon page.

What does WCK stand for?

WCK stands for Windward Caribbean Kulture, the culture of the Windward Islands. It is the founding band of bouyon, formed in Roseau in 1988: it fused cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. In the 1990s, its bouyon became the music of Roseau carnival and of village fetes. Full profile: WCK.

Where does the word bouyon come from?

In Antillean Creole, bouyon means something like a gumbo soup, a slow-cooked dish typical of the Caribbean. The name illustrates the principle of the genre: a mix of traditional and modern music. Jing ping, cadence-lypso, bele, quadrille, chante mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk all go into the same pot, where everything blends and nothing is lost. See the meaning of bouyon.

Styles and differences

What is the difference between bouyon and soca?

Bouyon was born in Dominica from jing ping and cadence-lypso, with a raw, fast, keyboard-heavy sound. Soca comes from Trinidad and mixes calypso with Indian influences. The two meet in bouyon soca, a fusion popular in Saint Lucia, Guadeloupe and Martinique, but in Dominica bouyon keeps a clear, distinct style. Full comparison: bouyon vs soca.

What is the difference between bouyon and zouk?

Zouk comes from Guadeloupe and Martinique, popularized in the early 1980s by bands like Kassav: a clean, danceable sound, romantic in its zouk love version. Bouyon, born in Dominica, takes the opposite path: faster, rawer, more carnival than ballroom. Zouk is even one of the ingredients of bouyon. See bouyon vs zouk.

Are bouyon and shatta the same thing?

No. They are two distinct genres. Bouyon is the historic genre, created in 1988 in Dominica, with its creators, its classics and its carnivals. Shatta is a dancehall genre born in Martinique, associated with the short, hard riddims of today's Antillean producers, made for dancing and social media. Their direct meeting point is shatta bouyon. See bouyon vs shatta.

Why is bouyon called jump up?

In Guadeloupe and Martinique, bouyon is commonly called jump up, because its fast tempo literally makes the crowd jump during fetes and carnivals. The term already described very rhythmic carnival music, notably after Exile One's L'hivernage in 1987: Dominican bouyon was naturally placed in that family. See jump up.

What is Wrecketeng?

Wrecketeng is a Dominican style derived from bouyon, alongside bouyon soca and bouyon-muffin. It is built on sampled melodies and rap vocals, and it is cited as a root of modern bouyon and of RnBouyon. Its official spelling, Wrecketeng, comes from DJ Cut himself, the founding member who pioneered the style; the 'reketeng' form spread across the internet is a misspelling that became common. See wrecketeng.

What is bouyon gwada?

Bouyon gwada, also called hardcore bouyon, is the branch of bouyon born from collaborations between musicians from Dominica and Guadeloupe, two islands that share Creole. Harder, faster and more electronic than the classic bouyon of live bands, it pushes drum machines, bass and vocals in Guadeloupean Creole. See bouyon gwada.

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the current and most visible form of bouyon on social media and in clubs: short, hard, danceable riddims made by the new Antillean and French generation. It keeps the markers of the genre created by WCK, fast tempo, looping keyboards, synth bass and Creole on the mic, with digital, minimal production. See shatta bouyon.

Rhythm, instruments and production

What is the BPM of bouyon?

Bouyon is a fast genre, and its tempo is part of its identity. The producer HYPNO, for example, claims a track at 160 bpm, Mauvais Plan. That speed comes from traditional Dominican dances and from carnival competition. But speed alone does not make bouyon: without the percussion and the spirit of Dominica, it is just a fast beat. See bouyon BPM.

What instruments are used in bouyon?

Bouyon combines traditional Dominican instruments with machines. On the traditional side: the lapo kabwit, a goatskin drum, the bele drum, the chakchak, the syak or gwaj, the jing ping accordion, the bamboo flute and the boom boom. On the machine side: keyboards up front, synthetic rhythm patterns and electronic bass. See bouyon instruments.

How do you make a bouyon beat?

Start with the rhythm: percussion inspired by lapo kabwit and jing ping, fast and steady drums, fills that imitate carnival calls. Add a short, repetitive keyboard loop, a heavy and simple synth bass, then crowd vocals. The final test: dance to it. The step-by-step method: how to make a bouyon beat.

Dance, carnival and festivals

How do you dance bouyon?

There is no single official step: every era and every riddim imposes its own moves, swaying on the choruses, collective jumps on the build-ups, danced answers to the calls of the singer or the DJ. At carnival, the dance is collective and free. On social media, producers dance on their own beats to launch the movement. See bouyon dance.

What is the link between bouyon and Dominica carnival?

Bouyon is the soundtrack of Dominica's carnival, Mas Domnik, celebrated in Roseau before Lent. In the 1990s it became its music, and every carnival season launches the competition of bouyon songs: bands and producers release their new tracks, and the public picks its favorites. See Dominica carnival.

What is the World Creole Music Festival?

The World Creole Music Festival, or WCMF, is Dominica's biggest music event: three nights of Creole music in Roseau, every year in October, during the island's independence celebrations. Bouyon, cadence, zouk and kompa share the stage. The 2026 edition runs from Friday 23 to Sunday 25 October at Windsor Park. See World Creole Music Festival.

Listening to bouyon and following the scene

What are the best bouyon songs?

For the classics, start with the founding bands WCK, Roots Stems and Branches and First Serenade, then the carnival songs of the 1990s and 2000s. On the recent side: Life Nice by Asa Bantan, Joli BB by Reo with Maurane Voyer, FIX UP by G'z Up, Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026) by Klass Money produced by Smokiller, and Mauvais Plan by HYPNO. See bouyon songs.

Where can I listen to bouyon in 2026?

The Bouyon Road Mix by Bouyon Shattaz brings together new releases and classics on Spotify and YouTube, mixing bouyon, bouyon soca and shatta bouyon. On Instagram, @bouyonshattaz publishes news about the genre and sends the playlist by DM to people who comment ROAD MIX.

Is bouyon popular in France, Guadeloupe and Martinique?

Yes. Guadeloupe is the second home of bouyon, where bouyon gwada was born, and Martinique lives it under the name jump up. France has become the genre's big market: bouyon songs have earned gold single certifications there, and Paris is home to a large Antillean and Dominican diaspora. See bouyon in France.

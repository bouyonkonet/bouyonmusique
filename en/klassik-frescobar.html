Klassik Frescobar: the Miami soca voice that plugged into bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Klassik Frescobar, real name Kamaal Burrowes, is a Caribbean soca singer based in Miami, formed between Kingston, the Bronx and Florida, who became in 2025 one of the faces of Dennery Segment with “Dansa”, ranked the number two soca song of the year by SocaRecords. He is neither Dominican nor, in the strict sense, a bouyon artist, and this profile says so at the door. His place on this site rests on three verified contact points: “Eye Spy”, his record with V’GHN released on January 16, 2026, described by 24Hip-Hop as grounded in Bouyon rhythmic backbone; the production of that record and of “Dansa” by Boogy Rankss, a craftsman of current Dominican bouyon; and his presence, with Trixzmigos, on the record “Take Dat”. This profile tells his full path, from his mother cassette player in Kingston to the viral charts of “Dansa”, then draws exactly where his link with bouyon stops.

Who Klassik Frescobar is: Kamaal Burrowes, and origin stories that vary

Klassik Frescobar is the stage name of Kamaal Burrowes. The civil name is the most solid point of the file: it appears in the writing credits of his records on Shazam, where “Dansa” is written by Kallicharran Randy Changoo, Kamaal Burrowes and Norris E. Hamilton, and “Boom (Fuego Riddim)” by Kamaal Burrowes with Lashley Winter, Kevin Alexander and Anwar Dacosta. 24Hip-Hop presented him the same way in January 2026: Klassik Frescobar, born Kamaal Burrowes. On the man behind the name, the sources converge on a path: a childhood marked by Kingston, a passage through New York, and a current base in Miami, where both The Source and 24Hip-Hop place him.

On the exact starting point, however, the published accounts do not all say the same thing, and this profile shows the disagreement instead of painting over it. Several detailed biographical portraits, published from 2021 onward by Film Daily, Muzique Magazine, HipHopSince1987 and The Source, describe an artist born in New York, raised in Kingston, Jamaica by his grandparents, then back in New York at the age of thirteen. His Deezer artist page, for its part, presents a Jamaican-American singer whose musical influence began in the early 1990s in his hometown of Kingston, in the Constant Spring Gardens neighbourhood, before an emigration to the Bronx. 24Hip-Hop, in 2026, says more briefly that he is originally from Jamaica and now based in Miami. The careful formulation, and the one used on this site, is therefore the following: an artist of Jamaican origin by upbringing and formation, born according to several portraits in New York, raised in Kingston, and based in Miami. No source ties him to Dominica, and he is never presented as Dominican.

The stage name itself has a story, told by a She Got Game Media portrait: as a teenager back in New York, he wrote his first rap lyrics under the name Sikk, which he later turned into Klassik, adding Frescobar, built, in that account, from “fresh” and from “bar”, the musical term. Deezer regularly prints the variant Klassik Escobar in its biography, a sign that the name circulates in neighbouring forms. Musically, the portraits agree on the blend that defines him: the intensity of Jamaican dancehall, the party spirit of soca and touches of calypso, the profile of a soca singjay rather than a live band singer.

The apprenticeship: Kingston, the Bronx, then Miami

The biography published by Deezer gives the musical starting point with rare precision. In the early 1990s, in Kingston, in the Constant Spring Gardens neighbourhood, the child he then was listened, on his mother cassette recorder, to the early records of Bounty Killa, Sean Paul and Beenie Man, the voices then dominating Jamaican dancehall. The older portraits add the soca and calypso heard on Jamaican radio: from childhood, his ear was therefore shared between the dancehall of his island of formation and the carnival musics that cross the whole English speaking Caribbean.

The New York stage is rap and dancehall loyalty at once. Deezer describes a teenager who emigrated to the Bronx and devoted most of his time to writing rap, while staying close to his roots by attending local dancehall parties, where he built relationships with DJs and producers, including Ricky Blaze, a Brooklyn native who, like him, comes from Jamaica. The 2021 portraits specify the return to the United States at thirteen, the Bronx battle rap scene and the first stage name, Sikk. That double culture, American rap on one side, Caribbean sound system on the other, explains the shape of his adult records: tight rapped and sung verses laid over party riddims.

The decisive move came in 2014. Deezer and the portraits agree: he settled in Miami, where he worked closely with Guyanese rapper Red Cafe, who introduced him to the local DJs and party scenes. The choice of Miami is a diaspora choice: South Florida concentrates a large Caribbean community, regular soca parties and an audience that lives carnival all year, where New York concentrates it in one season. It was in Miami that he moved from rap to soca as his main genre, encouraged, the portraits say, by figures around him such as LL Cool Blaze, and it was from Miami that he built, as an independent, the catalog of the next section.

The breakthrough: from “Boom” to “Looking Good”, building a catalog

The first record in the file verified by a platform song page is “Boom (Fuego Riddim)”, released on August 1, 2019 on Island Circle, at 109 BPM, with Kamaal Burrowes as lead writer. Deezer places the breakthrough the same year on the Miami soca scene, with three records: “Boom”, “Bee Sting” and “Chair”. The portraits of the time confirm the mechanism: “Boom”, carried by a dance challenge, installed his name in Florida soca parties. It was not yet a worldwide streaming success, but a ground level success, the kind that makes an artist a name DJs play and audiences request.

The first viral fact arrived in 2020 with “Looking Good”, on the Code Red Riddim. Deezer describes a record propelled on social media by the #LookingGoodChallenge, which gathered over three million views, a challenge inviting dancers around the world to film themselves to the song. The 2021 portraits add that the record was shared by Cardi B with her millions of Instagram followers: for an independent artist from the Miami soca scene, that is a change of scale, and the start of a method, records designed for dancing and social reuse as much as for radio.

What followed is a steady run of singles, which the Deezer biography lines up year by year: “Like Dat Remix” with GBM Nutron and “Too Much” with Marzville in 2021, “Eye Watch” and “Make Contact” in 2022, “Pose” and “Bigman” in 2023, “Instruction Time” and “Choppa Slide” in 2024. That same year, 2024, he gathered seven tracks on the Bronxghanistan EP, including “Forever”, released on December 27, 2024 under his own banner according to Shazam, written by Kamaal Burrowes alone. In 2025 came Sal Gason, then the record that changed his scale: “Dansa”. This catalog matters for understanding what followed: when “Dansa” exploded, he was no unknown, but an independent artist with six years of singles, an EP and an already loyal party audience.

“Dansa”: the Dennery record that went around the world

“Dansa” appeared in January 2025: Shazam dates the publication to January 24, 2025, and SocaRecords speaks of a late January release. The record is written by Kallicharran Randy Changoo, Kamaal Burrowes and Norris E. Hamilton, and produced by DJ Kalli and Boogy Rankss, two names this site knows well on the bouyon side, Boogy Rankss being notably the beatmaker credited on “Toxic Love” by Trixzmigos. The record is classed by its own pages and visuals as a Dennery Segment record, the party sound of Saint Lucia, and not as a bouyon record: that genre precision is central, and the next section returns to it.

The path of the record is that of a slow success turned global. SocaRecords, in its year end review, describes a record that passed almost unnoticed by soca charts in the first quarter, then climbed organically from the second quarter, with sharp acceleration in the second half of the year, driven by African markets, notably Kenya and Nigeria. In the SocaRecords year end chart, “Dansa” finished as the number two soca song of 2025, with close to ten million Spotify streams across the year. The Source, in June 2026, counted nearly fifteen million Spotify streams, iTunes World number ones in a long list of countries, from France to Uganda and Switzerland, a number three iTunes World peak in the United States, and a place in Spotify UK Carnival Sounds campaign as well as in the promotion of Notting Hill Carnival.

Institutional recognition followed. The Source and 24Hip-Hop both report Caribbean Music Awards nominations, for Soca Song of the Year and Dennery Song of the Year according to The Source, in a Best Soca and Dennery Song formulation according to 24Hip-Hop. The Source adds a fact that sums up the path: “Dansa” was reportedly the first soca track to enter Spotify Viral 100 chart in France in a decade, with a number two viral peak in Nigeria and a number one in Sierra Leone. Deezer, for its part, noted a number twenty one placing in overseas charts. The engine of all this, according to The Source, is one word: TikTok, where months of dance videos carried the record far beyond any promotional machine, the artist keeping the momentum alive with a visualizer filmed in front of a Florida liquor store.

The bouyon thread: “Eye Spy”, Boogy Rankss and “Take Dat”

Klassik Frescobar link with bouyon becomes explicit on January 16, 2026, with “Eye Spy”, his first collaboration with V’GHN, one of Grenada notable voices. 24Hip-Hop, which devoted a full article to the record, describes it as grounded in Bouyon rhythmic backbone, pulling in subtle influences that reflect both artists personalities. Production is by DJ Kalli and Boogy Rankss, the same pair as on “Dansa”. The V’GHN artist page on Shazam lists the single under the credit DJ Kalli, Klassik Frescobar & V’ghn, among his 2026 releases. The article describes a fast build: after connecting with the producers in New York, Frescobar helped map out the concept and structure in a short session, the record went to V’GHN, who completed his part within a day. The record also carries a sample from the anime Inuyasha, woven in, according to 24Hip-Hop, without gimmick, as a window onto the artist personal influences.

Boogy Rankss is the hinge of this whole file. A producer associated with the sound of the new Dominican wave, credited on the beat of Trixzmigos records, he produced both Klassik Frescobar biggest Dennery record and his explicitly bouyon one. And the link tightens one notch further: this site research on Trixzmigos documented “Take Dat”, a record shared by Trixzmigos with Klassik Frescobar and Boogy Rankss, listed in the group 2026 catalog. Klassik Frescobar is therefore no distant name for the Dominican scene: he sings on a record by one of its rising collectives, and he works with one of its producers, on two records, one of which claims the bouyon backbone in its own press presentation.

The third contact point is thinner, and this profile grades it as such. A record titled “Perfect”, credited to Reno and Klassik Frescobar, circulates in soca and bouyon mixtape tracklists, including the Jamz Podcast Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix. No platform song page for that record was opened during this research, so it does not enter the track list in the structured data of this page, and it is cited here for what it is: a trace of mixtape circulation, showing the DJs of the soca and bouyon zone placing his voice in their selections, without amounting to a verified release page. The distinction between a documented record and a record heard in a mix is exactly the kind of border this site maintains, artist by artist.

Exact position, and the limits of the file

Klassik Frescobar position can be summed up without dressing. He is a soca and Dennery Segment artist, of Jamaican origin by formation, born according to several portraits in New York, raised in Kingston, based in Miami, author of a record, “Dansa”, that ranks among the biggest independent soca successes of the streaming era. His link with bouyon is real but precise: one record, “Eye Spy”, presented by its press as built on the bouyon backbone, one hinge producer, Boogy Rankss, one feature in the Trixzmigos catalog, and a circulation in soca and bouyon mixes. It does not make him a bouyon artist, and this site does not file him as one: it documents him as a catalog neighbour, one of those through whom the sounds of the English speaking Caribbean actually mix, record by record.

That neighbourhood also has a genre logic the reader should keep in mind. Bouyon was born in Dominica, and bouyon soca, one of its sung forms, was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica, per the first hand testimony of Smokiller, before being popularized by Triple Kay. Dennery Segment comes from Saint Lucia. When a Miami artist formed in Kingston lays his voice over those two cousin rhythms, with the same producers, he draws the real map of the English speaking Caribbean party in 2026: distinct island sounds, shared workshops, and audiences who no longer hear the difference at night, even if the credits, rightly, must keep making it.

The limits of the file are the following. The published biographies diverge on the birthplace, New York according to the detailed portraits, Kingston as hometown according to Deezer and 24Hip-Hop, and this profile chose to show the disagreement rather than settle it without further proof. The “Dansa” figures are those published by The Source and SocaRecords on the dates stated and will keep moving. The record “Perfect” has no verified platform page to date in this research. For accurate listening, start with “Dansa” and “Eye Spy”, continue with “Boom”, “Looking Good”, “Too Much” and “Forever”, and find the Dominican side of the story on the Trixzmigos and bouyon soca pages, and in our bouyon playlist.

No, in the strict sense of the credits. “Dansa” is presented by its own pages and visuals as a Dennery Segment record, the party sound of Saint Lucia, carried by a DJ Kalli and Boogy Rankss production. Klassik Frescobar link with bouyon runs through another record, “Eye Spy” with V’GHN, described by 24Hip-Hop as grounded in Bouyon rhythmic backbone, and through his collaboration with the Dominican collective Trixzmigos on “Take Dat”.

What is Klassik Frescobar link with bouyon?

Three verified points. “Eye Spy”, released on January 16, 2026 with V’GHN and produced by DJ Kalli and Boogy Rankss, is described by 24Hip-Hop as built on Bouyon rhythmic backbone. Boogy Rankss, a producer of the new Dominican wave, also produced “Dansa”. And Klassik Frescobar appears, with Boogy Rankss, on “Take Dat”, in the Trixzmigos catalog. A record titled “Perfect”, with Reno, also circulates in soca and bouyon mixes, with no verified platform page to date.

Where can I hear Klassik Frescobar?

Through “Dansa” and “Eye Spy” for the recent side, then “Boom (Fuego Riddim)”, released in 2019, “Looking Good”, the viral 2020 record, “Too Much” with Marzville and “Forever”, taken from the Bronxghanistan EP in 2024.

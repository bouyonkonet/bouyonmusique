Tenslay: the Vcvc motif, bouyon, France and Guadeloupe

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Tenslay is an artist name you meet in bouyon streaming numbers without ever meeting it in the press. No article, no interview, no published portrait tells this singer story: everything the public record holds sits in platform credits and rankings. One dated, labelled single, “Apb Vcvc” with DJ TILOV, released on 10 June 2022 on WORLDFIRE at 160 BPM. A second track, “Doglife! Vcvc”, which Spotify files in its playlist The Sound of Bouyon. A handful of further tracks listed by the analytics tool Viberate, which presents Tenslay as an artist from France, files him under reggae fusion, and yet places his audience first in France, then in Guadeloupe and Martinique. This profile tells exactly that: a small, coherent catalogue, a title motif that works as a signature, and a filing disagreement that says a lot about how bouyon travels today.

Who Tenslay is: a name in capitals, a record in dotted lines

Start with what is established, because with Tenslay the list is short and it has to be kept clean. The platforms write his name in capitals, TENSLAY, or in lower case, tenslay, depending on their house style. Viberate, the music analytics tool that aggregates streaming data, presents him as an artist from France and attaches him to the reggae and reggae fusion genres. That is, to date, the only structured artist record the research for this profile found. No city is given on it, no checkable biography comes with it, and the long presentation text the platform displays is generic copy, interchangeable with that of hundreds of other artists: this site therefore does not repeat it as information.

What the platforms do give solidly is tracks, credits and rankings. The recurring partner in the catalogue is called DJ TILOV: he co-signs Tenslay two best documented tracks, “Apb Vcvc” and “Doglife! Vcvc”. Around them sit three more tracks Viberate lists among the artist most played: “Douxtcha”, “DOGLIFE #VCVC2” and “DO WHAT YOU WANT”. Five tracks, one regular collaborator, one label named once, one precise date: that is the record. It is thin, and this profile says so from the introduction instead of dressing it up as a portrait.

That thinness is no exception in today bouyon, it is close to the norm for a whole layer of its artists. The genre, born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s and carried since then by the scenes of Guadeloupe, Martinique and the diaspora in France, lives first in parties, playlists and clips passed from phone to phone. Many singers exist there fully for their audience without ever passing through a press sheet, an article or a written artist page. Tenslay visibly belongs to that layer: present in the numbers, absent from the narratives.

The rule of this site, already applied to the poorly documented producers of the genre early days, therefore applies here unchanged: publish what can be checked, attribute every fact to the platform that gives it, and name the gaps instead of filling them. The pages that follow contain no invented origin, no age, no imagined path. They contain a catalogue, a motif, one complete credit, an audience geography and a filing problem. For an artist with a record this size, that is already a story, and it is the only true one.

The Vcvc motif: three tracks, a signature with no explanation

The most striking fact in Tenslay catalogue fits in four letters: Vcvc. The motif appears in the title of the documented 2022 single, “Apb Vcvc”, in “Doglife! Vcvc”, and again in “DOGLIFE #VCVC2”, which presents itself, by its very title, as a second version or a sequel to the Doglife track. Three of the five tracks therefore carry the same string of letters, rare enough not to be a typing accident, repeated enough to work as a sound signature, a tag the listener recognises before the first bar has played.

What does Vcvc mean? None of the sources opened for this profile says. No interview in which the artist explains it, no presentation text that decodes it, no annotated lyrics page that translates it. This site therefore offers no invented etymology. In bouyon and dancehall titles, strings of letters often serve as rallying cries, onomatopoeia or codes internal to a crew, but assigning one of those functions to Vcvc without a source would be exactly the kind of claim this profile refuses to fabricate. The motif is documented. Its meaning is not. Both sentences stand together.

The rest of the catalogue completes the picture without contradicting it. “Douxtcha” tops the list of Tenslay most played tracks according to Viberate, ahead of the two Vcvc tracks signed with DJ TILOV. “DO WHAT YOU WANT”, written in capitals in the listings, closes that list of five. No verified release date comes with those three tracks in the open sources: this profile cites them as tracks listed by the analytics tool, no more, no less, and gives them no year, no label and no extra collaborator.

A five track catalogue can look small beside the discographies of the genre historic bands, such as WCK. It should be read for what it is: the platform trace of a singer and DJ project that chose to sign its tracks with one shared motif rather than multiply formats. In a genre where tracks often live one carnival season before dropping off the lists, building a recognisable recurrence is an identity move. Tenslay made it with four letters. What those letters mean remains, for now, his business.

The one complete credit: “Apb Vcvc”, 10 June 2022, WORLDFIRE, 160 BPM

Only one track in the catalogue carries a complete, dated credit, and this profile gives it in full, because it is the strongest piece in the record. According to the track page on Shazam, “Apb Vcvc (feat. DJ TILOV)” was released on 10 June 2022, on the label WORLDFIRE, in the French language, with a tempo measured at 160 BPM. The same page points to an associated video, titled “TENSLAY - APB VCVC (DJ TILOV)”. One single, one date, one label, one language, one tempo: that is little for a biography, but it is exactly the kind of fact a serious discography adds up, track by track, without inflating it.

The detailed credits of the same track add a piece of information that only shows when the page is read to the end. Shazam credits tenslay as performer, and assigns the writing of the track, in the composition and lyrics section, to a name printed “stanley henri”. This site reproduces that credit exactly as the platform publishes it, without making more of it than it is: it is the only civil style name attached to the Tenslay project in the open credits. No source confirms that it is the singer legal name, no document ties it to a city or a date of birth, and this profile therefore does not turn a songwriter credit into a civil record.

The tempo deserves a stop, because it ties the track to its musical family better than a genre label does. At 160 BPM, “Apb Vcvc” runs at the speed of modern bouyon club tracks, the speed of short loops and choruses built to be sung back at parties. The French language of the track does the rest of the positioning: Tenslay sings in the language of the bouyon scenes of Guadeloupe, Martinique and France, not in that of the English speaking scenes of Dominica or Saint Lucia. A platform credit cannot tell a life, but it can place a sound, and this one places it without ambiguity.

Finally, note what this credit does not contain. No producer is named beyond the DJ TILOV feature, no other writer, no publisher readable in the open sources. The label WORLDFIRE, cited for this single, is attached to no other track in the catalogue in the record consulted. The verified Tenslay discography therefore stands as one complete line and four partial lines. This profile publishes it that way, because an honest short discography is worth more than a long invented one, and because future corrections will be able to build on lines like this one.

Filed as reggae, shelved as bouyon: the platforms disagree

The Tenslay record holds a contradiction this profile does not smooth over, because it is instructive. On one side, Viberate files the artist under reggae and reggae fusion, and the neighbourhood the tool assigns him is that of reggae and dancehall artists from France and the Caribbean. On the other, Spotify files “Doglife! Vcvc” in its playlist The Sound of Bouyon, among tracks of the genre, with a duration of 2 minutes and 3 seconds. Both filings cannot be equally right, and no press article settles the question, for the simple reason that there is no press on this artist at all.

A third element leans toward bouyon, and it comes from radio. The broadcast logs of the station Hitz Africa FM, carried by the site Online Radio Box, show “Doglife! Vcvc” by Tenslay and DJ TILOV in rotation, in lists where bouyon tracks of the same generation also circulate. A rotation does not make an identity, but it shows where the track is actually programmed and heard: in an Afro-Caribbean flow where bouyon holds a central place, not in a roots reggae flow. The audience geography, as we will see, tells the same story as the Spotify playlist.

How, then, to explain the reggae fusion label? Analytics tools file artists from declared metadata, listening neighbourhoods and automatic texts, and they regularly get small Creole scenes wrong, scenes whose fine categories, bouyon, bouyon soca, shatta, Antillean dancehall, are badly cut in grids designed for Anglo-Saxon markets. Bouyon itself was long shelved under neighbouring labels, soca, jump up or dancehall, by outside observers. Tenslay inherits, at his scale, that old filing problem of the genre, documented on our bouyon gwada page.

The position of this site is therefore measured, and it is written in black and white: Tenslay appears among the bouyon artists of this site because his documented tracks circulate in the playlists and rotations of the genre, because his working language is the French of the bouyon scenes, and because his measured audience is that of the France, Guadeloupe, Martinique triangle. His reggae fusion label at Viberate is reported as a platform fact, not adopted as an identity. If a primary source, a text from the artist or his circle, one day specifies his positioning, this profile will be corrected and dated, like every page on this site.

An audience in France, Guadeloupe and Martinique: the geography that speaks

Lacking a biography, Tenslay has a geography, and it can be read in the audience map Viberate publishes for the artist. According to that map, consulted in October 2026, France accounts for 77.83 percent of his measured audience, ahead of Guadeloupe with 14.17 percent and Martinique with 2.68 percent. These figures are analytics measurements, valid for the period they cover, and this profile cites them as such. But their order is stable, and it draws, without any need to invent an address for the artist, the real territory of his listening.

That triangle is not just any triangle: it is the triangle of French speaking bouyon. The genre was born in Dominica, an English and Creole speaking island, at the end of the 1980s, but its second life, the life of club scenes, playlists and French language tracks, plays out between Guadeloupe, Martinique and mainland France, where a large Antillean diaspora lives. An artist heard first in France, then clearly in Guadeloupe, weakly but really in Martinique, occupies exactly the position of the singers of the Guadeloupe bouyon scene and its diaspora, whether his passport or his home city are known or not.

The same map also explains why the reggae fusion label is not enough to describe the project. The reggae scenes of France have their own geography, their own circuits of sound systems and festivals, and they do not spontaneously produce a Guadeloupe audience share of this size for an artist with no press and no documented tour. A double digit Guadeloupe share, for a singer of French language tracks at 160 BPM co-signed by a DJ, looks far more like the footprint of bouyon circulation: parties, genre playlists and sharing inside Antillean audiences.

The limit has to be repeated before closing this page: an audience map measures listening, not an origin. This profile deduces neither the birth, nor the residence, nor the identity of Tenslay from those percentages. It simply notes that the audience hearing his tracks is, in the available measurements, the audience of French speaking bouyon. For an artist with no press record, that is the most faithful portrait the data allows, and it is enough to justify his place in these pages, among the artists of the scene our bouyon playlist follows day by day.

What this profile does not know, and how to hear Tenslay

Close with the gaps, because they are part of the record and naming them protects the reader against any overly complete portrait they may meet elsewhere. No open source gives, for Tenslay, a confirmed full name beyond the songwriter credit “stanley henri”, nor a city, nor a date of birth, nor a path, nor a team, nor a home label beyond the 2022 WORLDFIRE single. No official social account was verified during the research for this profile: this page therefore links to no social network, and its schema holds no sameAs link, rather than a guessed link that could lead to a namesake.

No verified release date exists either, in the sources consulted, for “Douxtcha”, “DOGLIFE #VCVC2” and “DO WHAT YOU WANT”. Those tracks are real in the platform listing sense, they are played, “Douxtcha” at the head of the Viberate measurements, but their chronology remains to be documented. Likewise, the meaning of the Vcvc motif, the exact relation between Tenslay and DJ TILOV beyond their co-signatures, and the existence of a long project, album or EP, are established by no open source. This profile leaves those questions open, in both senses of the word.

Listening, on the other hand, can start right away, and it follows the order of the evidence. First “Apb Vcvc”, the track with the complete credit, to hear the tempo, the language and the motif in their dated 2022 form. Then “Doglife! Vcvc”, the track Spotify shelved in the sound of bouyon and radio put in rotation, to hear what the genre programmers kept from the project. Then “Douxtcha”, the most played track in the available measurements, to check with the ear what the numbers tell. Our bouyon songs page helps place these tracks among those of the genre.

Tenslay may tomorrow count among the documented names of the scene, with an interview, a label sheet and a complete discography. Until then, this profile holds the role this site holds for the whole genre: keep the exact trace of what is known, refuse what is not, and give the reader the means to listen before judging. A name in capitals, four letters as a signature, five listed tracks, one complete credit: that is the Tenslay record, neither shrunk nor inflated.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Tenslay?

Tenslay, written TENSLAY on the platforms, is a singer presented as from France by the analytics tool Viberate, which files him under reggae and reggae fusion. His documented catalogue turns around the Vcvc motif and collaborations with DJ TILOV, including the single “Apb Vcvc”, released on 10 June 2022 on WORLDFIRE. No press article and no published interview presents him to date.

Where is Tenslay from?

Viberate presents Tenslay as an artist from France, without naming a city. His audience map, consulted in October 2026, puts France first with 77.83 percent of measured listening, ahead of Guadeloupe with 14.17 percent and Martinique with 2.68 percent. That is the triangle of French speaking bouyon, but an audience map measures listening, not origin, and this profile infers no address from it.

What are Tenslay’s verified tracks?

Five tracks are listed by Viberate among the artist’s most played: “Douxtcha”, “Doglife! Vcvc” with DJ TILOV, “Apb Vcvc” with DJ TILOV, “DOGLIFE #VCVC2” and “DO WHAT YOU WANT”. Only “Apb Vcvc” carries a complete verified credit: released on 10 June 2022, label WORLDFIRE, French language, tempo of 160 BPM according to Shazam.

What is Tenslay’s most played song?

“Douxtcha” tops the list of Tenslay’s most played tracks according to Viberate, ahead of the two Vcvc tracks signed with DJ TILOV, “Doglife! Vcvc” and “Apb Vcvc”. No verified release date, label or collaborator comes with “Douxtcha” in the open sources: this profile therefore cites it as a track listed by the analytics tool, without lending it a year.

When was Tenslay’s “Apb Vcvc” released?

“Apb Vcvc (feat. DJ TILOV)” was released on 10 June 2022 on the WORLDFIRE label, according to its Shazam page, in French and at a tempo of 160 BPM, the speed of modern bouyon club tracks. The same page lists a related music video titled “TENSLAY - APB VCVC (DJ TILOV)”. It is the only track in the catalogue with a complete, dated credit.

Who is DJ TILOV, Tenslay’s partner?

DJ TILOV is the recurring partner of Tenslay’s catalogue: he co-signs his two best documented tracks, “Apb Vcvc”, where he is credited as feature, and “Doglife! Vcvc”, credited to Tenslay and DJ TILOV. Those are also the two tracks the platforms and radio exposed the most. The open sources do not specify their relationship beyond those co-signatures.

What does Vcvc mean in Tenslay track titles?

No open source explains it. The Vcvc motif appears in three catalogue tracks, “Apb Vcvc”, “Doglife! Vcvc” and “DOGLIFE #VCVC2”, which makes it a recurring signature of the project. With no interview and no text from the artist decoding it, this site offers no invented meaning: the motif is documented, its meaning is not yet.

Does Tenslay make reggae or bouyon?

The platforms disagree. Viberate files him under reggae fusion, but Spotify places “Doglife! Vcvc” in its playlist The Sound of Bouyon, the radio station Hitz Africa FM plays it in Afro-Caribbean rotation, and his measured audience concentrates in France, Guadeloupe and Martinique. This site therefore files him among the bouyon artists, while reporting the reggae label as a platform fact.

Where has “Doglife! Vcvc” been played?

Spotify places “Doglife! Vcvc” by Tenslay and DJ TILOV in its playlist The Sound of Bouyon, among tracks of the genre, with a duration of 2 minutes 3 seconds. The broadcast logs of the station Hitz Africa FM, carried by Online Radio Box, also show it in rotation, in lists where bouyon tracks of the same generation circulate.

Does Tenslay sing in French?

Yes for his best documented track: Shazam gives French as the language of “Apb Vcvc”. Tenslay therefore sings in the language of the bouyon scenes of Guadeloupe, Martinique and France, not in that of the English speaking scenes of Dominica, the island where the genre was born, or Saint Lucia. His measured audience follows the same French speaking map. See our bouyon gwada page.

What is Tenslay’s real name?

The only civil style name attached to the project in the open credits is “stanley henri”, credited as the songwriter of “Apb Vcvc” in the credits published by Shazam. No source confirms that it is the singer’s legal name, and no published biography gives his city or his date of birth. This profile reproduces the credit as it stands, without turning it into a civil record.

Where can I hear Tenslay?

On the streaming platforms, starting with “Apb Vcvc” and “Doglife! Vcvc”, his two tracks co-signed by DJ TILOV, then “Douxtcha”, his most played track according to Viberate.

Nessa Preppy: the Arima girl soca carried all the way to Brazil

Published October 20, 2026

Nessa Preppy, real name Vanessa John, is a soca singer from Trinidad and Tobago. This profile says it in the first line: she is not a bouyon artist, and nothing here dresses her up as one. Her place on this site follows the method of our parity pages: tell the big voices of the Caribbean carnival circuit for themselves, with their verified facts, then isolate the documented intersections with the bouyon world, only where sources establish them. For Nessa Preppy, those intersections are real but precise: the same Monk Music ecosystem as Machel Montano, whose two emblem records of bouyon soca are productions by the Dominican Dada, and a festival bill in Guadeloupe shared with the Dominican bouyon band Signal Band. The rest is a soca career, and it stands on its own: born in Germany, raised in Arima, revealed by “Tingo”, carried by “Issa Snack”, and, in July 2025, the first soca artist to sing at Festival Latinidades in Brasília.

A soca artist, not a bouyon artist: the frame of this profile

Nessa Preppy sings soca, the genre born in Trinidad and Tobago, heir to calypso, built for the Port of Spain carnival, its fetes and its road on Carnival Monday and Tuesday. Bouyon, for its part, was born in Dominica, in Roseau, at the end of the 1980s. The two genres have been in dialogue since Dada started bouyon soca in 2003, but they have neither the same address nor the same history, and this profile does not merge the two. No bouyon record, no bouyon riddim and no bouyon collaboration credited to Nessa Preppy was found in the sources consulted for this file, and none is therefore lent to her.

What the sources do document is a complete and verifiable soca path. A childhood between Germany and Arima. A debut single, “Tingo”, that became a hit in 2018 according to the Trinidad press. An anthem, “Issa Snack”, remixed with Machel Montano, widely presented as the King of Soca. A first album, “The Art of Flex”, then a second, “Little Miss Arima”, in 2024. A dream collaboration with the legend SuperBlue for the 2023 Road March race. And a historic first in Brazil in 2025. That file, soca from end to end, is what this profile tells.

The bouyon contact points are handled with the same rigour as on our Machel Montano page. Nessa Preppy releases on Monk Music, Montano label, and her album “Little Miss Arima” is distributed by Monk Music according to Caribbean press. Yet it is two productions by the Dominican Dada, “Famalay” and “Conch Shell”, sung by Montano, that the references hold up as emblems of bouyon soca. The neighbourhood is therefore real, but it runs through a label and a circuit, not through a bouyon record by Nessa Preppy, and this profile words it exactly that way.

The second contact point is a bill. The Karukera One Love festival, in Guadeloupe, programmes her on a line up that also features Signal Band, a bouyon band from Dominica, alongside Bunji Garlin among others. One stage, on Guadeloupe ground, for a soca singer from Trinidad and a bouyon band from Roseau: that is the carnival circuit as it exists, porous and shared, and it is at that documented level that this profile connects her to bouyon. Neither appropriation, nor exclusion.

Born in Germany, raised in Arima: Vanessa John before Nessa Preppy

According to the Trinidad and Tobago Newsday, Nessa Preppy was born in Germany and raised in Arima, a town in the east of Trinidad. Her artist biography on Last.fm fills in the family picture: Vanessa John, a ragga soca singer born in Germany to a German father and a Trinidadian mother, who moved to Trinidad at the age of five. Ebuzztt confirms three elements on its side: her real name is Vanessa John, her father is German, and her desire to enter the soca arena started with her love for Rapso, the spoken, engaged form cousin to calypso.

This profile prints no birth year. A single source opened for this file, the contributory Last.fm wiki, puts forward 1992 or 1993, with a question mark in the text itself. A hesitant date on a page anyone can edit is not enough to print an age, so no year is given here. What is solid, by contrast, is the geography: Germany for the birth, Arima for the childhood and the formation, and Trinidad and Tobago for the culture that shaped her, as the Newsday puts it when it says she began to be moulded by the country and its multifaceted culture.

Arima is not a backdrop in her story, it is an album title and a claim. In 2024, her second album is called “Little Miss Arima”, and she presents it as a love letter to her hometown, to her roots and to the community that raised her. The artwork, according to Caribbean press, honours her descent from the First Peoples of Trinidad and Tobago, the first inhabitants of the island. Few soca artists have made their childhood town the declared centre of a whole album: with her, identity comes before marketing, and the sources tell it in that order.

The musical path before soca, as the biographical summaries circulating about her tell it, runs through calypso in primary school, then hip hop and dancehall in secondary school, before Rapso as the doorway into soca. This profile repeats that sequence as a reported biographical account, not as an archive fact, but it sheds light on her style: a singer formed by the spoken and danced forms of Trinidad, who arrives at soca through the voice and the word as much as through melody.

Tingo, then Issa Snack: the breakthrough, from 2018 to the Machel Montano remix

The Newsday holds the official starting point: her debut single “Tingo” became a hit in 2018, and she asserted herself in quick time, to the point of being presented among the queens of soca. The official video credits specify the making: video directed by Pixel Banton, music produced by London Future, label Down The Islands. The Shazam page for the record carries a publication date of 30 November 2017, which reads without contradiction alongside the Newsday account: a record published at the end of 2017 that became the hit of the 2018 season.

The record that changes scale is “Issa Snack”. Ebuzztt recounts that her rise came that year with the success of her “Issa Snack” anthem, subsequently remixed with Machel Montano and dancehall artist Hoodcelebrityy. The Shazam page for the remix gives the facts: Travis World, Nessa Preppy, Machel Montano and Tina, known as Hoodcelebrityy, released on 22 February 2019 on Monk Music and Soca Universe, inside the compilation “Soca Universe 2019, Vol. 1”, with Vanessa John among the songwriters and Jonny Blaze, Travis Hosein and David Sheppard as producers. A remix with the King of Soca, on his own label, one year after the debut hit: the validation is complete.

Ebuzztt adds two stages that count as much as the remix. She made it to the Machel Monday stage, Montano annual carnival concert that became the most sought after event of the Trinidad carnival calendar. And she headed to New York, billed for Hot 97 FM, the city big urban station, with DJ Young Chow. The same article recalls that other Caribbean artists, including Bunji Garlin and Fay-Ann Lyons, had passed through that station before her. The path is that of a successful export: Arima, Port of Spain, then the Brooklyn diaspora.

That period also installs her public character. The nickname Snack Boss circulates in the descriptions of her collaborations, a direct echo of “Issa Snack”. The records that follow confirm a catalogue under rapid construction: “Right Now”, released on 25 January 2019, written by her and produced by DJ Spider and Sir Fingaz; “Tingin”, with R. City and Precision Productions, released on 1 March 2019 on Snafu Records; and the refit of “Matey”, with Sita and Mr Killa, produced by Mark Cyrus of Masterroom Studios for the 2020 carnival period. Three pages, three configurations, one name returning.

The Art of Flex, Splash and Party With Preppy: building a complete artist

In 2021, Nessa Preppy moves to the album format. The Shazam page for “Splash” ties the record to the album “The Art of Flex”, released on Monk Music, and dates the record to 12 February 2021. “Splash” is a collaboration with Patrice Roberts, one of the surest soca voices of her generation, and Travis World. Ebuzztt, in its portrait of the same season, also cites “Toat No Feelings”, her single with Motto, an artist from Saint Lucia. The album and those two collaborations draw an artist who works in a network, island to island, exactly as the carnival circuit functions.

The pandemic closes the stages, and she answers through the screen. Ebuzztt tells of her first virtual event, named “The Art of Flex” after her recent album, with Salty, Patrice Roberts, Preedy, Jahllano, Christo, Rheon Elbourne, Zerimar, Dev and Ricardo Drue on stage, and one to one talk segments where she spoke about her guests and defended their place in the business. The same spirit drives her web series “Party With Preppy”, launched on a 5 January on IGTV according to Ebuzztt: about 20,000 views in 24 hours for the first episode, episodes recorded in Belize, the Bahamas and Toronto, and a simple idea for the time, to show the real life of a soca artist, in real time.

Those two formats say something about her method. Where many soca artists live to the rhythm of one carnival season a year, Nessa Preppy builds a continuous presence: an album, a virtual event carrying her name, a series carrying her nickname. The Newsday places her, in its Queens of Soca article, alongside Destra Garcia, Patrice Roberts and Nailah Blackman. This profile repeats that placing for what it is, a press presentation for a concert bringing those artists together, and not an official title. But the company chosen by her country press is, in itself, a fact.

The catalogue of that period, as the platforms list it, widens the palette beyond the party hit: “Honeypot”, “Dollars”, “Touching It”, “Doh Wah Love”, “Nasty Flow” and “Party Gang” figure among her most listened records according to the platform listings consulted. This profile cites them as catalogue titles reported by those listings, without lending them unverified dates. In any case they show the material the sequel will work with: a voice capable of groovy as well as power, of the sensual record as well as the road anthem.

Dyam Ting with SuperBlue, then Little Miss Arima: consecration and the return to roots

The Newsday devotes a whole article to the 2023 collaboration, and its headline states the stake: securing the dream collab. For the Road March 2023 race, Nessa Preppy joins Austin “SuperBlue” Lyons, soca legend and father of the power style, on the record “Dyam Ting”. The article gives the full credits: produced by Johann Seaton, written by John, Lyons, Kern Rose and Kareem Thomas, live brass by All Stars Brass, and a title that is also an acronym, DYAM for Do You and Masquerade. She states there that working with him has always been a dream of hers, and he answers that he loves the track and that everything flowed.

The article specifies a point that distinguishes her in the race: power soca is not exactly her usual style, she says so herself, and entering the Road March race was never her intention at the start. The record was, in her words as reported by the Newsday, about doing an amazing power soca with a legend. That honesty about intention, in a genre where every January release presents itself as a title candidacy, is a trait the quick profiles never pick up, and one this file keeps as her mark: ambition with her runs through collaboration and learning, not only through competition.

In November 2024, the second album closes the loop opened in the previous section. “Little Miss Arima”, announced on 17 November 2024 by Caribbean press, holds 13 tracks blending soca, dancehall and island rhythms, with collaborations from M1, Lady Lava, Jahllano, Freetown Collective, Vghn and Yung Bredda. It is distributed by Monk Music, as part of the Soundbridge project. Her statement, reported by the press, fits in one sentence: she wanted to create a love letter to her hometown, to her roots and to the community that raised her. Among the album records is “Permission”, whose Shazam page credits her as songwriter with Kern Rose, produced by Madman Johann.

Between the two albums, the catalogue keeps travelling by riddims, the natural working form of the circuit. Billboard, in its monthly reggae and dancehall picks, highlights “By Mehself”, her take on Dan Evans 90 Degree riddim, and describes it as an ode to wining for yourself, not for a man or any other dance partner. That detail matters for reading her writing: in the middle of the party anthems, a share of her records speaks of autonomy and of pleasure taken for oneself, and the international press noticed it.

Brasília, Guadeloupe and the place of this profile: the shared circuit, stated exactly

On 26 July 2025, according to Soca News, Nessa Preppy became the first soca artist to perform at Festival Latinidades in Brasília, the largest festival in Latin America dedicated each year to the art and culture of Black women, before an audience of about 8,000 people for that edition. Her statement, reported by the paper, speaks of pride in sharing a piece of home with the people of Brasília. The festival direction, for its part, sees in it a recognition of the centrality of the Caribbean within the Black diasporas. A singer born in Germany, raised in Arima, the first soca in the history of a Brazilian festival: the export loop opened by Hot 97 in 2019 finds its furthest point here.

The French contact point of her circuit is documented by festival listings: the Karukera One Love Music Festival, in Guadeloupe, programmes her on a bill that also features, among many others, Bunji Garlin and Signal Band, the bouyon band from Dominica. This profile repeats it without inflating it: a shared bill is neither a duet, nor a joint record, nor a proven influence. It is the proof, more modest and more solid, that the soca and bouyon scenes physically cross, in Guadeloupe, before the same public, and that her name is part of those documented crossings.

To hear the branch that connects her world to ours, our bouyon soca page tells the fusion born in Dominica in 2003, our bouyon vs soca comparison sets the two genres side by side, and our bouyon playlist mixes bouyon, bouyon soca and new Dominican releases. Nessa Preppy does not figure there as one more bouyon artist. She figures there as what she is: one of the voices that made the soca of the 2020s, the immediate neighbour of bouyon, and a witness to the size the Caribbean carnival circuit has reached.

This profile finally states its limits. No verified birth year is printed, for lack of a solid enough source. No official account is linked in this page schema, for lack of having opened and verified an exact URL during this research, even though the press reports her handle as @nessapreppy. And no complete official discography was published in the sources consulted: the records cited here are those that the press, the video credits and the platform pages document one by one. If an official source completes this file, this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nessa Preppy a bouyon artist?

No. Nessa Preppy is a soca singer from Trinidad and Tobago, and no bouyon record credited to her name was found in the sources consulted for this profile. She figures on this site for her verified soca career and for her documented intersections with the shared circuit: Machel Montano Monk Music label and a festival bill in Guadeloupe shared with the Dominican bouyon band Signal Band.

What is Nessa Preppy real name and where is she from?

Her real name is Vanessa John, according to Ebuzztt. The Newsday states that she was born in Germany and raised in Arima, in Trinidad. Her Last.fm biography specifies a German father, a Trinidadian mother and a move to Trinidad at the age of five.

Which record revealed Nessa Preppy?

According to the Newsday, her debut single “Tingo” became a hit in 2018. The record that changed scale is “Issa Snack”, remixed on 22 February 2019 with Machel Montano and Hoodcelebrityy on Monk Music, according to the Shazam credits. She then made the Machel Monday stage and was billed for Hot 97 in New York, according to Ebuzztt.

What does DYAM mean in Dyam Ting, her record with SuperBlue?

According to the Newsday, DYAM is an acronym for Do You and Masquerade. The record, produced by Johann Seaton with live brass by All Stars Brass, was written by Vanessa John, Austin SuperBlue Lyons, Kern Rose and Kareem Thomas for the Road March 2023 race. She states there that working with SuperBlue has always been a dream of hers.

What is Little Miss Arima?

It is her second album, announced in November 2024: 13 tracks blending soca, dancehall and island rhythms, with M1, Lady Lava, Jahllano, Freetown Collective, Vghn and Yung Bredda, distributed by Monk Music. She presents it as a love letter to Arima, the town where she was raised, and its artwork honours her descent from the First Peoples of Trinidad and Tobago.

What historic first did Nessa Preppy sign in Brazil?

According to Soca News, on 26 July 2025 she became the first soca artist to perform at Festival Latinidades in Brasília, the largest festival in Latin America dedicated to the art and culture of Black women, whose that edition gathered about 8,000 people.

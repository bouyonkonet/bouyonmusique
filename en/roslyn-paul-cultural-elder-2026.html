Bouyon music: Roslyn Paul named Cultural Elder 2026

By Smokiller · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Heritage Day 2026 had a face. Roslyn Paul was honoured as Cultural Elder during the celebrations on Sunday October 18 in Paix Bouche, on the day the national heritage feast returned to the village after 37 years, per Dominica News.

A former Culture Minister sashed in Paix Bouche

Former Minister for Culture Roslyn Paul was sashed Cultural Elder at Heritage Day in Paix Bouche, and Chief Cultural Officer Earlson Matthew made the announcement, per the Dominica Gazette, carrying a DBC Radio report.

Dominica News also presents her as a former Public Works Minister and an active member of the National Cultural Council. The serving Public Works Minister, Fidel Grant, lauded her at the ceremony in the northern village.

The day programme: Mass, exhibition, gala

Heritage Day began with a 10:00 am Mass at Saint Matthew Catholic Church in Paix Bouche, centred on spiritual gratitude, community unity and the preservation of the Creole language and traditions, per Dominica News.

At 2:30 pm, the Heritage Day Exhibition at the Paix Bouche Resource Centre showcased Dominican history, photographs, artisan craft and local agricultural produce. At 3:00 pm, the Cultural Gala hosted the sashing ceremony for Roslyn Paul, followed by live entertainment from local artists.

An example set for the younger generation

Dominica News describes Roslyn Paul as a passionate community development advocate and an ardent cultural enthusiast, recognised as such by Dominicans. Her honour sets a powerful example for younger generations, the paper says: to take part in safeguarding and honouring their culture.

The Cultural Elder title rewards those who keep the island traditions alive, from bele dance to jing ping, the traditional musics from which bouyon directly descends. Honouring a former Culture Minister in Paix Bouche, the host village of Heritage Day 2026, ties that guardianship work to the whole independence season.

A note on the spelling of her name

This page follows the spelling Roslyn Paul, used by Dominica News and the Dominica Gazette, the two press sources opened for this article. A DOM767 listing writes her first name as Roselyn: the difference is flagged here rather than silently settled.

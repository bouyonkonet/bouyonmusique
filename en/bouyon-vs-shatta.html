Bouyon vs shatta: old and new, same family

Published September 7, 2026

Bouyon and shatta are often set against each other like two generations. In reality, shatta is the family of modern Antillean sounds fed among other things by bouyon, and shatta bouyon is their direct meeting point.

Bouyon, the historic genre

Created in 1988 in Dominica, bouyon has more than thirty years of history: live bands, carnivals, branches like bouyon soca and bouyon gwada. It is a genre with creators, classics and a clear territory of origin.

Shatta, the riddim generation

Shatta means the aesthetic of today’s Antillean producers: short, hard riddims, made for dance and social media. When these producers work the bouyon material, Dominican keyboards, tempo and Creole, it is called shatta bouyon.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of the genre, then the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band: the genre was created in 1988. Bouyon therefore has more than thirty years of history, with its live bands, its carnivals and branches like bouyon soca and bouyon gwada.

What is the difference between bouyon and shatta?

Bouyon is a historic genre, created in 1988 in Dominica, with its creators, its classics, its live bands and its carnivals. Shatta means the aesthetic of today's Antillean producers: short, hard riddims made for dance and social media. The two are not opposites: they belong to the same family.

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the direct meeting point between bouyon and shatta. It is called shatta bouyon when Antillean producers of the shatta generation work the bouyon material: Dominican keyboards, tempo and Creole, on short, hard riddims made for dance.

Is shatta newer than bouyon?

Yes. Bouyon was created in 1988 in Dominica and has more than thirty years of history. Shatta corresponds to the current generation of Antillean producers, the one of riddims made for dance and social media. It is a family of modern sounds fed, among other things, by bouyon.

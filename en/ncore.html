Ncore: the MFR band that became the rock bet of bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Ncore is a bouyon band from Dominica, documented under two names: Ncore, and its earlier name MFR, or MFR Band. The reference works that cite it credit it with an idea almost no other band in the genre has claimed: rythmcore, a fusion of rock, heavy metal and bouyon, carried by a single named “RIDDIM NATION”. The same sources place it, with Triple Kay, among the Dominican bands whose tours of the French Antilles gave birth to bouyon gwada. And the old name never left the stage: MFR played the carnival openings in 2008 and 2009, and MFR stands on the bill of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026, with a dated slot on Tuesday October 20, 2026. This profile gathers everything that can be verified, and says clearly what cannot: no source names the members, and no music platform page for the band was found.

Two names, one band: MFR, then Ncore

The kinship of the two names is written in black and white in the bouyon band lists. The list published by Alchetron, which carries the older reference article on bouyon, lines up the old school bands, WCK, First Serenade, RSB and Triple K, then the new school bands, Triple K International, Kross Vybez, Xs Groove, Signal and the others, and it enters “Ncore (formerly known as MFR)”. That is the clearest wording in the file: one band, two name eras.

The name MFR also circulates in the form MFR Band, and it is under that longer form that it appears in the stage documents: the Purely Dominica carnival reports of 2008 and 2009, the DJ Hothead video of 2023, and the official Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026 list. The name Ncore, by contrast, dominates in the survey texts, the ones that speak of the band sound and influence rather than its concerts. That split is no editorial accident: it draws a band thought about in books under one name, and still booked on posters under the other.

No source consulted dates the name change, explains it, or says what the letters MFR stand for. None names a single member of the band either, no singer, guitarist or drummer, and this profile fills none of those gaps. What is established is already enough to place the band: a Dominican new school bouyon act, active under the name MFR at least since the carnivals of 2008 and 2009, and read afterwards under the name Ncore by the works that tell how the genre spread towards Guadeloupe.

The context of those lists matters. Ncore stands there alongside the names that carried band bouyon through the 2000s and 2010s, in a period when the genre still lived through live formations, before the singles and studio economy took over. Holding that place in the lists, years later, says the band belongs to the reference landscape of Dominican bouyon, even if its public archive is thin. That is exactly why this profile exists: a name cited throughout the surveys deserves a page that separates the documented from the undocumented.

Rythmcore: rock and metal enter bouyon

The most singular fact in the file fits in one word, invented by the band itself according to the source that reports it. Alchetron writes that the Ncore Band continues its journey towards its aim of bringing forth a new era of music to Dominica and the world, a new inspiring sensation and an alternative twist to the bouyon genre they call rythmcore, a fusion of rock, heavy metal and bouyon. The word is a signature: where classic bouyon blends jing ping, cadence-lypso and traditional dances, Ncore claims the input of heavy guitars and metal energy.

The single named as carrying that idea is titled “RIDDIM NATION”. The same source presents it as the record that brings forth that feel and explains the power of bouyon music when it touches the mind, the body and the inner self. The wording reads like a manifesto more than a technical description: the riddim becomes a nation, a collective identity, and bouyon is thought of as a force acting on the whole listener. No release date, no label and no production credit is published with that mention, and this profile adds none.

The status of that record needs to be precise. “RIDDIM NATION” is documented by a reference work, not by a release page: no Shazam, Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer or YouTube page for the song was found during this research, despite targeted searches on the band name and on the title. The record is therefore told here as a reference fact, and it does not enter the recording list in the structured data of this page, which only holds releases verified by opening a platform or label page. That is the rule of this site, and Ncore is its textbook case.

The rythmcore bet, for its part, reads clearly in the history of the genre. Bouyon was born of a blend, and each generation added an outside ingredient to it: ragga and dancehall for bouyon-muffin, hip hop for the hybrid forms of the 2000s, soca for bouyon soca. A fusion with rock and metal, claimed by a Dominican new school band, fits that logic of successive additions, with a rarer colour: few Caribbean carnival formations take on the vocabulary of heavy metal. That the band coined a name for the blend says it thought of it as a lasting identity, not as a one off trial.

The bridge to Guadeloupe: Ncore, MFR and the birth of bouyon gwada

The second major mention of Ncore in the references concerns Guadeloupe. The Music of Guadeloupe article on Wikipedia, in its bouyon gwada section, writes that due to the popularity of Triple K International, Ncore, and the new generation of bouyon bands who toured the French Antilles, a popular offshoot of bouyon from Guadeloupe is called bouyon gwada. Alchetron adds Asa Bantan to that list of popular names. The band of this profile therefore belongs, in the survey texts, to the small number of Dominican players cited as a direct cause of an entire scene across the channel.

The same paragraph then names the flagship groups that democratized the genre in Guadeloupe: Triple Kay and MFR band. The text says that with the trade between the islands and the mass participation of Guadeloupe at the World Creole Music Festival, those groups opened the door to local artists, and it gives the example of the “Allo” remix by Triple Kay with Daly, and “Big Ting Poppin” by Daly alone. The earlier name of the band of this profile therefore stands at the centre of the transmission story: MFR Band is no extra there, but one of the two groups named as carriers of Dominican bouyon towards Guadeloupean singers.

That double presence, Ncore in the touring sentence and MFR Band in the carrier sentence, strengthens the reading of one band under two names, seen from two angles by the same text: the angle of the sound that travels, and the angle of the stage that hosts. It also explains why this profile carries the name Ncore in its title and the name MFR throughout the story: erasing either name would cut the file in two, while the sources give it whole.

The Guadeloupean context can be read in full on our bouyon gwada page, which follows how Dominican jump up became a local scene with its own stars, and on our World Creole Music Festival page, the Roseau meeting point the text cites as the place where the two islands met. Ncore and MFR hold a discreet but real place there: that of the touring generation bands, without whom bouyon gwada would have had no live models to take up.

The live trail: from the carnival openings to Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026

If the band catalog is invisible on the platforms, its stage trail, by contrast, runs across almost twenty years. First opened milestone: the opening of Dominica Carnival 2008 in Roseau. The Purely Dominica report describes the parade moving from Portersville Savannah to the Dame Eugenia Charles boulevard, with calypso king Derek st Rose, Carnival Queen Leandra Lander, steel pan, flag wavers, MFR Band, Sensay figures and the traditional mas costumes. The band appears among the named elements of the national celebration, as early as 2008.

A year later, the same paper covered the opening of Portsmouth Carnival, in the island second town, one week after the national opening. Despite the late start, the report writes, hundreds of spectators stayed, and the local MFR Band serenaded the massive crowd, making that opening one to be remembered. The word local matters: in Portsmouth as in Roseau, MFR Band is presented as a band of the island, rooted in the Dominican fete circuit, and not as a passing name.

The trail resumes in 2023 with a YouTube video titled “MFR BAND WELCOME BACK BY DJ HOTHEAD BOUYON 2023”, a return of the band saluted by a DJ, with a booking number carrying the Dominica 767 code. The title speaks of a comeback, therefore of an absence before it, but no source documents that absence or its reasons, and this profile stops at the fact: in 2023, the name MFR Band circulates again in the island bouyon news. In between, the tracklist of the Sewo Jam 2023-24 mix by Digga D places two live recordings of the band, “Doh Play”, captured at the Mad Festival, and “Monster”, alongside records by Signal Band, Triple Kay and First Serenade: mix traces, not release pages, and they are cited here as what they are.

The most recent milestone is also the most official. The confirmed artist list of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026, the Creole village of the independence celebrations in Roseau, holds MFR Band among the first twenty names announced by the Government of Dominica, alongside Shelly and Signal Band, TK International, Reo and Unstoppable Shax. And the day by day programme, published afterwards, gives the band a precise slot: Tuesday October 20, 2026 at 3:20 PM at the Botanic Gardens, just before First Serenade at 4:20 PM. A band cited in the books of the 2010s and programmed, under its old name, at the very hour this page appears: the longevity of MFR is the best dated fact in the whole file.

What the file does not hold, and why this page says so

This profile is shorter than most artist profiles on this site, and it is better to explain why than to inflate the text. For Ncore and MFR Band, the research carried out in October 2026 found no official biography, no member list, no interview, no artist page on the listening platforms, no release sheet for “RIDDIM NATION”, and no official account verified by direct opening. The aggregators and the name lists, for their part, have repeated the same two or three reference sentences for years, without adding anything verifiable.

That emptiness itself carries a meaning. Ncore belongs to the live band generation of bouyon, the formations that toured, played the parades and the village fetes, and whose music circulated through the stage, the DJs and live captures more than through the platforms. The traces found tell exactly that economy: parades of 2008 and 2009 reported by a local paper, a comeback saluted by a DJ in 2023, two live songs slipped into a mix, and an official booking in 2026. The band is a stage band in the archive, as it no doubt was in the life of the island.

Two questions therefore stay open, and this profile leaves them open. The first: does the band play today under the name Ncore, under the name MFR, or under both depending on the context? The recent documents, Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl included, use only MFR or MFR Band, while the surveys keep classifying the band under Ncore. The second: did rythmcore leave other records than “RIDDIM NATION”? No published discography allows an answer. On the day an official page, a platform sheet or an interview appears, this page will be completed and dated.

In the meantime, listening to the context starts with the neighbouring pages of this site: bouyon gwada, born in part from the tours of that generation, our bouyon playlist, which follows the current Dominican scene, and the profile of TK International, the other flagship band named in the same sentences as MFR Band. The name Ncore, for its part, stays what the sources make of it: the book name of a stage band named MFR, and the carrier of the most rock idea in the history of bouyon.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Ncore?

Ncore is a bouyon band from Dominica, classified among the new school bands in the reference lists of the genre. Alchetron presents it as the Ncore Band, carrier of a fusion called rythmcore, and its band list states: Ncore, formerly known as MFR. No source consulted names its members.

What is the link between Ncore and MFR Band?

The sources treat the two names as one band in two eras. The reference list writes Ncore, formerly MFR, and the bouyon gwada paragraph names MFR Band among the flagship groups that democratized the genre in Guadeloupe. On stage, it is the name MFR or MFR Band that appears, from 2008 to Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026.

What is rythmcore?

It is the name the band gives, according to Alchetron, to its twist on bouyon: a fusion of rock, heavy metal and bouyon. The source presents rythmcore as the tool of a new era of music for Dominica and the world, and cites the single RIDDIM NATION as the record that brings forth that blend.

Which Ncore records can be verified?

Only one title is named by the references: RIDDIM NATION, cited by Alchetron with no date, no label and no platform page found. Two further titles, Doh Play, live at the Mad Festival, and Monster, appear in the tracklist of the Sewo Jam 2023-24 mix under the name M.F.R. Band. None of those titles has a release page verified by opening, so none enters the structured data of this page.

Has Ncore played recently?

Yes, under the name MFR. The band stands among the first twenty confirmed artists of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026 in Roseau, and the official programme of Tuesday October 20, 2026 gives it a 3:20 PM slot at the Botanic Gardens, before First Serenade. Earlier, MFR Band played the carnival openings of Roseau in 2008 and Portsmouth in 2009, and a DJ Hothead video saluted its comeback in 2023.

Why is this profile shorter than the others?

Because the public file is thin, and this site does not inflate it. No biography, no named member, no artist page on the platforms and no verified official account were found in October 2026. This profile therefore publishes the documented facts, the references, the stage traces from 2008 to 2026 and the rythmcore project, and states its limits instead of hiding them. Listening to the context runs through our bouyon playlist and our bouyon gwada page.

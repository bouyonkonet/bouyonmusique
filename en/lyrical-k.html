Lyrical-k

Published October 20, 2026

Lyrical-k is an artist whose verifiable file fits into a single heading: “Lyrical-k Naughty (Bouyon 2027)”, a YouTube page opened during this research, which carries the name of the artist, the title “Naughty” and the billing of genre and vintage, bouyon 2027, and nothing else. No description accompanies the page, no writing, production or studio credit is published in it, no origin is named in it and no link appears in it. A second signal exists, and this profile presents it for what it is: the name “Lyrical K”, spelled with a space, appears in the keyword block of another bouyon video opened during the same research, “Break Me Down” by Bad Gyal Shyanne. That is a circuit signal, not a credit, and it does not suffice to formally identify the two spellings as one and the same file. This profile therefore publishes the heading, the genre billing, that framed signal, and the limit of the file, and nothing more.

One heading, one genre billing, and that is all

The full inventory fits into one line. “Lyrical-k Naughty (Bouyon 2027)” is the heading of a YouTube video where Lyrical-k appears as the artist of record, ahead of the title “Naughty”, followed in brackets by the genre, bouyon, and the vintage, 2027. That is the simplest publishing mode of the current scene: the title filed on the platform with its genre tag and its year, for the DJs and listeners who search for the sound by style and by season, as in our bouyon playlist. The opened page does not even specify the format of the release beyond its presence on YouTube, and this profile does not specify it either.

Everything a song page usually gives is missing here. No description to read, no credit roll to quote, no named producer, no studio, no lyricist, no label, no date more precise than the vintage carried by the heading. No other primary page opened during this research completed the file: no artist page, no second title, no interview. This profile therefore does not turn a heading into a biography: it records that an artist named Lyrical-k released at least one title filed under the bouyon of 2027, and that the rest is not documented in the opened sources.

What the heading says, and what it does not say

The heading says three things, and only three. It says an artist name, Lyrical-k, spelled with a hyphen and a lower case k in the opened page, a spelling this profile reproduces exactly. It says a title, “Naughty”, a single English word, from which this profile draws no conclusion about the language of the piece, its theme or its content, no lyrics and no description being published in the source. And it says a filing: (Bouyon 2027), the genre and season claim laid down by the page itself, which connects Lyrical-k to this site with no deduction on our part.

The 2027 vintage, read on October 20, 2026, is a season billing and not a precise release date, which no opened source gives for this title. The name itself calls for the same caution: “Lyrical” is a word that many rap and dancehall artists attach to their stage name, and searching on this kind of name returns numerous namesakes, none of which any verifiable element ties to “Naughty”. This profile therefore excludes every namesake, it attributes to Lyrical-k no other title, no collaboration and no territory, and it waits, in order to grow, for a primary source that names more than the heading.

The Lyrical K signal: a keyword in the circuit, not a credit

A second document was opened during this research, and it must be presented with particular precision. The YouTube page of “Break Me Down”, the bouyon open mic by Bad Gyal Shyanne, carries in its keyword block, among many names of the bouyon circuit, the form “Lyrical K”, written with a space instead of the hyphen. That block is a search listing laid down by the page: it mixes artists, groups and references of the scene, without distinguishing roles, and it credits nobody. The description of that same page, for its part, unrolls a full credit roll for “Break Me Down”, production, arrangement, bass, mixing, mastering and filming location, and the name of Lyrical K appears in none of those posts.

The exact reading is therefore the following, and this profile does not go beyond it. On one side, a primary heading under the name of Lyrical-k, with a hyphen, for a bouyon title of 2027. On the other, a “Lyrical K” mention, with a space, in the keyword block of a video of the Dominican bouyon circuit documented by that page, filmed according to its description at Roseau Market and the old Mill. The two forms resemble each other enough to be flagged together, and not enough, nor with enough support, to be merged into one verified identity: the spelling differs, no credit links the two pages, and no other shared element, producer, studio or title, was opened. That signal stands as a circuit indication only, it adds neither a title, nor a credit, nor an origin to the file of Lyrical-k, and it enters neither the structured data nor the identity links of this page.

That treatment may seem strict, but it is the rule of this site for every thin file: separate the levels of proof instead of mixing them. An opened heading stands as a verified title. A keyword on the page of another artist stands as a signal of presence in a circuit, nothing more. If a primary page one day appears under the name of Lyrical-k or of Lyrical K with a credit roll, an artist page or a published origin, this profile will read it, will check whether it attaches to “Naughty” through a concrete element, and will grow accordingly, with a new date.

The limits of the file, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they are almost all of its information. Single primary source for the artist: one opened YouTube page, carrying a heading and nothing else, empty description. No biography was found. No origin is named: not Dominica, not Guadeloupe, not Martinique, not any other island, and the circuit signal described above changes nothing in that silence, a keyword in a video filmed in Dominica not making a Dominican origin. No writing or production credit is published in the source, no other Lyrical-k title was verified, and no official link is therefore entered in the structured data of this page, beyond the title itself.

If Lyrical-k publishes a second title, a description, full credits or an artist page, this page will be completed and dated, like every page on this site. In the meantime, the listening fits into a single track: “Naughty”, the bouyon title billed for 2027. One name, one title, one genre, one vintage, and a circuit signal presented as such: that is one of the thinnest files on this site, and it is published as it stands, with no padding, on October 20, 2026.

Only one: “Naughty”, whose YouTube heading carries the (Bouyon 2027) billing. Its primary page was opened during this research. It is the only recording entered in the structured data of this profile.

No writing or production credit is published on the opened page of the title, whose description is empty. No songwriter, producer, studio or label is named there, and this profile invents none.

The two spellings are therefore flagged together, without being merged into one verified identity.

Why is this profile so short?

Because its source is one of the thinnest on the site: one opened page, one heading, an empty description, and nothing else. This site prefers the shortest and most exact profile to a portrait filled with deductions. It will grow if Lyrical-k publishes more primary sources.

Bouyon styles

  • Bouyon soca : Bouyon soca: the fusion of Dominica bouyon and Trinidad soca. Origin, sound and territories: Saint Lucia, Antigua, Guadeloupe and Martinique.
  • Bouyon gwada : Bouyon gwada: the hardcore branch of bouyon born between Dominica and Guadeloupe. History, raw sound and its place in the bouyon of the French Antilles.
  • Shatta bouyon : Shatta bouyon: the modern form of bouyon carried by Antillean producers and the French scene. Riddims, clubs, social media and its Dominican roots.
  • Bouyon-muffin : Bouyon-muffin: the branch of bouyon influenced by Jamaican dancehall and ragga. Raggamuffin voices over Dominica bouyon riddims, history and legacy.
  • Wrecketeng : Wrecketeng: a branch of bouyon born in Dominica, in the family of bouyon fusions with bouyon soca and bouyon-muffin. What you need to know about the style.
  • Jump up : Jump up: in Guadeloupe and Martinique, it is the everyday name for the bouyon music of Dominica. Why this name, and how jump up lives at the Antillean carnival.
  • Old school bouyon : Old school bouyon: the bouyon of the live bands of the 1990s in Dominica, the era of WCK, RSB and First Serenade. The original sound that fans still defend.
  • Gospel bouyon : Gospel bouyon: when the bouyon rhythm of Dominica serves Christian praise in the Caribbean. A real, much searched branch of bouyon music.
  • Modern bouyon : Modern bouyon 2026: digital riddims, shatta bouyon, remixes and producers of the new generation, between Dominica, Guadeloupe, Martinique and Paris.
  • RnBouyon : RnBouyon fuses Dominica bouyon with R&B: the term, spelled RnBouyon, is carried by singer Fallon (Deezer Next 2026) and by Smokiller's instrumentals.
  • New Bouyon : New Bouyon names three realities: Ricky Bishop's concept launched in 2024, TiiTii NBA's New Bouyon Age EP from 2023, and the generic label for 2020s bouyon.

Sukie: Sukie Burn Brain, bouyon, Dominica

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Sukie is an artist from Dominica whose full stage name, Sukie Burn Brain, already announces the persona: a rave voice, frontal, built for the most charged bouyon parties. His real name is Shane Edwards, and it is under that name that the Caribbean press told the other half of his story: an arrest in Grenada in December 2022, on the eve of a concert where he was due to perform, a six month prison sentence of which he served four, and songs recorded behind bars that he planned to release once he was out. This profile holds the two halves together, with their sources: the stage, the interview that presented him as a controversial artist, the published court file in its sometimes contradictory versions, and the single title verified title by title in the sources consulted. Where the public record stops, this profile stops too, and it says so.

Who Sukie is: Shane Edwards, Sukie Burn Brain, rave artist from Dominica

Every source consulted for this profile tells of the same man under two names. For the stage, he is Sukie, or Sukie Burn Brain. For the press, he is Shane Edwards, and the articles take care to connect the two: Nice FM, reporting the Grenada Broadcasting Network, writes that Sukie, whose real name is Shane Edwards, also goes by the name Sukie Burn Brain. DOM767, the Dominican news site that signed the fullest account of his return, presents him as Shane Edwards, popularly known as Sukie Burn Brain, a Dominican rave artiste. The label matters: where many artists on this site are band singers or producers, Sukie belongs to the rave world, the bouyon party pushed to its highest setting, voice over the crowd and sound system energy.

His age, at the time of the events of December 2022, is given by the press reports: 36. That is about all the public record gives as dry biography. No official artist page was confirmed on the streaming platforms consulted during this research, no label is published in the sources, and this profile therefore fills in neither a date of birth, nor a home village, nor a childhood path that nothing documents. Sukie file is of a particular kind: thick in press articles, thin in catalogued music. That asymmetry is the very subject of this profile, and it is treated here without makeup.

What the music press does confirm is his place in the current bouyon generation of Dominica. In March 2025, the reports on the first Bouyon Road March title of Dominica, won by “RAGS” by Trilla G, Shelly and Skinny Fabulous, describe the momentum of the moment as carried by recent releases from artists such as Sukie Burn Brain, Mr. Ridge, Kenny G, Reo and Signal Band. Sukie therefore appears, in the 2025 press, among the active names of the wave, alongside artists documented elsewhere on this site, such as Ridge. But those reports name none of his titles: they establish an activity, not a catalogue.

The Jael Joseph interview: the controversial artist and his come up story

The second document that structures this profile is an interview. The Dominican journalist and producer Jael Joseph, whose Jael Joseph LIVE platform interviews figures of Caribbean music, hosted Sukie for an interview that the press later cited as a reference point of her format. The Bajan Reporter, in a profile of Jael Joseph published in November 2023, writes that her interview with controversial Bouyon artist Sukie Burn Brain amassed 25k views, with 1,000 people tuning in live. The word controversial is that article word, and it is carried here as attributed framing, not as a judgement by this site: it says how the regional press presented Sukie to the public before the viewer even pressed play.

The figures of that interview deserve to be read for what they are. Twenty five thousand views and a thousand live viewers place that conversation at the level of the biggest interviews on the platform cited in the same article, ahead, in live audience, of established soca artists the same text mentions. For an artist with no confirmed streaming catalogue, that is a scene fact in its own right: Sukie audience follows him for the persona and for the talk as much as for the records, and discussion platforms have become part of his stage. Circulating clips indeed tell his own account: a TikTok clip drawn from this kind of interview is titled Sukie on Bouyon vs Soca and How He Brought Bouyon Back, His Real Come-Up. That brought bouyon back story is his, presented as such by the clip that carries it; this profile records it as the artist version, without turning it into a fact of genre history, whose documented chapters are told on our history of bouyon page.

There is a logic to the interview becoming his most watched piece. Sukie is an artist whose public fame rests as much on what happened to him as on what he released. A long, live interview format, where the artist answers and narrates, is precisely the place where that kind of fame is worked. That the press calls the artist controversial in the same sentence where it counts his views says the essential of the phenomenon: with Sukie, controversy is not an accident of the path, it has become part of the audience itself, and any honest profile must document it as a fact of reception, distinct from the facts of justice treated below.

Fire and Ice: the Grenada concert he never played

In December 2022, Sukie was in Grenada for a concert. The event was called Fire and Ice, billed as the ultimate Concert, at the Grenada National Cricket Stadium. According to the press reports, he was due to perform there with another Dominican artist, Mr Ridge, and the Jamaican dancehall singer Shenseea was also expected on the programme. It is the kind of bill that marks a step up for a bouyon artist: a national stadium, a regional headliner, and Dominica represented by two of its party voices. Our page on Ridge documents the path of the other Dominican on that bill.

The concert went ahead without him. According to DOM767, shortly before his Fire and Ice performance with Ridge at the National Cricket Stadium, a Guyanese teenage girl who had come to Grenada from Canada, staying in the same resort hotel as him, reported to police that he had touched her inappropriately. He was arrested and charged, then brought before Judge Teddy St. Louis in St. George, the capital of Grenada, for indecent assault. According to Caribbean Times, which covered the hearing, the 36 year old artiste appeared in a Magistrate Court on a Monday, was denied bail, and was remanded to Richmond Hill prison. The same report stated that if convicted of the summary offence, he faced up to five years in prison.

The reports do not agree on the number of counts at the arrest stage, and this profile publishes the disagreement instead of smoothing it over. Nice FM, citing the Grenada Broadcasting Network, speaks of two counts of indecent assault. Caribbean Times speaks of one count. Both versions are arrest stage reports, written in the urgency of the first days; the sentencing account, published later by DOM767, settles the outcome without returning to that initial count. No source consulted names the complainant, and this profile does not name her either: she is described here only in the terms the published press chose.

The published court record: Dominica in September 2022, Grenada after

The Grenada case was not Sukie first published court file, and the December 2022 reports take care to recall it. According to Nice FM, in September 2022 the entertainer had been arrested in Dominica and charged with battery on a female, as well as threats. Remanded into custody, he secured bail a month later, in October, and then performed at several shows around the region. The trial for the threat and battery offences was expected to commence on December 6, 2022 in the Roseau Magistrate court. DOM767 adds, in its March 2023 account, that this case was still pending before a magistrate court in Roseau when the Grenada incident occurred. No source consulted for this profile gives the outcome of that Dominican case: it is therefore published here at exactly that stage, charge and pending process according to the reports, with no verdict invented in either direction.

For Grenada, the DOM767 account, published on March 29, 2023, gives the full sequel. Sukie was sentenced to six months in prison for touching a teenage girl inappropriately on December 26, 2022. He was released after four months, for good behaviour. The Acting Commissioner of Prisons in Grenada, Rupert Neckles, quoted by the article, explains the calculation: Grenadian prison regulations take one third off a sentence for good behaviour and industry, so four months served correspond to the six months handed down. The same official states that the artiste had pre qualified himself for that early release by his behaviour, and that he had spoken personally with him before his release.

This profile reports that file because it is part of the public record of the artist, published by the Dominican and regional press with names, dates and quotes from officials. It reports it in the terms of its sources, with no graphic detail, no victim name, and without turning either the Dominican charge into a conviction, or the Grenada conviction reported by DOM767 into anything other than what it is: the central, documented court fact of Sukie public life. A profile that passed over that file in silence would describe an artist who exists in none of the sources.

The prison songs and the return to Dominica

The most singular detail of the file comes from the same DOM767 article. According to Prisons Commissioner Rupert Neckles, Sukie, during his detention at Richmond Hill, recorded songs. The artiste told him he planned to release them after his incarceration to detail his prison experience. The information is given by the official who ran the institution, quoted by name, and it is published here with that precise attribution: these are statements reported by an article, not a verified discography. No title of those prison songs is given by the source, no release date is confirmed in the sources consulted, and this profile therefore cannot say whether that project came out, under what name, or where it can be heard.

The return, meanwhile, is told by the same account. Back in Dominica in the spring of 2023, Sukie was sighted by many people, who uploaded videos of him to social media. It was through those amateur images, and not through a statement, that the scene learned of his release. The rest of the record then places him back in the musical landscape: the Jael Joseph interview counted by the press in November 2023, then the mention of his recent releases in the Bouyon Road March reports of March 2025, alongside Ridge, Kenny G and Reo. The thread is thin but continuous: the artist Grenada had interrupted in the middle of a bill became again, in the press of his island, an active name of the bouyon wave.

There remains the question this profile raises without being able to close it: what became of the songs recorded at Richmond Hill? If they ever appear under an identifiable title, with a verifiable source, this profile will be completed and dated. In the meantime, they exist in the public record as an announcement made by a prison commissioner to a journalist, and it is exactly in that form, no more romanticised and no more hushed, that they are published here.

The verified catalogue: one title, and what that gap tells

Now to the catalogued music, because that is where this file is thinnest, and it must be written plainly. After research across the platforms and published tracklists consulted, a single Sukie title is verified title by title: “Say My Name”, by Keks Mafia featuring Sukie Burn Brain, present in the published tracklist of the Blaqrose Supreme podcast devoted to the scene. It is a featuring credit, not a solo single, and it is the only recording carried in the schema of this page. The group Keks Mafia has its own page on this site. Every other title attributed to Sukie in unsourced lists was set aside rather than copied.

That relative gap is not an absence of activity, and the distinction must be made properly. The 2025 reports speak of recent releases by Sukie Burn Brain without naming them. The interview and its clips speak of his path without delivering a tracklist. The rave scene and the parties, which are his ground according to every source, by nature leave fewer catalogued traces than streaming releases: a voice that works a fete does not need an album page to exist in the memory of its audience. But a site that documents must work from pieces, and the musical pieces here count to one verified title, one announcement of prison songs without titles, and mentions of releases without names. This profile publishes that exact state rather than a display discography.

To hear Sukie world while waiting for better, our bouyon playlist gathers the verified records of his Dominican generation, and our pages on Ridge and Keks Mafia document the two names his story crosses most directly: his Fire and Ice bill partner, and the group of his only verified feature. Sukie remains, in the public record, an artist the press has narrated more than the platforms have catalogued. That is a real form of fame, an uncomfortable one, and that is why it is written here in full.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Sukie?

The regional press presents him under those two connected names: Nice FM writes that Sukie, whose real name is Shane Edwards, also goes by Sukie Burn Brain, and DOM767 describes him as a Dominican rave artiste who was 36 in December 2022.

Are Sukie and Sukie Burn Brain the same artist?

Yes. Sukie is the short form, Sukie Burn Brain the full stage name, and Shane Edwards the civil name under which the press covered his cases. Nice FM, reporting the Grenada Broadcasting Network, explicitly connects the three, and DOM767 presents him as Shane Edwards, popularly known as Sukie Burn Brain. Every source in this profile speaks of the same man.

Where is Sukie from?

The sources publish no date of birth and no home village, and this profile invents none. He came back to Dominica in the spring of 2023.

Why does the press describe Sukie as a controversial artist?

Because the Bajan Reporter, in a November 2023 profile of journalist Jael Joseph, calls her guest exactly that: her interview with controversial Bouyon artist Sukie Burn Brain amassed 25k views, with 1,000 people tuning in live. The word is that article's word. It reflects the public file of the artist, marked by the court cases published in Dominica and Grenada.

What was the Fire and Ice concert in Grenada?

Fire and Ice, billed as the ultimate Concert, was held at the Grenada National Cricket Stadium in December 2022. According to the press, Sukie was due to perform there with another Dominican, Mr Ridge, and the Jamaican dancehall singer Shenseea was also expected. He was arrested before his performance, and the concert went ahead without him. Our Ridge page documents the other Dominican on the bill.

What happened to Sukie in Grenada in December 2022?

According to DOM767 and the regional reports, Sukie was arrested shortly before the Fire and Ice concert, after a teenage girl staying in the same hotel reported to police that he had touched her inappropriately. Charged with indecent assault, he was denied bail and held at Richmond Hill prison. DOM767 then reports a sentence of six months in prison.

When was Sukie released from prison?

After four of the six months handed down, for good behaviour, according to DOM767 on March 29, 2023. Grenada's Acting Commissioner of Prisons, Rupert Neckles, explains that the regulations take one third off a sentence for good behaviour and industry. Back in Dominica in the spring of 2023, Sukie was sighted by many people, who uploaded videos of him to social media.

Did Sukie really record songs in prison?

That is what DOM767 reports, quoting by name the Acting Commissioner of Prisons in Grenada, Rupert Neckles: Sukie recorded songs during his detention at Richmond Hill and planned to release them after his incarceration to detail his prison experience. No title and no release of those songs is confirmed in the sources consulted.

Do the sources contradict each other about his arrest?

Yes, on one precise point. Nice FM, citing the Grenada Broadcasting Network, speaks of two counts of indecent assault at the arrest stage, and Caribbean Times of one count. This profile publishes both versions with their sources instead of silently choosing one. The reports agree on the arrest, the bail refusal, the detention at Richmond Hill and the missed Fire and Ice concert.

What happened to Sukie's case in Dominica?

According to Nice FM, Sukie had been arrested in Dominica in September 2022 and charged with battery on a female and threats, then granted bail in October. The trial was expected to start on December 6, 2022 in the Roseau Magistrate court, and DOM767 says the case was still pending in March 2023. No source consulted gives its outcome, and this profile invents no verdict.

Is Sukie part of the 2025 Bouyon Road March wave?

Yes, according to the music press. In March 2025, the CAT Radio and CaribMagPlus reports on Dominica's first Bouyon Road March title describe the momentum of the moment as carried by recent releases from artists such as Sukie Burn Brain, Mr. Ridge, Kenny G, Reo and Signal Band. Those reports name none of his titles, though: they establish an activity, not a catalogue.

Kompa, the Haitian root of bouyon

By Smokiller · Published November 6, 2026

Before bouyon, there was kompa. Created in Haiti by Nemours Jean-Baptiste in 1955, this modern merengue of brass and tanbou crossed the Caribbean, then turned into cadence-lypso before giving birth to Dominican bouyon in the late 1980s. To understand kompa is to understand where Dominica's sound comes from.

Kompa, born in Haiti in 1955

Kompa, also written konpa or compas, is a dance music derived from merengue, born in Haiti. The genre was created by saxophonist Nemours Jean-Baptiste (1918-1985) after he founded Ensemble Aux Callebasses in 1955, renamed Ensemble Nemours Jean-Baptiste in 1957. The word compas means measure or rhythm in Spanish, and the mark of the genre is the steady beat of the tanbou, the Haitian barrel drum.

The kompa sound blends electric guitars, saxophones and a solid brass section, over more structured rhythms and more worked-out harmonic arrangements than the salon merengue it came from. In the early 1960s, konpa spread beyond Haiti, to Curacao, Aruba, Saint Lucia, Dominica and above all the French islands of Martinique and Guadeloupe. In 2025, compas was recognised as intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO.

From cadence rampa to cadence-lypso

The history of kompa is also a story of rivalry. Webert Sicot, who had played alongside Jean-Baptiste, split away to create his own variation, cadence rampa, or kadans. From that cadence came cadence-lypso, perfected by the Dominican and Guadeloupean band Exile One through the 1970s. Exile One's leader, Gordon Henderson, is generally credited with coining the name cadence-lypso in the early 1970s.

In the 1970s, groups such as Exile One, the Grammacks, the Midnight Groovers, Liquid Ice and Bells Combo combined these sources into cadence-lypso: the cadence half comes from Haitian kompa, the lypso half from calypso and the rhythms of the islands. Kompa itself is described as a slower variation on Latin merengue, with a jazz and big band feel and a strong emphasis on melody.

Bouyon, the direct heir of cadence-lypso

Bouyon is the genre that came directly out of cadence-lypso in Dominica. It appeared in the late 1980s, mainly through the band Windward Caribbean Kulture, WCK, formed in 1986. WCK took the melodic side of cadence-lypso and mixed it with the fast rhythms of jing ping, adding drum machines and keyboards.

The result was faster, heavier and more electronic than the horn-driven cadence-lypso of the 1970s. That break made bouyon a genre of its own, while keeping the family line: kompa, then cadence rampa, then cadence-lypso, then bouyon. The three genres are still cousins, and they are still heard side by side on Caribbean stages.

Kompa on the WCMF 2026 stage

The World Creole Music Festival 2026 showed that this kinship is still alive. On the second night, Saturday October 24 at Windsor Park in Roseau, Extasy Band opened the musical programme by bringing Dominica's interpretation of konpa to the main stage, with guests including Luc Leandre, Pudaz, Paris and Khadija Joseph.

The same night, the legendary Haitian group Tabou Combo brought decades of kompa history to the Roseau stage. As the festival press noted, konpa remains an important part of the musical relationship between Dominica, Haiti and the French Caribbean, and its grooves sit comfortably alongside bouyon, cadence-lypso, zouk and soca.

Frequently asked questions

What is kompa?

Kompa, or konpa, is a Haitian dance music created by Nemours Jean-Baptiste in 1955, derived from merengue, with electric guitars, brass and the steady beat of the tanbou. It was recognised as intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2025.

How did kompa lead to bouyon?

Through a chain in three steps: Webert Sicot's cadence rampa, then Exile One's cadence-lypso in the 1970s, then WCK's bouyon in Dominica in the late 1980s, when the band mixed cadence-lypso with the fast rhythms of jing ping, drum machines and keyboards.

Where can you hear kompa in Dominica?

On the World Creole Music Festival stage, where Extasy Band carries Dominica's interpretation of konpa, and alongside invited Haitian groups such as Tabou Combo, present on the second night of the 2026 edition.

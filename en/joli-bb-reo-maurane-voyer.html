Bouyon songs: Joli BB, Reo and Maurane Voyer

By Smokiller · Published November 5, 2026

Released on October 9, 2026 on the Mauve label, Joli BB, the duet by Dominican bouyon artist Reo and Martinique singer Maurane Voyer, passed 115,000 YouTube views in a week, according to CaribMagPlus. Behind the number, a story of a meeting between two islands that can see each other without talking enough.

A meeting in Saint Barts, a song in Guadeloupe

The two artists met in July, while performing at the same festival in Saint Barts. Voyer approached Reo about working together in the studio, and the idea became reality when they reunited in Guadeloupe, at HMC Studio, to develop the song, according to CaribMagPlus.

Reo developed the concept for the song and Voyer brought her vocals. The beat was produced by YSN of Guadeloupe, and the track was mixed in Dominica by Dernz, that is Dernel Green. Reo insisted on adding live drums, to preserve the Dominican identity of this bilingual song. The song record on this site gives the writing, credited to Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume, and the running time of 2 minutes 43.

The first bouyon comes from Guadeloupe, the real bouyon from Dominica

For Voyer, the collaboration was a chance to compare the two cultures and discover what brings them together as much as what sets them apart. She explains that in Martinique, the first bouyon people know comes from Guadeloupe, and that for about three years interest has been growing in the real bouyon, the one from Dominica.

It was very interesting to see what differences we have and also what brings us together, and I really enjoyed it, she told CaribMagPlus. Reo shares that reading: it is a good opportunity to connect Dominica and Martinique, because the two islands do not have that much of a musical relationship, and it has to be done to push bouyon in Martinique and build that relationship.

115,000 views in a week

The official Joli BB video passed 115,000 YouTube views in a single week. The response has been encouraging, says Voyer: she receives many messages from women, men, people of all kinds of ages, happy to like the song, and the radios too, in her words reported by CaribMagPlus.

The release extends Reo international momentum after his La Vie Dou tour in New York and London. CaribMagPlus recalls his multi-million-view songs: Sa Ka Bay with DJ Bling at 2.9 million, Doudou with Litleboy at 3.8 million, You Mad at 1.5 million and La Vie Doux with Tehilah at 2.8 million. Bouyon Shattaz published its reel on Joli BB on the day of the release.

A bridge for Dominican bouyon in Martinique

The ambition of both artists goes beyond the song. They hope the collaboration will encourage more musical exchanges between Caribbean territories that do not always connect as often as they could, with stronger relationships between artists, radio stations, DJs, promoters and audiences.

Martinique, for example, is another market where bouyon artists can build their fan base and get bookings, said Reo, who added that you can literally see Martinique from Dominica and that the island should not feel so far away. The timing was right: Voyer was preparing her first visit to Dominica for the World Creole Music Festival, where Reo also performed, and she described working together before her first visit as an exciting chance to build the connection before discovering the island. Reo himself was nominated for Bouyon Artist of the Year at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards.

Frequently asked questions

Who produced Joli BB?

The beat is by YSN of Guadeloupe, that is Nicolas Lanclume. The track was mixed in Dominica by Dernz, that is Dernel Green, with live drums added at Reo request. The writing is credited to Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume.

When was Joli BB released?

On October 9, 2026, on the Mauve label. The song runs 2 minutes 43, and Bouyon Shattaz published its reel on the day of the release.

Why does Joli BB matter for Dominican bouyon?

Because it carries the Dominican sound into Martinique, a market where the bouyon people know comes mostly from Guadeloupe. The video passed 115,000 YouTube views in a week, and both artists want it to become a lasting bridge between the Dominica and Martinique scenes.

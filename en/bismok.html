Bismok: two verified credits, no biography

Published October 20, 2026

Bismok is a name from Guadeloupe bouyon that appears twice in the verified credits of a single season: as the headline name on the single “Boué'y”, released on 7 November 2023 on iMD- DRECORDS, then among the nine names of “Mas La Rivé”, the collective record by 1T1 released on 12 January 2024. This profile reads those two credits line by line, including the one copied profiles never read, the voice of “Boué'y” credited to Kurtys Alexis, places the name inside the 1T1 circle, rules out the namesakes, and states plainly what the public record does not hold: no biography, no published origin and no full name.

Who Bismok is: a name in capitals, two credits, no biography

Bismok is an artist name documented in Guadeloupe bouyon at the turn of 2023 and 2024. That is nearly everything the public archive says about him, and this profile starts by saying so. Where the headline names of his scene have platform biographies and complete discographies, Bismok left, in the verified sources consulted for this profile, only two traces: a single under his own name and a featured credit on a collective record. No artist biography, no press article and no confirmed official page was found under this name.

The form of the name itself varies between the credits, and the detail is noted here because it structures the research. On his single, the name is written in capitals: BISMOK. On the collective record by 1T1, it is written in ordinary case: Bismok, among the other guests. The platforms treat both occurrences as the same artist name inside the same ecosystem, Guadeloupe bouyon, two months apart. This profile keeps the “Bismok” spelling of the page slug, which is the one in the collective credit, and flags the capitalised form of the single.

One limit has to be written in this first section, because it commands everything that follows. The public credits do not say whether Bismok is a single person, a duo or a project name, and they give no full name, no origin and no background. On “Boué'y”, the performer credit is BISMOK, but the voice is credited to a distinct name, Kurtys Alexis, who also signs the lyrics. This profile therefore does not merge the two names: it documents the name Bismok as the credits carry it, and it names the people the credits name, each in their place.

Why publish a profile of a name this poorly documented? Because its two credits place it at precise, verifiable points of the scene: a single distributed internationally through a professional circuit, and a seat in the widest documented gathering of the circle of 1T1, the producer the Creole press names among the figures of present day Guadeloupe bouyon. A name can be rare in the archives and real in the credits. This profile writes both things at once.

“Boué'y”: the single of 7 November 2023, read credit by credit

The first verified credit of Bismok is a single under his own name: “Boué'y”. The song page on Shazam dates it to 7 November 2023, places it in the World genre, gives the language as French and the label as iMD- DRECORDS, and assigns it a tempo of 162 BPM. That tempo is not a gratuitous technical detail: it places the record in the speed zone of bouyon and fast Caribbean party music, far from the tempos of zouk love or slow dancehall. The title itself, “Boué'y”, is a Creole spelling that announces the colour: music from the French Antilles, sung in the street language of Guadeloupe.

The full credits of the record deserve to be read slowly, because they are the part of the file that summary profiles never reproduce. Performer: BISMOK. Vocals: Kurtys Alexis. Lyrics: Kurtys Alexis. Composers: Daryl Clarisse and Ulrich Bienvenu. In other words, the name Bismok carries the record, but the singing and the writing are credited to Kurtys Alexis, and the composition to two further names. That split is common in studio bouyon, where the artist name can designate the project while the credits detail the hands and the voices. It forbids one thing in any case: writing that Bismok “sang” or “wrote” this record. The credits say something else, and this profile follows the credits.

The release circuit also says something about the level of the project. iMD- DRECORDS is the distribution channel that carried the single onto the international platforms, to the point that Shazam gives it a full page with detailed credits, language and analysed tempo. A self produced record without structured distribution does not leave that kind of trace. “Boué'y” is therefore a single built for the platforms, released two months before the 2024 carnival, the window in which the bouyon scene places its season records.

What the file does not contain must follow immediately. No verified source consulted gives the listening figures of the record, no official video was confirmed, and no follow up single under his own name was found after this one in the sources consulted. The single exists, its credits are public and precise, and that is about all. The next section shows where the name reappears, two months later, and why that reappearance matters.

“Mas La Rivé”: the name inside the widest gathering of the 1T1 circle

The second verified credit of Bismok is collective. On 12 January 2024, 1T1 released “Mas La Rivé”, a record lining up nine names: 1T1, Theomaa, Aknose, DJ SOFTEE, Dj TKrys, Bismok, Jicypie, Dj Kylled and Lejuh. The published discography of 1T1 on Qobuz gives the date, the exact guest list and the World genre. It is, in the documented catalogue of the producer, the widest gathering of his circle: his regular partners Theomaa, Aknose and Lejuh, several DJs of the scene, and guest voices including Bismok.

The place of this credit in the chronology of the circle helps weigh it. In June 2023, “Piney” brought together three names. In September 2023, “Moustik” installed the duo of 1T1 and Aknose. In December 2023, “Pied au Plancher” lined up 1T1, Tiitii NBA and Lejuh. “Mas La Rivé” opened 2024 by widening the format to the maximum, two weeks before the carnival season. And in September 2024, the same circle would release “Leave Me Alone”, with another rare guest, Binksdrick. The method is constant: the catalogue advances through gatherings, and each gathering is a photograph of the network at a given moment. The photograph of January 2024 contains Bismok.

The Creole press gives the scale of that network. In an interview published by Caraib Creole News on the state of bouyon in Guadeloupe, the artists named as the most emblematic of the moment on the island are Lejuh, 1T1, Theomaa, Piscine OSP, Aknose and Shanika. Four of the nine names on “Mas La Rivé” are direct collaborators of that list or the list itself. Bismok is not in it, and this profile does not claim he is: but his name is printed, in the official credits, on the same record as the core of the scene the local press describes. For an artist with no public biography, that is the strongest trace that exists.

There remains the question this profile will not settle without proof: are the Bismok of “Mas La Rivé” and the BISMOK of “Boué'y” the same person or the same project? The two credits share the name, the scene, the territory and a two month window, which makes the identity very plausible, and the platforms treat them as a single artist name. But no public document consulted states it explicitly, and the credits of “Boué'y” name other people on vocals and composition. This profile therefore presents the two credits as the two verified appearances of the name Bismok, and leaves the identity question open, written in plain sight, rather than settled in silence.

A name not to confuse: the namesakes ruled out

The name Bismok arrives in search engines in the middle of a crowded neighbourhood, and part of the work of this profile is to rule out what has nothing to do with him. Bismok is not Bismarck, the German battleship and the Johnny Horton country title of 1960. Bismok is not Bismut, the Dutch psychedelic rock band from Nijmegen. Bismok is not Beo Smook, the American rapper of the Shazam hip hop catalogue, and he obviously has nothing to do with Big Smoke. No fact, no date and no title from those namesakes is used in this profile, and none should be attached to the bouyon artist.

The most delicate confusion is internal to the file itself, and it concerns Kurtys Alexis. Because his name appears on the vocals and lyrics of “Boué'y”, it is tempting to conclude that Bismok is the stage name of Kurtys Alexis. No source consulted says that. The credits distinguish the performer, BISMOK, from the singer and lyricist, Kurtys Alexis, just as they distinguish the composers Daryl Clarisse and Ulrich Bienvenu. Until a source publicly connects those names, this profile keeps them separate. It is less spectacular than a revealed identity, and it is the only version the documents support.

The same discipline applies to origin. The circle in which the name appears is Guadeloupean, the single is in the French Creole of the Antilles, and both credits are classed World by the platforms. But no source consulted publishes the origin of Bismok himself: no commune, no island of birth, no current base. This profile therefore attaches the name to the Guadeloupe scene through its credits, which are facts, and refrains from giving it a geographical biography, which would be invention.

What these two credits say about the scene

Two credits, a single and a feature, are enough to describe how the scene that produced them works. The bouyon gwada of the 2020s is a music of producers and gatherings: a central composer holds the catalogue, collective records maintain the network, and guest names circulate from one credit to the next. In that economy, the trace of an artist is not measured by the number of his albums but by the quality of his placements: a cleanly distributed single, then a seat on the most collective record of the most visible producer of the scene. On those two criteria, the small file of Bismok is coherent and dated at the heart of the 2023 2024 season.

Bouyon itself gives the frame. Born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s with WCK, adopted and hardened in Guadeloupe in the branch called bouyon gwada, it works today between Roseau, Pointe-à-Pitre and the platforms. The records of 1T1 turn in Dominican carnival mixes, and Dominican records feed Guadeloupe parties. The two credits of Bismok belong to that circuit: a Creole single at 162 BPM built for the platforms, and a gathering record built for the fete. Our bouyon playlist places those records inside the whole of the current sound.

Finally, there is a lesson about the archives themselves. For headline artists, the platforms write biographies. For credit names like Bismok, they write only lines of metadata: a date, a label, a tempo, names on the vocals and the composition. This profile is built exactly from those lines, read slowly and placed back inside the catalogue of the circle. It is less visible work than copying a ready made biography, and it is the only honest work available when the biography does not exist.

What this profile does not yet know

Finally, this profile states its limits, because they are part of the record. No biography of Bismok was found in the verified sources consulted: no full name, no origin, no background, no formation. No official account of the artist was confirmed during the research, so this page links to no social profile. No consolidated listening figure is published here, because none was found in a reliable source, and no record under his own name after “Boué'y” was documented in the sources consulted.

The identity question flagged above remains open, and it is the first one that could move this profile forward: if a public source one day connects Bismok, Kurtys Alexis, Daryl Clarisse and Ulrich Bienvenu inside one named project, or if the artist publishes an official page, this profile will be completed, dated and corrected like every page on this site. In the meantime, it documents what is verifiable today: two credits, two dates, one circle, and a name that deserves not to be confused with its namesakes.

Listening, meanwhile, is simple: “Boué'y” for the single under his own name, then “Mas La Rivé” to hear the name among the nine voices of the 1T1 circle, all placed back inside our history of bouyon page. Two short records, two exact credit lines, and a profile that prefers to say what it does not know rather than fill in the blanks.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Bismok?

Bismok is an artist name from Guadeloupe bouyon documented by two verified credits: the single “Boué'y” under his own name, released on 7 November 2023 on iMD- DRECORDS, and a feature among the nine names of “Mas La Rivé” by 1T1, released on 12 January 2024. No biography of the artist has been published in the sources consulted.

Who actually sings on “Boué'y”?

The credits of the record distinguish the roles, and it is the answer copied profiles never give: the credited performer is BISMOK, but the vocals and lyrics are credited to Kurtys Alexis, and the composition to Daryl Clarisse and Ulrich Bienvenu. No source consulted says that Bismok is the stage name of Kurtys Alexis, and this profile therefore does not claim it.

What are the verified records of Bismok?

Two records: “Boué'y”, his single of 7 November 2023, in French, classed World by the platforms, at 162 BPM; and “Mas La Rivé”, the collective record by 1T1 of 12 January 2024, where Bismok appears among the guests with Theomaa, Aknose, DJ SOFTEE, Dj TKrys, Jicypie, Dj Kylled and Lejuh.

Is the Bismok of “Mas La Rivé” the same as the BISMOK of “Boué'y”?

Very probably, but it is not stated by any public source consulted. The two credits share the same name, the same Guadeloupe scene and a two month window, and the platforms treat the name as a single artist. This profile documents the two appearances of the name and leaves the question written as open, rather than settled without proof.

Where does Bismok come from?

The public record consulted does not say. The circle of 1T1, in which the name appears, is Guadeloupean, and “Boué'y” is a record in the French of the Antilles, but no source publishes the origin of Bismok himself: no island, no commune, no full name. This profile attaches the name to the scene through its credits and refrains from inventing the rest.

Is Bismok connected to Bismarck or Bismut?

No. Those are unrelated namesakes: Bismarck points to the German battleship and a country title from 1960, Bismut is a Dutch psychedelic rock band, and Beo Smook is an American rapper. No fact from those neighbouring names belongs to the bouyon artist Bismok.

Baps!: the producer behind Diamond Boy and the bouyon riddims of @prodbaps

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Baps!, known as prod.baps on Instagram and @prodbaps on YouTube, is a producer who presents himself, in the description of his own channel, as a 23 year old multi-genre producer. His name enters the documented history of bouyon through one precise credit: he is a co billed lead artist and co-producer, with DJ Wizz767, of “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”, the official audio presented as a bouyon 2026 record. Around that credit, his YouTube channel shows a dense and recent catalog of bouyon riddims, warm ups and mashups, heir to years of type beats published on the same channel. This profile also says, from the introduction on, what the file does not hold: the origin of Baps! is stated in no opened source, no island, no town and no real name, and this profile therefore leaves it unstated rather than deducing it from his collaborations. It is a thin file, treated as such: one verified lead credit, one opened artist channel, one opened Instagram profile, and nothing invented around them.

Island: not stated in the sources · All islands

Who Baps! is: 23, multi-genre, and an origin nobody states

The only self portrait opened for this profile fits in one sentence, the one in the description of his YouTube channel @prodbaps: a 23 year old multi-genre producer, who gives a contact address for professional inquiries. Everything is there, and everything is missing. The age is the one the artist declares at the time the page was opened, with no birth date to fix it. The multi-genre claim is confirmed by the visible catalog of the channel, which runs from hip hop type beats to bouyon and gospel bouyon, as we will see. And the origin, the place of residence, the real name, the mother tongue: nothing. None of the three sources opened for this profile, the official audio of “Diamond Boy”, the YouTube channel and the Instagram profile, says where Baps! comes from, and this profile refuses to fill that void by deduction.

The temptation to deduce exists, and it must be named in order to refuse it properly. Baps! co-produces with DJ Wizz767, a producer from Dominica whose name carries the 767 area code of the island, and his channel works with names of the Dominican bouyon scene and its orbit. But collaborating with a Dominican producer does not make anyone Dominican, and programming bouyon records does not name an island: bouyon is produced today in Dominica, in the French Antilles, in Saint Lucia and in the diasporas. This profile therefore files Baps! where his documents place him, in the bouyon circuit of 2025 and 2026, and leaves the origin box empty until a source of the artist fills it. It is the same discipline this site applies to the other thin files of this wave: an empty and dated box is better than an invented provenance.

His public identity rests on three addresses, all given by his own pages. On Instagram, he is prod.baps, a creator type account, displayed as “Baps!” followed by a star, with 1211 followers and 710 following at the time of opening, and a website link pointing to a Linktree page under the name bappoexclusive. On YouTube, he is @prodbaps, the channel described below. And for buying his productions, the notes of “Diamond Boy” point to a BeatStars storefront at the address bappoexclusive.beatstars.com: the name of the store, Bappo Exclusive, extends the stage name with an element, Bappo, that the artist uses for his commercial banners. This profile flags those addresses for what they are: the BeatStars storefront could not be opened during this session, and neither could the Linktree page, so neither enters the structured links of this page, which keep only the two profiles that were actually opened, YouTube and Instagram.

The exclamation mark is part of the name, and the sources carry it everywhere: Baps! in the credits, Baps in the video titles of his channel, prod.baps and @prodbaps for the handles. This profile headlines the credit form, Baps!, and flags the other forms where they appear. On a scene where producers are tracked by a handle, those forms are not details: they are the keys that make his credits findable, and they are the ones this profile followed to assemble this file, without ever merging his name with the other Baps or Bappo acts that search engines may return and that are not him.

Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix): the credit that puts him on the top bill

The founding document of the file is an official audio published on YouTube: “Dj Wizz767 x Baps!, Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”, presented in its very title as a “Bouyon 2026” record. The description of the video is formal: official audio for “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)” by Dj Wizz767 and Baps!, production credited to both names, “Produced: Dj Wizz767 & Baps!”, graphic design and visualization credited to Derrent “Dj Wizz767” Durand, and listening links pointing to Audiomack and SoundCloud. For Baps!, the line that matters is double: he is not only the co-producer in the credit list, he is a co artist in the title, in the “x” format which, in the usages of bouyon, places two names at the same billing level. It is that co lead status on a bouyon titled release that sets his file apart from a simple production credit spotted in a tracklist, and that justifies this standalone profile beside the one of DJ Wizz767.

What is known about the record itself, beyond its credits? The opened sources say nothing about the original that this remix reworks: no artist of the original “Diamond Boy” is named in the description, and this profile assumes nothing on the subject. A bouyon remix, in the economy of the genre, is first a tool for the dancefloor: the point is to place a tune or a voice of the moment over the rhythm of the island, and the “Bouyon 2026” mention in the title files it in the production of the current season, on the same footing as the records of his own channel tagged Bouyon 2026. The listening links in the description, Audiomack and SoundCloud, are truncated in the page as it was opened: this profile can therefore neither follow them nor print them in full, and it flags “Diamond Boy” as a record documented by its official YouTube page, period. It is the only Baps! record kept in the structured data of this page, because it is the only one whose individual release page was opened.

The context of the record deserves a detour through its partner. DJ Wizz767, real name Derrent Durand, is a producer, promoter and graphic designer from Dominica, documented on this site by his own profile, which shows him programming the whole scene in his mix series and naming WCK, Triple Kay and DJ Flip Tha Boss among his influences. That the bouyon 2026 record of that producer is co signed by Baps! places the young producer, from his first credit documented here, in the company of an established passer of the Dominican scene. The description of the video moreover plays that passer role in the other direction: it is the description that publishes, under a “FOLLOW Baps!” block, the three addresses of the co-producer, his Instagram, his BeatStars storefront and his YouTube channel. In other words, the document that proves the credit of Baps! is also the document that presents him to the public of the scene: the notes of “Diamond Boy” work as his first visiting card in documented bouyon.

The measure of what this credit establishes, and of what it does not, must be kept. It establishes that in 2026, a record presented as bouyon, signed by a confirmed Dominican producer, carries Baps! as co artist and co-producer. It does not establish since when the two have worked together, who did what in the production, or whether other joint records exist elsewhere than on YouTube. The rest of the file shows, on the other hand, that “Diamond Boy” is no isolated accident in the work of Baps!: at the same moment, his own channel lines up a whole bouyon catalog, produced solo or with other partners. It is that catalog that must now be read, with its exact status: a list of videos displayed on the page of his channel, opened for this profile, of which no individual page was opened.

The @prodbaps channel: a 2025 and 2026 bouyon catalog in full production

The page of the @prodbaps channel, opened for this profile, displays a dense and very recent bouyon production, whose titles follow the working formats of the genre. First format, the named riddim, the baptized instrumental that serves as a support for voices: “Love Bug Riddim”, presented as a Keyshia Cole remix and tagged Bouyon 2027 in its title, a sign that the channel already publishes beyond the current season; “Mainstay Riddim”, a Rod Wave remix tagged Bouyon 2026; “Ratata Riddim”, a Pink Blush remix; “Never Doubt Riddim”; and “Survival Riddim”, co signed with Era. Second format, the warm up, the version made for DJs: “Slickdance”, billing Tems and Victony; “Pour It Up”, a Rihanna remix; and “Stay.”, another Rihanna remix. Third format, the bouyon remix of a named record: “I Just Wanna Dance”, a remix of the Ghetto Love Story of BabyChiefDoIt, which displays 22K views at the time of opening, by far the highest figure of the recent bouyon catalog of the channel; “Road Trips!”, a Drake remix; “When I See U”, a Fantasia remix; and “Simple”, a bouyon instrumental co signed with Hashy.

The channel also shows the producer at work on the material of the scene itself. One title displays “1T1 x LMFAO, Party Rockers Medley”, a Baps bouyon mashup bringing together 1T1 and LMFAO, featuring Lejuh and Theomaa. Another displays “Pudaz x MiiMii x Faithii, TMTD Riddim”, a Baps mashup over voices of Pudaz, Miimii KDS and Faithii. And a third carries the tag “Gospel x Bouyon” for a “Step By Step” signed prod. Baps!: the crossing of gospel and bouyon, which our gospel bouyon page documents as a branch of the genre in its own right, is therefore part of his declared playground. Lower in the list, the previous seasons carry the same signature: “Billie Jean Riddim” and “Kiss Me Thru The Phone Riddim” for 2025, and a “Work Dat” presented as a Baps bouyon remix. The whole draws a producer who works exactly like the young generation of the genre: in baptized riddims, in reworks of worldwide hits and in mashups of the voices of the scene, published in a continuous flow rather than in albums.

The status of this list must be stated clearly, as this site does for every tracklist. These are titles and counters displayed on the page of the artist channel at the time of its opening, on October 20, 2026: they prove the public existence of those videos under those titles, with those bouyon tags, and they date the activity. They are not song pages opened one by one, and none of those titles therefore enters the track list of the structured data of this page, which stays limited to “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”. The view counts quoted are those of the day of opening and will move: they serve here to show the scale of a catalog, in which most recent records count in hundreds or thousands of views, with two or three records standing out, and not to establish a ranking.

This way of publishing has a name in the current economy of bouyon: the riddim as a product. A producer who baptizes his instrumentals, declines them into warm up versions for DJs and into remixes of hits for the networks, builds a catalog meant to be taken, voiced and shared by others, singers, DJs and content creators. Our bouyon riddim page explains that collective mechanics of the genre, and the bouyon type beat page shows how those instrumentals circulate as goods with their storefronts. The channel of Baps! is a complete specimen of that model in 2026, and it is on that ground, as much as for “Diamond Boy”, that his profile has its place in the map of the producers of the genre drawn by our bouyon producers page.

Before bouyon: the type beat years visible on the same channel

The same channel page tells, in its oldest layers, another Baps!: the one of the type beats of the early 2020s. The displayed titles speak another language there, the one of the worldwide market of hip hop instrumentals: a sad lofi type beat, “Lost & Found”, dated 2021 in its title and credited prod. Baps, displaying 26K views at opening; “Grace”, a type beat announced as free for profit use, under the names of Kenny Mason and JID, at 23K views; “Vai Jogando”, a Brazilian funk type beat, at 19K views; “Far Eye”, a reggae and soul type beat of 2021 co-produced with Chubby Beatz, at 13K views; “Silence”, a type beat co-produced with Abbey_orjay in 2021; and an alternate beat version of a Travis Scott and Young Thug record, at 30K views, the maximum counter of the channel at the time of opening.

Those figures and dates, all read on the opened page, draw a readable trajectory without inventing anything. In the early 2020s, Baps! works the generic market of the type beat, lofi, American rap, Brazilian funk, reggae soul, with the free or commercial use mentions that are the grammar of that market and of his BeatStars storefront. From 2025, the channel page changes its center of gravity: the titles become bouyon, the riddims carry names, the remixes aim at the voices of the Caribbean scene as much as at worldwide hits, and in early 2026 comes the first co lead credit documented by this site, “Diamond Boy” with DJ Wizz767. The pivot is visible in the counters themselves: the biggest figures of the channel remain those of the type beat years, while the bouyon catalog, more recent, builds its audience record by record, with the breakthrough of “I Just Wanna Dance” at 22K views as a first signal of comparable scale.

That pivot is no break in trade, and that is what makes it interesting for the history of the genre. The know how is the same on both sides: build an instrumental that can carry a voice, baptize it, decline it, put it in the store. What changes is the scene the product addresses: from the worldwide and anonymous market of the type beat, where the buyer can be any connected rapper, to the bouyon circuit, where the partners have names, islands and publics, DJ Wizz767, 1T1, Pudaz, Miimii KDS, Faithii. Baps! thus illustrates, at the scale of a single channel, the movement this site documents across a whole generation of producers: the tools of online beatmaking, BeatStars, YouTube and Instagram, plugged into the rhythm of Dominica. Our bouyon instrumental page follows the same matter from the side of the instrumentals of the genre.

The limit of this reading must be restated: all of it comes from a single page, the home page of his channel as displayed at opening, which shows a selection of videos and not an exhaustive catalog. Older or intermediate records may exist without appearing there, the counters are those of a given day, and the titles summarize videos that this profile did not open one by one. The type beat then bouyon trajectory is therefore the visible trajectory, the one the artist lets be read on his own showcase, and it is reported as such. If his channel or his storefront one day publishes a biography, a classified discography or a stated origin, this profile will be completed and dated.

The Bappo Exclusive model: storefront, networks and networked work

The third pillar of the file, after the credit and the channel, is commercial. The notes of “Diamond Boy” send buyers to BeatStars, the platform for selling and licensing instrumentals, at the address bappoexclusive.beatstars.com, and the Instagram profile of Baps! points to a link page under the same Bappo Exclusive name. The message is consistent from one page to the other: Baps! does not only publish videos, he sells productions, and his intended public includes the artists looking for a bouyon instrumental ready to voice. BeatStars is precisely the tool of that trade among the producers of his generation, with its use licenses that turn a riddim published on YouTube into a buyable product. This profile could not open the storefront itself during this session, and it therefore describes neither its prices, nor its licenses, nor its store catalog: it reports the address exactly as the artist notes publish it, and nothing more.

The networked work, for its part, reads in the credits displayed on his channel. Besides DJ Wizz767 on “Diamond Boy”, the opened titles credit Hashy on “Simple”, Era on “Survival Riddim”, Chubby Beatz on the 2021 “Far Eye” and Abbey_orjay on “Silence”, while his mashups headline the voices of 1T1, Lejuh, Theomaa, Pudaz, Miimii KDS and Faithii. Two remarks of method are required. First, a mashup displaying the name of a singer does not prove a collaboration with that singer: it is a rework published by the producer, and this profile files it as such, without turning reworked voices into partners. Second, the named co-productions, Hashy, Era, Chubby Beatz and Abbey_orjay, are reported at the status of channel titles, like the rest of the list: real as publications, not verified as song pages.

There remains the question of the place of Baps! on the scene, and it is answered with prudence. Proven document: a co-producer declaring 23 years, active in a continuous flow on the central format of current bouyon, the riddim, with a first shared lead credit on a bouyon 2026 release by an established Dominican producer, and a storefront that sells his catalog. Absent document: any trace of press, stage, ranking or interview, any stated origin, any page on the streaming platforms. The reputation of a producer of this generation is built first in the credits of others and in the counters of his own channel, and that is exactly where this profile found Baps!: credited by DJ Wizz767, and lining up his own riddims week after week. What follows will tell whether the singers of the scene turn his riddims into records, as the genre is in the habit of doing, and whether his name moves from channel titles to platform pages.

In the meantime, the documented listening starts with “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)” with DJ Wizz767, the only record whose individual page was opened, and continues on his @prodbaps channel through the recent riddims, “I Just Wanna Dance”, “Love Bug Riddim” and “Mainstay Riddim”, then through the scene mashups, the 1T1 medley and the “TMTD Riddim”. The context is read on our bouyon riddim and DJ Wizz767 pages, and the current production of the genre is followed in our bouyon playlist.

The limits of the file: a thin profile, and it says so

This profile is thin, and it ends by saying so as clearly as its fuller counterparts. Only one record, “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”, has an individually opened release page, and it is the only one kept in the structured data of this page. The rest of the catalog quoted comes from the page of his channel, opened but read as a list of publications, not as song pages. No artist page of Baps! on Shazam, Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, SoundCloud or Audiomack was opened and verified during this research, even though “Diamond Boy” points to Audiomack and SoundCloud for listening under the account of his partner. No origin, no island, no town and no real name is stated in the opened sources, and the age of 23 is a declaration of his channel, not a civil record. The structured links of this page therefore carry only the two official addresses opened and verified: his YouTube channel @prodbaps and his Instagram prod.baps.

Why publish such a thin profile? Because the credit that founds it is exactly the kind of document on which the history of producers is written: the name of a young producer as co lead and co-producer of a bouyon titled release, with an established passer of the Dominican scene, with his addresses published in the notes of the release itself. The producer profiles of this site, from DJ Wizz767 to DJ Flip Tha Boss, show that those names count in the genre long before the press or the platforms tell them. Publishing the file as it is, with its gaps named and dated, allows it to be completed as releases come rather than rebuilt later from memory. If Baps! publishes a biography, states an origin, or if his riddims appear as platform pages, this profile will be updated and dated like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Baps!?

He is a producer known under the handles prod.baps on Instagram and @prodbaps on YouTube, who presents himself in the description of his channel as a 23 year old multi-genre producer. He is co artist and co-producer, with DJ Wizz767, of “Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”, presented as a bouyon 2026 record. His origin is stated in no source opened for this profile.

What is the bouyon record of Baps! with DJ Wizz767?

“Diamond Boy (Bouyon Remix)”, whose official audio on YouTube credits production to Dj Wizz767 and Baps!, with graphic design and visualization by Derrent “Dj Wizz767” Durand. The video points to Audiomack and SoundCloud for listening and publishes the addresses of Baps!: Instagram prod.baps, YouTube @prodbaps and a BeatStars storefront.

Where does Baps! come from?

No source opened for this profile says so: neither the official audio of “Diamond Boy”, nor his YouTube channel, nor his Instagram profile states an island, a town or a country. He works with the Dominican producer DJ Wizz767 and over voices of the bouyon scene, but this profile does not deduce an origin from those collaborations and leaves the question open.

Which bouyon riddims has Baps! published on his channel?

The page of his @prodbaps channel, opened for this profile, displays among others “Love Bug Riddim”, “Mainstay Riddim”, “Ratata Riddim”, “Never Doubt Riddim”, “Survival Riddim” with Era, “Billie Jean Riddim”, “Kiss Me Thru The Phone Riddim”, as well as warm ups and remixes such as “I Just Wanna Dance”, at 22K views at the time of opening. Those are channel titles: only the page of “Diamond Boy” was opened individually.

Does Baps! sell his productions?

His own pages point to a BeatStars storefront at the address bappoexclusive.beatstars.com, published in the notes of “Diamond Boy” with a purchase beats mention, and his Instagram profile points to a link page under the Bappo Exclusive name. The storefront could not be opened during this research: no price and no license is therefore described here.

Has Baps! always made bouyon?

No, according to the page of his own channel: his oldest and most viewed videos are type beats of the early 2020s, lofi, rap and Brazilian funk, such as “Lost & Found” of 2021 at 26K views, while the bouyon catalog dominates his 2025 and 2026 publications. The passage from type beat to bouyon riddim is the trajectory visible on his channel.

Bouyon music: Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026, the October 22 finale

By Smokiller · Published October 22, 2026

Ti Vilaj Kweyol means the little Creole village. From Monday October 19 to Thursday October 22, 2026, the Botanic Gardens in Roseau become that village: Dominican cuisine, crafts, live music and the Kweyol language, for the fifth edition of the event, under the theme Tout Biten Domnik, everything Dominican. Today, Thursday October 22, is finale day, the eve of the opening of the World Creole Music Festival.

Four days of Creole village at the Botanic Gardens

The event runs from noon to 8 p.m. each day, with more than 90 entrepreneurs present, according to Dominica News: traditional food vendors, artisans and small businesses from across the island. On the stall menus are the dishes the sources list: crab backs, callaloo, broth, smoked meats, green fig and saltfish, sweets and local drinks. General admission is 10 Eastern Caribbean dollars, free for students in uniform and children under five.

Ti Vilaj Kweyol was launched in October 2022 to replace Creole in the Park as the lead-in event of festival week. Its five elements, as the organisers describe them in Dominica News reporting, are cuisine and traditional gastronomy, culture and live entertainment, creativity and wellness, commerce and small business support, and community with youth engagement.

The Thursday October 22 finale: the verified programme

The official finale day programme, published by Tourism Dominica and carried by Dominica Weekly, starts at 12:05 p.m. with the Kalinago Dancers, then UMOJA at 12:25, Castle Bruce Primary School at 12:45 and the Waitukubuli Dance Theatre Company at 1:05. The afternoon builds in bouyon power: Explosive Band at 1:36, Ignition and Erwinchy at 2:32, Derick Rah Peters at 3:29, then at 4:26 a block bringing together Reo and Faithii, Trilla G and Prymex.

The two headliners close the day: Asa Bantan, widely called the King of Bouyon, at 5:43 p.m., then Shelly with Signal Band closing at 7 p.m. The day's DJs are DJ Shil until 4 p.m., then DJ Snow and the Unstoppable DJs. It is a full day of Dominican music, from the Kalinago dances at noon to the bouyon of the evening, on the eve of WCMF.

Why Ti Vilaj matters for bouyon

The Creole village is not a simple market: it is where festival week shows its roots. Traditional dance groups, schools and village cultural groups share the same stage as the bouyon bands who will play Windsor Park at the weekend. Reo, Trilla G and Asa Bantan sing today at the Botanic Gardens before their festival nights, and Shelly with Signal Band close Ti Vilaj before playing WCMF night 3, on Sunday October 25.

For a visitor who arrived for the festival, Ti Vilaj is also the most direct doorway into the culture bouyon carries: the Kweyol language, the cuisine, the costumes and the drums that have fed the genre from Roseau. The two venues, the Botanic Gardens and Windsor Park, are walking distance from each other in the heart of Roseau.

Practical reference points for the finale

Facts kept for this page, because the opened sources cited confirm them: Botanic Gardens, Roseau, Thursday October 22, 2026, from noon to 8 p.m., admission 10 Eastern Caribbean dollars, free for students in uniform and children under five, and the artist by artist programme above, announced by the ministry as subject to change. Tomorrow the week continues with night 1 of the World Creole Music Festival at Windsor Park.

Frequently asked questions

When is Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026?

From Monday October 19 to Thursday October 22, 2026, at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau, Dominica, from noon to 8 p.m. each day. Thursday October 22 is finale day, the eve of the opening of the World Creole Music Festival.

Who plays the Ti Vilaj Kweyol 2026 finale?

The official Thursday October 22 programme lists the Kalinago Dancers opening, Explosive Band, Reo and Faithii, Trilla G and Prymex in the afternoon, then Asa Bantan at 5:43 p.m. and Shelly with Signal Band closing at 7 p.m.

How much is admission to Ti Vilaj Kweyol?

10 Eastern Caribbean dollars for general admission, according to Dominica News and Dominica Weekly. Admission is free for students in uniform and children under five.

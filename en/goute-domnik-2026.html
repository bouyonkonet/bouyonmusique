Bouyon music: Goute Domnik 2026, Starline Ital Kitchen wins

By Smokiller · Published November 3, 2026

Dominica now has a food festival of its own. Goute Domnik, the country first national culinary festival, ran from July 19 to 26, 2026, and its opening contest, the One Pot Challenge in Layou, was won by Starline Ital Kitchen, per DOM767.

A first national festival for Dominican cuisine

The Ministry of Tourism launched the country first national culinary festival, Goute Domnik: Taste of Dominica, in July 2026 in Roseau, per Dominica News. The festival is organised by the Ministry of Tourism, the Discover Dominica Authority and Dominica Festivals, under the theme Where Flavour Meets Nature.

Goute Domnik means Taste Dominica in Kweyol, per Writeups 24. The stated aim: celebrate the island culinary heritage, support local farmers, restaurants, chefs and food businesses, and promote Dominica as a culinary tourism destination.

The Layou One Pot: seven teams, one winner

The festival opened on Sunday July 19 at the Hillsborough Exit Point in Layou, from noon to 5 pm, with the Friendly One Pot Competition, per Writeups 24. Chefs, home cooks and community groups prepared traditional Dominican one-pot dishes with fresh local ingredients and recipes handed down through generations.

Seven teams competed, and Starline Ital Kitchen was crowned winner of the first edition of the One Pot Challenge, per DOM767. Starline Ital Kitchen is an Ital and vegan restaurant in Roseau, known for plant-based cooking rooted in Ital philosophy, per its Guide.dm listing.

A week of restaurants across the island

The festival week continued with Restaurant Week: nineteen restaurants across Dominica served fixed-price menus built on local produce and Creole-inspired dishes, per Writeups 24. Three tiers were offered: Island Bite at XCD 60, Creole Delight at XCD 120 and Taste of Nature at XCD 200, with Eat Local plates under XCD 60 as well.

The Discover Dominica Authority also launched the Goute Domnik Food Guide, a stamp passport: diners collected a stamp at each restaurant visited, and the most stamps made them eligible for prizes, per Writeups 24.

Food, the other stage of Creole culture

Director of Tourism Marva Williams said food has become an important part of how travellers experience a destination, and that the festival celebrates the people, flavours and stories behind Dominican cuisine, per Writeups 24.

Goute Domnik joins the cultural calendar that carries the island identity, alongside the World Creole Music Festival, Jounen Kweyol and the independence celebrations: bouyon music makes Dominica dance, its cuisine lets the world taste it.

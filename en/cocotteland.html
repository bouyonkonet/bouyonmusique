Cocotteland: Gwo Dada with Natoxie and Mikado, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Cocotteland is an artist name that appears, in the checkable public record, on exactly two records: “Gwo Dada”, released on March 28, 2025 with Natoxie and Mikado, and “I Don’t Know”, a DJ Leguste record crediting Cocotteland as featured artist, released on April 11, 2024. No biography, no official page, no interview and no verified social account for Cocotteland was found during the research for this profile. This text therefore states what is established, record by record and credit by credit, describes the Natoxie and Mikado ecosystem in which the name circulates, and writes out in full what the public record does not say: no confirmed real name, no precise origin, no career path. A short profile, because the facts are short, and one that refuses to fill the blanks with invention.

What is established: two records, two labels, two dates

The verifiable Cocotteland file fits in two catalogue lines, and this profile starts by giving them exactly. First record: “Gwo Dada”, published under the name Cocotteland with Natoxie and Mikado featured, released on March 28, 2025, issued by the Natoxie label, with a listed tempo of 160 BPM on the song page at Shazam, and an official video published under the title Cocotteland Ft Natoxie and Mikado, Gwo Dada (Clip Officiel). Second record: “I Don’t Know”, published under the name DJ Leguste with Cocotteland featured, released on April 11, 2024, issued by the DJ Leguste label, also listed at 160 BPM.

Those two lines are enough to place the name in a precise landscape: the fast productions of the French Antilles, between bouyon and shatta, where collaborations between singers, beatmakers and DJs are released as singles with videos, under labels that often carry the name of the producer artist himself. The label on “Gwo Dada” is Natoxie, named for the collaborator. The label on “I Don’t Know” is DJ Leguste, named for the lead artist. In both cases Cocotteland arrives inside someone else machine, as a guest voice who becomes the lead name on the second record, or the reverse depending on the order in which the dates are read.

The chronological order matters, and this profile follows it: the name appears first as a feature, in April 2024, on a DJ record, then as lead artist, in March 2025, on its own single with two guests. That is the classic path of emerging voices on the current Antillean scene: a first appearance with an established producer, then a record under your own name when the first one worked. The public record allows nothing more to be said about the inside of that sequence, nor about any album, nor about concerts.

The rest of this profile is organised around that honesty: one section per record, one section on the documented collaborators, and a final section listing the unanswered questions. A reader looking for a birth date, a home town or a real name will not find them here, because no verifiable source publishes them, and this site prefers an incomplete profile to an invented one.

“Gwo Dada” (2025): the record that carries the name

“Gwo Dada” is the record that makes Cocotteland exist as a lead artist. RadioVideo.Music, which selected it in its Music For The Dancers roundup of new videos in March 2025, describes it as a Creole titled track that filters French Antillean heritage through a Caribbean prism, released in March 2025, leaning on Guadeloupe and Martinique influences, with upbeat rhythms and lyrics that oscillate between celebratory and wryly observant. The same notice credits the music video direction to Maza Creatives.

The collaborators on the record are, by contrast, documented names on the scene. RadioVideo.Music presents Natoxie as an artist of several trades: beatmaker, DJ, actor and dancer. Mikado appears, in platform listings, as a prolific composer and producer on the same Antillean scene, credited on a large number of recent singles. That Cocotteland first lead single brings together those two profiles, a DJ beatmaker and a studio producer, situates the project: a voice surrounded by the riddim makers of its generation, released on the label of the first of them.

The listed tempo of the record, 160 BPM, places it in the high zone of current bouyon and shatta production, the zone of records built for dancing and for social platforms. This profile will not describe the lyrics beyond the notice quoted, nor the video shot by shot: no verifiable source publishes the song text, and the RadioVideo.Music notice is the only independent editorial description found. It is enough to establish the tone, festive with a point of irony, and the cultural territory claimed by the record itself, the French Antilles.

One clarification is required on the title, because readers of this site will look for it: “Gwo Dada” shows, in no credit, no label page and no article found, any link with Dada, the Dominican producer who started bouyon soca in 2003 according to the signed testimony published on our Dada page. The two names resemble each other, the two territories touch each other, and no source connects them. This profile writes that absence down instead of letting a search engine turn it into a false lineage.

“I Don’t Know” (2024): the appearance with DJ Leguste

The first verifiable appearance of the name is a year older: “I Don’t Know”, by DJ Leguste, with Cocotteland featured, released on April 11, 2024 under the DJ Leguste label. The song page at Shazam lists it at 160 BPM, the same tempo as “Gwo Dada”, and gives the only writing credit in the file: composition and lyrics list Ryan Auguste and Djenae Castelneau as songwriters.

That credit deserves a careful reading, and this profile gives it out loud. The surname Auguste visibly points to the lead artist, DJ Leguste, whose writer surname would be Auguste according to this credit. The second name, Djenae Castelneau, could correspond to the person who sings under the name Cocotteland, or to an outside writer on the record: no public source found confirms it, so this profile does not assign that name to the artist. It publishes it as what it is, a verifiable writing credit on a specific record, and will wait for confirmation before turning it into an identity.

Musically, the public record offers nothing more on this record: no editorial notice, no article, no video description found during the research. Its documented existence alone is enough to push the history of the name back a year, and to show that Cocotteland was already singing, in 2024, inside the circuit of Antillean DJs and producers who release their records with featured voices, a central format of bouyon and shatta production in the 2020s.

For a listener who discovers the name through “Gwo Dada”, this 2024 record is therefore the chronological starting point: first the guest voice with DJ Leguste, then the lead single with Natoxie. Our bouyon playlist places these fast French Antilles productions inside the wider current of bouyon born in Dominica.

The Natoxie and Mikado ecosystem: what the collaborators tell us

When an artist has no public biography, the collaborators become the best indirect source, as long as they are only made to say what they establish. Natoxie, collaborator and label on “Gwo Dada”, is documented as a beatmaker, DJ, actor and dancer by RadioVideo.Music, and his label name returns on many records from the scene, for example productions with Mikado such as “Coucoune aw doux”, released on April 21, 2022 with TKD and Masly, again under the Natoxie label. The circuit is stable: same names, same formats, fast singles with videos.

Mikado, the other guest on “Gwo Dada”, is listed by platforms as a composer on a continuous flow of recent Antillean singles, often alongside the same circle of producers. His presence on Cocotteland first lead single indicates a professional studio circle, not an isolated home production. At this stage of the file, it is the most concrete signal about how far the name is inserted in the local industry: those two collaborators are not gathered by chance.

What this ecosystem does not tell us, however, is the artist herself or himself: the public record does not even establish whether Cocotteland is a female singer, a male singer, a duo or a collective project, nor on which French Antillean island the name is based. The notices speak of Guadeloupe and Martinique influences for the record “Gwo Dada”, not of an origin for the artist. This profile therefore keeps the exact wording of the sources: an artist name from the French Antilles, documented by its records.

That caution has a reason: across the web, mass generated pages happily hand small catalogues complete biographies, towns, ages and influences, copied or invented. Here, the blank is left blank, dated and signed as a state of research on October 20, 2026, to be completed if the artist or their circle publish a verifiable file.

What this profile does not know, and why it exists anyway

The list of unknowns is longer than the list of facts, and it is published here without varnish. Real name: not confirmed, the Djenae Castelneau writing credit on “I Don’t Know” is a published clue, not an established identity. Precise origin: not published, only the Guadeloupe and Martinique influences of the record “Gwo Dada” are documented. Path before 2024: no verifiable trace found. Concerts, tours, radio play: none documented in the open sources. Official accounts: none verified by direct opening, so this page links to no social profile and the sameAs field of the schema stays empty.

Why publish a profile this incomplete? Because the name exists in the living catalogue of bouyon and its Antillean branches, with a lead single in 2025 alongside two of the most active makers on the scene, and because an honest profile, even a short one, serves readers and search engines better than a silence that leaves the field to invented biographies. This profile fixes the verifiable state of the file at its publication date: two records, two dates, two labels, three named collaborators, one credited video.

It also fixes the method of its own correction: if Cocotteland publishes an official page, a press file or a wider catalogue, this text will be completed and redated, like every page on this site, and the sections of unknowns will shrink accordingly. In the meantime, the listening fits in two records and under ten minutes: “I Don’t Know” for the 2024 guest voice, “Gwo Dada” for the 2025 lead name, then our bouyon soca page for the historic branch these fast productions descend from, the one started in Dominica in 2003.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Cocotteland?

The public record does not say yet. Cocotteland is an artist name from the French Antilles documented by two verified records: “I Don’t Know”, by DJ Leguste with Cocotteland featured, released on April 11, 2024, and “Gwo Dada”, its lead single with Natoxie and Mikado, released on March 28, 2025. No biography, interview or official artist page was found during the research for this profile.

What are Cocotteland verified records?

Only two records are established by platform listings: “I Don’t Know” (DJ Leguste feat. Cocotteland, April 11, 2024, DJ Leguste label) and “Gwo Dada” (Cocotteland feat. Natoxie and Mikado, March 28, 2025, Natoxie label, video directed by Maza Creatives according to RadioVideo.Music). No Cocotteland album is listed in the open sources at the date of this profile.

Is “Gwo Dada” connected to Dada, the bouyon soca producer?

No documented connection. Dada is the Dominican producer who, according to the signed testimony of Smokiller published on this site, started bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003. For Cocotteland “Gwo Dada”, no credit, label page or article found mentions Dada or Dominica: the resemblance of the titles is a coincidence this profile flags instead of turning into a lineage.

Where is Cocotteland from?

It is not published in any verifiable way. The only editorial notice found, the RadioVideo.Music piece on “Gwo Dada”, speaks of Guadeloupe and Martinique influences and a French Antillean heritage for the record, not of a town or island of origin for the artist. This profile therefore describes Cocotteland as a name from the French Antilles, without specifying further.

Who wrote “I Don’t Know”?

The song page at Shazam credits Ryan Auguste and Djenae Castelneau with composition and lyrics. The first name visibly points to DJ Leguste, the lead artist. For the second, no source found confirms that it is the person who sings under the name Cocotteland, so this profile publishes the credit without assigning it as the artist identity.

Where can I hear Cocotteland?

On the platforms that list the two records: “Gwo Dada” on the Natoxie label, with its official video, and “I Don’t Know” with DJ Leguste. To place these productions inside the current of the genre, our bouyon playlist follows bouyon from Dominica to its French Antilles branches.

What is Cocotteland’s first record?

The first verifiable appearance of the name is “I Don’t Know”, by DJ Leguste featuring Cocotteland, released on 11 April 2024 on the DJ Leguste label, at 160 BPM. The single under the artist’s own name, “Gwo Dada”, followed on 28 March 2025. It is the classic path of emerging voices on today’s French Caribbean scene: first a guest of an established producer, then the headliner once the first appearance has worked.

Who is DJ Leguste, with whom Cocotteland sings I Don’t Know?

DJ Leguste is a producer and DJ of the French Caribbean bouyon scene, tied to Guadeloupe in public credits. He releases a steady catalogue of singles, almost all collaborations and almost all at 160 BPM, under his own label. His songwriting credits carry the name Ryan Auguste, the same as on “I Don’t Know”. See our DJ Leguste page.

Who are Natoxie and Mikado, the guests on Gwo Dada?

RadioVideo.Music presents Natoxie as a beatmaker, DJ, actor and dancer, and his name is also the label of “Gwo Dada” and of other records from the scene, including “Coucoune aw doux” with Mikado, TKD and Masly, released on 21 April 2022. Mikado is listed by the platforms as a prolific composer and producer of recent French Caribbean singles. Their presence places Cocotteland within a professional studio circle.

What is Gwo Dada by Cocotteland about?

According to the RadioVideo.Music notice, the only editorial description found, “Gwo Dada” is a Creole track that filters the heritage of the French Caribbean through a Caribbean lens, leaning on Guadeloupe and Martinique influences, with upbeat rhythms and lyrics that swing between celebration and wry observation. No verifiable source publishes the lyrics, and this profile does not describe them any further.

Who directed the Gwo Dada music video?

The “Gwo Dada” video is directed by Maza Creatives, according to RadioVideo.Music, which picked it in March 2025 for its Music For The Dancers selection of new videos. It is published under the title “Cocotteland Ft Natoxie et Mikado, Gwo Dada (Clip Officiel)”. Together with the two track pages, this video is one of the few verifiable pieces of Cocotteland’s public record.

Does Cocotteland make bouyon or shatta?

Both records belong to the fast productions of the French Caribbean, between bouyon and shatta, and both are listed at 160 BPM, the cruising tempo of modern bouyon gwada. No source classifies the artist more precisely, and this profile sticks to those facts. The line between the two sounds, and what sets them apart, is explained on our bouyon vs shatta page.

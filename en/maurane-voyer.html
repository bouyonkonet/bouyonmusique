A voice from Martinique, not a passing guest

Start by placing Maurane Voyer correctly: she is a singer from Martinique. That matters, because her story reads from the French Caribbean, from an island where Creole music is part of everyday life, and not from a distant scene discovering bouyon by chance. Her presence in bouyon in 2026 comes from that ground, from a culture shared with neighbouring Dominica, separated by a stretch of sea but joined by language, dance and Creole history.

Online she builds her audience as @mauraneetc, on Instagram and on X alike. The verified Instagram account, at about 89.6K followers at our October 2026 check, shows an artist who already had her own audience before bouyon. That detail changes how 'Joli BB' should be read: Maurane Voyer does not arrive on the record as a borrowed voice for one season, she enters it with her audience, her island and her musical identity already in place.

That independence is why Bouyon Shattaz talks about her as half of the record, not as a decorative guest. On many collaborations the female voice carries a chorus and then disappears behind the main name. Here the situation is reversed: the record needs both of its shores to exist. Without Maurane Voyer's Martinique, 'Joli BB' loses its geographical meaning. Without Reo's Dominica, it loses its bouyon source. The record holds because both artists are at home on it.

Reggae, dancehall and French Caribbean sounds

Maurane Voyer's musical ground is reggae, dancehall and French Caribbean sounds. That base can be heard in the way she places her voice: singing that stays melodic while keeping a strong sense of rhythm and dance. For listeners discovering bouyon from France or from the French Caribbean, that wider culture is a natural doorway, because it links bouyon to styles they already know without erasing what makes bouyon distinct.

Bouyon itself was born in Dominica, and its full story is told on our history of bouyon page. In Martinique, bouyon is also danced as jump up. That movement between islands is not new: sounds travel, dances travel, and artists carry that back and forth in their own training. Maurane Voyer fits into that movement with a voice shaped by reggae and dancehall, able to sit on a bouyon riddim without forcing her identity into a new shape.

Listening to Maurane Voyer before 'Joli BB' therefore helps you understand the 2026 record. You hear a singer who is not trying to imitate Dominican bouyon, but who brings her own French Caribbean colour inside the genre. That is exactly what makes the collaboration interesting for promoters and for audiences: she does not copy the source, she talks with it. Martinique and Dominica do not play the same role in bouyon history, and the record is stronger for keeping that difference audible.

Joli BB with Reo: the bridge between Martinique and Dominica

'Joli BB' was released on October 9, 2026 on the Mauve label. The song runs for 2 minutes and 43 seconds, a short and direct format built for repeat listening and for dancing. It was written by Maurane Voyer, Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume. Those credits matter: they show shared writing work, with the Martinique singer listed as a writer of the song she performs with Reo, and not only as a guest singer brought in for a chorus.

Bouyon Shattaz published 'Joli BB' on release. That editorial choice shows the place the song holds in our reading of bouyon 2026: a record that builds a bridge between Martinique and Dominica, the two Creole shores of the genre. On one side Dominica, the island where bouyon was born. On the other Martinique, where the same energy is also danced as jump up. Between the two, a voice and an artist answering each other, each from their own island, in a shared musical language.

For French audiences, 'Joli BB' is also a good doorway into current bouyon. The song is sung, melodic and easy to remember, while keeping the energy of the riddim. It lets you discover Reo, the Dominican artist covered on his own page, and Maurane Voyer in the same move. Hear the song, then read both artist profiles, and you get a fairer picture of bouyon in 2026: a Dominican genre, alive, moving between Creole islands and growing through meetings like this one when they respect the source.

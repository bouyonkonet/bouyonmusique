Maurane Voyer: Martinique at the heart of bouyon 2026

Published September 17, 2026

Maurane Voyer is a singer from Martinique, known on Instagram as @mauraneetc, with a verified account that stood at about 89.6K followers at our October 2026 check. In 2026 she sings 'Joli BB' with Dominican artist Reo, a record that links Martinique and Dominica, the two Creole shores of bouyon. This page covers her background, her songs and the place she holds among the women of bouyon.

A voice from Martinique, not a passing guest

Start by placing Maurane Voyer correctly: she is a singer from Martinique. That matters, because her story reads from the French Caribbean, from an island where Creole music is part of everyday life, and not from a distant scene discovering bouyon by chance. Her presence in bouyon in 2026 comes from that ground, from a culture shared with neighbouring Dominica, separated by a stretch of sea but joined by language, dance and Creole history.

Online she builds her audience as @mauraneetc, on Instagram and on X alike. The verified Instagram account, at about 89.6K followers at our October 2026 check, shows an artist who already had her own audience before bouyon. That detail changes how 'Joli BB' should be read: Maurane Voyer does not arrive on the record as a borrowed voice for one season, she enters it with her audience, her island and her musical identity already in place.

That independence is why Bouyon Shattaz talks about her as half of the record, not as a decorative guest. On many collaborations the female voice carries a chorus and then disappears behind the main name. Here the situation is reversed: the record needs both of its shores to exist. Without Maurane Voyer's Martinique, 'Joli BB' loses its geographical meaning. Without Reo's Dominica, it loses its bouyon source. The record holds because both artists are at home on it.

Reggae, dancehall and French Caribbean sounds

Maurane Voyer's musical ground is reggae, dancehall and French Caribbean sounds. That base can be heard in the way she places her voice: singing that stays melodic while keeping a strong sense of rhythm and dance. For listeners discovering bouyon from France or from the French Caribbean, that wider culture is a natural doorway, because it links bouyon to styles they already know without erasing what makes bouyon distinct.

Bouyon itself was born in Dominica, and its full story is told on our history of bouyon page. In Martinique, bouyon is also danced as jump up. That movement between islands is not new: sounds travel, dances travel, and artists carry that back and forth in their own training. Maurane Voyer fits into that movement with a voice shaped by reggae and dancehall, able to sit on a bouyon riddim without forcing her identity into a new shape.

Listening to Maurane Voyer before 'Joli BB' therefore helps you understand the 2026 record. You hear a singer who is not trying to imitate Dominican bouyon, but who brings her own French Caribbean colour inside the genre. That is exactly what makes the collaboration interesting for promoters and for audiences: she does not copy the source, she talks with it. Martinique and Dominica do not play the same role in bouyon history, and the record is stronger for keeping that difference audible.

The BBW album and the recent singles Pran Swen Dew, Pa Bizwen Yo and Brennen

In 2022 Maurane Voyer released the album 'BBW'. The album is the base of her recent catalogue and lets you place her beyond a single viral song or a single collaboration. For a new listener, starting with 'BBW' gives the full picture: the voice, the themes and the colours that make her signature, before the bouyon step of 2026. It is also a useful reminder that an artist's journey does not begin on the day a wider public discovers her.

After the album come recent singles whose titles appear in her official listings: 'Pran Swen Dew', 'Pa Bizwen Yo' and 'Brennen'. Those three titles, written in Creole in their spelling, already speak of the link to language and island. Presenting them together shows an active artist who keeps releasing music under her own name, alongside the collaborations that widen her audience towards Dominica and the bouyon scene.

For a coherent listening path, run the album and those singles first, then arrive at 'Joli BB'. On that path Maurane Voyer appears from two complementary angles: a solo artist with a catalogue of her own, and one half of a duo that marked the autumn of 2026. Joli BB with Reo: the bridge between Martinique and Dominica

'Joli BB' was released on October 9, 2026 on the Mauve label. The song runs for 2 minutes and 43 seconds, a short and direct format built for repeat listening and for dancing. It was written by Maurane Voyer, Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume. Those credits matter: they show shared writing work, with the Martinique singer listed as a writer of the song she performs with Reo, and not only as a guest singer brought in for a chorus.

Bouyon Shattaz published 'Joli BB' on release. That editorial choice shows the place the song holds in our reading of bouyon 2026: a record that builds a bridge between Martinique and Dominica, the two Creole shores of the genre. On one side Dominica, the island where bouyon was born. On the other Martinique, where the same energy is also danced as jump up. Between the two, a voice and an artist answering each other, each from their own island, in a shared musical language.

For French audiences, 'Joli BB' is also a good doorway into current bouyon. The song is sung, melodic and easy to remember, while keeping the energy of the riddim. It lets you discover Reo, the Dominican artist covered on his own page, and Maurane Voyer in the same move. Hear the song, then read both artist profiles, and you get a fairer picture of bouyon in 2026: a Dominican genre, alive, moving between Creole islands and growing through meetings like this one when they respect the source.

WCMF 2026: Maurane Voyer listed for Night 1 in Roseau

According to published WCMF 2026 guides, Maurane Voyer performs on Night 1, Friday October 23, 2026, at the World Creole Music Festival in Roseau, Dominica. The festival is Dominica's major Creole music gathering, covered in full on WCMF, the Creole festival. For a singer from Martinique, playing that first night in Roseau, a few days after the release of 'Joli BB', gives the collaboration a logical next step on stage, in front of the audience of the source island.

Night 1 carries a large share of the attention at the 2026 edition, with artists from Dominica and the neighbouring islands. In that context, Maurane Voyer's presence is not simple bill filling: it extends the bridge that 'Joli BB' opened between Martinique and Dominica. The Roseau audience will hear a French Caribbean voice who has just released a bouyon song with a Dominican artist, and that context gives the performance a particular charge.

To prepare for that night, the advised listening stays simple: 'Joli BB' first, then Maurane Voyer's recent singles, then Reo's songs. That sequence lets you arrive at the festival with both voices in your head and an understanding of why their meeting matters this season. A central female voice in bouyon 2026

Our women of bouyon chapter defends a simple reading: the women of bouyon are not decoration around producers and male singers, they carry songs, audiences and islands. Maurane Voyer illustrates that reading clearly in 2026. She arrives with an album, singles under her own name, a verified audience on social media, a writing credit on 'Joli BB' and a WCMF date. None of those elements makes her a secondary guest.

That central place also helps new listeners enter the genre more easily. From a distance, bouyon can look dominated by riddims, producers and party voices. Singers like Maurane Voyer are a reminder that the genre also lives through singing, melody and writing. Her contribution to 'Joli BB' sits exactly there: a vocal line that stays in the head, an openly Martinique Creole presence, and the ability to hold the song as an equal with a Dominican artist on his own ground.

The logical next step, if you want to follow her, is to keep her name in regular rotation: play 'BBW' again, follow 'Pran Swen Dew', 'Pa Bizwen Yo' and 'Brennen', then return to 'Joli BB' with festival ears. That is how a lasting bouyon culture gets built, from catalogues and not only from viral moments. In 2026 Maurane Voyer is one of the voices giving that culture a face, an island and a next chapter. One final marker helps keep her path in memory: a solo artist first, with 'BBW' and her singles, then a duo partner on 'Joli BB', then a festival artist listed in Roseau. Those three steps read in order, without inventing a fourth. That clarity is what makes her profile useful to new listeners and to promoters preparing the 2026 bouyon season and looking for solid Creole voices, clearly identified and already followed by a real audience on their own islands.

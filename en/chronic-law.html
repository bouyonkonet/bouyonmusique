Chronic Law: Empty and the pain music of St. Thomas, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Chronic Law is a Jamaican dancehall artist, real name Akeeme Campbell, from St. Thomas, in the east of Jamaica. He is not a bouyon artist, and this profile lends him no bouyon record: his place on this site rests on one precise and verified collaboration. On May 27, 2026, the single “Asphalt”, by Kkrytical and Chronic Law, was released with both voices credited, written by Ronald Longmore and Akeeme Campbell and produced by Topranks Ent and Mr. Chin, according to the Shazam page of the record. Behind that point of contact, the file is that of one of the most productive voices in modern dancehall: a first recording as early as 2009, a breakthrough in 2018 with “Government”, the 6ix collective, a record, “Empty”, that World Music Views measured as the most streamed song in YouTube Jamaica history, and a whole EP, I.C.E., born of a detention in the United States. This profile follows that path source by source, and says where the pages contradict each other.

Who Chronic Law is: Akeeme Campbell, St. Thomas and the 6ix collective

Chronic Law is the stage name of Akeeme Campbell. The Akeeme spelling is the one in the Shazam writer credits, where he signs “Asphalt” beside Ronald Longmore, and the one used by DancehallMag, the dancehall reference that carries an artist page for him. An older profile, published in February 2019 by DancehallReggaeWorld, spells the same name Ackeme, without the second e: the two spellings name the same man, and this profile holds the platform credit spelling, Akeeme, while flagging the variant.

His documented territory is the east of Jamaica. Shazam gives his hometown as St. Thomas, Jamaica, and DancehallMag presents him as an artist from St. Thomas who emerged from the music scene of the east of the island. The 2019 profile names the town: Yallahs, a small town on the east coast, and names his secondary school, Morant Bay High School. The same article then states, a few lines later, that he comes from the parish of Portland, which contradicts Yallahs, which sits in the parish of St. Thomas. This profile therefore holds St. Thomas, attested by two independent sources and by his own record “St. Thomas Native”, and flags the Portland mention as an internal error of that older page.

His other names are documented by DancehallMag: his official pages present him under the names Chronic Law, 1Law and Lawboss, the last one often written Law Boss, as the 2019 profile does when it calls him the lawboss. Those aliases are not other artists: they are signatures of the same deejay, and mistaking them for collaborators would be a misreading of the credits. Shazam also gives his birth date, December 17, 1993, and files his genre as Modern Dancehall; no other source opened for this profile confirms that exact day, which is therefore published here as platform data, attributed.

His collective is the second pillar of his public identity. Shazam presents him as a member of the 6ix collective, based in Montego Bay, and DancehallMag describes him the same way. The 2019 profile adds that the Montego Bay 6ix team is headed by Squash, and so explains his huge fan base in the parish of St. James, in the west, far from his native St. Thomas: an artist from the east, carried by a western collective, and heard across the whole island.

2009 to 2018: a first record, then the Government breakthrough

The published chronology holds a surprise: when the public discovered Chronic Law in late 2018, he had been recording for nearly ten years. According to the DancehallReggaeWorld profile, written in February 2019, his first recording dates to 2009: a hardcore dancehall record titled “War Dem A Ask fah”, produced by Sting G Music Ltd on the Cemetary Riddim. That early trace was not found on the platform pages opened for this profile, and it is therefore published at the level of its source, a specialist press profile, without entering the structured data of this page.

The breakthrough, by contrast, is dated to the month by the same source. In August 2018 he teamed with producer Romaine McFarlane for “Stress Free”, on the EastSyde Records label. In September 2018 came “Government”, produced by Cashflow Rinse and KevStar Records, which the profile describes as an instant hit with hardcore dancehall fans, to the point that everyone wanted to know who the voice behind the record was. DancehallMag confirms the role of that record: the Chronic Law breakthrough is commonly associated with the rise of “Hillside”, then with records such as “Government”, “Cyan Kool” and “Hilltop Badness”, with “Cyan Kool” credited to Triple B Ent in the release list of the 2019 profile.

Around those records, the 2019 profile lines up the first run: “Celebrate”, “Born Fi Diss”, “Never Stop Try”, “Circumstances”, “Born Killa”, “Hilltop Gyallis”, “Gyal and Gun”, “Money Hunter” and “Bless Me”. The Shazam biography sums up what followed in one sentence: a Jamaican dancehall star in his own right, releasing a steady stream of hard hitting singles since 2018. The model is set in that year, and it never changes: no event album, but continuous singles, riddim after riddim.

That model explains the shape of his present catalog. Where an album artist concentrates a year on one record, Chronic Law holds the ground week after week, and the Jamaican YouTube charts, which World Music Views follows record by record, still show him at the top of the artists in 2025 and 2026. The 2018 breakthrough is therefore not a peak: it is the start of a permanent presence, whose substance the next section describes.

Pain music: a documented style, between street narrative and melody

DancehallMag gives the clearest reading of his style. The artist is described there for a distinctive blend: raw street narratives, melodic phrasing and emotionally direct writing. His music is framed there as socially aware pain music, with recurring themes: survival, faith, resilience and the realities of inner city life. His tone is called vivid in storytelling, reflective and often somber. This profile carries that characterisation with its attribution, because it comes from a specialist newsroom and not from label promotional copy.

The records confirm the grid. “Empty”, whose Shazam page gives publication on June 23, 2022 and whose recording itself names JanoDon in its opening lines, speaks of loss and a heavy heart from its chorus on. “Corrupt”, which the Shazam lists place among his most streamed songs, and “Corrupt Pt3”, which would top the YouTube Jamaica chart in February 2025, attack corruption in the title itself. “Still a Bleed” circulates in the best known song lists drawn up by tracking platforms, on the same level as “Walk With Faith”, whose title alone states the faith side of the DancehallMag grid.

That writing sets him apart inside his own collective and his generation. Modern dancehall holds many voices of confrontation; his adds a confessional share that every source notes, from the 2019 profile that discovered him to the reports of 2026. The lawboss nickname might suggest a character of pure authority; the catalog, by contrast, documents a chronicler of loss and endurance, which explains why his public reaches beyond the circle of hard record listeners.

To place this dancehall against the genres of this site: it shares with bouyon the culture of the riddim, the single and the sound system, and nothing else that is documented. Bouyon soca, started in Dominica by Dada in 2003 according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller and popularized by Triple Kay, follows another island and another history. Chronic Law has no bouyon record known to the sources consulted; his point of contact with this site is a man, Kkrytical, and a record, “Asphalt”, treated in the next section after the two measured peaks of his catalog.

Empty and St. Thomas Native: the two measured peaks of the catalog

The central record of the file is “Empty”. Its Shazam page, opened for this profile, gives publication on June 23, 2022. World Music Views, the outlet that compiles YouTube Jamaica figures, published the result that fixes its scale: “Empty” dethroned “Protocol” by Skeng to become, according to the data compiled by that outlet, the most streamed song of all time on YouTube Jamaica. It is a national platform record, attributed here to the source that measures it, and it places a slow, mourning pain music record at the top of a market dominated by confrontation records.

The second peak is a guest record. “St. Thomas Native”, by Popcaan featuring Chronic Law, names his home parish in its very title. On the Chronic Law artist page on Shazam, opened for this profile, it is the most identified record of all time, with about 240,000 identifications counted at opening, ahead of his own lead name records. World Music Views documents the continuation of the collaboration: on January 3, 2025, Popcaan and Chronic Law released “Kick It Wid Myself”, presented by the outlet as an anthem for the youth of St. Thomas. The eastern parish is therefore at once his origin, his subject and his argument to the widest dancehall public.

The other measures confirm a chart head presence, not a flash. The most identified records on his Shazam page line up “Badness Upgrade 2.0”, with DJ Mac, at about 173,000 identifications, “NY Girls”, with CJTheChemist, at about 165,000, “Higher Life”, with Kranium, at about 136,000, and “Weekend (Bup Bup)”, with Notnice, at about 80,600, figures counted when the page was opened and bound to move. In February 2025, “Corrupt Pt3 (Not a Valentines)” debuted at the top of the World Music Views YouTube Jamaica chart with 250,632 streams counted by the outlet. In June 2025 the same outlet crowned him the most viewed artist of the month, and for the week ending October 1, 2026 he still holds first place among artists on YouTube Jamaica.

Those figures draw the exact profile: a singles artist whose biggest personal record is a mourning ballad, whose biggest record overall is a Popcaan feature, and whose consistency is measured in years of national charts. It is inside that catalog, at full strength, that his collaboration with the artist this site already documented takes its place.

Asphalt with Kkrytical: the point of contact with this site

The point of contact is a 2026 single, “Asphalt”, credited to Kkrytical and Chronic Law. Its Shazam page, opened for this profile, gives publication on May 27, 2026 and details the full credits: Kkrytical and Chronic Law on vocals, Ronald Longmore and Akeeme Campbell as writers, Topranks Ent and Mr. Chin as producers. The record was released as a standalone single, and the same page files it among the featured records of Kkrytical, beside “Loyalty”, “7.62” and “Hustla”.

The other side of the credit is documented by the specialist press. Kaboom Magazine, in an article published on August 6, 2026 about the signing of Kkrytical to exclusive worldwide management with Showtime Services, presents the artist as a Jamaican born, New York based dancehall artist, real name Ron Longmore, and names “Asphalt” featuring Chronic Law among the records that built his recognition, with “Loyalty”, “Hustla” and “7.62”. The writer credit first name, Ronald Longmore, and the article name, Ron Longmore, designate the same man. The Kkrytical profile on this site already carried “Asphalt” in its data: this profile completes the file from the other voice of the record.

The official video of the record, referenced by Shazam in the popular video list of Chronic Law as in dancehall mix descriptions, presents the song as a Kkrytical record featuring Chronic Law, produced by Topranks and Mr. Chin on the Asphalt Riddim. The format is that of riddim dancehall: one rhythm, several voices, and a collaboration between a deejay of the New York diaspora and the established voice of St. Thomas. For Chronic Law it is one feature among many; for this site it is the line that links his catalog to an artist followed here record by record.

Around “Asphalt”, his recent collaborations draw the same network of producers and voices: “Whats Cooking”, with Sheff G and CJTheChemist, referenced on his Shazam page; “NY Girls”, with CJTheChemist, and its remix with French Montana; “Higher Life”, with Kranium; “Dark”, with NeeQah; “Me Alone”, with Java; “Bad N Boasy”, with Demarco. None of those collaborations is a bouyon record, and this profile presents them only for what they are: the address book of a dancehall deejay at the top of his market, one line of which runs through Kkrytical.

I.C.E. with Notnice, and the limits of the public record

The most discussed project of his recent period is an EP built with producer Notnice, I.C.E., whose expanded title is Inside, Cold, and Empty. The detailed review published by Hunnypot Unlimited describes a nine track project, produced largely by Notnice, written after a 73 day detention in a US immigration facility. The review follows the record track by track: an intro built from news clips covering the detention, then “Like Samson”, presented as the centre of the project, “Prayer Work”, “Tree Limb”, a phone skit titled “Call”, “Ride or Die”, “Pain Pt. 2”, “It s Hard” and, closing, “If A Neva God”, turned toward faith. This profile reports that context at the level of that review, without detailing an ongoing legal procedure beyond what the music itself documents.

The choice of Notnice as lead producer closes a loop in the catalog: Notnice already co signs “Weekend (Bup Bup)”, one of the most identified records on his Shazam page. Two of the EP records, “Like Samson” and “Ride or Die”, also appear in the popular video list of his Shazam artist page opened for this profile, which anchors them in the structured data of this page on the same level as the singles. The other EP records are cited here from the review, and not from a platform page opened record by record.

The limits of the file are real, and this profile states them. The full catalog is too prolific to list record by record: hundreds of singles circulate under his name since 2018, and this profile holds only those that opened pages document. His home parish is contradicted by the 2019 profile, which calls him a Portland artist after placing him in Yallahs; this profile holds St. Thomas, attested by Shazam and DancehallMag. His full name circulates in two spellings, Akeeme in current credits and Ackeme in the 2019 press. His full birth date comes from Shazam alone. And the figures quoted, Shazam identifications and World Music Views charts, are those counted at the time of research.

If a reader keeps only one listening path, here it is: “Government” and “Hillside” for the 2018 and 2019 breakthrough, “Empty” for the peak, “St. Thomas Native” with Popcaan for the parish record, “NY Girls” and “Higher Life” for the collaborations, “Like Samson” for I.C.E., and “Asphalt” with Kkrytical for the thread that links this catalog to this site. The genre context is read on our bouyon soca page and in our bouyon playlist, where Chronic Law, quite logically, does not appear as a bouyon artist.

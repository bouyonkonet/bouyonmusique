How to make a bouyon beat, step by step

Published September 4, 2026

Making a bouyon beat is not just speeding up a dancehall beat. It is rebuilding, with today’s tools, the mechanics of a Dominican carnival band.

Step 1: the rhythm first

Start with the rhythmic grid: percussion inspired by lapo kabwit and jing ping, fast and steady drums, build-ups that imitate the calls of carnival. Work the loop until it makes you want to move with no vocal on it at all.

Step 2: the keyboard loop and the bass

The keyboard carries the melodic signature: a short, repetitive, almost hypnotic motif, inherited from the WCK method. The synth bass follows, heavy and simple. Add background vocals, shouts and responses, to recreate the crowd.

Step 3: test it dancing

The final test of bouyon is physical: dance to it, or watch someone dance. If the body does not follow, the beat is not finished. That is why so many producers launch their riddims in dance videos before the official release.

Frequently asked questions

Who produces bouyon today?

Producers like Smokiller (Bouyon Shattaz), Denmath, HYPNO! and Savion work the bouyon sound across riddims, remixes and original productions.

What is a bouyon riddim?

A riddim is the instrumental several artists record over. Riddims sit at the heart of bouyon and dancehall production. A bouyon riddim is built starting from the rhythm, then the keyboard loop and the synth bass, and many producers launch it in a dance video before the official release.

Where do you start a bouyon beat?

Start with the rhythm: a grid of percussion inspired by lapo kabwit and jing ping, fast and steady drums, and build-ups that imitate the calls of carnival. Work that loop until it makes you want to move with no vocal on it at all, before adding the keyboard and the bass.

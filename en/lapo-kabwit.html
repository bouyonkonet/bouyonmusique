Lapo kabwit, the drum of the Dominican carnival

By Smokiller · Published May 3, 2026 · Updated May 3, 2026

Lapo kabwit means goat skin. The drums known by that name are the most prominent part of Dominica's instrumental tradition. Traditional carnival music, played with chante mas, lapo kabwit became one of the ingredients of bouyon, when WCK took up its native drum rhythms to build the Dominican sound.

A drum made of goat skin

The drums called lapo kabwit are the most prominent part of Dominica's instrumental tradition. Carnival music begins with the goatskin drums of J Ouvert, which open Carnival Monday in Roseau, from 4am to 7am.

Today, the skins are often stretched over plastic and metal bodies rather than the traditional hollowed tree trunks, according to Caribbean Beat. A lapo kabwit street band plays with drums made of goatskin and a variety of metal objects to make music, as the student documentary This is Bouyon recalls, quoting WCK's Cornell Phillip on the original instruments of bouyon.

Chante mas and the tambou lele

Lapo kabwit goes with chante mas, the masquerade song, the traditional carnival music of Dominica whose cultural origins go back to the 19th century. Masquerading partygoers parade for two days: a lead vocalist, the chantwel, followed by a responsorial chorus, the lavwa, with drummers and dancers dancing backwards in front of the drummer, on a tambou lele.

Chante mas lyrics are traditionally based on gossip and scandal, addressing the personal shortcomings of others, and the songs are almost all in French Creole. The traditional instrumentation includes the lapo kabwit drums, the tambou lele, the chak chak, the scraper-rattle, the cowbell, the tambourine, the triangle, the conch shell, iron and several horn players.

From carnival to bouyon

In the early 1960s, calypso and steelpan replaced chante mas and lapo kabwit as the music of carnival in Roseau. After a fire in 1963, the traditional carnival was banned, although calypso and steelpan kept growing. Several villages, such as Grand Bay, preserved the tradition.

In modern Dominica, chante mas and lapo kabwit have become a part of bouyon music. WCK began using native drum rhythms such as lapo kabwit, along with elements of the music of jing ping bands and ragga-style vocals. As Shelly Alfred, of Signal Band, put it, bouyon was born as a modernization of the country's traditional instruments, and its fans dance to the booming beats of the lapo kabwit, the rich notes of the accordion, guitar and electronic keyboard.

Frequently asked questions

What does lapo kabwit mean?

Lapo kabwit means goat skin. The drums known by that name are the most prominent part of Dominica's instrumental tradition, and carnival music begins with them at J Ouvert.

What is chante mas?

Chante mas, the masquerade song, is the traditional carnival music of Dominica, played with lapo kabwit. Its origins go back to the 19th century: a lead singer, the chantwel, followed by a responsorial chorus, the lavwa, with lyrics in French Creole based on gossip and scandal.

Where can you hear lapo kabwit today?

At the J Ouvert of the Roseau carnival, from 4am to 7am on Carnival Monday, when the lapo kabwit bands open the two days of celebration, and in bouyon, which took up the rhythms of these drums as one of its ingredients.

Bouyon artists from Dominica: the source island roster

By Smokiller · Published October 16, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

Dominica is not one chapter of bouyon among others: it is the source island, where the genre was born and where its founders, its bosses and its new wave still come from. This page gathers the Dominican roster followed by Bouyon Shattaz, from the creators of 1988 to the names billed at the 2026 World Creole Music Festival, the WCMF, in Roseau. Every artist has a full profile on this site. To understand where the sound comes from, start with our history of bouyon page, then come back here: a lineage, not a simple list.

The founders: WCK creates bouyon in 1988

Every Dominican roster starts with WCK. The band created bouyon in 1988 in Roseau, the capital of Dominica. That founding act still matters today, because everything that carries the name bouyon, in Guadeloupe, in Martinique, in France or in the diaspora, points back to this Dominican invention. WCK is therefore not just an old name to cite out of respect: it is the starting point of the genre, the proof that bouyon has one island, one town and one precise birth date.

Placing WCK at the top of this page also sets the reading rule for the whole site. When a sound travels, credits get lost easily. Here they are written in black and white: bouyon was created in Dominica in 1988, and the artists on this page inherit that work, transform it or defend it. The generations that follow, from the jump up bosses to today's producers, are all measured against that heritage, whether they claim it in interviews or simply on stage, in front of the Roseau crowd.

The cadence-lypso legacy: Midnight Groovers

Even before bouyon, Dominica already had a great band tradition, and Midnight Groovers is its legendary name. Formed in Grand Bay in the late 1960s, this cadence-lypso band is led by Phillip "Chubby" Mark and counts more than 39 albums. A catalogue like that says something essential about the island: live band music, rooted in a village and a language, was an institution there long before streaming platforms.

Midnight Groovers belongs in this roster because bouyon was not born from nothing. It grows on ground prepared by cadence-lypso, by village fetes and by musicians who recorded album after album for their public. Understanding that depth helps explain why Dominica defends its invention with such conviction: behind bouyon there are decades of Dominican music, and Grand Bay, the village of Midnight Groovers, remains one of its beating hearts.

The boss: Asa Bantan, from Grand Bay

Asa Bantan comes from Grand Bay, the same village as Midnight Groovers, and he is widely called the King of Bouyon, and Dominica Festivals has identified him as the Bouyon Boss. Those are attributed titles, the ones the public and the scene give him, and they describe his place in the genre: the boss, the reference artist the others have to face on stage and in the playlists. His name comes up whenever Dominican bouyon is discussed in the present tense, not only in the past.

Asa Bantan represents the continuity between the invention and the current scene: a carnival presence, a repertoire the public knows by heart and a natural authority live. His profile is the doorway into Dominican bouyon in its most direct form, jump up.

The titled generation: Reo and Ridge

Reo is billed as the Prince of Bouyon: again, that is an attributed title, the one the scene presents him under, and it suits an artist placed in the direct line of the bosses. Reo is a 2026 Caribbean Music Awards nominee, a recognition that reaches beyond the island. In 2026 he also carries one of the most visible bridges in current bouyon, "Joli BB" with Martinican singer Maurane Voyer, a record that links Roseau to Fort-de-France.

Ridge, known as Mr Ridge, is the other Dominican name nominated at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards, and he is the one who won the bouyon artist of the year award there, according to the results published in September 2026. A keyboardist and producer, he is a reminder that bouyon is not only about singers: the musicians who build the sound, the arrangements and the riddims hold the genre up as much as the voices do. Ridge is also billed on Night 1 of WCMF 2026. His profile completes Reo's: on one side the voice and the prince title, on the other the keyboard, the production and the studio work.

The live band engine: Signal Band and TK International

Signal Band is the band behind two trending tracks by Shelly, the Dominican singer presented further down this page. That role shows the importance of bands in today's bouyon: a solid band turns a good song into a track that runs everywhere, because it knows how to play it loud, fast and long, the way the carnival and fete public demands. Signal Band is therefore worth following as an engine of the scene, not as a simple backing act.

TK International, the Triple Kay Band, belongs to the same culture of great Dominican bands and is billed on Night 1 of WCMF 2026. The presence of these groups at the festival shows that, in Dominica, bouyon remains band music and stage music, inherited from cadence-lypso and built for live performance. When you prepare your festival, keep these two names in mind: they are the ones who carry the island's sound when the lights go up at Windsor Park.

The voices and the solos: Shelly and Colton T

Shelly is a Dominican singer whose track "Bye to de Old Me (Remix)" marks his path, with Signal Band behind two of his trending tracks. His place in this roster is that of an island voice establishing himself in today's bouyon, with songs that speak of leaving, changing and standing up for yourself. His profile is to be read alongside the other voices on this site.

Colton T represents the solo artist side of the roster. He is billed on Night 1 of WCMF 2026, alongside the bands and names already cited. That place on the bill of the home festival, in Roseau, works as a confirmation: the Dominican scene trusts its solo artists to open the biggest Creole stage on the island. Colton T thus completes a picture where historic bands, bosses, a producer and solo voices coexist, all branches of the same tree.

Street and carnival names: Pudaz and Gwada G

Pudaz, known as Dirty Dawg Pudaz, carries the track "Shake Something", a title that says everything about his function at a party: make people move. Dominican bouyon always keeps a place for these pure-energy artists, whose songs are dance orders as much as tracks. Pudaz embodies that direct, festive branch of the roster, the one that links playlists to carnival streets and to the parties where the genre is tested for real, in front of a public that does not forgive a weak track.

Gwada G needs one clarification, and it matters: despite his name, which points to Guadeloupe, Gwada G is a Dominican artist, a homegrown talent confirmed in the official Discover Dominica Authority release. His case is a reminder of why this site verifies every origin instead of guessing it from a stage name. In a genre that travels between islands with close names, a stage name can tell a wink, a journey or a public, but only the official source fixes the origin.

The new wave: BONNA, Ras B and Litleboy

BONNA is the new talent of this roster: an artist from La Plaine, in Dominica, signed to More Sewo, and carried by the track "Not Real". His signing to a label and the circulation of his name in 2026 show how the Dominican next generation is getting structured: a village, a track that runs, a team behind it. BONNA is the name to watch for anyone who wants to hear where the source island's bouyon goes next, and his profile on this site follows that rise step by step.

Ras B works on the border of rap and bouyon, with the track "Come Here". That crossing matters: bouyon has always absorbed the sounds of its time, and rap brings it a flow, a writing style and a young public. Litleboy LSBeats767 completes this trio on the production and credits side: he is credited on "Pow Pow / Allo Doudou", the track certified gold by SNEP, and the 767 in his name is a reference to Dominica's area code. Even in a credit read from France, the source island stays signed in the name.

The remix producer: Smokiller

Smokiller closes this roster with a different profile: Dominican-born, he is the producer behind the Bouyon Shattaz remixes, the ones that flip tracks from other worlds into bouyon. His place here is logical: a Dominican-born producer extends the sound of Roseau towards new publics. He is also the editorial signature of this site, published by Bouyon Shattaz.

Smokiller's work shows another way to defend the origin: through production. A successful bouyon remix imposes Dominica's rhythm and energy on songs the world already knows, then sends the listener back to the artists on this page.

The home meeting point: WCMF 2026

This roster meets on one stage: the 2026 World Creole Music Festival in Roseau, whose Night 1 bill includes Ridge, TK International and Colton T. Our page on the WCMF, Dominica's Creole festival, details the event. For the source island, this festival is not one more concert: it is the annual showcase where Dominica presents its music to the Creole world, in front of the public that watched it being born.

Preparing for WCMF with this roster changes the listening: every name read here becomes a set time not to miss. To extend the party, our bouyon playlist gathers the tracks to know, from the founders to the new wave. This roster is not an archive: it is a living programme.

Frequently asked questions

Who created bouyon in Dominica?

Bouyon was created by WCK, Windward Caribbean Kulture, in 1988 in Roseau, the capital of Dominica. The band fused cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. That is the starting point of the whole genre and the first name of the Dominican roster presented on this page.

Why do Asa Bantan and Reo carry king and prince titles?

Asa Bantan is widely called the King of Bouyon, and Dominica Festivals has identified him as the Bouyon Boss. Reo is billed as the Prince of Bouyon by 13th Street Promotions. These are titles given by the scene, the public and promoters, not official titles, and this site always presents them as such, with their source.

Which Dominican artists play Night 1 of WCMF 2026?

Night 1 of the 2026 World Creole Music Festival, Friday October 23 in Roseau, features on the Dominica side Ridge, TK International (also known as Triple Kay Band), Colton T, Pudaz and the Midnight Groovers, according to the official communication and published guides. Festival details are on our WCMF Creole festival page.

Which bouyon artists are from Dominica?

The Dominican roster followed by this site runs from founders WCK and the cadence heritage of the Midnight Groovers to Asa Bantan, Reo and Ridge, the bands Signal Band and TK International, the voices of Shelly and Colton T, Pudaz, Gwada G, BONNA and Ras B, and producer Smokiller, born in Dominica. Each name has a full profile on the site.

Which bands founded bouyon in Dominica?

WCK is the reference band, formed in Roseau in 1988. This site's research places it in a founding generation alongside other bands from the genre's origins, such as Roots, Stems and Branches, known as RSB, and First Serenade. In the 1990s, their bouyon became the music of the Roseau carnival and of village parties. See WCK.

Which bouyon artists come from Grand Bay?

Grand Bay, in the south of Dominica, has given bouyon two major names on this roster: the Midnight Groovers, a cadence-lypso band formed in the late 1960s and led by Phillip “Chubby” Mark, and Asa Bantan, real name Asa Edwards, widely called the King of Bouyon. The root and the modern boss share the same village.

Is Gwada G from Dominica or Guadeloupe?

Gwada G is a Dominican artist, despite a name that evokes Guadeloupe. The official Discover Dominica Authority media release for WCMF 2026 lists him among Dominica's homegrown artists. He plays Night 3 of the festival, Sunday October 25, 2026. His case shows why this site checks every origin instead of guessing it from a stage name.

Which Dominican artists are nominated at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards?

Reo and Ridge, known as Mr Ridge, are both nominated in the Bouyon Artist of the Year category at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards, and Mr Ridge won it, according to the results published in September 2026. The track “La Vie Doux”, by Reo with Tehilah, is also nominated for Bouyon Song of the Year. These nominations carry the current generation of Dominican bouyon beyond the island. See Reo.

Who is Ridge, aka Mr Ridge?

Ridge, or Mr Ridge, is a Dominican bouyon artist, keyboardist and producer, known on Instagram as @crossdbridge. Winner of the bouyon artist of the year award at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards, he plays Night 1 of WCMF 2026 with a live band that includes Nikita Christmas on drums and musical direction. He is a reminder that bouyon also stands on its musicians. See Ridge.

Which live bands carry Dominican bouyon today?

Signal Band and TK International, also known as Triple Kay Band. Signal Band is the band behind two of Shelly's trending tracks, “Where Did You Come From” and “What YOU Say”, and plays Night 3 of WCMF 2026. TK International plays Night 1. In Dominica, bouyon remains band and stage music, inherited from cadence-lypso.

Who sings Bye to de Old Me (Remix)?

The remix of “Bye to de Old Me” is performed by Shelly, a singer from Dominica, with Nice and Pudaz. It stands at 691K views on the popular shelf of Smokiller's YouTube channel and appears in the ROAD MIX playlists. Shelly then followed it with two trending tracks with Signal Band. See Shelly.

Who are the new bouyon artists from Dominica?

BONNA, from La Plaine, signed to More Sewo and carried by “Not Real”, is the newest talent on this roster. Ras B works on the border of rap and bouyon with “Come Here”. Colton T represents the solo generation, on the bill for Night 1 of WCMF 2026. Three ways to hear where the source island's bouyon is heading.

Who produces the Bouyon Shattaz bouyon remixes?

The Bouyon Shattaz remixes are produced by Smokiller, Dominican-born artist and producer, founder of Bouyon Shattaz, born in Dominica. He flips tracks from other worlds into bouyon, from Coi Leray to Ayra Starr, and so imposes Dominica's rhythm on songs the world already knows. See Smokiller.

Why is Dominica the source island of bouyon?

Because the genre was born there: WCK created it in Roseau in 1988, and in the 1990s bouyon dominated the island's music scene, from the Mas Domnik carnival to village parties. Everything that carries the name bouyon, in Guadeloupe, Martinique, France or the diaspora, goes back to that Dominican invention. See the history of bouyon.

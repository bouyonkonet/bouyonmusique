Les Ptits du RZT: Tata Yéyé and Malpalé, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Les Ptits du RZT are a group whose name travels through the credits of recent bouyon and shatta, but for whom no verified public biography was found during the research for this profile. No confirmed official page, no interview located, no published list of members. What does exist, and can be checked record by record, comes down to three recordings and one party: a feature on LESTEF KJF BOYZ “Malpalé” in February 2024, a single of their own, “Tata Yéyé”, in June 2024, a co-credit on “Look My Style” in 2025, and a party under their name at L’Empire Club in Chilly-Mazarin in October 2026. This profile tells exactly that, no more and no less, and it states clearly where the public record stops.

What the public record holds, and what it does not

We start with honesty, because it shapes this whole profile. For many artists on this site, research turns up biographies, interviews, label statements and complete discographies. For Les Ptits du RZT, the research carried out for this page found none of those documents. No published text says who the members of the group are, where they come from, or when the group formed. No official account could be verified to the point of being linked here, so this page links to no social profile.

That gap is not a reason to stay silent about the group, and it is not a reason to invent either. The name Les Ptits du RZT appears in official streaming platform credits, alongside artists whose paths are documented. A verified feature is worth more than a fabricated biography. This profile therefore follows one simple rule: every factual sentence below points to a platform credit or an event page that any reader can open and check.

Even the spelling of the name varies between sources, and that detail says something about how lightly documented the group is. Platforms write “Les p’tits du RZT”, “Les p’tits Rzt” or, in masked form, “Les p’t**s Rzt”. Their party poster writes “Les P’Tits Du Rzt”. This site uses the form “Les Ptits du RZT”, the one in this page slug, and it flags the variants instead of pretending that a single official spelling has been confirmed by the group.

First verified credit: “Malpalé”, with LESTEF KJF BOYZ, in 2024

The group first verified discographic trace is a feature. On February 6, 2024, LESTEF KJF BOYZ released the single “Malpalé”, billed “feat. Les p’tits du RZT”, on the label 4360476 Records DK. The track runs 3 minutes and 4 seconds. Writing and composition are credited to Stephan Cornano, the central writer of the LESTEF KJF BOYZ catalogue. The feature therefore places Les Ptits du RZT, from their first checkable appearance, inside the network of a Guadeloupe group active since 2021, whose “KJF” series and collaborations with DJ Luchshiy are documented on this site.

What does a feature tell us, when we have nothing else? At minimum, two things. First, that the group already existed as a recording act at the start of 2024, with a name established enough to be billed as a feature on another group release. Second, that its ground is the ground of that scene: the bouyon and shatta of the French Antilles, sung in French and Creole, released through the independent, DistroKid type circuits that the platform label credits point to. Everything else, background, town, age, would be speculation, and this profile does not speculate.

“Malpalé” remains, to date, the only track where the group name appears as a feature on another artist release in the catalogues consulted. That is a small amount of evidence, but it is dated, credited and verifiable, which separates it from social media mentions that no stable source confirms.

“Tata Yéyé”: the single under their own name

Four months later, the group released under its own name. “Tata Yéyé” came out on June 21, 2024, as a single, on the label 7156982 Records DK, with the language listed as French on the platform record. The writing credit pairs Les p’tits Rzt with 1t1, an artist whose path in bouyon gwada is documented on this site. That co-writing credit is, after the “Malpalé” feature, the group second verified anchor point: it connects them to an established figure of the scene, not as a guest this time, but as co-writer of their own title.

The June 21 date, the day of the Fête de la musique in France, can be noted as a calendar fact, without drawing any conclusion about the group intention, which no source documents. Likewise, the platform record for the track provides no presentation text, no biography and no usable official link. The single exists, its date exists, its writers exist: that is the level of certainty this profile claims, and it holds to it.

With “Malpalé” and “Tata Yéyé”, the year 2024 holds most of the group public trace: two tracks, two different configurations, one feature received and one single carried. For an emerging group on this scene, that pattern is common: collaborations open doors before the own catalogue grows. But the frequency of a pattern does not turn a reading into a fact, and this profile presents that reading as a reading, not as a biography.

2025: “Look My Style” and the return to the LESTEF catalogue

The third verified trace arrives on June 18, 2025. On that day, LESTEF KJF BOYZ released the EP “Riddim Aleatoire”, whose track “Look My Style”, featuring Zepek, is co-credited to LESTEF KJF BOYZ and Les Ptits du RZT on the platform record for the song. The group therefore reappears where it first appeared: in the LESTEF catalogue, a year and a half after “Malpalé”. The relationship between the two groups is the strongest thread in the file: two collaborations spaced apart, working in both directions of visibility, a feature on one side and a co-credit on the other.

Here again, precision matters. The EP record on some platforms credits only LESTEF KJF BOYZ, while the record for the track “Look My Style” names both groups. That kind of gap between album metadata and track metadata is common in independent distribution. This profile keeps the track record, the more precise one, and it flags the gap instead of smoothing it over. That is also why “Look My Style” appears in this page list of recordings: it is verified at track level, by the platform itself.

After June 2025, the catalogues consulted show no new track under the group name at the time of research. That absence is dated and revisable: it describes the state of the sources in October 2026, not the end of a career.

The stage: a Birthday party at L’Empire Club in Chilly-Mazarin

If the group catalogue is thin, its stage presence left a complete, dated trace. On Saturday, October 10, 2026, the Bizouk ticketing platform listed “Les P’Tits Du Rzt Birthday” at L’Empire Club, 3 route de Longjumeau, in Chilly-Mazarin, in Essonne, from 11:30 p.m. to 6 a.m. The format is that of the big Afro-Caribbean parties of the Paris region: two rooms, two atmospheres, an announced dress code, black and gold, and a showcase line-up built around the celebrated name.

The party billing names Miimii KDS, Pern, Lucky, Dinero and MK in show, plus surprise artists, and at the decks Thomm, Djabkilla, Zerfly, Lil Killa, DJ Softee, Juiicy, Will and Junior. Two of those names, Miimii KDS and DJ Softee, have their profile on this site, which places the party inside the bouyon and shatta circuit this site documents week after week. The club itself keeps the party in its pages of past party photos, which confirms the event did take place on the announced date.

What this party establishes, factually, is limited but real: in October 2026, the name Les Ptits du RZT carried a full party in a Paris region club, surrounded by artists and DJs of the scene. A name that fills that role is not unknown to its audience, even if search engines barely know it. That is the whole gap this profile documents: an attested stage existence, and an almost empty editorial existence.

To place this scene in the wider landscape of the genre, our pages on bouyon in Paris and the bouyon playlist give the context of the parties and tracks that carry these names between the Antilles and the Paris region.

Why this profile exists, and how it will be completed

A site that documents bouyon has two ways to handle a group like Les Ptits du RZT. The first would be to publish nothing until a complete biography exists: the group would then stay invisible, including for readers who search its name after a party or a feature. The second, chosen here, is to publish exactly what is verified, declaring the limits of the file in the text itself. Between invention and silence, this site chooses the dated, sourced trace.

Concretely, here is what this profile does not yet know and does not pretend to know: the members names, their number, their island or town of origin, their formation date, the existence of a label or management, and the address of an official account. None of that information was confirmed by an openable source during the research, so none of it is printed here. The meaning of the initials RZT, in particular, is explained by no source found: this profile offers no expansion of the initials.

If the group, its circle or its labels publish those elements, or if new tracks appear, this profile will be completed and corrected, with an updated modification date, like every page on this site. In the meantime, listening starts with the three verified tracks: “Malpalé”, “Tata Yéyé” and “Look My Style”, then with the context of the scene on our bouyon gwada page.

Who are Les Ptits du RZT?

Which Les Ptits du RZT tracks can be verified?

Three recordings are verified at platform record level: “Malpalé”, by LESTEF KJF BOYZ featuring Les Ptits du RZT, released on February 6, 2024; “Tata Yéyé”, their single of June 21, 2024; and “Look My Style”, co-credited to both groups on the “Riddim Aleatoire” EP of June 18, 2025.

What does RZT mean in Les Ptits du RZT?

No source found during the research explains the initials RZT, and this profile offers no invented expansion.

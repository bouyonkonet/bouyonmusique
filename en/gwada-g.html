Gwada G: Dominican, despite the name

Published September 25, 2026

Gwada G is a Dominican bouyon artist, and that is the first fact to set down clearly. Despite a name that suggests Guadeloupe, he is not Guadeloupean: the official Discover Dominica Authority media release for WCMF 2026 counts him among Dominica's homegrown acts. Love Bouyon also billed him in its Bouyon Talks as a new generation Dominica bouyon artist set for WCMF 2026. His Instagram is @gwada_g. Per published festival guides, he plays Night 3 on Sunday, October 25, 2026, alongside Shelly and Signal Band.

The name misunderstanding, and why it must be corrected

The Gwada G case is a standing lesson for Bouyon Shattaz: names travel, but credit must stay accurate. A stage name can suggest another island, a style, a nickname or a personal story without defining an artist's nationality. When a site, a poster or a media outlet takes that name and deduces a Guadeloupean origin, the error then repeats everywhere, in search engines and festival introductions alike.

The source that settles it here is official. The media release from the Discover Dominica Authority, the body behind the WCMF, lists Gwada G among Dominica's homegrown acts. Homegrown means from home, formed on the ground, presented as a talent of the island. Against a source at that level, a deduction drawn from a stage name carries no weight. This page therefore follows the official source and calls Gwada G Dominican, full stop.

This is not a vocabulary detail. The origin thesis Bouyon Shattaz defends is that Dominica created bouyon and must receive its credit, bookings and revenue. If even Dominican artists get relabelled toward neighbouring islands because of their names, the leak of credit becomes automatic. Correcting the record on Gwada G, calmly and with the source, is exactly the work this site exists to do.

An artist of Dominica's new generation

Love Bouyon, in its Bouyon Talks series, billed Gwada G as a new generation Dominica bouyon artist on his way to WCMF 2026. A full interview was billed for September 30, 2026 on the Love Bouyon Music YouTube channel. That billing matters because it comes from a platform specialised in bouyon, speaking to the same scene and choosing its guests among the names in motion.

New generation does not mean without roots. In Dominica, young bouyon artists grow up with the island's classics, carnival seasons, sound systems and tracks that circulate from phone to phone before they ever see a clean release. Being spotted by Bouyon Talks and held in the festival's official communication places Gwada G in the group of young names that the scene's institutions and media are already watching closely.

Our dossier holds no detailed discography to list for Gwada G, and this page will not invent one. No tracks attributed without a source, no assumed listening figures, no collaborations added to fill space. His documented profile rests on three verifiable pillars: the official DDA source, the Love Bouyon billing and his published WCMF 2026 booking. That is small in quantity but solid, and it is enough to present him correctly.

WCMF 2026: Night 3 with Shelly and Signal Band

Per published World Creole Music Festival 2026 guides, Gwada G plays Night 3 on Sunday, October 25, 2026, alongside Shelly and Signal Band. The third night closes the festival at Windsor Park in Roseau. It gathers the crowd that stays to the end of the Creole weekend, often the most loyal one, the crowd that comes for Dominica's own music as much as for visiting headliners.

Sharing the night with Shelly places him beside a Dominican artist who is central to the recent playlists and collaborations followed by Bouyon Shattaz, including the remix cited on the Pudaz page. Signal Band, for its part, represents the island's band tradition. That combination, a young bouyon artist, an established voice and a band, sums up how the WCMF builds its nights: passing music down, mixing generations and keeping Dominica at the centre of its own festival.

To prepare for the night, read our WCMF, the Creole festival page, then listen to the bouyon playlist. And if you keep only one fact from this profile, keep this one: when the bill says Gwada G, it is talking about an artist from Dominica. The island's full artist picture is on artists from Dominica.

Instagram, a verified contact route and the Rights Clinic

Gwada G's Instagram is @gwada_g. It is stated in artists' video descriptions and in the podcast billings that concern him. On this site, a handle is published only when it appears in that kind of direct source, not because an account with a similar name exists. That rule avoids sending the public and professionals to the wrong profile, a frequent error when several accounts look alike.

Gwada G is in the twenty artist audit of the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic, with a verified contact route in our records. That makes the concrete discussions possible, checking credits, seeking permissions, proposing distribution or collaboration, without random messaging.

The Rights Clinic does not promise to turn an artist into a signing. It puts order into what already exists: who owns what, who must be credited, who can authorise a use. For a new generation artist announced on his island's biggest stage, getting that order early can prevent the errors that cost dearly later, when a track gains value and everyone suddenly goes looking for the paperwork.

What his path says about bouyon today

Gwada G shows how Dominican bouyon works in 2026. A specialised platform like Love Bouyon spots and interviews, the festival's official authority classifies and books, guides publish the nights, and the public often discovers the name before there is a full discography to hear. In that system, the quality of information becomes almost as important as the music itself: a wrong origin label can follow an artist for years.

He also shows the permanent closeness between Dominica and Guadeloupe in this music. The two islands share a Creole language, audiences, producers and stages, to the point where a Dominican name can sound Guadeloupean and the reverse. That closeness is a strength for bouyon's circulation, as long as it does not erase origins. Bouyon was born in Dominica: saying so for each artist, with the right source, maintains the memory that lets the island benefit from its own creation.

What comes next for Gwada G will need documenting like the beginning: through tracks when they are published with their credits, stages when organisers announce them and interviews when they appear. Until then, this profile gives the reader exactly what is established, no more and no less, plus a clear way in: WCMF 2026 Night 3, the @gwada_g account and the playlists where the new generation of Dominican bouyon can be discovered in music.

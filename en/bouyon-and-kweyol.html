Bouyon and kwéyòl: the genre speaks Creole

A language of celebration and commentary

Since cadence-lypso, Dominican music has told the story of island life in its own language: the politics, the village stories, the carnival rivalries. Bouyon has kept that function, with a faster flow and a style close to the shout and the toast.

Creole as a passport

Dominican kwéyòl is close to the Creoles of Guadeloupe, Martinique and Saint Lucia. It is this linguistic passport that allowed bouyon to cross the sea and become the jump up of the French Antilles.

Frequently asked questions

What language is bouyon sung in?

Bouyon is sung first of all in kwéyòl, the Creole language of Dominica, mixed with English. The language is not a decoration: it carries the calls, the jokes, the social commentary and the energy of chanté mas. Like the language of Dominica's carnival, bouyon lyrics move constantly between kwéyòl and English.

Why is it called bouyon?

Bouyon means soup in kwéyòl: many ingredients go in, everything mixes and nothing is lost. The genre, launched by the band WCK in Roseau in 1988, stirs Dominica's traditional music, jing ping and cadence-lypso, together with keyboards and fast electronic rhythms.

Is bouyon part of Creole culture?

Yes. Bouyon was born in Dominica's village fetes and carnival, and it is sung in kwéyòl, the island's Creole language. It inherits from cadence-lypso, which already told the story of island life in its own language, and today it soundtracks Creole celebrations across the French Caribbean.

Why did kwéyòl help bouyon in Guadeloupe and Martinique?

Because Dominican kwéyòl is close to the Creoles of Guadeloupe, Martinique and Saint Lucia. That closeness worked as a linguistic passport for bouyon: it allowed the genre to cross the sea and become the jump up of the French Antilles. See also Gwada from Dominica.

What do bouyon lyrics in kwéyòl talk about?

Bouyon lyrics tell the story of island life, as cadence-lypso did before them: the politics, the village stories, the carnival rivalries. Bouyon has kept that commentary role, with a faster flow and a style close to the shout and the toast, carried by calls and jokes delivered in kwéyòl.

What does "Mas Domnik" mean?

Mas Domnik is the Creole name of Dominica's carnival, in Roseau. It keeps its old forms: chanté mas, lapo kabwit drum groups, traditional characters and sensays. Since the 1990s, bouyon has been its music: the year's tracks play in the bands, the sound systems and the fetes. See Mas Domnik.

What is chanté mas?

Chanté mas is the tradition of Dominica's carnival songs, built on double meanings and collective response. Bouyon inherited from it its direct, teasing and often bawdy language, its calls thrown to the crowd and the energy it carries in kwéyòl. Chanté mas percussion strikes are also among the sounds bouyon producers still sample.

Why does bouyon mix kwéyòl and English?

Because Dominica is an island that speaks both English and Creole. Bouyon moves from one language to the other like the language of the island's carnival, without losing the thread. For a French Caribbean listener, the shared Creole is a familiar way in, while revealing an island where English is at home too.

Is Dominican kwéyòl the same Creole as in Guadeloupe?

It is close to it. Dominican kwéyòl is close to the Creoles of Guadeloupe, Martinique and Saint Lucia, and Dominica, Guadeloupe and Martinique also share neighboring carnival traditions: chanté mas, street groups, Creole dances. That kinship is what has kept bouyon moving between the three islands since the 1990s.

Is bouyon sung in French?

Bouyon from Dominica is sung in kwéyòl and English. In Guadeloupe, bouyon gwada pushes vocals sung or rapped in Guadeloupean Creole, and part of French Caribbean production also comes out in French, like the tracks of DJ Skaytah, released in French and Creole. See bouyon gwada.

What does "Tout Biten Domnik" mean?

Tout Biten Domnik, which this site renders as "everything is Dominican", is the theme of the fifth edition of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl, the Creole village of Independence, held from October 19 to 22, 2026 at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau. Bouyon shares the bill there with quadrille, cultural groups and tradition showcases.

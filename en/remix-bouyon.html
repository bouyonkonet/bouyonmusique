Remix bouyon: the hit-converting machine

By Smokiller · Published October 4, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

The bouyon remix takes a track everyone knows, an Afro, pop, rap or R&B hit, and replays it with the codes of bouyon: raised tempo, keyboard loop, Dominican percussion and carnival energy.

On this page

  2. Smokiller, the in-house reference
  3. Frequently asked questions

The method

The remixer keeps the voice and the emotion of the original, but rebuilds the whole ground underneath: the rhythmic grid comes from jing ping and lapo kabwit, the bass becomes synthetic and fast, the build-ups imitate a carnival band. The audience arrives for the song, it discovers a genre.

Smokiller, the in-house reference

It is the specialty of Smokiller, the Bouyon Shattaz remix project: its bouyon versions of Afro and pop tracks draw a new audience toward bouyon, then toward the original artists of Dominica. A deliberate gateway strategy to grow the genre.

Frequently asked questions

What is a bouyon remix?

A bouyon remix takes a track everyone knows, an Afro, pop, rap or R&B hit, and replays it with the codes of bouyon: raised tempo, keyboard loop, Dominican percussion and carnival energy. The remixer keeps the voice and emotion of the original, but rebuilds the whole rhythmic ground from jing ping and lapo kabwit.

Which bouyon remixes has Smokiller made?

Smokiller has flipped Coi Leray’s Nothing Into Something and Players, Shenseea’s Vulnerability and Ayra Starr’s Heaven Baby into bouyon. His Nothing Into Something flip, published September 20, 2026, reached 854.7K views on TikTok per his own screenshots of October 19, 2026, and Coi Leray reshared it.

Why make bouyon remixes?

Because the remix is a gateway: the audience arrives for a song it already knows and discovers a genre. Smokiller’s bouyon versions of Afro and pop tracks draw a new audience toward bouyon, then toward the original artists of Dominica. It is a deliberate strategy to grow the genre.

Who produces bouyon today?

Producers like Smokiller (Bouyon Shattaz), Denmath, HYPNO! and Savion work the bouyon sound across riddims, remixes and original productions. Remixes are Smokiller's specialty, while Denmath releases his own riddims, Tropical and Harley, and HYPNO made his name with Mauvais Plan, built at 160 BPM and shared as a producer reel. See the bouyon producers.

What is a bouyon riddim?

A riddim is the original instrumental several artists record over, like Denmath’s Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim. It is the reverse of a remix: the riddim starts from a new base waiting for its singers, while the remix starts from an existing song and rebuilds its rhythm in bouyon. Both now launch as clips that producers dance to on social media.

How do you make a good bouyon remix?

A successful bouyon remix is not just about speeding a track up. If the audience recognises the original hit but dances to another machine, the flip has worked: the keyboards carry the melody in the language of bouyon, the tempo installs the fete without breaking the track, and the energy stays direct, made for call and response. It is arrangement work, not a filter. See Smokiller.

Is the bouyon remix linked to Wrecketeng?

Yes. Wrecketeng, Dominica's sampled-melody, rap-vocal style, is one of the bouyon branches born from the spoken delivery of the origins, and Smokiller's remix method comes straight from it: the sample as material, the voice as engine. Wrecketeng is the official spelling according to DJ Cut, the founding pioneer; “reketeng” is a widespread internet misspelling. See Wrecketeng.

Can a bouyon remix add new singers?

Yes, in Dominican bouyon the remix also serves to widen a hit. “Bye to de Old Me”, a Signal Band record with Shelly, was taken up in a remix by Shelly, Nice and Pudaz, presented in November 2025 as cut for Carnival 2026. Trilla-G's album likewise holds “Take Me As I Am (feat. V'ghn) [Remix]”. See Nice.

Where can I download bouyon remixes for free?

On smokiller.com, Smokiller's link hub: it offers as free downloads the remix of Coi Leray's Nothing Into Something in WAV, the Bouyon Shattaz remix of Shenseea's Vulnerability in WAV and that of Ayra Starr's Heaven Baby in M4A. The hub adds instrumentals and remix videos, including Bryson Tiller's Outside and the RnBouyon. See RnBouyon.

Which other producers make bouyon remixes?

Fr3EzE, a producer from Dominica, makes them part of his catalogue: “To The Moon Bouyon Remix”, a Fr3EzE Bouyon Remix version of Yung Miami's “Spend Dat”, and a Mariah Carey “All I Want For Christmas is You” turned into bouyon. DJ Taffy also counts “Lighter, Bouyon Remix” among his tracks listed by Viberate. See Fr3EzE.

T.C: the co-writing initials of We Trippy, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

T.C is a bouyon artist who exists, in the public archives consulted, on one record only: “We Trippy”, where he is one of the three headline artists and one of the three writers, alongside Dr Eevil and Sticky, over a production by Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze, recorded at Riddim Box Studio and finished by Yw3 Ent. No source consulted expands his initials, gives his origin, his biography or an official account, and this profile guesses none of it. With no place verifiable, it is the circuit of the record itself, the trio, the studio and the finishing house, that anchors this profile. It publishes the complete credit roll, what it establishes, and everything it does not allow to be said.

Who T.C is: initials in the headline billing, and nothing verified behind them

Let us start with the exact inventory, because the whole file fits inside it. T.C is credited on one record only opened during this research: “We Trippy”, published on YouTube under the heading “DR Eevil, T.C AND Sticky - We Trippy (Bouyon)”. His name appears there twice, in two posts. In the heading, he is the second of the three headline artists, between Dr Eevil and Sticky, joined at the same level by an “AND” in capitals, with no feature mention: T.C is therefore a lead artist of the record, not a guest. In the description, the writing line credits him among the songwriters: “written by: Dr Eevil ,sticky and T.C”. No other primary page under this name, tied to this credit roll, was opened or verified.

The name itself is the most fragile point of the file, and this profile treats it as such. T.C appears with its dots in both credits, the heading and the writing line, and no source consulted expands those initials into a full name. This site therefore proposes no expansion: no first name, no family name and no expanded nickname is published here, because none is published by the sources. Initials are also the weakest of all search keys: across every platform they return dozens of artists, producers and records with no connection at all, and none of those results could be tied to the “We Trippy” credit roll by a verifiable element, a shared collaborator, studio or producer. Every namesake is therefore kept entirely out of this profile.

What the file does not contain must be stated from the outset, with the same clarity as what it contains. No biography, no interview, no official photograph, no social media account and no personal origin for T.C was found or verified. The “We Trippy” page does publish a contacts block, but it is headed “Connect with dreevil” and gives Dr Eevil details only: it documents nothing for T.C, not even a handle. This profile is therefore a credit roll profile, in the strict sense: it fixes the verified presence of a name on one record, in two posts, performer and writer, with the whole manufacturing chain around it, and it stops exactly where the sources stop.

We Trippy: the only verified record of T.C

The only documented appearance of T.C is also a complete record, and that is what makes this profile publishable despite its thinness. “We Trippy (Bouyon)” brings together three headline artists, Dr Eevil, T.C and Sticky, in the order of the heading. The (Bouyon) mention, at the end of the heading, is the genre claim of the record, published by the page itself: it is what connects T.C to this site. No release date is given on the opened page, and this profile puts forward none. The record is documented by its credit roll, not by a calendar, and any date circulating elsewhere without a source should be read with the caution that silence imposes.

That credit roll gives T.C the strongest post a credit roll artist can hold: songwriting. The line “written by: Dr Eevil ,sticky and T.C” makes the three performers the three writers of the record, in unspecified shares but at the same level of credit. Around them, the chain is named in full: musical production by Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze, executive production by Riddim Box Studio, recording at Riddim Box Studio, mixing and mastering by Yw3 Ent. T.C is therefore not a passing voice on the record of another artist: he is co-writer and co-performer of a record made by a complete team, and his name is inscribed in the two places where records keep their memory, the billing and the writing credit.

What the page does not say must be stated as clearly. It specifies neither the place of T.C in the record, nor the nature of his contribution, chorus, verse or bridge, nor even the language of his performance, and no listening documented by a source describes it. This profile therefore does not describe his performance: it establishes his presence, his double credit and the full frame of the record, which are the facts the platform publishes. For an artist with no biography, that frame is already career information: it says who he writes with, who he sings with, where he records and in which genre he appears, bouyon, through the door of a workshop trio. Co-writer without a biography: what the credit roll establishes, and what it keeps silent

A credit roll read carefully says more than it seems to, provided it is not made to say more. That of “We Trippy” first establishes a fact of collaboration: T.C writes and sings with Dr Eevil and Sticky, two artists whose profiles, published in the same wave of this site, rest on the same page. It then establishes a fact of workshop: the record passed through Riddim Box Studio for executive production and recording, through Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze for production, and through Yw3 Ent for finishing. An artist with only one record can thus be placed inside a precise working network, with names, posts and an order of manufacture, without a single sentence of biography having been invented to connect him to that network.

The same credit roll, on the other hand, keeps silent about everything that would make a portrait. It does not say who T.C is outside this record: neither whether he sang before, nor whether he sings elsewhere under another name, nor whether the initials are his usual stage name or a one off signature. It does not say either who, of the three writers, wrote which part, or who sings which part: the collective credits of small scenes credit the team, not the individual contributions, and turning a collective credit into individual roles would be an invention. This profile therefore leaves the three names level on both posts, exactly as the page puts them there, and flags that unresolved sharing as a fact of the file, not as a gap to fill with guesses.

The method of this site, on thin files, is to separate the levels of proof rather than mixing them. Here, everything rests on one primary page, opened in full: the heading and the description of the YouTube video of “We Trippy”. That is enough for the structured data, which keeps that record only, and it is the limit of everything else: searches by initials, aggregators and result listings brought no tieable element, and they are cited nowhere as sources. One name can carry facts of very different levels depending on the artist: that of T.C carries only one level, the strongest, an opened page, and this profile prefers a file of one certain record to a discography of probable records.

The Riddim Box circuit: the anchor of the file, for want of a published origin

For want of a published origin for T.C, this profile is anchored by the circuit of his only record, and that anchor deserves to be described for itself. Riddim Box Studio holds two posts in it: executive production, that is the organisation of the session, and the recording. The word riddim, at the heart of its name, is that of the instrumental loops over which the voices of bouyon are laid, which our page on the bouyon riddim presents. Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze sign the musical production of the record, and Yw3 Ent its mixing and mastering. The anchor of T.C is therefore not a supposed birthplace: it is a named manufacturing chain, post by post, in the very description of the record.

The name of Yw3 Ent gives that chain a depth verifiable on this site. The same finishing name appears, in the form Yw3 Ent (DiYoute), in the recording, mixing and mastering posts of “Come Here”, the record by Ras B with Skrazy and Dboss, and in the form Yw3 Studio on the mixing and mastering of “Tropikini”, by Real Reel. “We Trippy” was therefore finished by a workshop that works for other artists of the same bouyon basin, which places the record, and the record only, inside a documented studio circuit. That cross check says nothing about the origin of T.C: it places his recording, not his person, and this profile keeps that distinction in every sentence where the circuit is mentioned.

The context of the genre recalls why such a circuit matters to this site. Bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s. Bouyon soca was started in Dominica in 2003 by Dada, according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer and a participant in that era. A generation later, the genre also lives in these workshop sessions where three voices write a record, lay it down at a studio that produces and records, and have it finished by a mixing and mastering house, before a direct publication on YouTube. T.C is, to date, a voice of one of these sessions, named in the billing and in the writing credit: that is little, and it is exactly the kind of trace a profile by name can fix before it gets lost inside the credit rolls.

What the file does not say: the limits, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they form half of its information. The initials T.C are expanded by no source consulted: any expansion read elsewhere should be held as unverified until a primary page publishes it. No other record by T.C, as lead artist, in a duo or as a feature, was verified during this research. No origin, no biography, no interview, no official photograph and no account confirmed by direct opening was found, so the structured data of this page links to no external account. Even the date of his only record is not published on the opened page.

The verified scope fits into one record and one double credit. “We Trippy”, opened in full on YouTube: T.C is the second name in its heading, among the three headline artists, and the third name in its writing line, among the three writers. Production by Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze, executive production and recording by Riddim Box Studio, mixing and mastering by Yw3 Ent. Nothing else is stated in this profile, and nothing else should be lent to it: no described voice, no detailed role, no path. Anything circulating elsewhere under the initials T.C with no demonstrable link to this precise credit roll, same collaborators, same studio, same producers, should be read as a namesake until proof to the contrary.

The listening, while waiting for what comes next, fits into one record: “We Trippy”, to be heard as the entire document of the file, the trio of Dr Eevil, T.C and Sticky inside the frame of Riddim Box Studio and Yw3 Ent. The profiles of his two partners, Dr Eevil and Sticky, published in the same wave, give the rest of the credit roll, each in his post. If T.C one day publishes a record under an expanded name, an artist page, a biography or official accounts, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site. In the meantime, it publishes what the credit roll gives, no more and no less: initials in the headline billing and in the writing credit of a bouyon record, and the complete team that made it.

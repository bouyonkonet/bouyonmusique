Bouyon BPM: speed as a signature

By Smokiller · Published September 13, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Bouyon is a fast genre, and its tempo is part of its identity. When producer HYPNO claims a track at 160 bpm, Mauvais Plan, he shows his colors: bouyon does not seek comfort, it seeks movement.

On this page
  1. Why so fast
  2. Tempo as an argument
  3. Frequently asked questions

Why so fast

The tempo of bouyon comes from its roots: traditional Dominican dances, carnival and competition between bands push toward maximum energy. The keyboard loops and fast percussion create a continuous tension that leaves the body no choice.

Tempo as an argument

In today’s producer scene, announcing your BPM has become an argument: it is a promise of dance and a challenge to other producers. But speed alone does not make a bouyon: without the percussion and the spirit of Dominica, it is just a fast beat.

Frequently asked questions

What is the BPM of bouyon?

Bouyon is a fast genre, and its tempo is part of its identity. There is no single number, but the examples documented on this site sit around 160 BPM: producer HYPNO built Mauvais Plan at 160 BPM, and TiiTii NBA’s New Bouyon Age EP is measured around 160 BPM.

Why is bouyon so fast?

Its tempo comes from its roots: traditional Dominican dances, carnival and competition between bands push toward maximum energy. Keyboard loops and fast percussion create a continuous tension. But speed alone does not make a bouyon: without the percussion and the spirit of Dominica, it is just a fast beat.

Who produces bouyon today?

Producers like Smokiller (Bouyon Shattaz), Denmath, HYPNO! and Savion work the bouyon sound across riddims, remixes and original productions. On tempo, HYPNO shows 160 BPM right in his Mauvais Plan producer reel, and DJ Leguste, linked to Guadeloupe, releases singles nearly all at 160 BPM. See the bouyon producers.

What is a bouyon riddim?

A riddim is the instrumental several artists record over, at the heart of bouyon and dancehall production. It sets the tempo for everyone who takes it up: J2MO's “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim” brings Mad General, MIKTY and Miky Ding La together on the same rhythm at 160 BPM. Riddims often carry a name, like Denmath’s Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim.

Is bouyon always 160 BPM?

No, but nearly. Most records documented on this site are listed between 157 and 160 BPM on their platform pages. There are clear exceptions: “KPT”, tagged bouyon warm up 2025, shows 127 BPM, as does DJ Skaytah's “Mass (Welcome To Guadeloupe)”, and the Extended version of Dcamp and Lewismelo's “BAY LA BÈT” drops to 134 BPM. See DJ Softee.

Is bouyon from Dominica also at 160 BPM?

Yes, the documented Dominican records sit in the same zone. DJ MJ's “Work Dat” (2025) and “You Can Do What You Want” with Gwada G (2026) are both listed at 160 BPM, as are Faithii's three documented records. “Bwé Rhum”, by DJ Joe with Asa Bantan, shows 157 BPM. See DJ MJ.

Was bouyon already at 160 BPM in the 2010s?

Yes, according to the published listings of records from that period. Gaza Girls Crew's “Sa Zot Vlé”, released on May 1, 2011 on Bouyon Concept, shows 160 BPM, as does “Sa Ka Débodé” by Bilix and Mr Boka, released on January 12, 2016 on Ôréveil. J2MO's “Si kontwol”, in 2015, runs at 157 BPM. See Gaza Girls Crew.

Why are some bouyon tracks labelled reggae on streaming platforms?

Because platforms identify bouyon poorly. On Shazam, Faithii's three documented records are filed under reggae, even though they run at 160 BPM and live in DJs' bouyon mixes. “Chocolate”, by Shanika and Le Bluff, is classed as afro-beat at 160 BPM. The tempo and the mixes that play a record say more about its genre than the label does. See Faithii.

What is the BPM of bouyon gwada?

Around 160 BPM, the cruising tempo of modern French Antilles bouyon. Nearly all of DJ Leguste's catalogue, from “LE GARS CHAUD” to “I Don't Know” with Cocotteland, is listed at 160 BPM, with a single clear exception, “Pa di mwen JTM STP”, at 157 BPM. “Chocolate”, a duet by Shanika and Le Bluff, voices of Guadeloupe bouyon, is also listed at 160 BPM. See DJ Leguste.

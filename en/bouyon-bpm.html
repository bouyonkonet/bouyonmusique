Bouyon BPM: speed as a signature

By Smokiller · Published September 13, 2026 · Updated October 20, 2026

Bouyon is a fast genre, and its tempo is part of its identity. When producer HYPNO claims a track at 160 bpm, Mauvais Plan, he shows his colors: bouyon does not seek comfort, it seeks movement.

Why so fast

The tempo of bouyon comes from its roots: traditional Dominican dances, carnival and competition between bands push toward maximum energy. The keyboard loops and fast percussion create a continuous tension that leaves the body no choice.

Tempo as an argument

In today’s producer scene, announcing your BPM has become an argument: it is a promise of dance and a challenge to other producers. But speed alone does not make a bouyon: without the percussion and the spirit of Dominica, it is just a fast beat.

Frequently asked questions

What is the BPM of bouyon?

Bouyon is a fast genre, and its tempo is part of its identity. There is no single number, but the examples documented on this site sit around 160 BPM: producer HYPNO built Mauvais Plan at 160 BPM, and TiiTii NBA’s New Bouyon Age EP is measured around 160 BPM.

Why is bouyon so fast?

Its tempo comes from its roots: traditional Dominican dances, carnival and competition between bands push toward maximum energy. Keyboard loops and fast percussion create a continuous tension. But speed alone does not make a bouyon: without the percussion and the spirit of Dominica, it is just a fast beat.

Who produces bouyon today?

Producers like Smokiller (Bouyon Shattaz), Denmath, HYPNO! and Savion work the bouyon sound across riddims, remixes and original productions. Denmath, for example, is behind Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim, and HYPNO’s reference track is Mauvais Plan, built at 160 BPM and shared as a producer reel.

What is a bouyon riddim?

A riddim is the instrumental several artists record over. Riddims sit at the heart of bouyon and dancehall production. They often carry a name, like Denmath’s Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim, and the clips producers dance to on social media have become the first step in launching a bouyon track.

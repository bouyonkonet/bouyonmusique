The E.O. LeBlanc Trophy: the award that guards Dominica culture

By Smokiller · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026

Every year, at the heart of the independence celebrations, one trophy honours the people who keep Dominican culture alive. The E.O. LeBlanc Trophy carries the name of Edward Oliver LeBlanc, the island first Premier, per DOM767.

The name of a founder of national culture

The trophy is named after Edward Oliver LeBlanc, the first Premier of Dominica, who played a pivotal role in promoting the island cultural heritage, per the DOM767 Dompedia entry. The award is presented annually during the National Cultural Gala to individuals or groups contributing to the country artistic development.

LeBlanc himself initiated the National Cultural Gala in 1965, to highlight and preserve the island intangible cultural heritage, per DOM767.

A gala that crowns competitions from across the country

The gala forms part of the independence celebrations and culminates in performances by winners from cultural competitions held across the country, in traditional dance, song and music, per DOM767.

The 2026 edition of the gala was held on Sunday November 1 at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau, at 3 pm, with traditional dances such as bele, quadrille and mazouk on the programme. At the time this page is written, no press source opened for it names the 2026 trophy recipient: this page will be updated when the name is published.

A recipient that shows the way: Possie Pan

Among the recipients cited by DOM767 is the Possie Pan Cultural Group from Portsmouth, known for its steel band, traditional dance and jing ping ensemble. Jing ping is Dominica traditional music of accordion and lapo kabwit drum, one of the direct roots of bouyon.

The trophy remains a symbol of cultural pride: it recognises those actively preserving Dominica traditional art forms, from dance to jing ping, per DOM767.

Why this trophy matters for bouyon

Bouyon was born in the late 1980s from the mix of jing ping, cadence-lypso and traditional dances such as bele and chante mas. The gala and its trophy keep those raw materials alive: without jing ping ensembles and traditional dance groups, bouyon would lose its source.

The E.O. LeBlanc memory also runs through the independence season with the E.O. LeBlanc Memorial Lecture, listed on the official celebrations calendar.

Mas Domnik: carnival under its real name

Published September 24, 2026

Mas Domnik is Dominica’s carnival said in Creole. Two words that set the tone: here, carnival has remained a matter of traditions, the street and bouyon music.

A carnival in kwéyòl

The name Mas Domnik is a reminder that Dominica is a Creole island above all. The language of carnival, like that of bouyon, blends kwéyòl and English. The singers’ calls, the refrains taken up by the crowd and the names of the bands tell the island’s story in its own language.

Bouyon, the official music of the Mas

Since the 1990s, bouyon has been the music of Mas Domnik: the year’s tracks are played in the bands, the sound systems and the fetes that precede the two big days. That is where artists’ reputations are made.

Frequently asked questions

What is the big bouyon festival?

The World Creole Music Festival, or WCMF, held every year in October in Roseau, Dominica. The 2026 edition runs October 23 to 25 at Windsor Park. It should not be confused with Mas Domnik: the festival takes place during the independence celebrations, while carnival is lived before Lent. See the World Creole Music Festival.

When is Dominica carnival?

Dominica carnival, Mas Domnik, takes place every year in Roseau before Lent, with its peak days on Carnival Monday and Tuesday, preceded by J'ouvert at dawn, a high point for bouyon and soca. The fetes and sound systems of the weeks before launch the year's bouyon tracks.

What does Mas Domnik mean?

Mas Domnik is Dominica's carnival said in Creole. The name is a reminder that Dominica is a Creole island above all: the language of carnival, like that of bouyon, blends kwéyòl and English, from the singers' calls to the refrains taken up by the crowd and the names of the bands.

What music is played at Mas Domnik?

Bouyon above all: since the 1990s, it has been the music of Mas Domnik. The year's tracks are played in the bands, the sound systems and the fetes that precede the two big days, and that is where artists' reputations are made. Soca, calypso, chanté mas and lapo kabwit are heard too.

Which traditions has Mas Domnik kept?

Where other carnivals have become tourist products, Mas Domnik still keeps its old forms: chanté mas, lapo kabwit bands, traditional characters and the sensays. That is why Dominica carnival, in Roseau, is considered one of the most traditional in the Caribbean.

Since when has bouyon been the music of Mas Domnik?

Since the 1990s. The band WCK created bouyon in Roseau in 1988, and in the following decade the genre dominated the island's music scene: it became the music of Roseau carnival and of the village fetes. That double foothold, the capital and the villages, gave it a deep popular base. See the WCK page.

What is chanté mas?

It is the carnival song tradition of Dominica, built on double meanings and collective response: a singer calls, the crowd answers. Bouyon inherited from it its direct, teasing and often lewd language, and its singers who work the crowd. Chanté mas is also one of the traditional dances bouyon blends with jing ping and bèlè. See bouyon and Kwéyòl.

Which drum do you hear in the Mas Domnik bands?

The lapo kabwit, a goat-skin drum, the drum of chanté mas and of Dominica's carnival. At Mas Domnik, whole bands play lapo kabwit. It is one of the traditional sounds that form the soul of bouyon, and good producers still sample it in their riddims. See bouyon instruments.

What is Carnival City in Roseau?

It is the name the bills gave to Pottersville Savannah, the historic outdoor stage of Roseau's big carnival nights. The February calendar of Domnitjen magazine places calypso contests, the carnival queen crowning and festival concerts there, including a Bouyon Festival with Triple Kay, Swinging Stars and WCK. See Cross Vibes Band.

How do you dance bouyon at Mas Domnik?

In the street, jumping: bouyon comes down from dance music, the jing ping of traditional balls, bèlè, quadrille and the chanté mas of carnival. Its fast tempo, looping keyboards and electronic bass are made for the street, the fete and the parade. In Guadeloupe and Martinique, its nickname jump up sums up that energy. See the bouyon dance.

D-Mitri: two verified records, and a profile that states its limits

Published October 20, 2026

D-Mitri is an artist whose name appears in the tracklists of soca and bouyon DJs, and almost nowhere else. After a full search, the verifiable public record comes down to two traces: one song, “Idk Her Name”, performed with Lyte and Latest and carried by at least two reference mixes, and a bouyon remix of Lil Durk and J. Cole “All My Life”, published on YouTube with a production credit shared with Daddy Keyz. This profile publishes those two facts, explains the context that makes them visible, carefully separates D-Mitri from his many namesakes, and states plainly everything the sources do not establish: neither his full name, nor his island of origin, nor his official accounts are confirmed by the public record consulted.

Who D-Mitri is: one name, three spellings, almost no archive

D-Mitri name is written three ways across the sources, and that instability is part of the record. The tracklist of DJ Digga D mix “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)”, published on Podtail, writes it “D’Mitri”. The tracklist of the Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix”, published on Listen Notes and on Castbox, writes it “DMitri”. This site uses the spelling “D-Mitri” for its page, without claiming it is the artist official spelling: no verified official page of the artist was found during the research to settle the question.

The name is also a namesake trap, and this profile rules the namesakes out one by one so that nothing is borrowed from their biographies. D-Mitri is not Dimitri from Paris, the French house producer born in Istanbul whose radio and recording career has been documented since the 1980s. He is not IHeartDmitri, the stage name of an American songwriter named Claude Dmitri Blinov Jr. according to his Spotify statistics page. He is not Demitrean, the hip hop artist from Fresno, California listed by Shazam. He is not Demetri, the artist from Ypsilanti, Michigan described by Music Metrics Vault. He is not the Romanian pop singer named Dimitri tracked by the Viberate platform either. No fact from those five careers is used here.

What remains, once the namesakes are set aside, is modest but real: an artist credited as a performer on a song played by soca and bouyon DJs, and as a producer, under the handle @dmitribts, on a bouyon remix published on YouTube. That is little, and this profile does not inflate it. In a scene where many songs travel from mix to mix and from phone to phone before they exist on platforms, a trace in DJ tracklists is often the first form of documentation an artist gets, and sometimes the only one for a long time.

Idk Her Name: the song that carried D-Mitri into the mixes

The first verified fact in the record is a song: “Idk Her Name”, credited to D’Mitri, Lyte and Latest. It appears as track 34 of “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)”, an episode of the Soca Podcast presented by Digga D, whose full tracklist is published on Podtail. The company the song keeps in that mix is the soca and carnival scene of the English speaking Caribbean in 2023: Boyzie, Mr Killa, Tallpree, Lavaman and Lil Kerry hold neighbouring slots. The D-Mitri song is programmed there as a party record among party records, with no special mention, which is exactly how a song settles into DJ rotation.

The same song returns two seasons later in another mix, and that second trace counts double. The Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix”, whose tracklist is published on Listen Notes and on Castbox, programmes “IDK Her Name” under the name “DMitri”, inside a selection that mixes soca and bouyon: Patrice Roberts, Machel Montano with Tano, Skinny Fabulous, Nailah Blackman, Klassik Frescobar and Trilla G all appear in the same list. A song programmed in 2023 and programmed again in a 2025-26 season mix has therefore survived at least two carnival cycles in DJ selections, which separates a lasting trace from a one-off appearance.

On the content of the song itself, the public record consulted gives no lyrics, no official release date, no label and no production credit. This profile does not invent them. It holds only to what the tracklists establish: the song exists, it is performed by D-Mitri with Lyte and Latest, and soca and bouyon DJs programme it. Neither Lyte nor Latest is identified beyond a stage name in those sources, and this profile assigns neither of them an island or a biography.

All My Life Bouyon: the remix published on YouTube

The second verified trace is of a different kind: a remix. The YouTube video titled “D’Mitri - All My Life Bouyon feat. Daddy Keyz” presents a bouyon remix of the song “All My Life” by Lil Durk, featuring J. Cole. The video description is precise on three points, and this profile sticks to those three points. First, it states that the uploader does not own rights to the vocals used in the remix. Second, it names the original song and its performers. Third, it credits production to two handles: @dmitribts and @daddykeyz.

That production credit is the only item in the record that shows D-Mitri behind the machines and not only behind a microphone. The handle @dmitribts works there as a producer signature, placed next to that of Daddy Keyz, who is credited as featured artist in the video title and as co-producer in its description. This profile cites that handle as a published credit, and nothing more: no active official profile under that name was verified, so this page links to no account.

The move itself, on the other hand, can be read without inventing anything. Taking an American hip hop hit and sewing it back onto a bouyon rhythm is a documented practice of the scene: bouyon also advances through these grafts, which bring voices the young public already knows into the tempo of Dominican fetes. To place this remix in its frame, our bouyon soca page tells the story of the branch born in Dominica, started in 2003 by producer Dada according to the signed testimony of Sean “Smokiller” Francis, and popularised afterwards by Triple Kay. The D-Mitri remix belongs to that producer culture of fusion work, even though its maker remains, for now, almost without a public biography.

What this profile does not know: the limits of the public record

An honest profile has to name its blanks. First blank: identity. No source consulted gives D-Mitri full name, so this profile prints none. Second blank: origin. No source consulted establishes his island, his town or his nationality. The fact that his songs travel in soca and bouyon mixes dominated by the English speaking Caribbean scenes locates his audience and his programmers, not his birth, and this profile does not turn a DJ booking into a certificate of origin.

Third blank: the full discography. Two verified records do not make a catalogue. Other songs may exist under other spellings of the name, on platforms the research could not connect to this artist with certainty; rather than risk attributing a namesake song to D-Mitri, this profile keeps a short and safe catalogue. Fourth blank: official accounts. No social or streaming profile was verified as his by direct opening, so the schema of this page contains no external artist link.

Those limits are not a reason to silence the artist, they are the reason to write the profile this way. The bouyon scene counts dozens of names that live first in DJ mixes, remix videos and the public phones, long before press biographies. Documenting the exact trace, with its dates and its sources, then saying what is missing, is worth more than an invented portrait. If the artist or his team confirm an identity, an origin or official accounts, this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site.

Why D-Mitri appears on a bouyon site

Because bouyon can also be read in its documented margins. The two D-Mitri traces touch the two moves that keep the scene alive day to day: the party song DJs keep in their selections from one season to the next, and the producer remix that grafts an international voice onto the Dominican rhythm. “Idk Her Name” illustrates the first move, alongside Lyte and Latest. “All My Life Bouyon” illustrates the second, with Daddy Keyz. Together, they justify a short, sourced and careful page, rather than an absence or a novel.

To hear the scene in which these traces make sense, our bouyon playlist gathers bouyon, bouyon soca and new releases followed by Bouyon Shattaz, and our history of bouyon page traces the genre from Roseau. D-Mitri holds a place there in proportion to his record: real, recent, and waiting for a sequel that only new facts can write.

Frequently asked questions

Who is D-Mitri?

D-Mitri is an artist credited on the song “Idk Her Name”, with Lyte and Latest, programmed in Digga D mix “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)” and then in the Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix”, and credited as producer, under the handle @dmitribts, of the “All My Life Bouyon” remix published on YouTube with Daddy Keyz. The public record consulted establishes neither his full name nor his origin.

Which song by D-Mitri do DJs play?

“Idk Her Name”, performed by D’Mitri with Lyte and Latest. It appears as track 34 of “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)” on Podtail, then in the tracklist of the Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix” on Listen Notes and Castbox, where the name is written “DMitri”.

Did D-Mitri remix “All My Life” by Lil Durk?

Yes, according to the YouTube video “D’Mitri - All My Life Bouyon feat. Daddy Keyz”. Its description states that the uploader does not own rights to the vocals used, names the original song by Lil Durk featuring J. Cole, and credits production to @dmitribts and @daddykeyz.

Is D-Mitri Dimitri from Paris or IHeartDmitri?

No. Dimitri from Paris is a French house producer born in Istanbul, active since the 1980s. IHeartDmitri is the stage name of an American artist named Claude Dmitri Blinov Jr. according to his statistics page. Demitrean from Fresno, Demetri from Ypsilanti, and the Romanian pop singer Dimitri are yet other people. None of their facts belong in this profile.

Where is D-Mitri from?

The public record consulted during the research for this profile does not establish it, and this page does not guess. His songs travel in soca and bouyon mixes from the English speaking Caribbean, which documents his audience and his programmers, not his birthplace or his nationality.

Where can I hear the bouyon of D-Mitri’s scene?

Start with the two verified traces, “Idk Her Name” with Lyte and Latest and the “All My Life Bouyon” remix with Daddy Keyz. For the scene around them, comment ROAD MIX under an @bouyonshattaz post on Instagram to get the Bouyon Road Mix by direct message, or open our bouyon playlist, which gathers bouyon, bouyon soca and new releases from Dominica.

Is it spelled D-Mitri, D’Mitri or DMitri?

All three forms exist, and no verified official page of the artist settles it. The tracklist of Digga D’s “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)” writes “D’Mitri”, the Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix” writes “DMitri”, and the video of the “All My Life Bouyon” remix also reads “D’Mitri”. This site uses “D-Mitri” for its page, without claiming it is the official spelling.

Who produced All My Life Bouyon?

The description of the YouTube video “D’Mitri - All My Life Bouyon feat. Daddy Keyz” credits production to two handles, @dmitribts and @daddykeyz. Daddy Keyz is therefore both the featured artist, in the video title, and co-producer. It is the only piece of the record that shows D-Mitri behind the machines, not only at the microphone. No official account was verified under these handles.

What is a bouyon remix like All My Life Bouyon?

It is a hit from elsewhere, here “All My Life” by Lil Durk with J. Cole, stitched back onto a bouyon rhythm. This graft is a documented practice of the scene: it brings voices young audiences already know into the tempo of Dominica’s parties. D-Mitri’s video even states that its author does not own the rights to the vocals used. See our bouyon remix page.

Which mixes feature D-Mitri?

Two DJ mixes with published tracklists. Digga D’s “Sweet Fuh Daze 2023 (Vol. 13)” for the Soca Podcast places “Idk Her Name” at track 34, near Boyzie, Mr Killa, Tallpree, Lavaman and Lil Kerry. The Jamz Podcast “Soca/Bouyon 2025-26 Mix” programmes it again with Patrice Roberts, Machel Montano, Skinny Fabulous, Nailah Blackman, Klassik Frescobar and Trilla G. See our bouyon mix page.

Does D-Mitri make bouyon or soca?

Both worlds, going by his traces. “Idk Her Name” circulates in English-speaking Caribbean soca and bouyon mixes, from one carnival season to the next, and the “All My Life Bouyon” remix is explicitly bouyon. No source classifies the artist more precisely. The line between the two genres, often mixed together in carnival sets, is explained on our bouyon vs soca page.

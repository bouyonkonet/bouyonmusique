Kassidje: the name on one documented record, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Kassidje is an artist name whose verified public trace fits in one tracklist line: “Doc J and Kassidjé: A pa taw”, in the Guadeloupe episode of the Faya podcast, devoted to bouyon. No biography was found, no platform artist page that could be assigned to the name without a namesake risk, no interview, no documented label. This profile tells that line and the document carrying it, describes the Guadeloupean scene in which the record was played, honestly flags a second, unconfirmed mention, and states clearly everything it cannot claim. It is a short profile, openly so, and its verified recordings list holds a single title, the only one that is sourced.

Who Kassidje is: one documented collaboration, and nothing else verified

Start with the fact, in its exact form. The Faya podcast, a show by Renaud Brizard for Nique, la radio, devoted one episode to the bouyon of Guadeloupe, titled “Voicenotes from Guadeloupe: le bouyon”. The published tracklist of that episode lines up, among some twenty records, the entry “Doc J and Kassidjé: A pa taw”. The name is printed there with an accent, Kassidjé; this page keeps the unaccented spelling of its own address, Kassidje, and flags the variant so that readers searching for the record find the same line.

That credit says three things, and only three. Kassidje recorded at least one bouyon record, titled “A pa taw”, a title in Creole like much of the output of that scene. The record is a duet, shared with Doc J, the musician the reference articles cite among the popular names of bouyon gwada and bouyon hardcore. And the record circulated enough to be kept by a reference podcast on the world club music scenes, produced in France and carefully documented, tracklist and guests included.

Beyond that line, the research carried out for this profile in October 2026 found nothing verifiable that belongs to this Kassidje. Search engines return dozens of namesakes and near namesakes, singers, rappers, DJs and bands with no documented link to Guadeloupean bouyon, and none of their information is used here. No date, no home town, no full name, no confirmed official account: the Kassidje file is, to date, a one record file. This page prefers to say so in its first section rather than let readers believe in a biography it does not have.

“A pa taw”: the record and the show that carries it

The document carrying “A pa taw” deserves describing, because it is what gives the record its dated, placed context. Faya presents itself as the podcast of the global dancefloor: each episode explores the club scene of one city or territory, with guests from the scene and a tracklist published record by record. The Guadeloupe episode takes the listener to Pointe-à-Pitre and sets the scene from its introduction: for ten years, bouyon has been slipping into every Guadeloupean party; it is a very fast music, with raw lyrics, that even came close to being banned, a real outlet for Guadeloupean youth, one that gets people talking, often badly, but above all gets them dancing.

The guests of the episode draw the scene from the inside: Le Cok Sportif, DJ Beaw, Tytys, Doc J himself and Yu Meï, of the duo Ultimes Pétasses. The tracklist, for its part, works as a photograph of a Guadeloupean bouyon party programme of that period: Le Cok Sportif and La Mokerie open with “Dlo Coco”, DJ Beaw lines up his riddims, Daly passes with “Big Ting Poppin”, Reo and DJ Bling with “Sa ka bay”, then come Man Smooky, Haterz’Be, Tytys, the Yellow Gaza Girls and the Ultimes Pétasses. It is in that precise company that “A pa taw” by Doc J and Kassidje takes its place, between Le Cok Sportif “Cho MM” and Daly “Big Ting Poppin”.

The position of the record in the tracklist is information in itself. A documentary podcast does not pick its records at random: its tracklist reflects what was turning in the parties and mixes of the scene at the time of recording. “A pa taw” appears there as a current repertoire record of Guadeloupean bouyon, not as an exhumed curiosity. For an artist with no other verified trace, that presence is worth the start of an archive: it proves the record existed, that it was played, and that a duo billed Doc J and Kassidje was part of the sound landscape the episode documents.

What the tracklist does not give must be stated just as clearly. It gives neither the release date of the record, nor a label, nor a producer, nor a duration. No song page attributable to “A pa taw” on a streaming platform was found during the research, so no writing or composition credit is published here. If the record one day appears on a platform with its credits, they will be added to this page, with their source, and the page will be dated again.

The Guadeloupean bouyon around the record: the scene the episode documents

To place Kassidje, the scene in which his only documented record was played has to be placed. Bouyon was born in Dominica, where WCK started the style in 1988, and its Guadeloupean form, bouyon gwada, was born of the local adoption of that sound by Guadeloupean artists, after years of Dominican bands touring the French Antilles. The Faya episode describes the result of that adoption from the party point of view: a fast, raw sound, everywhere in the island parties, carried by a generation of artists and DJs recording in Creole and in French.

The Kassidje collaborator on “A pa taw” is one of the established names of that scene. Doc J is cited by the reference articles on the music of Guadeloupe among the popular musicians of bouyon gwada, in the neighbourhood of bouyon hardcore, alongside Wee Low, Suppa and Yellow Gaza, and he is one of the guests of the Faya episode, where he also appears solo with “Valey pa crachey”. Recording a duet with Doc J therefore places Kassidje, at least for the length of one record, inside the circle of voices that scene recognises. This profile does not turn that neighbourhood into a biography: it says the duet exists, that it is documented, and that it is the only verified anchoring of the name.

The episode also documents the internal diversity of the Guadeloupean scene, and that diversity helps read the place of a record like “A pa taw”. The same tracklist mixes DJ producers riddims, rapper duos, the female voices of the Yellow Gaza Girls and the Ultimes Pétasses, and a zouk classic, Franky Vincent “Alice ça glisse”, slipped into the selection as a nod to earlier generations. The Guadeloupean bouyon of the episode is not a block: it is a party ecosystem where records circulate fast, where collaborations happen between neighbouring scenes, and where many artists exist in mixes first before existing in databases. Kassidje is, in the current state of the file, one of those artists.

The second mention: a published lead, not confirmed

The research turned up a second mention of the name, and this profile flags it for what it is: a lead, not a proof. The professional directory of the LANDR Network, which lists music service providers offering their work, carries the profile of a beatmaker, composer and audio engineer based in Toulouse. In the list of artists he states he has worked with appear, side by side, skydimex, kassidjé and fuckly. The spelling is the same as in the Faya tracklist, accent included.

That mention cannot be tied to the Kassidje of “A pa taw” with the level of certainty this site requires. The profile gives no title, no date and no link to a recording, and nothing allows a check that the artist named by that Toulouse beatmaker is the same as the Doc J partner on a Guadeloupean bouyon record. The hypothesis would not be implausible in itself, the Guadeloupean bouyon scene regularly works with beatmakers based in mainland France, but a plausible hypothesis is not a fact, and this page leaves it in its place: mentioned, attributed to its source, and marked unconfirmed.

The same caution governs the rest of the file. No social media account has been assigned to Kassidje, because no account found ties back verifiably to the record “A pa taw” or to Doc J. The sameAs field of this page schema therefore stays empty, as for every artist whose links were not opened and verified one by one. And the recordings list of the schema holds the single sourced record, “A pa taw”, credited to Doc J and Kassidje: one real, documented record is worth more than a discography rebuilt sideways.

What this profile claims, and what it leaves open

Let the file be summed up, because on a profile this thin, clarity beats emphasis. Claimed and sourced: a bouyon record titled “A pa taw” is credited to Doc J and Kassidje, spelled Kassidjé, in the published tracklist of the Guadeloupe episode of the Faya podcast, a show by Renaud Brizard for Nique, la radio. Claimed and sourced as well: that episode documents the bouyon scene of Guadeloupe, its guests include Doc J, and its introduction describes ten years of bouyon in the island parties. Everything else is open.

Open means: Kassidje origin not published, full name unknown to this file, discography publicly limited to that single record found, current activity not verified, identity of the LANDR mention not confirmed. Publishing a profile under these conditions answers this site mission: document bouyon and its scenes with the evidence available, stating the level of evidence. A record played in Guadeloupean parties and carried by a reference podcast deserves a page that passes it on exactly for what it is, rather than a silence that would let it vanish with the tracklist carrying it.

To hear the current of Kassidje, the scene around the record is therefore the place to listen: our bouyon gwada page describes the Guadeloupean form of the genre, our history of bouyon page retraces the path of the sound from Dominica, and our bouyon playlist follows the current scene of Guadeloupe and Dominica. If the Kassidje file thickens, if a record, a credit or an official page appears, this page will grow with it, dated and corrected like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Kassidje?

Kassidje is an artist whose only verified credit found in October 2026 is a bouyon record, “A pa taw”, recorded with Doc J. The record appears, spelled Kassidjé, in the published tracklist of the Guadeloupe episode of the Faya podcast, devoted to the bouyon of Guadeloupe. No verified biography of Kassidje was found.

Kassidje or Kassidjé: how is the name spelled?

The Faya podcast tracklist prints the name with an accent, Kassidjé, in the line “Doc J and Kassidjé: A pa taw”. This page keeps the unaccented spelling of its own address, Kassidje, and flags the variant so that readers searching for the record find the same line. The mention found in the LANDR directory also carries the accent, in lower case: kassidjé.

What is the known record of Kassidje?

Only one: “A pa taw”, a duet with Doc J, a musician cited by the references among the popular names of bouyon gwada. The Faya tracklist places it between Le Cok Sportif “Cho MM” and Daly “Big Ting Poppin”, in the middle of the records that were turning in the Guadeloupean bouyon parties documented by the episode.

Who is Doc J, Kassidje partner on “A pa taw”?

Doc J is one of the established names of bouyon gwada: the reference articles on the music of Guadeloupe cite him among its popular musicians, in the neighbourhood of bouyon hardcore, alongside Wee Low, Suppa and Yellow Gaza. He is also one of the guests of the Faya Guadeloupe episode, where he appears solo with “Valey pa crachey”.

What is the Faya podcast, the source of this profile?

Faya is the global dancefloor podcast of Renaud Brizard, for Nique, la radio: each episode explores one club scene, with guests from the scene and a published tracklist. Its episode “Voicenotes from Guadeloupe: le bouyon” lands in Pointe-à-Pitre, hosts Doc J among others, and describes a very fast bouyon, with raw lyrics, present for ten years in every Guadeloupean party.

Which other artists appear in the Faya Guadeloupe episode with Kassidje?

The guests of the episode are Le Cok Sportif, DJ Beaw, Tytys, Doc J and Yu Meï, of the duo Ultimes Pétasses. The tracklist opens with “Dlo Coco” by Le Cok Sportif and La Mokerie, then runs through Daly, Reo and DJ Bling with “Sa ka bay”, Man Smooky, Tytys, the Yellow Gaza Girls and the Ultimes Pétasses, with even a zouk classic, Franky Vincent “Alice ça glisse”.

Where does the bouyon gwada of the Kassidje scene come from?

It comes from Dominica: bouyon was born there, with WCK starting the style in 1988, and its Guadeloupean form was born of the local adoption of that sound, after years of Dominican bands touring the French Antilles. The Faya episode describes the result from the party side: a fast, raw sound, carried by artists recording in Creole and in French.

Is Kassidje Guadeloupean?

The file does not allow stating it. The only documented anchoring of Kassidje is Guadeloupean by context: his known record was played in an episode devoted to the bouyon of Guadeloupe, as a duet with Doc J, an artist of the bouyon gwada scene. No open source publishes his origin, his town or his path, and this profile does not invent them.

Does the Kassidje mention on LANDR concern the same artist?

It is not confirmed. A beatmaker, composer and audio engineer based in Toulouse lists “kassidjé” among the artists he has worked with, in the professional directory of the LANDR Network. The spelling matches the Faya tracklist, but the profile gives neither title nor date, and no source allows checking that it is the same artist. This profile flags the mention as an unconfirmed lead.

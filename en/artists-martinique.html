The profile of the wave: Maurane Voyer

Maurane Voyer is a singer from Martinique and the Martinican profile of the current bouyon wave. Her album "BBW", released in 2022, establishes her world before bouyon: a voice, a writing style and a presence that give her a public of her own. That past matters for understanding what follows: when she arrives on a bouyon track in 2026, she is not a passing guest, she brings a world already built and an audience that follows her from one genre to another.

Her profile on this site documents that path without adding anything to it: the album, the island, then the meeting with Dominican bouyon. For Martinican readers, she is a familiar doorway into a neighbouring genre; for Dominican readers, she is the discovery of a Fort-de-France voice who chooses their sound. Artist hubs exist exactly for that: turning a collaboration heard in a playlist into a story you can follow, with dates, titles and verified credits.

Joli BB: the bridge record of 2026

"Joli BB" was released on October 9, 2026, and brings together Maurane Voyer and Reo, the Dominican artist billed as the Prince of Bouyon. The track was released on the Mauve label and was written by Voyer, Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume. Those credits are the very material of the bridge: a Martinican singer, an artist from the source island, a named writing team, an identified label. Nothing vague, nothing invented: a record you can place, date and credit, as this site commits to do for every track it covers.

Why call it a bridge record? Because "Joli BB" makes bouyon circulate in both directions. The Martinican voice enters the Dominican sound, and Reo's public discovers Maurane Voyer while Voyer's public enters bouyon through a voice from home. In 2026, few tracks tell the geography of the genre as clearly: two Creole islands, one track, and a listenership that crosses the sea in both directions, from Fort-de-France to Roseau and back.

How Martinique dances bouyon: jump up

On the bouyon map, Martinique has its own way of receiving the genre: there, bouyon is danced as jump up, that jumping dance that turns a track into a joyful endurance test. Jump up is not a folk detail: it is the proof that a sound truly travels when another public takes it over with its body, its fetes and its habits. Dominican bouyon, in Martinique, becomes music for the dancefloor and the carnival as much as a track to listen to.

That takeover through dance also explains the role of the Martinican public in the economy of the genre. A track that makes Fort-de-France jump then runs everywhere, because dance videos travel faster than press releases. Dominican artists know it and aim for that public; Martinican artists like Maurane Voyer live that culture from the inside. This honest hub says it clearly: Martinique counts in bouyon through its dance and its public as much as through its artist profiles.

The Creole passport: the language that links the islands

Martinique shares with Dominica a passport that neither borders nor flags issue: the Creole language. It is what allows a bouyon track sung or written in the Dominican orbit to be understood, covered and sung in Martinique without translation, and a Martinican voice to sound right on a Roseau riddim. "Joli BB", written in part by Maurane Voyer herself, lives on that linguistic obviousness: Creole is the working language there, not a decoration.

For the reader discovering bouyon through this site, that point changes the view. Bouyon is not an exotic product from far away: it is inter-island Creole music, of which Martinique is natural territory. Collaborations like "Joli BB" are therefore not marketing moves between strangers, but reunions between publics who understand each other word for word. That is also why the French edition of this site exists: a large part of bouyon's current story is written and heard in that shared Creole world.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the Martinican bouyon artist of 2026?

Maurane Voyer, a singer from Martinique, who released the album “BBW” in 2022. In 2026 she is the voice of “Joli BB” with Dominican Reo, released on October 9, 2026, on the Mauve label, and she plays Night 1 of WCMF on Friday October 23, 2026, in Roseau. See Maurane Voyer.

How does Martinique receive bouyon?

In Martinique, bouyon is danced as jump up, a jumping dance that turns a track into a joyful endurance test, in clubs as at carnival. The island shares the Creole language with Dominica, and its public is one of the core publics of the Bouyon Shattaz ROAD MIX playlist, which makes it natural territory for the genre.

What style of music does Maurane Voyer sing?

Maurane Voyer's musical ground covers reggae, dancehall and the sounds of the French Caribbean, with the album “BBW” in 2022. She came to bouyon in 2026 with “Joli BB”: she is no passing guest there, she brings an already built world and an audience that follows her from one genre to another. She posts as @mauraneetc.

Who wrote Joli BB by Reo and Maurane Voyer?

“Joli BB” is written by Maurane Voyer, Dario Yankey, Reo's real name, and Nicolas Lanclume. The track was released on October 9, 2026, on the Mauve label and runs 2 minutes 43 seconds. A Martinican singer, an artist from the source island, a named writing team, an identified label: nothing vague in these credits.

Who is Reo, Maurane Voyer's partner on Joli BB?

Reo, real name Dario Yankey, is a Dominican bouyon artist billed as the Prince of Bouyon by 13th Street Promotions, an attributed title. He is nominated for Bouyon Artist of the Year at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards and plays Night 2 of WCMF 2026. With “Joli BB”, he links Roseau to Fort-de-France. See Reo.

Why is Joli BB a bridge record?

Because “Joli BB” moves bouyon in both directions. Maurane Voyer's Martinican voice enters Reo's Dominican sound, Reo's audience discovers Maurane Voyer, and Voyer's audience enters bouyon through a voice from home. Two Creole islands, one track, and listening that crosses the sea there and back.

Who is Koko Bwa?

Koko Bwa is a Martinican artist, and “Tarif”, released in April 2025 with Denmath featured and Boutcha Bwa, is his first single. Bouyon Shattaz agreed to publish the track, which places Koko Bwa in the site's editorial circulation. The production side of the collaboration is on the Denmath page.

Why is bouyon called jump up in Martinique?

Because the term jump up already existed in the French Antilles for very rhythmic carnival music, notably after Exile One's “L'hivernage” in 1987, nicknamed The Yo. When Dominican bouyon crossed the sea, the Martinican public filed it in that family. For many, bouyon and jump up are the same sound. See jump up.

Is shatta from Martinique?

Yes: shatta is a Martinican dancehall genre, a cousin of bouyon and not a branch born from bouyon. Natoxie is among the known voices of Martinican dancehall. Shatta bouyon names the meeting point of the two, when producers of the shatta generation work with bouyon material. See bouyon vs shatta.

Is bouyon from Martinique?

No. Bouyon was born in Dominica, created by WCK in Roseau in 1988. Martinique, which shares the Creole language and close carnival traditions with Dominica, adopted it and dances it as jump up. In 2026, “Joli BB” shows the link: a Dominican creation, a Martinican voice, an audience on both sides of the sea.

Why does Creole link Martinique to bouyon?

Because Dominican Creole and Martinican Creole understand each other. That shared language lets a bouyon track be understood, picked up and sung in Martinique without translation, and lets a Martinican voice sound right on a Roseau riddim. “Joli BB”, co-written by Maurane Voyer, lives on it: Creole is the working language there, not a backdrop.

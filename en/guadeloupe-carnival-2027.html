Guadeloupe Carnival 2027

By Smokiller · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026

Guadeloupe Carnival is the biggest popular gathering in the archipelago. The 2027 season opens on 3 January and ends on Ash Wednesday, 10 February, with the jours gras on 7 to 9 February and Mi-Careme on 4 March. The detailed programme has not been published yet, but the dates and the traditions are known: Sunday parades, groupes a po and groupes a synthe, and Vaval, the king of carnival, burned at the end of the festival.

The 2027 carnival dates

The carnival season runs from the first Sunday of January to Ash Wednesday. In 2027, it opens on 3 January and closes on 10 February. The jours gras are Dimanche gras on Sunday 7 February, Lundi gras on Monday 8 February and Mardi gras on Tuesday 9 February, followed by Ash Wednesday on 10 February. Mi-Careme, the mid-Lent festival, falls on Thursday 4 March 2027.

The main parades take place on Sunday afternoons. The Dimanche gras Grande Parade is held in Pointe-a-Pitre, and the Lundi gras and Mardi gras parades are held in Basse-Terre. On Ash Wednesday, black-and-white mourning processions cross the whole island and Vaval is burned at sunset. One source gives 6 January, Epiphany, as the opening date, and another gives 10 January, but 3 January, the first Sunday of January, is the most widely repeated date.

Four families of groups

Four categories of groups make the carnival. The groupes a po play with goat-skin drums, chachas and conch shells: their music is gwo siwo in Basse-Terre and Sen Jan in Grande-Terre. The emblematic groups are Akiyo, founded in 1979, and Voukoum, founded in 1988. The groupes a caisses claires mix brass, snare drums and choreography, and the mas groups carry the traditional carnival characters.

The groupes a synthe are the modern face of carnival: synthesisers, electric guitars and drum machines set up on flatbed trucks. Named groups include Pikan, Ti Bwa and Phoenix. The carnival also has its rituals: the election of the Carnival Queen, the children parades, the cavalcades, the Leve Pyjama and the deboules every Sunday in Pointe-a-Pitre.

Vaval, the king of carnival

Vaval is the king of carnival, a satirical effigy that stands for the year gone by. He is presented at the start of the season, he parades during the jours gras, then he is burned on Ash Wednesday in black-and-white mourning processions. The rite marks the end of carnival and the start of Lent.

The carnival is organised by the Federation Guadeloupeenne du Carnaval, based in Pointe-a-Pitre. The detailed programmes by town are published by the town halls shortly before each edition, often on their Facebook pages. For 2027, as of 8 November 2026, no official programme and no theme has been published by the federation.

Bouyon, the contemporary sound of carnival

Bouyon is now an unmissable musical phenomenon in Guadeloupe, with artists such as Shanika, Theodora, KJF, 1T1 and Natoxie. In Guadeloupe and Martinique it is also called jump up. Its energy and its synthesisers bring it naturally close to the carnival groupes a synthe, which revolutionised the carnival landscape with their flatbed trucks.

That should be said plainly: the traditional musical heart of carnival remains gwoka, gwo siwo and Sen Jan from the groupes a po, not bouyon. No bouyon artist or group has been announced for the 2027 carnival, since the official programme does not exist yet. Bouyon is the contemporary sound of the season, not a traditional carnival music. Note also that the theme An Zafe Nou, published for 2027, belongs to the Martinique carnival, not to Guadeloupe.

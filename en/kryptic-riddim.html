Kryptic Riddim: Dada's bouyon Riddim from Dominica (2025)

By Smokiller · Published October 28, 2026

The Kryptic Riddim is a bouyon riddim produced, mixed and mastered by Krishna 'Dada' Lawrence at the Dadamanufakture studio in Dominica. The album reached Deezer on July 6, 2025 with eight vocal tracks and the instrumental, two days after the dadamanufakture YouTube channel put the videos up in one batch. Its most watched song, by a wide margin, is “Shake Something” by Pudaz and Paris.

A Dada riddim, mixed in Dominica

The press text pasted under every video settles the basics. The Kryptic Riddim was produced by Krishna 'Dada' Lawrence and built on bouyon, and he mixed and mastered the whole project at Dadamanufakture Studio in Dominica. Two players are credited on top of the programming: Keith Goddard on bass guitar and Monelle Mondesire on rhythm guitar. The same text pitches it as a summer release.

On Deezer, the “Kryptic Riddim” album is dated July 6, 2025, on the Dadamanufakture label. It has nine tracks: eight vocal cuts plus “Kryptic Instrumental”, credited to Dada Manufakture, for DJs and for singers who want their own go. Most songs sit around two minutes twenty; Pumpa's “Savage” is the longest at 2:49.

A regional cast around a Dominican core

Dada cast well beyond the island. The press text names Pumpa from the US Virgin Islands with “Savage”, Sackie and Zan from Trinidad and Tobago with “G O A L S”, Fadda Fox from Barbados, and Shanna Raymond (Jamaica/St Kitts) with “Hustle Hard”. Fadda Fox shares “Bess Jam” with Faithii, the “Bouyon Barbie”, one of the local voices.

The Dominican side has Pudaz (Dirty Dawg Pudaz) with Paris, Zwady on “Mal Palan”, Major Mystic on “Taste”, and Jael Joseph, known as Foxy Jael, whose “Ah Coming” is billed as her debut on the bouyon scene. Two more songs came after the album: Mr Gwada's “Backdoor”, uploaded July 15, 2025, and King Dice's “Me” on August 28. Neither is on the Deezer album.

Shake Something, the song that carried the riddim

On the dadamanufakture channel, most of the riddim's videos sit between 2,800 and 23,000 views. “Shake Something”, written by Udi Prince and Paris Bell, was past 1.5 million when read on October 28, 2026. That is the song that took the Kryptic Riddim beyond riddim collectors.

A year on, on July 7, 2026, Extasy Band released a remix of “Shake Something” with Dirty Dawg Pudaz and Paris; its description notes that the original is featured on the Kryptic Riddim and that the YouTube licence sits with Dada Manufakture. To hear the whole set in one go, DJ Alfy's official mix, posted by dadamanufakture on July 19, 2025, runs through the versions. For how the format works, see our pages on the bouyon riddim and bouyon in Dominica.

Frequently asked questions

Who produced the Kryptic Riddim?

Krishna 'Dada' Lawrence, who also mixed and mastered it at Dadamanufakture Studio in Dominica, according to the official video descriptions.

When did the Kryptic Riddim come out?

The videos have been up on the dadamanufakture channel since July 4, 2025, and the Deezer album is dated July 6, 2025, on the Dadamanufakture label.

Which artists are on the Kryptic Riddim?

Fadda Fox and Faithii, Zwady, Sackie and Zan, Pudaz and Paris, Shanna Raymond, Pumpa, Major Mystic and Foxy Jael on the album, then Mr Gwada and King Dice on later uploads.

What is the biggest song on the riddim?

“Shake Something” by Pudaz and Paris, past 1.5 million YouTube views in October 2026, and remixed by Extasy Band in July 2026.

Is the Kryptic Riddim bouyon?

Yes. The official press text sets it squarely in bouyon, and it presents “Ah Coming” as Foxy Jael's debut on the bouyon scene.

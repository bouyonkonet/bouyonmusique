Bouyon Music: The Sound Born in Dominica That Makes the Antilles Dance

Bouyon music is a fast dance genre created in Dominica in 1988 by the band WCK. It mixes jing ping, cadence-lypso, zouk and traditional Creole rhythms with keyboards and electronic bass, and it drives carnival from Roseau to Guadeloupe and Paris. What is bouyon music?

The word bouyon comes from Antillean Creole and means a soup, a gumbo: a dish where everything mixes together. Bouyon music does exactly the same thing. It mixes jing ping (Dominica's traditional music, with accordion and lapo kabwit drums), cadence-lypso, traditional dances like bele, quadrille and chante mas, with mazurka, zouk and other Caribbean sounds. On top of that, producers add electronic keyboards, drum machines and very fast synthetic bass.

The result: a fast, raw sound built for dancing and for carnival. In Guadeloupe and Martinique, bouyon is also called jump up. Today, bouyon music goes far beyond the Caribbean: it lives in Gwada, Martinique, French Guiana, Paris and across the whole diaspora, with forms like bouyon soca, bouyon gwada and shatta bouyon.

Bouyon in 5 facts

  • Origin: Roseau, Dominica, in the late 1980s.
  • Creators: the band WCK (Windward Caribbean Kulture), formed in 1986, with Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade among the original groups.
  • Ingredients: jing ping, cadence-lypso, bele, quadrille, chante mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk.
  • Instruments: bele drum, lapo kabwit, shak shak, accordion, rhythm guitar, keyboards and drums.
  • Also called: jump up in Guadeloupe and Martinique.

Frequently asked questions

What is bouyon music?

Bouyon is a music genre created in Dominica in the late 1980s. It mixes jing ping, cadence-lypso, traditional dances like bele, quadrille and chante mas, mazurka and zouk with electronic keyboards, drum machines and very fast synthetic bass. A raw sound built for dancing and for carnival. The full guide: what is bouyon.

Where does bouyon music come from?

Bouyon comes from Roseau, the capital of Dominica, where the band WCK (Windward Caribbean Kulture) was formed in 1986, with Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade among the original groups. The genre then crossed the sea to Guadeloupe and Martinique, which share Creole with Dominica, and went on to France. The full timeline is on the history of bouyon page.

What is bouyon called in Guadeloupe and Martinique?

In the French Antilles, bouyon is also called jump up. The name sums up its effect: a fast tempo that makes everyone jump during fetes and carnivals. For many people in Guadeloupe and Martinique, bouyon and jump up mean the same sound, which came from Dominica. More details on the jump up page.

What are the different styles of bouyon?

The main styles are classic bouyon from Dominica's live bands, bouyon soca (the fusion with Trinidad soca), bouyon gwada (the hardcore branch born with Guadeloupe), shatta bouyon from Antillean producers and the French scene, and the branches influenced by dancehall and ragga, bouyon-muffin and Wrecketeng. An example of a modern style: shatta bouyon.

Yes. Music bouyon, bouyon music and musique bouyon all name the same genre: bouyon, born in Dominica in the late 1980s. English says bouyon music, French says musique bouyon, and music bouyon mixes the two, as many searches do. In Guadeloupe and Martinique, the same sound is also called jump up, a name that sums up its fast tempo.

What does bouyon mean?

In Antillean Creole, bouyon means a soup, a gumbo, or the koubouyon pwason, a fish broth typical of the Caribbean: a dish where you put in everything you have. The name fits the genre, which mixes jing ping, cadence-lypso, bele, quadrille, chante mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk. A name from everyday cooking, not from a studio. See the meaning of bouyon.

Who invented bouyon music?

The band WCK, short for Windward Caribbean Kulture, formed in 1986 in Roseau, Dominica. Its musicians fused cadence-lypso and jing ping with keyboards up front, synthetic rhythm patterns and a fast tempo. Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade are among the other original groups. From the 1990s, bouyon dominated Dominica's carnival. Full profile of the band: WCK.

Is bouyon from Dominica or Guadeloupe?

From Dominica, where WCK created bouyon in 1988 in Roseau. Guadeloupe received it afterwards, through the shared Creole language and the back and forth of fetes and carnivals, then built a major scene: collaborations between Dominican and Guadeloupean musicians gave birth to bouyon gwada, the hardcore branch of the genre. The invention remains Dominican. See bouyon gwada.

Is bouyon from the Dominican Republic?

No. Bouyon comes from Dominica, a small English-speaking and Creole-speaking Caribbean island, not to be confused with the Dominican Republic. Its capital, Roseau, is the city where WCK created the genre in 1988. That is also why bouyon is sung in a mix of English and Dominican Creole, Kwéyòl. Everything about the genre's island: bouyon in Dominica.

For the new generation, Reo, presented as the Prince of Bouyon, and Miimii KDS, an artist from Guadeloupe credited on a gold single. Behind the sound, producers like Smokiller and HYPNO. How is bouyon music danced?

With no official step: every riddim imposes its own moves. People sway on the choruses, jump together on the build-ups and answer the calls of the singer or the DJ. The fast tempo comes from carnival and traditional Dominican dances, hence the nickname jump up. On social media, producers dance on their own beats to launch each new sound. Is bouyon the same as soca?

No. Soca was born in Trinidad in the 1970s, from calypso and Indian rhythms like the dholak. Bouyon was born in Dominica in the late 1980s, from jing ping and cadence-lypso, with a faster and rawer sound. The two meet in bouyon soca, launched in Dominica by Dada in 2003 according to the testimony of Smokiller. Comparison: bouyon vs soca.

Is bouyon popular in France?

Yes, France has become the big market of bouyon. Songs earn certifications there: "Pow Pow / Allo Doudou", by Niska, Litleboy LSBeats767 and Miimii KDS, was certified Single Or (gold) by the SNEP at 15 million stream equivalents. Paris and the big French cities are home to a Guadeloupean, Martinican, Guianese and Dominican diaspora that keeps bouyon nights alive. See bouyon in France.

Le Dext: “Open That”, “Stick It”, “FAT CAT”, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Le Dext is a bouyon artist whose verified public file fits into three records: “Open That”, a single released on 30 August 2024, “Stick It”, an official audio presented as a Bouyon 2K25 record, and “FAT CAT”, a single shared with Team Bwè Tou Sa and listed in 2026. No published biography was found, no interview, no published origin this site can verify, and social links truncated in the only description that prints them. This profile tells those three records with their exact credits, separates Le Dext from a near identical namesake, Le Det, and states clearly everything it cannot claim. It is a short profile, openly so.

Who Le Dext is: one name, three records, almost no archive

Start with what can be verified, because on this file it is quickly said and it is the only honest way to begin. Le Dext is the artist name credited on three recordings the platforms document: a single under his own name, “Open That”, released in 2024; an official audio in collaboration with two beatmakers, “Stick It”, presented in its own title as a Bouyon 2K25 record; and a single as a duet with an established collective of the scene, “FAT CAT”, billed to Le Dext and Team Bwè Tou Sa. Three records, three documents, and that is close to the whole public file.

No published artist biography was found during the research for this profile. The platform pages carrying his records publish neither his path, nor his town, nor his full name, nor a verifiable press photo. The writing credits of his best documented single name two authors, Malik Ismael Paul and Emmanuel Toussaint, and this profile cites them exactly for what they are: the credited writers of the record. It turns neither of them into the identity of Le Dext, because no open source makes that link, and guessing which of the two, or neither, stands behind the artist name would be invention.

The context, for its part, can be read in the documents themselves. His single is sung in French, according to the record page. His most complete video tags his world with the codes of the French Antilles and Dominica: 971 for Guadeloupe, 767, the Dominica area code, 972 for Martinique and 973 for French Guiana, next to the keyword bouyonmusic. Those tags are the artist own, published under his own video: they draw the circuit he addresses, the bouyon of the French Antilles fed by the sound of Dominica, without publishing his place of birth or residence. This profile reports them as tags, not as a birth certificate.

Why publish a profile of an artist this poorly documented? Because those three records exist, they circulate on the platforms and channels of the scene, and readers searching for this name deserve a page that sorts the true from the confused. The sorting matters here more than elsewhere, because an artist with a near identical name, Le Det, also releases bouyon on the same platforms. A whole section of this profile is devoted to that confusion, because it is the first trap of the file.

“Open That”: the documented single, released on 30 August 2024

“Open That” is the best filed record of Le Dext, and its page measures what the platforms know about him. According to Shazam, which carries the distributor data, “Open That” was released on 30 August 2024, on the label 5417166 Records DK, as a single titled “Open That - Single”. The page states the language of the record, French, and its tempo, 160 BPM, a very fast tempo, above even the usual tempos of party bouyon, which says the energy the track claims. The composition and lyrics credits name two authors: Malik Ismael Paul and Emmanuel Toussaint, both credited as songwriters.

The label number, 5417166 Records DK, deserves one sentence, because it places the mode of production. That kind of numbered identifier followed by the letters DK is the mark of releases distributed as self releases through the digital distribution platforms open to independent artists. In other words, “Open That” is, in the state of the documents, an independent release, put online by the artist or his close circle, with no documented record company behind it. That is the ordinary release mode of the new bouyon generation of the Antilles: a beatmaker, a studio, a digital distributor, and the record is worldwide the same day.

The video linked to the record completes the collaboration picture. Shazam links “Open That” to an official audio titled “Le Dext X Mkinder Beatz - Open That (Audio Official) Bouyon 2K25”. The name Mkinder Beatz, also written Mkinderbeatz in other credits, is that of the beatmaker associated with the record. The Bouyon 2K25 tag attached to the video, while the single is dated August 2024, reads as the season vintage of the record promotion rather than as its release date: this profile reports both dates exactly as the sources give them, without merging them into one.

What the “Open That” page does not give must be stated just as clearly. It gives no commented duration, no lyrics, no shot video clip, and no element of the artist path. The play counter shown by the platform is a usage figure of that platform, not a career measure, and this profile converts it into neither popularity nor ranking. The record stands as the main piece of the file for one simple reason: it is the only one whose date, label, language, tempo and writers are all published and verifiable in one place.

“Stick It”: the video, Tidji, Mkinderbeatz and brk.beats

The second verified record of Le Dext is documented not by a platform page but by its own video. The official audio of “Stick It”, published on YouTube, presents the record as a new title by the artist, in collaboration with Mkinderbeatz and brk.beats. The video description gives the full technical credits, which is rare on this file and precious for that reason: production by Tidji, of Blockstudio, and beatmakers Mkinderbeatz and brk.beats. The video title carries, like that of “Open That”, the vintage Bouyon 2K25.

That description also says how the artist presents himself. It points to a Songwhip page under the name Le Dext, the service that gathers an artist links to every platform, and it prints his network addresses, an Instagram account and a Snapchat account, in forms truncated by the platform display. The account names are readable there, the full addresses are not verifiable, and the rule of this site is strict: an official link enters the schema of a page only if it could be opened and verified character by character. The sameAs field of this page therefore stays empty, and this profile prefers to explain why rather than publish an approximate link.

The keywords of the video complete the portrait of the scene addressed: tendance, blingnation, bouyonmusic, then the series of codes 971, 767, 972, 973 and the word caribbean. An artist who tags a release that way addresses the whole arc of French speaking and Creole speaking bouyon, from Guadeloupe to French Guiana by way of Dominica and Martinique. That matches the geography of the genre: born in Dominica with WCK, bouyon became the shared party music of the French Antilles, shaped locally as bouyon gwada, and its independent artists release their records for the whole arc at once, not for a single island.

On “Stick It” itself, caution stays the rule. The video publishes neither a platform release date for the single, nor a label, nor writing credits, and no song page attributable without risk was found during the research. This profile therefore keeps from the record what its source document establishes: its existence, its official audio format, its two beatmakers, its producer and his studio, and its displayed vintage. The rest, lyrics, reception, figures, is not published here, for lack of a source.

“FAT CAT”: the collaboration with Team Bwè Tou Sa

The third verified record changes scale, because it pairs Le Dext with an established collective name of the Guadeloupean bouyon scene: Team Bwè Tou Sa. The artist page of Team Bwè Tou Sa on Shazam lists, among the collective releases, a single titled “FAT CAT”, billed to Le Dext and Team Bwè Tou Sa, and dated 2026. The same title appears in the list of the most streamed songs of Team Bwè Tou Sa on the platform, alongside records of the collective such as “KILOT” and its collaborations with other names of the scene.

That presence in the catalogue of a followed collective says something precise about the place of Le Dext, with no need to add to it. Team Bwè Tou Sa releases regularly, chains season singles and collaborates with an identifiable circle of artists and DJs of the Gwada scene. That one of its recent singles is co billed to Le Dext places him, at least for the length of one record, inside that working circle. This profile does not turn that co billing into membership: no source presents Le Dext as a member of Team Bwè Tou Sa, and he is therefore described here as a collaborator on the record, nothing more.

The date of the record also matters for reading the file. With “Open That” in 2024, “Stick It” vintaged 2K25 and “FAT CAT” listed in 2026, the file draws a regular activity, at the rhythm of at least one documented release per season, over three years. For an artist with no biography and no interview, that regularity is the only career curve the documents allow tracing, and this profile traces it exactly that way: three dated points, linked by the same beatmaker names and the same circuit of tags, and nothing between the points that is published.

No other record by Le Dext could be verified during the research. Search engines and platforms return, under neighbouring spellings, the records of other artists, starting with Le Det, treated in the next section. The recordings list of this page schema therefore holds three titles, those the opened documents establish: “Open That”, “Stick It” and “FAT CAT”. If an album, an EP or a new single appears with its credits, it will be added here, with its source, and the page will be dated again.

Le Dext or Le Det: the confusion this profile corrects

This point matters, and that is why this profile treats it in a full section. There is another artist, whose name is spelled Le Det, without the x, who also releases bouyon music on the same platforms. Shazam credits him with a single titled “Bouwé’y”, released on 4 July 2023 on the label ILB PRODUCTION, at a tempo of 157 BPM, with a songwriter credit under the name Le Det. Date, label, tempo and credit: everything differs from the Le Dext file, whose single “Open That” was released on 30 August 2024 on another label, with two other named writers.

The confusion is easy, and it is documented by the platforms themselves: their song pages point to each other in neighbouring title sections, and a search on the shortened name mixes the two catalogues. A reader, a DJ or a promoter searching for Le Dext can therefore be offered “Bouwé’y” as if it were his record, and the reverse. This profile decides with the only valid means: the exact spelling of the name, Le Dext with an x, and the complete page of each record. “Bouwé’y” does not appear in the discography published here, because it is not by Le Dext according to the only page that documents it.

The same vigilance applies to the other near namesakes the research crossed: singers, rappers and DJs carrying names in Dext, Dest or Dexta, on other scenes and other continents, with no documented link to the bouyon of the Antilles. None of their information is used in this profile. On a short artist name built on a nickname, namesakes are the norm rather than the exception, and the three record discography published here is worth precisely what it is because it was cleared of those neighbours.

What this profile claims, and what it leaves open

Let the file be summed up, because on a thin profile, clarity beats emphasis. Claimed and sourced: Le Dext released “Open That” on 30 August 2024, on 5417166 Records DK, a record in French at 160 BPM, written by Malik Ismael Paul and Emmanuel Toussaint according to the published credits. Claimed and sourced: his official audio “Stick It”, vintaged Bouyon 2K25, is produced by Tidji, of Blockstudio, on beats by Mkinderbeatz and brk.beats. Claimed and sourced: the single “FAT CAT”, billed to Le Dext and Team Bwè Tou Sa, is listed among the 2026 releases of the collective. Everything else is open.

Open means: origin and path of Le Dext not published, full name unknown to this file, identity behind the two writer names of “Open That” not established, official links not verified character by character and therefore not published, stage activity not documented in the open sources. Publishing a profile under these conditions answers this site mission: document bouyon and its scenes with the evidence available, stating the level of evidence, rather than let the platforms mix this name with those of its namesakes with no page doing the sorting.

To hear the current of Le Dext, start with the three records themselves, in the order of the file: “Open That”, “Stick It”, then “FAT CAT” with Team Bwè Tou Sa. Our bouyon gwada page describes the Guadeloupean form of the genre in which his collaborations sit, our bouyon soca page covers the neighbouring branch, and our bouyon playlist follows the current scene of the Antilles and Dominica. If the Le Dext file thickens, this page will grow with it, dated and corrected like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Le Dext?

What are the verified records of Le Dext?

Three: “Open That” (single, 30 August 2024, 5417166 Records DK), “Stick It” (with Mkinderbeatz and brk.beats, produced by Tidji, Blockstudio) and “FAT CAT” (with Team Bwè Tou Sa, listed in 2026). They are the only records the documents opened for this profile assign to Le Dext without a namesake risk.

Who wrote “Open That” by Le Dext?

The published credits of the record, carried by Shazam, name two writers: Malik Ismael Paul and Emmanuel Toussaint, both credited as songwriters. No open source says which of those names, or neither, corresponds to the identity of Le Dext, and this profile does not guess it.

Are Le Dext and Le Det the same artist?

No source says so, and their pages differ on everything: Le Det, without an x, released “Bouwé’y” on 4 July 2023 on ILB PRODUCTION, at 157 BPM, with a writer credit under his name alone. Le Dext released “Open That” on 30 August 2024 on 5417166 Records DK, at 160 BPM, with two other writers. The platforms mix the two names in their suggestions.

Who is Mkinder Beatz, the Le Dext beatmaker?

Mkinder Beatz, also written Mkinderbeatz, is the beatmaker tied to the first two Le Dext records. Shazam links “Open That” to the official audio “Le Dext X Mkinder Beatz - Open That (Audio Official) Bouyon 2K25”, and the “Stick It” video credits him with brk.beats, under production by Tidji. Was “Open That” by Le Dext released in 2024 or 2025?

The Bouyon 2K25 tag attached to its official audio reads as the vintage of the promotion season, not as the release date. No source presents him as a member: he is the collaborator of Team Bwè Tou Sa on the single “FAT CAT”, billed to Le Dext and the collective and dated 2026. The collective artist page on Shazam lists the record among its releases and most streamed songs, next to “KILOT”. That co billing places him inside its working circle, for the length of one record.

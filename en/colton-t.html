Colton T: the solo generation of Dominican bouyon

Published September 22, 2026

Colton T, written ColtonT in his own follow blocks, is an artist from Dominica. On Friday October 23, 2026, he plays Night 1 of the World Creole Music Festival, alongside Ridge and Pudaz, as a representative of the solo-artist generation of Dominican bouyon. His public routes are gathered under one name: @coltontmusic on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, and the website coltont.com.

One name, one signature: ColtonT

Colton T signs his public identity in a consistent way: ColtonT, in one word, in his own follow blocks, and @coltontmusic everywhere the public can find him. That consistency is a working tool for a solo artist today: one name on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, and his own website, coltont.com, as a stable address when platforms change.

This profile uses the Colton T spelling in text, while noting the ColtonT form he uses himself. The detail may seem tiny, but documenting a scene starts with exact names: an artist spelled wrong is an artist credited wrong, and credits are at the heart of Bouyon Shattaz work on the Dominican scene.

Colton T belongs to the solo-artist generation of bouyon. Where the genre was born with bands and live formations still dominate part of the scene, solo artists build their audience track by track and platform by platform. His place on the 2026 festival bill shows that this generation now has its place on the island biggest stage.

WCMF 2026: Night 1, alongside Ridge and Pudaz

Colton T is on the WCMF 2026 bill as a Night 1 act, on Friday October 23, 2026, per the DDA-linked festival records and published guides used by Bouyon Shattaz. The opening night of the World Creole Music Festival launches three nights of Creole music in Roseau, and appearing on it places an artist in front of the widest audience of his year.

He represents, on that opening night, the solo-artist generation of Dominican bouyon, alongside Ridge and Pudaz. That reading of the bill helps a new audience find its way: the same night can gather the band heritage and today solo names, and that meeting makes the festival a complete portrait of the scene, not just a run of concerts.

For the festival context, read our World Creole Music Festival page, and for the other faces of Night 1, the profiles of Midnight Groovers and TK International. Between the Grand Bay cadence band, the established bouyon formation and the solo artist, the night of October 23 covers nearly sixty years of Dominican music.

Understanding bouyon before the name

To read the story of Colton T properly, bouyon first has to be put back at its source. The genre was created in Dominica, and its full story is told on our history of bouyon page. That reminder matters, because bouyon now travels through Guadeloupe, Martinique, France and the diaspora, sometimes far from the island that created it. Keeping the names of Dominican artists at the centre of the story keeps the map of the genre readable for new listeners.

Bouyon is band music, street music and party music as much as it is listening music. A track can live in a playlist in the morning, in a fete in the evening and on a big stage the following month. That is why a profile like this one is not limited to a list of songs: it is also about the stage, the audience and the place the island holds inside its own creation.

This page therefore follows a simple rule: verified facts only, titles attributed when they are a matter of reputation, and no invented numbers. When something is not confirmed, it is not written. That discipline is part of the work of Bouyon Shattaz, which documents the Dominican scene for French-speaking listeners as well as for the island's own English-speaking audience.

Rights, the other stage

Behind the party sits a question the Dominican scene is now asking more loudly: who owns the music, who signs the contracts and who is paid when a track travels? A song can fill a room and circulate online without its creators being properly protected. That is exactly the ground of the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic, an audit carried out with artists and bands on the island to check contact routes, credits and the protection of their work.

Colton T is part of that Rights Clinic audit cohort, with a verified contact route. The detail may sound administrative, but it changes everything that follows: an artist who can be reached, who is correctly credited and who is well advised can turn one strong season into a lasting career. Bouyon Shattaz argues this point across its whole network strategy: Dominica created bouyon, so its artists should also benefit from its value.

The subject also returns often in public conversations around the scene, around contracts, royalties and intellectual property. Putting those questions on the same level as releases and concerts is a way of recognising that Creole music deserves the same professional standards as any other music industry.

Listening and following the scene

To move from reading to listening, our bouyon playlist page gathers the Bouyon Shattaz ROAD MIX selections, built as a doorway into the Dominican sound of today. Hearing an artist inside a selection, among other voices from the island, helps you understand a style by comparison: the tempo, the voice, the band energy and the way a chorus is built.

The Dominica artists hub then lets you move between profiles: the historic bands, the singers of the new generation and the live formations carrying bouyon to the festival. Each profile points to the others, because no single artist tells the island's story alone. The scene is an ensemble, and it is that ensemble Bouyon Shattaz works to make visible.

Finally, the event that brings all of these worlds together remains the World Creole Music Festival, the WCMF, three nights in Roseau that place Dominica's Creole music in front of an audience from across the Caribbean. It is in that setting that the profile of Colton T takes its most concrete meaning in 2026.

Midnight Groovers: the cadence roots of bouyon, still on stage

Published September 10, 2026

Midnight Groovers is a cadence-lypso band from Grand Bay, Dominica, formed in the late 1960s and led by Phillip "Chubby" Mark. With 39 plus albums per their DOM767 profile, this is a genuine legacy act, still on the World Creole Music Festival bill in 2026. Their story explains where bouyon comes from: before the 1988 genre, there was the cadence generation, and Midnight Groovers is one of its living voices.

Grand Bay, late 1960s: a formation that lasts

A band formed in the late 1960s and still programmed in 2026 crosses more than half a century of Dominican music. Midnight Groovers comes from Grand Bay, a village in the south of Dominica whose name returns constantly in the island musical history. It is also Asa Bantan village: the legacy band and the modern bouyon boss come from the same place, and that neighbourhood tells the continuity of the scene better than any speech.

The band is led by Phillip "Chubby" Mark. In formations of this generation, longevity often rests on a figure who holds the course, the repertoire and the link with the village audience as well as the capital. Midnight Groovers has held that line for decades, through changes of formats, fashions and listener generations.

Their DOM767 profile credits the band with 39 plus albums. That figure, attributed to that source, gives the scale of the work: this is not a one-hit band, but a catalogue built record after record, in an era when recording demanded organisation and audience loyalty that today platforms no longer measure. That catalogue is a sound archive of Grand Bay and Dominica.

The cadence-lypso generation, matrix of bouyon

Midnight Groovers belongs to the cadence-lypso generation, the 1970s sound carried across the Caribbean by bands like Exile One and the Grammacks. Cadence-lypso made Dominica a music exporter ahead of its time: its bands toured, its records circulated, and its mix of Haitian cadence, calypso and Creole language spoke to the whole region.

Bouyon was born from that heritage. In 1988 its creators fused that cadence tradition with jing ping, the island traditional dance music, plugging electronic machines into it. Reading our history of bouyon with Midnight Groovers in mind makes the line concrete: the new genre was not born from nothing, it was born from bands like this one, their stages, their audiences and their language.

That is why this profile stands among the site Dominica artists, even though its own genre is cadence-lypso and not bouyon. Without the cadence generation, there is no bouyon to tell. And as long as Midnight Groovers takes the stage, that line is not only a matter of books: it can be heard, live, with the musicians who carried it from the origin.

WCMF 2026: Night 1, the past on the present programme

Midnight Groovers is on the official WCMF 2026 bill, per the DDA media release. Per the festival published programme structure, recorded in Bouyon Shattaz sprint records of the lineup, the band is a Night 1 act, on Friday October 23, 2026. Opening the festival with a Grand Bay band formed in the late 1960s places the roots of Dominican music at the start of its biggest weekend.

Their presence on the 2026 bill links the festival bouyon present to its cadence roots. The same weekend will see today bouyon acts hold the festival stages, and the audience will be able to hear, a few hours apart, the music that inspired the genre and the genre itself. Few festivals offer that live demonstration of their own history.

For the full programme, read our World Creole Music Festival page. And to measure the journey between generations, compare this profile with those of the Dominican acts in the Bouyon Shattaz countdown: the distance between 1970s Grand Bay and 2026 Roseau is exactly the story this site documents.

The Pilot 5 legacy pick and the Rights Clinic

Midnight Groovers is the legacy pick of the Bouyon Shattaz Pilot 5 plan. In a first pilot group designed for distribution, retaining the cadence-lypso band alongside current bouyon acts is a strong editorial gesture: the value of a catalogue of 39 plus albums should not stay outside modern distribution and rights circuits.

The band is also in the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic audit cohort. For an old catalogue, rights questions take a particular form: decades of recordings, credits to verify and a body of work whose value continues every time cadence is cited, sampled or programmed. Protecting that heritage is protecting part of Dominica musical memory itself.

No public contact for the band is highlighted in this profile, and that is a choice: verified routes pass through the audit work, not through addresses copied without a source. What the public can do is simple: hear the band at the festival, cite its name when telling the birth of bouyon, and never present cadence as a footnote. For Midnight Groovers, it is the main text.

Understanding bouyon before the name

To read the story of Midnight Groovers properly, bouyon first has to be put back at its source. The genre was created in Dominica, and its full story is told on our history of bouyon page. That reminder matters, because bouyon now travels through Guadeloupe, Martinique, France and the diaspora, sometimes far from the island that created it. Keeping the names of Dominican artists at the centre of the story keeps the map of the genre readable for new listeners.

Bouyon is band music, street music and party music as much as it is listening music. A track can live in a playlist in the morning, in a fete in the evening and on a big stage the following month. That is why a profile like this one is not limited to a list of songs: it is also about the stage, the audience and the place the island holds inside its own creation.

This page therefore follows a simple rule: verified facts only, titles attributed when they are a matter of reputation, and no invented numbers. When something is not confirmed, it is not written. That discipline is part of the work of Bouyon Shattaz, which documents the Dominican scene for French-speaking listeners as well as for the island's own English-speaking audience.

Rights, the other stage

Behind the party sits a question the Dominican scene is now asking more loudly: who owns the music, who signs the contracts and who is paid when a track travels? A song can fill a room and circulate online without its creators being properly protected. That is exactly the ground of the Bouyon Shattaz Rights Clinic, an audit carried out with artists and bands on the island to check contact routes, credits and the protection of their work.

Midnight Groovers is part of that Rights Clinic audit cohort, with a verified contact route. The detail may sound administrative, but it changes everything that follows: an artist who can be reached, who is correctly credited and who is well advised can turn one strong season into a lasting career. Bouyon Shattaz argues this point across its whole network strategy: Dominica created bouyon, so its artists should also benefit from its value.

The subject also returns often in public conversations around the scene, around contracts, royalties and intellectual property. Putting those questions on the same level as releases and concerts is a way of recognising that Creole music deserves the same professional standards as any other music industry.

Hearing an artist inside a selection, among other voices from the island, helps you understand a style by comparison: the tempo, the voice, the band energy and the way a chorus is built.

The Dominica artists hub then lets you move between profiles: the historic bands, the singers of the new generation and the live formations carrying bouyon to the festival. Each profile points to the others, because no single artist tells the island's story alone. The scene is an ensemble, and it is that ensemble Bouyon Shattaz works to make visible.

Finally, the event that brings all of these worlds together remains the World Creole Music Festival, the WCMF, three nights in Roseau that place Dominica's Creole music in front of an audience from across the Caribbean. It is in that setting that the profile of Midnight Groovers takes its most concrete meaning in 2026.

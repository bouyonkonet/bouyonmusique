Tedla: one verified credit only, bouyon, Guadeloupe

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Tedla is a name that fits, across the whole public catalog consulted, inside a single credit: a feature on “VIN TCHEK NOU”, the third track of “Premières Notes”, the album by Lewismelo dated November 1, 2024 on the Deezer listing opened for this research. On that record, Tedla sings alongside Le Bluff and Leyou, and the published writing credits name Youri Ellapin, Keran Landre and Ted Clarin. No artist page under the name Tedla, no solo record, no biography and no second credit were found. This profile therefore publishes that single record with all of its checkable context, the album that carries it, the collaborators around it, and the question this name asks the whole site: is Tedla the same person as TedLa, the voice of the 2022 singles with LeYou, to whom our Ted La page is devoted? The answer published here is the answer of the sources: a strong hypothesis, never demonstrated, and treated as such from start to finish.

Who Tedla is: one name, one credit, no artist page

Start with what does not exist, because it is half of the file. Tedla has no verified artist page on the platforms consulted: no Spotify page of his own, no Shazam page of his own, no Deezer page of his own. The name appears only inside another listing, that of the Lewismelo album “Premières Notes”, carried by the Shanika page on Deezer, where the track listing credits him as a feature on a single song. No press article, no interview, no official account and no photograph attached to this name were confirmed during this research. Tedla is, in the strict sense of the public archive, a credit name and nothing more.

What does exist is precise and dated. The Deezer listing opened gives the album “Premières Notes” under the Lewismeloproduction banner, with a date of November 1, 2024, and a list of eight songs, seven of them shown in detail. The third track, “VIN TCHEK NOU”, running 3 minutes 17, is credited to Lewismelo as lead artist, with three guests: Le bluff, Leyou and Tedla. It is the only line of the public catalog where the spelling Tedla, one word with no internal capital, appears as an artist name. All the material of this profile fits inside that line, inside the album around it and inside the writing credits of the record.

It is worth measuring what this kind of trace means in current Guadeloupe bouyon. The scene moves through collective albums and sessions: a lead artist opens his record to a circle of voices, and those voices exist in public through those invitations before they exist through their own releases, when they ever do under their own name. Leyou, one of the two other guests of the same record, is in exactly that case on this site: a single verified trace, this same “VIN TCHEK NOU”. Tedla therefore shares his condition with his record partner. It is no archive accident proper to this name, it is the normal regime of the session voices of this generation.

This profile is therefore, by construction, a short profile, and it says so. It will tell no childhood, no town and no path, because no source publishes them. It will tell one record, one album, three credited writers, two feature partners, and an identity hypothesis this site already states on its neighbouring page and repeats here without hardening it. For a credit name, that is exactly the right size of file: everything that is known, nothing that is not.

“VIN TCHEK NOU”: the only record, read line by line

“VIN TCHEK NOU” reads, in the Creole of the French Antilles, as an invitation: come and see us, come check us, come meet us. The record sits in third position on “Premières Notes”, right after the introduction and “5 KILOS”, the song that brings together Admiral T and Shanika, and that place is not neutral. In a collective album, the opening records serve as a team presentation: the lead artist shows his circle there before unrolling the collaborations one by one. Lewismelo therefore places Tedla inside his presentation, on the same level as Le Bluff and Leyou, before the sessions of Dcamp, Lejuh, Virtuose and Jixels that structure the rest of the album.

The listing of the record, as Deezer publishes it, gives three writers and composers: Youri Ellapin, Keran Landre and Ted Clarin, written in lower case in the original listing. No source consulted shares those three names out among the voices of the record, and this profile does not do it either. It keeps the raw fact, which is the central piece of the file: among the writers of the only record where a voice named Tedla is credited, there is a writer whose first name is Ted. That overlap is published here as an overlap, not as an identification. The section devoted to the TedLa hypothesis explains why that distinction matters and why it is maintained.

What the listing does not publish must be stated just as clearly. No separate producer is credited for “VIN TCHEK NOU” on the page opened, no confirmed video accompanies the record in the sources consulted, and no individual song page on Shazam or Apple Music was found for this track during this research: the trace lives inside the album listing. The record therefore has no counted public counter, no single date and no life separate from the album. Tedla exists, to date, only as the third name of a tracklist line, and it is in that exact form that this site archives him.

There remains the musical matter the position of the record allows to describe without invention. “VIN TCHEK NOU” is a team record, with three guest voices, inside a bouyon gwada album whose other tracks show the same regime: chained features, writers rotating from one track to the next, and a lead artist, Lewismelo, holding the album as a showcase of his network. To hear Tedla is therefore to hear one voice among three, on a presentation record, and any more precise description of his part, his tone or his verse would go beyond what the written sources establish. This profile stops where the listings stop.

“Premières Notes”: the album that carries the credit, track by track

To place the Tedla credit, the whole album must be read, because the album gives the name its only context. “Premières Notes” by Lewismelo, released under Lewismeloproduction and dated November 1, 2024 on the listing opened, shows eight songs, seven of them detailed on the page consulted. The opening, “INTRO”, running 1 minute 16, is written and composed by Lewis Wayne Campbell, the published real name of Lewismelo. The second track, “5 KILOS”, brings Admiral T and Shanika as features, with writing credits to Christy Campbell and Maëva Alphonse. It is right after that record, carried by the most established name of the album, that “VIN TCHEK NOU” and its three guests arrive.

The rest of the album confirms the method. “POMPÉ” brings together Dcamp and KEKS MAFIA, with writers Lewis Wayne Campbell, Dylan Davis Campbell and Kevin Lennard. “ALOUETTE” showcases Lejuh, with Julio Jordan Polydamas as sole published writer, a name readers of this site know as a recurring writer of the LGC catalog. “KEY” invites Virtuose, on a text by Yann-Yves Milon. “HULLABALOO” invites Jixels, on a text by Jonathan Elusca. Every track has its team, every team has its writers, and no track looks like filler: the album works as a map of the gwada network of Lewismelo, name by name.

Inside that map, the Tedla line has a feature the other lines do not. The other guests of the album, Admiral T, Shanika, Dcamp, Lejuh, Virtuose and Jixels, all have catalogs of their own documented elsewhere, and this site profiles several of them. Le Bluff and Leyou, the partners of Tedla, have narrow but real traces: Le Bluff is credited on several records of the same circuit, and Leyou shares with Tedla the status of a voice with a single trace, at least under that spelling. Tedla is therefore the thinnest name of an otherwise dense album, and that is precisely why he gets his own page: so that his credit line is neither inflated, nor absorbed into the catalog of a neighbouring name.

The listener who wants to hear the album in order will usefully start with “5 KILOS” for the weight of the established guests, then “VIN TCHEK NOU” for the team presentation where Tedla appears, then “ALOUETTE” and “POMPÉ” for the rest of the network. Our Lewismelo page documents the lead artist and his path, and our bouyon playlist places this album back inside the Guadeloupe wave it sums up so well.

Tedla and TedLa: the hypothesis, stated as a hypothesis

Here is the question every reader of this site will ask, and that this profile takes head on. There exists, on the neighbouring Ted La page, a catalog published under the spelling TedLa: a Spotify artist page carrying 2022 singles made with LeYou, “ALWAYS HAVE”, “Shake It” with Totone, and “She tell me she want” in The Sound of Bouyon selection. Are Tedla and TedLa the same artist under two different entries of his name? The position of this site, already published on the Ted La page and repeated here word for word in substance, fits in three points that no new research has changed.

First point: no source consulted claims the identity of the two spellings. No article, no listing and no cross credit says that the voice of the 2022 singles is the guest on the Lewismelo album. Second point: no source presents two distinct artists either, by a biography, a territory or a separately claimed repertoire. Third point: the two spellings share a published collaborator and a circuit. LeYou is the constant partner of the TedLa singles, and Leyou, the same name in another case, is one of the three guests of “VIN TCHEK NOU” alongside Tedla. To that common point is added the writer credit Ted Clarin on that same record. Those are listing facts, and they make the single artist hypothesis the most economical one, without turning it into a fact.

The practical consequence of that discipline is visible in the organisation of this site itself. Tedla has his own page, with his own slug, tedla.html, distinct from ted-la.html, and his schema holds a single recording, “VIN TCHEK NOU”, the only one published under his spelling. The three TedLa records stay on the Ted La page, where they were published. If the two names are one day confirmed as one person by a primary source, the artist, Lewismelo or their teams, the two pages will be brought together and the correction will be dated. If the distinction is confirmed, neither page will have lent one name the catalog of the other. Merging right now, as automatic profiles do, would mean fabricating a four record discography nobody signed.

Finally, note that unstable spellings are the norm, not the exception, in this circuit. The same album writes Le bluff with a lower case letter, Leyou as one plain word, and the platform listings write LeYou with an internal capital elsewhere. The names of this scene are entered by different distributors, on different dates, with no shared style sheet. In that landscape, two neighbouring spellings for one artist first name are a banal event, and that is one more reason to publish every credit under its original spelling, as this site does for Tedla, for TedLa, and for every name in its indexes.

The namesakes, the limits of the file, and the place of this credit

The name Tedla also touches unrelated namesakes that search engines gladly mix, and that this profile sets aside explicitly. Tedla is an attested Ethiopian and Eritrean first name: a search on this name returns for example a song titled “Tedla Gualu” from Ethiopian music and public people carrying that first name in contexts with no link to bouyon, to Guadeloupe or to Lewismelo. None of those occurrences belongs to this file, and this profile keeps none of them. The only occurrence kept is the one whose network cross checks with the scene documented by this site: Lewismelo, Le Bluff, Leyou and the published writers of the record.

The limits of the Tedla file are total outside his credit line, and they are stated here without detour. His real name is unknown, the writer credit Ted Clarin being publicly attached to neither of the two artist spellings, as are his island or town, his age, his beginnings and any other record. To date, not even a second occurrence of the name inside a music catalog is known. The Guadeloupe attachment used in this profile is that of his published scene, the Lewismelo album and its Guadeloupe guests, and not a biography of the artist. No official account was verified, so the sameAs field of the structured data of this page stays empty, as it should when nothing was opened and confirmed.

What is a single record profile worth, then? It is worth what credit accuracy is worth in a genre built on credits. Current bouyon gwada is a network music, where careers are read in the feature lines of the albums of others. Archiving Tedla under his own spelling, with his record, his partners, his writers and his open question, makes that line readable for the listener who will meet it on “Premières Notes”, and refuses the two symmetrical errors: erasing it by melting it into TedLa, or inventing it by lending it a life the sources do not give.

Listening fits inside one record: “VIN TCHEK NOU”, on “Premières Notes” by Lewismelo, to be heard between “5 KILOS” and “POMPÉ” to feel its team presentation place. The context is then read on our Le Bluff and Leyou pages, his partners on the record, on the Ted La page for the other spelling and its separate catalog, and on our bouyon Guadeloupe page for the whole branch of the genre. If a second credit under the name Tedla one day appears, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Tedla?

Tedla is an artist name credited once only in the catalogs consulted: as a feature on “VIN TCHEK NOU”, the third track of “Premières Notes”, the Lewismelo album dated November 1, 2024 on Deezer, alongside Le Bluff and Leyou. No biography, no artist page of his own and no other Tedla record were verified, and this profile says so openly.

What is the only verified Tedla record?

“VIN TCHEK NOU”, by Lewismelo featuring Le bluff, Leyou and Tedla, third track of “Premières Notes”, running 3 minutes 17 according to the Deezer listing opened. Its published writers and composers are Youri Ellapin, Keran Landre and Ted Clarin. No second recording under the Tedla spelling was found during this research.

Are Tedla and TedLa the same artist?

No source consulted says so, and none presents two different artists either. The two spellings share a published collaborator, LeYou on the TedLa singles and Leyou on “VIN TCHEK NOU”, inside the same Guadeloupe circuit, and the Tedla record counts a writer named Ted Clarin among its credits. This site therefore presents the junction as a strong but undemonstrated hypothesis, keeps two separate pages, including the Ted La page, and attributes every record to the spelling under which it was published.

Who wrote “VIN TCHEK NOU”?

The Deezer listing of the “Premières Notes” album publishes three writers and composers for this track: Youri Ellapin, Keran Landre and Ted Clarin. No source consulted says which writer name matches which guest voice, and this profile does not make that match in its place.

Has Tedla released a solo record or an album?

No, in the state of the sources consulted. No solo record, no EP and no album under the name Tedla is documented. His only verified trace is a feature inside the album of another artist, Lewismelo, which is the normal regime of the session voices of this scene.

Where can I hear Tedla?

On “VIN TCHEK NOU”, the third track of “Premières Notes” by Lewismelo, alongside Le Bluff and Leyou, best heard between “5 KILOS” and “POMPÉ” to feel its place as the team presentation record. For the other spelling and its distinct catalog, the Ted La page documents the 2022 singles with LeYou.

What does “Vin tchek nou” mean?

In the Creole of the French Antilles, “vin tchek nou” reads as an invitation: come and see us, come check us, come meet us. The title fits its place on the album, that of a presentation record where Lewismelo shows his circle, with three guest voices, Le Bluff, Leyou and Tedla, before unrolling the collaborations one by one.

What is the Lewismelo album “Premières Notes”?

The album that carries Tedla's only credit, under the Lewismeloproduction banner, dated November 1, 2024 on the Deezer listing, with eight songs. After “INTRO”, “5 KILOS” brings together Admiral T and Shanika, then come “VIN TCHEK NOU”, “POMPÉ” with Dcamp and KEKS MAFIA, “ALOUETTE” with Lejuh, “KEY” with Virtuose and “HULLABALOO” with Jixels.

Who sings with Tedla on “VIN TCHEK NOU”?

Lewismelo, the lead artist of the record, and two other guest voices, Le Bluff and Leyou. Le Bluff is credited on several records of the same circuit, while Leyou shares with Tedla the status of a single trace voice, at least under that spelling. The record runs 3 minutes 17 and credits no separate producer on the listing opened.

Is Tedla the Ethiopian singer of “Tedla Gualu”?

No, nothing links these names. Tedla is an attested Ethiopian and Eritrean first name, and a search returns for example an Ethiopian song titled “Tedla Gualu”, with no link to bouyon, Guadeloupe or Lewismelo. The only Tedla of this profile is the one whose network cross checks with the scene documented here: Lewismelo, Le Bluff, Leyou and the record's writers.

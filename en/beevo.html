Beevo: the name that appears twice in the bouyon mixes of Dominica

Published October 20, 2026

Beevo is an emerging bouyon name whose verified public trace, to date, fits in two DJ tracklist lines: “Doe do me Dat”, with Trixx, in the pre-carnival selection of the DJWILLYWONKA FACTORY, and “Fall In De Trap”, with Trixx and Freddy, in the Dj Close Connections podcast, two mixes built around the bouyon and soca scene of Dominica. This profile says exactly that, no more and no less: no invented biography, no assumed origin, no solo track the sources do not show. It is a short profile, owned as such, until the artist publishes a record that allows it to be completed.

Who Beevo is: what the sources say, and what they do not say

Start with the honesty of the record. In October 2026, there is, in the open sources consulted for this profile, no Beevo biography, no reference artist page, no interview and no solo discography. The name appears only as a collaborator, twice, alongside the same artist, Trixx, in DJ selections centred on Dominica. Everything this profile states comes from those two appearances and from the verifiable context of the mixes carrying them. The rest is presented as unknown, because it is unknown.

That situation is not exceptional in current bouyon. Part of the new Dominican scene circulates first through DJ mixes, shared riddims and collaborations between young artists, before existing in the form of official pages and press releases. Names that Roseau audiences hear in carnival selections can stay almost invisible to online research for months. Beevo is, at the date of this profile, in that case: a name DJs play, which documentation has not yet caught up with.

This profile therefore refuses two mirror errors. The first would be to assign Beevo the facts of a namesake: platform statistics sites list other artist entries under the name Beevo, and under neighbouring names, with no source connecting them to the Dominican mixes cited here. None of those entries is used in this record. The second error would be to invent an origin: Beevo appears in Dominica mixes, alongside Dominican artists, which places his circulation, not his birth. This site does not turn a tracklist presence into a certificate of origin.

The format of this page follows from that finding. Where complete profiles on this site line up biography, dated discography and event dates, the Beevo profile lines up two verified tracks, their mix contexts and a method. It is shorter than the site standard, and it says so in its introduction as in this section. If Beevo releases a solo track, an official page or a biography, this profile will be rewritten and completed from those sources, as the site does for every artist on the scene, from Reo to Asa Bantan.

“Doe do me Dat”: Beevo with Trixx in the DJWILLYWONKA FACTORY selection

The first verified Beevo appearance is a tracklist line. The DJWILLYWONKA FACTORY podcast publishes a selection announced as a treat for its listeners before the carnival season kicks off, built on the soca and bouyon of the Dominican and Caribbean scene. At position ten of that selection stands “Doe do me Dat”, credited to Trixx and Beevo. The spelling of the title is kept here exactly as the tracklist publishes it, with no silent correction.

The neighbourhood of that line places the track better than any commentary. The same selection lines up “WDMA” by Ridge with King Kembe, “Someone Else” by Litle Boy, Quan and Trilla G, “Stronger Remix” by Signal Band with Nice, “Da Man Don’t Cheat” by Triple K, “Not on my Head” by Asa Bantan, “Doh trust dem” and “Still Love Me” by Reo, and “Leave Me Alone” by 1T1 with Dinho, Lejuh, Theomaa and Binksdrick. Beevo therefore stands there, with Trixx, among the names carrying current Dominican bouyon, in a mix made for the island carnival audience.

This track must also be distinguished from a neighbouring title with almost identical words, because the confusion is easy and this site does not let it pass. The tracklist of another mix, the Dj Close Connections podcast, credits Khallion and Reo with a “Rum Dat Do Me Dat”. That is not the same recording as “Doe do me Dat” by Trixx and Beevo: neither the same artists nor the exact same title. The two lines coexist in the documentation of the scene, and this profile keeps them separate, each with its published performers.

No published release date, label or production credit was found for “Doe do me Dat” in the open sources consulted. This profile therefore assigns it none. The track appears in the structured data on this page as one of two verified Beevo recordings, with its published co-performer, Trixx. That is little, but it is exact, and in a scene where tracks often circulate without a complete credit roll, a tracklist line signed by a DJ of the scene is a real trace, which this site prefers to document soberly rather than ignore.

“Fall In De Trap”: Beevo with Trixx and Freddy in Dj Close Connections

The second verified appearance confirms the first and widens it. The Dj Close Connections podcast publishes a long soca and bouyon selection carrying “Fall In De Trap”, credited to Trixx, Freddy and Beevo. The Beevo name therefore returns a second time, still alongside Trixx, and this time with a third artist, Freddy, who also appears elsewhere in the tracklists of the Dominican scene, for example alongside Quan and Litle Boy on other tracks in these same mixes.

The context of this mix is the same world as the first appearance, widened to the Caribbean of Carnival 2026. The selection lines up “Make Her Bawl” by Shelly, Trixx and DJ Taffy, “Take Me As I Am” by Trilla G, “La Vie Doux” by Reo and Tehilah, “Bouwey” by 1T1 and Theomaa, “Superstar” by Faithii, “Se Miimii” by Miimii KDS, tracks by Asa Bantan, Signal Band and TK International, and major Trinidad soca names such as Machel Montano, Bunji Garlin and Nailah Blackman. In that set, “Fall In De Trap” is one of the Dominican lines, and Beevo one of the young names it carries.

Two appearances, twice with Trixx: that is the central pattern of the Beevo record. It draws an artist who works, at minimum, in regular collaboration with Trixx, to the point of appearing twice alongside him in two different selections. This profile reports that pattern as a tracklist fact. It turns it neither into an official duo, nor into a label signing, nor into any contractual relation, because no source documents those elements. The difference between a repeated pattern and an official structure is exactly the kind of distinction this site maintains, including for the smallest records.

As with “Doe do me Dat”, no published date, label or production credit roll was found for “Fall In De Trap”. The track is kept in the structured data on this page, with its published co-performers, Trixx and Freddy. Together, the two tracks form the whole verified Beevo discography as of 20 October 2026: two collaborations, zero documented solo tracks. This profile says it plainly, because a reader searching for Beevo deserves to know whether platform silence comes from incomplete research or from a genuinely thin record. Here, after research, the record is thin.

The context: the mix scene where the Beevo name circulates

To understand why this site opens a page for an artist with two tracklist lines, the role of mixes in Dominican bouyon has to be understood. The genre was born in Roseau, Dominica, in 1988, with WCK, and it has lived since in an ecosystem where the DJs of the scene and its diaspora are central passers: their pre-carnival selections assemble, each season, the established tracks and the new names, and decide in practice what the fete audience will hear. Appearing in those selections, alongside Signal Band, Triple K or Reo, is a real form of circulation, prior to any documentation.

The two mixes carrying Beevo illustrate that system. The DJWILLYWONKA FACTORY builds its selection as a pre-carnival meeting point for the Dominican scene. Dj Close Connections assembles a wider selection, Dominican and Trinidadian, where young Roseau names stand next to soca stars. In both cases, the logic is that of the fete: a DJ lines up what works for his audience, without worrying whether each name already has a reference page. Audiences then search for the names they heard, and this page exists for those audiences.

Beevo therefore stands in a generation of emerging bouyon names this site documents as their records fill out. Some, like Reo, already have nominations, dated tracks and documented festival stages. Others, like Beevo, still have only the trace of the mixes. Between the two, the method does not change: only sourced facts are published, attributed when they must be, and gaps are named as gaps. The bouyon playlist of this site follows the same discipline for listening.

This page therefore also has an open call function, owned as such. If Beevo, Trixx or their circle publish a biography, a solo track or complete credits for “Doe do me Dat” and “Fall In De Trap”, this profile will be updated with those verified elements, as the site updates all its profiles when documentation advances. Until then, it stays what it must be: the exact, dated and sourced trace of a name circulating in the bouyon of Dominica in 2026.

Beevo in the bouyon landscape: a place to build, a record to follow

Bouyon today has several circulation routes, and Beevo route is the oldest one in the genre: the DJ selection. Bouyon soca, started in Dominica in 2003 by Dada according to the first-hand testimony of Sean “Smokiller” Francis and popularised by Triple Kay, travelled through riddims and big stages. Wrecketeng, the official spelling given by DJ Cut, travelled through sampling and rap, while reketeng remains the misspelling of social media. Other names travel through certifications or awards. Beevo travels through tracklists, like generations of Dominican fete artists before him, from the era when the genre passed from fete to fete before platforms.

It would be easy, and it would be a fault, to inflate this record with the general context of the genre, as if the history of WCK or that of the Roseau carnival belonged to Beevo biography. They do not belong there. They only explain the world in which his two tracks circulate. This profile therefore keeps context in its place, in this section and the previous one, and the personal record in its own: two verified collaborations, one recurring partner named Trixx, a third name, Freddy, on the second track, and no other claim.

Why Beevo matters, at this modest stage: because the tracklists of scene DJs are one of the rare places where the Dominican next generation becomes visible before platforms, and because documenting those appearances early, with their limits, prepares what follows. The day Beevo releases a track in his own name, this page will already hold the trace of his public beginnings: “Doe do me Dat” with Trixx, “Fall In De Trap” with Trixx and Freddy, in the mixes that circulate the bouyon of Dominica. Start with those two tracks, continue with the artists surrounding them in the same selections, from Litle Boy to Asa Bantan, and come back to this page: it will grow with the record, fact by fact. Find the general context in our history of bouyon.

Frequently asked questions

Has Beevo released a solo track?

No solo Beevo track was found in the open sources consulted in October 2026. His verified discography is limited to two collaborations: “Doe do me Dat”, with Trixx, and “Fall In De Trap”, with Trixx and Freddy, both spotted in tracklists of DJ mixes from the Dominican scene.

Where is Beevo from?

His origin is not documented by the open sources consulted. Beevo appears in mixes built around the bouyon and soca scene of Dominica, alongside Dominican artists such as Reo, Asa Bantan and Signal Band. This site therefore places his circulation in that scene, without assigning him a birthplace or a commune that no source confirms.

Who is Trixx, the recurring Beevo partner?

Trixx is the artist alongside whom Beevo appears on both his verified tracks, “Doe do me Dat” and “Fall In De Trap”. Trixx also appears, in the same mixes, on tracks such as “Make Her Bawl”, with Shelly and DJ Taffy. This profile reports those tracklist credits without turning them into an official duo, which no source documents.

Are “Doe do me Dat” and “Rum Dat Do Me Dat” the same track?

No. “Doe do me Dat” is credited to Trixx and Beevo in the DJWILLYWONKA FACTORY selection. “Rum Dat Do Me Dat” is credited to Khallion and Reo in the Dj Close Connections podcast selection. The titles look alike, but the published performers are different, and this site keeps them separate.

Why is the Beevo profile shorter than the other profiles on this site?

Because his verified record is thin: two collaboration appearances in DJ tracklists, with no biography, no official page and no documented solo track in October 2026. This site prefers a short and exact profile to a portrait filled with unverifiable detail. The profile will be completed as soon as verified sources on Beevo are published.

Is the Beevo on platform statistics sites the same artist?

No consulted source allows that to be stated. Statistics sites list artist entries under the name Beevo, with no established link to the Dominican mixes documenting “Doe do me Dat” and “Fall In De Trap”. This profile does not merge those entries with the tracklist artist, and its structured data carries no unverified official link.

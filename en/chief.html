Who Chief is: one name, three posts, a single title

The exact inventory must be set out from the start, because this whole profile follows from it. Chief is the artist of record on “Strictly Bouyon 2027”, a video published on YouTube under the heading “Chief- Strictly Bouyon 2027”. The description of the page, opened in full during this research, credits him in so many words: “Written & Composed - Chief”, written and composed by Chief. He therefore holds, on this record, the two posts that anchor a work over time, the headline performance and the authorship of the piece, the writing of the words as much as the composition of the music, the page drawing no further distinction between the two gestures and this profile drawing none either.

That double credit sets Chief apart from thin file artists who appear only as a guest voice on the record of another artist. Here the title carries his name alone in the headline billing, with no announced feature, no other artist at the same level, and the writing and composition line names him alone. In the grammar of bouyon credit rolls, where records often circulate in teams, duos or crossed features, “Strictly Bouyon 2027” therefore presents itself as a writer’s record: a work signed from end to end by the artist who sings it, surrounded by a manufacturing team named post by post. Our bouyon songs page follows these releases record by record, through the credit rolls they publish.

What the file does not contain must be stated with the same clarity. The opened page gives no biography, no origin, no date of birth, no official photograph and no link to a confirmed artist account. No other primary page under the name of Chief, tieable to this precise chain of credits, was opened during this research: no second title, no artist page, no interview. This profile is therefore a credit roll profile in the full sense: it fixes, for one name and one title, who did what, with the names of every collaborator published by the source, and it stops exactly where that source stops.

The name itself must be read as a sign, not as a ranking. “Chief” is a common English word, denoting a leader, an authority, and stage names built on this kind of word are frequent in urban and Caribbean music, where they display a posture before the first listen. This site therefore treats it as a proper name, the one the page of the record publishes, and not as a title in a bouyon hierarchy: no rank, no crown and no place in any order of the scene is either stated or suggested by this profile, for want of a source that sets them out and because artist titles belong to appreciation, not to established fact.

Strictly Bouyon 2027: the title, the vintage and the genre claim

The title itself is the first information in the file. “Strictly Bouyon 2027” joins a promise, strictly, wholly, entirely, and a label, bouyon, followed by a vintage, 2027. The heading of the opened page reads “Chief- Strictly Bouyon 2027”, with no format mention, neither audio nor video clip nor feature, and the description repeats the same heading on its first line before unrolling the credits. The genre is therefore not deduced by this site from listening: it is claimed by the page itself, in the very name of the piece, which connects Chief to this site with no interpretive detour.

The 2027 vintage deserves a pause, because it states a positioning and not a release date. This page was opened on October 20, 2026, and it publishes no precise release date: the 2027 of the title is a season billing, the way part of the bouyon scene tags its new releases for the cycle to come, the one that DJs, playlists and listeners sort by year. This profile therefore does not turn that vintage into a calendar: it records that an artist named Chief filed his release under the bouyon banner of 2027, in the new releases slot that our bouyon playlist follows from one vintage to the next.

The description ends with three English hashtags, #newsong, #youtubemusic and #trending, new song, YouTube music and trending. They are search tags laid down by the page, not facts: they say that the release is looking for the new releases audience on the platform, and nothing more. This profile notes them because they are part of the opened document, but it converts them into neither success, nor chart placing, nor audience, no figure being published in the source consulted. A full credit roll, a billed vintage, three platform tags: that is the entire frame of the release, and it is published here as it stands.

The word “Strictly” calls for the same reading discipline. Set at the head of the title, it promises a bouyon with no blend, held entirely inside the genre, and that promise is part of the billing: it is the positioning the artist gives his release, on the same footing as the vintage. This site reproduces it as an element of the heading, and not as a certificate delivered after listening: no sound analysis documented by a source describes the piece, its possible borrowings or its purity of genre, and this profile therefore settles none of that. It records that “Strictly Bouyon 2027” presents itself as bouyon, nothing but bouyon, and that this presentation is the public fact around which the whole file is organised.

The full credit roll, post by post

The richness of this thin file is its credit roll, and it reads as a manufacturing chain in five posts. First post, the writing and the composition, held by Chief alone, as seen above. Second post, the riddim: the description credits it to ShakoProduction, in the form “Riddim Produced By @ShakoProduction”. The riddim is the instrumental loop over which the voice is laid, the rhythmic matter of bouyon, which our page on the bouyon riddim presents for itself. That the page distinguishes the riddim from the general production is a precise credit choice, and this profile respects it: ShakoProduction is credited with the riddim, and with nothing else here.

Third post, the production: “Produced By Skyobeats”. The page therefore separates two gestures that many credit rolls blur together, the making of the riddim, entrusted to ShakoProduction, and the production of the record, entrusted to Skyobeats. Fourth post, the finishing: “Mixed & Mastered @bouyonkitchen3111 Studios”, the mixing and mastering are credited to bouyonkitchen3111 Studios, a studio name that carries the word bouyon in its own sign. Fifth post, the supporting voices: “Background Vocals Camisha Rolle & Siicka Dan”, the background vocals are credited to Camisha Rolle and to Siicka Dan, two names published by the source, on the same footing as all the others, and reproduced by this profile exactly as they appear there.

Each of these names must be read at its right measure. They are collaborators on this record, credited on this page, and none of them is presented here as an artist of this site: no standalone release by ShakoProduction, Skyobeats, Camisha Rolle or Siicka Dan was opened for this profile, and no biography is lent to any of them. Their presence establishes a fact of working network around Chief: his record is not a file filed with no apparatus, it is a complete session, with a riddim commissioned or chosen, a production, a named finishing studio and credited background vocals. For an artist with no published biography, that chain is the only verifiable form of context, and it stands as such: it places the recording, never the person.

The very order of the credit roll in the source deserves to be kept in black and white, because platform descriptions are often rewritten or cut over time. After the writing and composition line of Chief, the page lists the riddim by ShakoProduction, then the mixing and mastering by bouyonkitchen3111 Studios, then the production by Skyobeats, then the background vocals by Camisha Rolle and Siicka Dan. The post by post presentation above reorganises that chain in manufacturing order, riddim, production, finishing and background vocals, without changing any name or any post. If the description were to be changed on the platform, it is this state of the credit roll, read on October 20, 2026, that this profile would go on citing as its source.

A heavily shared name: the namesake method and the credit roll anchor

The most fragile point of the file is the name itself. “Chief” is one of the most shared stage names in the musical world: artists, producers and groups carry it across every platform and every genre, with no connection between them. A search on that name alone returns a mass of unusable results, and none of them can be tied to “Strictly Bouyon 2027” without a verifiable shared element. The rule applied here is therefore strict: only what attaches to the opened chain of credits enters this profile, Chief as writer and composer, riddim by ShakoProduction, production by Skyobeats, finishing by bouyonkitchen3111 Studios, background vocals by Camisha Rolle and Siicka Dan. Everything else is namesake material, and it is kept out, entirely.

That caution has a direct consequence for the structured data of the page: no official link is entered in it. The opened YouTube page is the page of the record, not an artist page verified as his by opening, and no social media account, no website and no other platform for Chief was confirmed during this research. Entering a namesake account in the identity links of an artist would be a file error, and this site prefers an empty field to a false link. If Chief publishes an artist page, an account claimed in the description of a next record or a second credit roll consistent with this one, those elements will be verified by opening and added to this file, which will then be redated.

The context of the genre, for its part, is documented on this site and it explains why such a credit roll has its place here. Bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s, and bouyon soca was started in Dominica in 2003 by Dada, according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer and a participant in that era. A generation later, the genre also lives in these full credit roll releases published directly on YouTube, where a writer and composer lays his voice over the riddim of a named producer, has the record produced and finished by named hands, and files the whole under a vintage. Chief is, to date, exactly that in the opened sources: a writer’s name on one of these releases, and this profile fixes it before the credit roll gets lost in the flow of new releases.

The limits of the file, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they form half of its information. Single source: one YouTube page opened in full on October 20, 2026, its heading, its description and its credit roll. No origin is named in it: not Dominica, not Guadeloupe, not Martinique, not any other island or country, and this site assigns Chief none. No biography, no interview, no precise release date, no label and no verified second title completes the file. Even the language of performance of the record is not described by the page, and this profile describes neither the voice, nor the flow, nor the theme of “Strictly Bouyon 2027” beyond what its heading and its credits publish.

The verified scope therefore fits into one title and one complete chain. “Strictly Bouyon 2027”: written and composed by Chief, riddim produced by ShakoProduction, production by Skyobeats, mixing and mastering by bouyonkitchen3111 Studios, background vocals by Camisha Rolle and Siicka Dan. Nothing else is stated in this profile, and nothing else should be lent to it: no path, no scene of belonging, no coming project. Any result carrying the name of Chief with no demonstrable link to this precise credit roll, same collaborators, same studio, same riddim, should be read as a namesake until proof to the contrary, and aggregators, consulted where relevant for discovery only, are cited nowhere as sources.

The listening, while waiting for what comes next, fits into one title: “Strictly Bouyon 2027”, to be heard as the entire document of the file, the voice and the pen of Chief over the riddim of ShakoProduction, in the production of Skyobeats and the finishing of bouyonkitchen3111 Studios, with the background vocals of Camisha Rolle and Siicka Dan. If a second title appears with a consistent credit roll, if an artist page opens, if an origin is published in a primary source, this page will grow and will be dated again, like every page on this site. In the meantime, it publishes what the source gives, no more and no less: a bouyon writer and composer of record, a full credit roll, and a shared name that only this chain of credits makes it possible to identify.

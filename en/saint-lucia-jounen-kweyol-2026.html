Bouyon music: Jounen Kweyol 2026 in Saint Lucia

By Smokiller · Published October 30, 2026

Five days before Dominica, Saint Lucia held its own Jounen Kweyol: on Sunday 25 October 2026, the last Sunday of October, the island closed its Creole Heritage Month with its great national day of Kweyol, organised by the Folk Research Centre under the theme Kweyol-la Se Sa Nou Tout, Kweyol belongs to all of us.

Three official venues, an island in madras

Travel and Tour World gives the three venues of 25 October: La Clery Playing Field, La Caye in Dennery South and Vieux Fort Recreational Park. The month had launched on 30 September at Laborie Market, followed on 1 October by the An ti Pawol Kweyol lecture at the Alliance Francaise in Castries.

Along the way, the programme lined up the Traditional Games Festival at Corinth Playing Field on 11 October and Solo Night, an evening of Kweyol music at Fond Assau Court on 17 October, per the same source. Writeups 24 describes the day itself: communities exhibiting their practices, language and costumes, with folk music, drums, quadrille dance, storytelling, crafts, Creole cuisine and madras dress, and green fig and salt fish among the dishes of the day.

Dennery, the other shore of bouyon

For readers of this site, one of the three venues counts double: La Caye stands in Dennery, the valley that gave its name to the Dennery Segment, the Saint Lucian sound that talks with Dominican bouyon across the channel.

On 25 October, Dennery did not only play its own feast: the French Guiana delegation TOE LOBIE performed there from 3 p.m., after its La Clery set from 1 p.m. to 2 p.m., per SASCA Online. Saint Lucia's Creole Day and the bouyon map read on the same page.

Guests from French Guiana and Guadeloupe

The Folk Research Centre welcomed two visiting delegations, SASCA Online reports: TOE LOBIE, from French Guiana, representing the Samaka community, and ECLATS D'OR DU GOSIER, from Guadeloupe, a member of the FREGAQ quadrille federation with about 110 members, carrying its quadrilles, biguine and waltz.

On 21 October, TOE LOBIE had visited the C.A.R.E. School in Odsan for traditional dances and demonstrations of embroidery and calabash engraving. On 25 October, ECLATS D'OR DU GOSIER played in Soufriere from 2:30 p.m. to 3 p.m., then in Vieux Fort from 4:15 p.m. to 5 p.m., and both groups met again on 28 October at the National Cultural Centre for International Creole Day.

A story that began on the radio, in 1983

The Saint Lucia Tourism Authority tells the birth: when International Creole Day was first marked on 28 October 1983, the island answered on the airwaves, with almost fifteen hours of Kweyol on national radio, news included.

A year later, on Sunday 28 October 1984, the first community celebration was held in Mon Repos: a mass in Kweyol led by Father Patrick Anthony, founder of the Folk Research Centre, and a tribute to folk singer Sesenne Descartes. Fond Assau took up the torch in 1985, the Folk Research Centre has led the feast since, and the founder, Monsignor Patrick Anthony, opened the 2026 edition by urging Saint Lucians to embrace their language as part of their identity, per Writeups 24.

Frequently asked questions

When did Saint Lucia celebrate Jounen Kweyol 2026?

On Sunday 25 October 2026, the last Sunday of October, at La Clery, La Caye in Dennery and Vieux Fort, closing the Creole Heritage Month organised by the Folk Research Centre.

What was the theme of Jounen Kweyol 2026 in Saint Lucia?

Kweyol-la Se Sa Nou Tout, Kweyol belongs to all of us, per Writeups 24 and Travel and Tour World. The month had launched on 30 September at Laborie Market.

Who were the foreign guests of the 2026 edition?

Two delegations hosted by the Folk Research Centre, per SASCA Online: TOE LOBIE from French Guiana, for the Samaka community, and ECLATS D'OR DU GOSIER from Guadeloupe, a quadrille group of about 110 members.

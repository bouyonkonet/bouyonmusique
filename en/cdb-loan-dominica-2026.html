Dominica: CDB approves a US$10 million loan

By Smokiller · Published November 7, 2026

The Caribbean Development Bank, the CDB, has approved a US$10 million loan to Dominica to support reforms in public financial management, debt sustainability, financial sector stability and climate resilience. The approval was announced on a Friday and reported on 3 November 2026.

A US$10 million loan, second and final part

The US$10 million loan is a policy-based loan. It is the second and final loan under a US$30 million programme: the first loan, of US$20 million, was disbursed in April 2025.

The programme is delivered in collaboration with the World Bank. It supports reforms that touch public finance, the financial sector and resilience to climate change.

The reforms supported

The reforms include digital tax administration, publication of a Medium-Term Debt Strategy, updated procurement procedures and standardised bidding documents, and modernising the legal framework for electronic payments.

They also include regulatory requirements for the Dominica Agricultural and Industrial Development Bank, as well as stronger disaster-response arrangements, climate governance, protection of natural assets, marine ecosystems and nature-based tourism and the blue economy.

Climate resilience at the centre

The climate half of the programme aims to strengthen disaster response and climate governance, and to protect the country's natural assets, its marine ecosystems and its nature-based tourism. For a hurricane-exposed island, these reforms are presented as protection as much as investment.

The loan also supports the blue economy, the sustainable use of the resources of the sea, alongside nature-based tourism. Ian Durant, Director of the Economics Department of the CDB, is quoted in the announcement of the loan.

What comes next for the programme

With this second loan, the US$30 million programme comes to an end: US$20 million disbursed in April 2025, then US$10 million approved in November 2026. The two loans form a single reform programme, delivered with the World Bank.

The programme covers public finance, debt, the financial sector and climate resilience. With this second and final part, the series of loans planned for these reforms is complete.

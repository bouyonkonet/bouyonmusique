DJMK Malachi: one name, one record, and tagging that says Dominica

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

DJMK Malachi is a bouyon artist known, at the time of this research, through a single verified release: “New Dance Moves”, published on YouTube under the full title “DJMK MALACHI - NEW DANCE MOVES BOUYON 2026 (OFFICIAL MUSIC)”. The release listing carries no biography, no production credits and no links to official accounts: it carries hashtags, a lot of hashtags, and one positioning line that presents the record as new Creole dance music for the 2026 season. Among those hashtags, several tie the record to Dominica: #dominicabouyon, #bouyondominica and #dominicacreole. That tie comes from the artist himself, in his own release listing, and no independent source consulted confirms it: this profile therefore presents it exactly as what it is, a claim by the artist, not a fact established by a third party. The rest of the file is thin, and it is written here without padding.

Who DJMK Malachi is: a stage name and almost nothing else public

The artist public name is DJMK Malachi. It is the form carried by the title of his official release, and the one repeated in the hashtags of his own listing: #djmkmalachi, #djmkmalachimusic and #djmkmalachiofficial sketch a complete stage identity, with its search variants, but with no text explaining it. No source consulted gives a full name, an age, a place of birth or residence, or the meaning of the initials DJMK. This profile guesses none of it: when an artist does not publish a biography, the page documenting him should say so, not invent one.

The research for this page also produced a useful warning. The name Malachi, alone or preceded by DJ, belongs to several other musicians with no connection to bouyon: a DJ from Providence, Rhode Island, presented by The Hype Magazine in 2021 as a pastor son trained in church; a British house producer, formerly one half of the duo Audiowhores, covered by DMC World; a South African trap soul singer interviewed by Texx and the City. None of those portraits mentions bouyon, Dominica or a record called “New Dance Moves”, and nothing connects any of them to DJMK Malachi. Mixing them up would be the classic music database error, where namesakes blend together as soon as a stage name is not anchored by an official page. This site does not do that: DJMK Malachi is documented here only from his own bouyon release.

What can be said about him therefore fits in one sentence, and it is fully sourced: DJMK Malachi is the artist of record on a bouyon record published on YouTube for the 2026 season, which he presents as Creole dance music and tags himself as Dominican. Everything else, his path, his collaborations, his live work, his other records, remains to be documented, and this page is built to be completed on the day the artist publishes more.

New Dance Moves: reading the release listing, word by word

The verified record is titled “New Dance Moves”. Its official YouTube page presents it as “DJMK MALACHI - NEW DANCE MOVES BOUYON 2026 (OFFICIAL MUSIC)”: the artist name first, the title next, then the genre and the year, bouyon 2026, and the official music tag. It is the only primary page opened for this artist during this research, and it alone feeds the structured data of this profile. No other record, no earlier single, no feature and no remix under his name was verified on a primary page during this research.

The release description deserves to be read as a document, because it is the only text the artist publishes with the record. It opens with the identity hashtags, #malachi2, #djmkmalachi, #djmkmalachimusic, #djmkmalachiofficial and #djmkmusic, then runs through the genre and season tags: #bouyon2026, #bouyon, #bouyonriddim, #bouyonmusic, #2026bouyon, #newbouyon and #newmusic2026. Two forms catch the eye of genre watchers: #instrubouyon, which suggests an instrumental colour, and #bouyonremix, which suggests remix work, but the listing does not say whether the record is an instrumental, nor which record it would remix. This profile flags both tags without deciding: a tag chosen by the artist is a search intention, not a technical sheet.

The description closes on one line in capitals, the only full sentence in the listing: “DJMK WE THENE NOW NEW CREOLE MUSIC DANCE NEW DANCE MOVES NEW CREOLE SEASON FOR 2026”. The original spelling, “THENE” for what reads as “the new”, is kept here exactly as printed, because it is part of the document. The message itself is clear: the artist presents himself as the bearer of a new Creole music, centred on dance and on new moves, for the new Creole season of 2026. The title says the same thing: “New Dance Moves”. The release therefore positions itself on the side of the dancefloor and the Creole festival season, which the hashtags #newdancemove, #newdancemoves and #creoledance confirm. The context of that positioning is read on our bouyon dance page, and the wave of the season releases on our new bouyon 2026 page.

The Dominica link: what the tagging says, and what it does not prove

Three hashtags in the listing tie the record to Dominica: #dominicabouyon, #bouyondominica and #dominicacreole. A fourth, #bouyonlouwanj, plays on the word louange, praise, in a bouyon setting, and the whole set draws a coherent claim: this record wants to stand as Dominican, Creole bouyon, made for the 2026 season. The nature of that evidence needs to be stated precisely. A hashtag is a declaration by the artist or by whoever publishes the video. It says how the artist wants his record to be found and filed. On its own, it does not say where the artist was born, where he lives, or where the record was made.

No independent source opened during this research speaks about DJMK Malachi: no Caribbean music press, no festival bill, no streaming platform page with a biography, no Dominican media article. The Dominica tie therefore rests, to date, only on the artist own tagging. This site files the profile among its artist pages while flagging exactly that, rather than printing an origin nobody has verified. The nuance matters all the more because Dominica is the home country of bouyon: tying a record to it invokes a lineage, and a lineage is documented, not decreed through hashtags, even when the hashtags come from the artist.

That said, the tagging is not nothing. In the current economy of bouyon, where records circulate first on YouTube and social platforms before any press coverage, choosing #dominicabouyon and #bouyondominica is a deliberate positioning move: the artist files his record inside the Dominican repertoire of the genre, the one our bouyon Dominica page documents record by record, and he does it at Creole season, when the attention of the island and its diaspora is turned towards new releases. If an independent source one day confirms that tie, or if the artist publishes a biography, this page will be updated and dated, like every page on this site.

A dance record in the bouyon 2026 season

The positioning of “New Dance Moves” also says something about the moment it appears in. The bouyon of 2026 is a genre of short records, turning riddims and dance steps that serve as signatures: a record called “New Dance Moves” and tagged #creoledance claims exactly that function, supplying the season with its movement. The listing names no specific step, no choreographer and no attached dance challenge, and this profile invents none: it records the stated intention, new moves, new season, and leaves it as an intention until a dance video or coverage documents it.

Two hashtags open leads that the future will settle. #bouyon2099 projects the artist name towards a distant future, like a series signature or a brand slogan, and #bouyonboss claims a boss status in the genre. On this site, titles and statuses of that kind are always attributed, never stated as facts: #bouyonboss is therefore read here as the self portrait the artist chooses for his search profile, not as an established rank in the scene. Likewise, #smartmusic, which closes the series, stays a tag with no definition in the listing, and no interpretation is lent to it.

For the listener, the essential stays simple: one record exists, it is public, its author files it under Dominican bouyon of 2026, and it can be heard in full on its official page. Our bouyon playlist follows these season releases as they are verified, and our bouyon riddim page explains the place of the riddim, which the listing invokes through its #bouyonriddim tag, in the making of the genre.

What the file does not say: the limits, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they are the most useful information it can give about DJMK Malachi today. No production, mixing or mastering credit appears in the opened listing of “New Dance Moves”, no label is named there, and no precise release date beyond the year 2026 shown in the title appears there. No official social media account is linked in the release description, and no separate artist page was confirmed by direct opening during this research: the structured data of this page therefore contains the release itself as its only reference link, with no invented social account.

No second record was verified. That does not mean none exists: it means that as of October 20, 2026, across the platforms and the music press consulted, only one record could be opened and confirmed as his. The distinction is the heart of this site method: an artist documented through one verified record is worth more than a catalogue rebuilt from aggregator lists, namesake pages or unopened search results. Aggregators serve discovery, never proof, and no record read only on an aggregator enters the structured data of this page.

While waiting for what comes next, the context of the genre is read on our bouyon soca page, which recalls the lineage of the sound: bouyon soca was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica, per the first hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born artist who lived those years from the inside. If the artist publishes a second record, credits or a biography, this profile will grow with him, and the modification date will say when.

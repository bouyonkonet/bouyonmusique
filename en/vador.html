Vador: a name that only exists in the credits

Published October 20, 2026

Vador is the most unusual profile on this site, and it says so in its first line: no source consulted documents a performing artist named Vador, with a solo catalog, a biography or an official account. The name exists publicly only in compound forms, in the credits of Guadeloupe bouyon: Vadore Concept and Vadore Concept Groupe, a production banner and label behind the Bouyon Kings between 2021 and 2025, and once, in 2025, in the exact English form VADOR CONCEPT GROUP, credited with producing “LEVRETTE”. This profile documents what those credits establish, record by record, from “Je l’ai vu”, a SNEP Gold single, to the Yellow Gaza records, and answers the question other profiles never ask: are the artist Vador and the Vadore Concept imprint one and the same entity?

The name Vador: one root, three credit forms

Let us start with the exact inventory, because the whole file fits inside it. The most frequent form is Vadore Concept Groupe, with a final e on Vadore: that is the label name on the platform pages, from 2021 to 2025. The short form Vadore Concept appears as the label of the Yellow Gaza single “Sa ki bon (Bouyon King)” in 2021, and in the production credits of the official “Je l’ai vu” video, where production is shared between Vadore Concept, Voodoo Studio, The Best Prod and DJ Raptor Martinique. The third form is the closest to the name of this profile: VADOR CONCEPT GROUP, in capitals and without the e, credited as producer on the page of “LEVRETTE”, the Yellow Gaza Girls single released on October 16, 2025.

No other occurrence of the name was found in the music this site documents. No Vador artist page on the platforms consulted, no record performed by a Vador, no poster, no interview. The other Vadors the search engines return belong to other worlds: a South African rapper documented by music statistics databases, and songs titled “Vador” in French rap, by Bojack or AmadeusMurdock, with no link to bouyon. This profile does not mix them: the Vador of this page is only the one in the Guadeloupe credits.

That scarcity does not stop the file from being solid, because the banner itself has a public, dated and coherent catalog. Vadore Concept Groupe is one of the houses that structure bouyon gwada, the Guadeloupe form of the bouyon born in Dominica. Its name returns on records by Holly G, Yellow Gaza and Yellow Gaza Girls, DJ Joe, Yeahman’C, Deejay Enzo, Edday and Anonymous G., almost always around 157 BPM, the dance tempo of this scene, and often under the Bouyon Kings banner. The name Vador therefore does not designate a documented performing career: it designates the root of a banner, and it is as a banner root that this profile documents it.

One hypothesis remains, and this profile states it as a hypothesis, without turning it into a fact. On this scene, banners often carry the name or nickname of their founder: a Vador, a person or a producer, may well stand behind Vadore Concept, as the 2025 production credit, in the name of VADOR CONCEPT GROUP, suggests. But no source consulted names that founder, photographs him or presents him. Until one does, Vador stays, in the public archive, a credits name and not a face.

“Je l’ai vu”: the banner Gold record

The record that gives the file its weight is “Je l’ai vu”, by Holly G and Arèndi, with VJ Ben on the video. Released on October 22, 2021 on Vadore Concept Groupe, in the Bouyon Kings Mixtape series, the record tells a nightclub meeting, between glances, dancing and Hennessy, over the modern bouyon of the Guadeloupe scene. The official video credits DJ Flip tha boss with composition, HollyG and Arèndi with writing, and a production shared between Vadore Concept, Voodoo Studio, The Best Prod and DJ Raptor Martinique. The biography published by Qobuz adds that the video passed 10 million views, and places Holly G in its territory: a family trio from Trois-Rivières and Vieux-Habitants, in Basse-Terre, Guadeloupe.

The recognition is institutional, and it can be checked in one line. The official certifications list of the SNEP, the French recorded music body, records “JE L’AI VU”, by Holly G and Arèndi, as a Gold single, with the label line “VADORE CONCEPT GROUPE / BELIEVE”, release date October 22, 2021 and certification date July 10, 2025, after three years and nine months. A French Gold record for a bouyon title released by a Guadeloupe banner of this size is a rare fact, and it writes the Vadore Concept name into the official register of French music, not only into platform metadata.

The record also circulates in the forms that make bouyon live: a Charles V remix appears on the “Shatta vs Bouyon Connexion” compilation, and a shatta and bouyon selection on the Quel-DJ site holds “Je l’ai vu” among its picks, describing it as an anthem to the nights where bouyon rhythms take control. For the banner, “Je l’ai vu” plays the role that “What She Like” plays for Trixzmigos: a single record that carries the name far beyond the rest of the catalog, and that makes the name searchable, therefore documentable.

It is also through this record that the banner name enters the wider story this site tells. Our bouyon in Guadeloupe page places this production inside the scene that adopted Dominican bouyon and remade it in its own way, between Basse-Terre, the studios and the Bouyon Kings compilations. Vadore Concept holds the role of a second generation house there: not the inventor of the genre, but one of the operators who industrialised it on the gwada side, with series, mixtapes and a certification to show for it.

The Vadore Concept catalog: Bouyon Kings, Yellow Gaza and the rest

Around the Gold record, the banner catalog can be rebuilt page by page. On the Yellow Gaza side, the most loyal universe of the house: “Sa ki bon (Bouyon King)”, a single of December 14, 2021, label Vadore Concept, composed by VADORE CONCEPT GROUPE and written by Letin Sylia and Letin Severine; “PLAN A 3”, with Yumei, on September 7, 2024, on Vadore Concept Groupe, in French; and “LEVRETTE”, by Yellow Gaza Girls, on October 16, 2025, again on Vadore Concept Groupe, with the production credit in the name of VADOR CONCEPT GROUP. Three records, three different roles for the same name: label, composer, producer. That is the signature of a house that builds the records as much as it publishes them.

The Bouyon Kings series gives the banner its compilation format. “Bouyon Kings Party”, by Yeahman’C, was released on May 1, 2022 on Vadore Concept Groupe, in the album “Bouyon Kings (Trouble Beat)”. The same day brought “La dose”, by Deejay Enzo and BOSSLO, on the same banner. On December 20, 2022, “Bad GayL”, by Anonymous G., appeared in the album “BOUYON KINGS II MIXTAPE (By Vadore Concept)”, the “by” wording pointing to a mixtape carried by the banner itself. And on February 5, 2023, “Bwé Rhum”, by DJ Joe featuring Asa Bantan, was released on Vadore Concept Groupe under the Bouyon Kings Mixtape tag, connecting the Guadeloupe series to one of the biggest voices of Dominican bouyon, Asa Bantan.

The catalog also reaches toward Dominica through another record, one the readers of this site already know. On February 26, 2022, “Backshot”, by SupaLyne featuring Trixz Migos and Kenny Gee, was released on Vadore Concept Groupe, in English and at 157 BPM. The same banner therefore published, in the same year, Guadeloupe records in French and a record of the new Dominican wave in English: the bouyon of both shores of the Caribbean Sea passes through the same small house. This profile crosses the Trixzmigos profile here, published in the same wave, for which “Backshot” is the first verified trace.

The more recent records confirm the continuity. On March 19, 2024, “Toto - Chaben - Monss”, by Edday featuring DJ Luchshiy, was released on Vadore Concept Groupe, at 160 BPM. In 2024 and 2025, the Yellow Gaza and Yellow Gaza Girls records extend the line, up to the production credit of “LEVRETTE”. In total, about ten verified records between October 2021 and October 2025 carry the banner name as label, and several carry it in addition as composer or producer. For a house with no official site found and no documented public communication, that is a substantial catalog, fully readable in the metadata.

157 BPM: the house sound signature

One detail runs through almost every catalog page, and it works as a signature. “Sa ki bon”, “Bouyon Kings Party”, “La dose”, “Bad GayL”, “Bwé Rhum”, “Backshot” and “PLAN A 3” all show 157 BPM on their Shazam pages. “LEVRETTE” and “Toto - Chaben - Monss” rise to 160 BPM. That regularity is no metadata accident: 157 BPM is the working tempo of the bouyon gwada of this generation, fast enough for carnival and party dancing, stable enough to chain records in a mix. A banner whose records share the same tempo across four years builds, even without declaring it, a mix compatibility that serves DJs as much as audiences.

The geography of the credits draws the rest of the signature. The artists are Guadeloupean on the French branch of the catalog, Holly G from Basse-Terre, Yellow Gaza, Yeahman’C, Edday, and Dominican or English speaking on the bridge records, SupaLyne, Trixz Migos, DJ Joe and Asa Bantan. The studios and partners named, Voodoo Studio, The Best Prod, DJ Raptor on the Martinique side, tell a French Antilles network to which the Dominican branch connects record by record. Vadore Concept therefore works like a node: small in appearance, but placed at a crossing where bouyon gwada meets the Dominican bouyon of origin.

That node role explains why this site documents the name even though no solo artist publicly carries it. The history of bouyon is not written with singers only: it is written with the houses that choose the records, pay for the studios, build the series and file the metadata. When those houses stay anonymous, their catalogs become unreadable and their records lose their context. Recording the Vadore Concept credits gives back to about ten records, including a Gold single, the address that connects them.

Artist or banner: what the sources allow anyone to say

The question put to this profile was direct: are the artist Vador and the Vadore Concept imprint the same entity, related entities, or two separate things? The honest answer, after research, holds in three points. First point: no source consulted documents a performing artist named Vador in bouyon, or anywhere else in Antillean music. No solo catalog, no stage, no biography. Second point: the name Vador, in its short form, appears only once, in the production credit of “LEVRETTE” in 2025, VADOR CONCEPT GROUP, where the label of the same record writes Vadore Concept Groupe with the e. Third point: every other occurrence is a banner form, label, collective composer or mixtape house.

What the sources therefore support, no more and no less, is this: Vador is the root of the name of a Guadeloupe production banner active from 2021 to 2025, and the short form of the name serves at least once as a production credit. To say that Vador is an artist separate from the banner would be to invent a performer. To say that Vador is the person who runs the banner would be to invent a founder. To say the two names have nothing to do with each other would be contradicted by the shared root and by the 2025 credit. This profile keeps the only wording the documents carry: one name, one banner, and a person or a team that the public archive does not name.

That caution has a precedent in the catalog of this site. The same method was applied to the songwriters Letin Sylia and Letin Severine, whose names also exist only in credits, and to the singer Hussey, who exists only in the Trixzmigos features. Current bouyon produces many of these credits names, invisible on posters and decisive in metadata. Documenting them for what they are, without inflating them into stars or dissolving them into footnotes, is exactly the work of this site.

If the person or team behind Vadore Concept one day publishes a name, a biography or an official account, this profile will be rewritten around that source, dated and corrected like every page on this site. In the meantime, listening runs through the banner catalog: “Je l’ai vu” for the commercial peak, the Yellow Gaza records for the loyalty of the house, the Bouyon Kings series for its format, and “LEVRETTE” for the only credit that carries the name of this page. The context is read on our women of bouyon and bouyon gwada pages, and in our bouyon playlist.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the artist Vador?

No source consulted documents a performing artist of that name. The name Vador appears publicly only in the compound forms Vadore Concept, Vadore Concept Groupe and, once, VADOR CONCEPT GROUP, credited with producing “LEVRETTE” in 2025. This profile therefore documents the name as the root of a Guadeloupe production banner, and not as a singing career, of which there is no verified public trace.

Are Vador and Vadore Concept the same thing?

That is the conclusion the documents support, with one precision. Every verified occurrence of the name belongs to the same family: the Vadore Concept or Vadore Concept Groupe label between 2021 and 2025, the VADORE CONCEPT GROUPE composition credit on “Sa ki bon” in 2021, and the VADOR CONCEPT GROUP production credit on “LEVRETTE” in 2025. No artist Vador separate from the banner appears in the sources. On the other hand, no source names the person or team behind the banner, and this profile does not invent one.

What is the biggest record released on Vadore Concept Groupe?

“Je l’ai vu”, by Holly G and Arèndi, released on October 22, 2021 in the Bouyon Kings Mixtape series. The official SNEP certifications list records it as a Gold single, certified on July 10, 2025, with the line “VADORE CONCEPT GROUPE / BELIEVE”. The biography published by Qobuz adds that its video passed 10 million views.

Which artists released records on Vadore Concept?

The verified pages name Holly G and Arèndi, Yellow Gaza and Yellow Gaza Girls, DJ Joe with Asa Bantan, Yeahman’C, Deejay Enzo and BOSSLO, Anonymous G., Edday with DJ Luchshiy, and SupaLyne with Trixz Migos and Kenny Gee. The banner therefore covers the Guadeloupe scene in French and, with “Backshot” in 2022, one record of the new Dominican wave in English.

Why are almost all the banner records at 157 BPM?

Because 157 BPM is the working tempo of the bouyon gwada of this generation, built for dancing and for chaining in a mix. The pages of “Sa ki bon”, “Bouyon Kings Party”, “La dose”, “Bad GayL”, “Bwé Rhum”, “Backshot” and “PLAN A 3” all show 157 BPM, and the recent records “LEVRETTE” and “Toto - Chaben - Monss” rise to 160 BPM. The regularity works as the house sound signature.

Where can I hear the Vadore Concept catalog?

Start with “Je l’ai vu”, the Gold record, then the Yellow Gaza records: “Sa ki bon (Bouyon King)”, “PLAN A 3” and “LEVRETTE”. Continue with the Bouyon Kings series, “Bouyon Kings Party”, “Bad GayL” and “Bwé Rhum”, and with “Backshot”, the bridge record toward Dominica. Our bouyon playlist and our bouyon gwada page place these records back in their scene.

Quan: the singer the credits also call Quan Dan and Quan Dawg

Published October 20, 2026

Quan is a bouyon singer whose voice audiences first know through the records of producer and singer Litleboy LSBeats767: “Pa ka tan / Man allu looking for” in 2022, “Someone Else” with Trilla G, “Leaving” in 2024 and “Fell For You” in 2025. The same voice reappears under other credit forms: Quan Dan in a music video, Quan Dawg in the big 2025 collaboration “Without You”. This profile does the work the platforms do not do: it follows the credits record by record, establishes what the sources allow us to say about those three names, tells the documented path of the singer, and states clearly where the public record stops. The record is thin, and this profile does not hide it.

Who Quan is: a voice, a writer, and a name in the credits

Quan first presents himself as a credit name. On the platforms, he appears as a featured artist on the records of Litleboy LSBeats767, and the metadata of those records gives his writer name: Quan Denis, spelled out in full, and once in the fuller form quan kyren denis. On “Fell For You”, released on 11 January 2025 on Lsbeats767, Shazam credits him as a performing artist on vocals, under the name Quan Denis, alongside Foster Marcel Xavier, the published civil name of Litleboy, and as a co-writer of the record. On “Pa ka tan / Man allu looking for”, released on 18 October 2022, he is already a co-writer, under the form quan kyren denis.

That precision matters, because it separates Quan from a mere session singer called in for a chorus. From record to record, over more than two years, he writes with the same partner, sings with him, and returns in his releases as a regular associate rather than a passing guest. The Summer Of Carnivals compilation, published on Qobuz, confirms the place of the duo in the landscape: its record “Someone Else”, by Litleboy with Quan and Trilla G as featured artists, sits there alongside carnival records from across the region, and its writing credits name Quan Denis alongside Foster Marcel Xavier and Gael Jno Baptiste.

What the sources do not give is just as clear, and this profile says it from the start: no published biography, no interview found, and no verified official profile was opened during this research. We therefore know neither his age, nor his home community, nor his path before 2020, the year of the earliest record documented here. This profile tells only what the credits, the videos and the official lists allow us to tell, and it flags the nature of the source each time. For a young bouyon artist, that is a frequent situation: the music travels faster than the documentation.

There is, however, a geographic anchor the sources do support. The video of “Kay Ve Yo”, his 2020 record with Bobby, publishes booking contacts including two numbers in 1-767, the Dominica area code, one of them presented as Quan own number. His main partner releases as Litleboy LSBeats767, where the 767 is part of the stage name, and the Dreamer World session that presents him later shows a dreamersworld767 contact. This profile therefore ties him to the bouyon scene of Dominica on the basis of those published credits, without inventing a town or a biography.

Quan, Quan Dan, Quan Dawg: what the sources allow us to conclude

The name question is the heart of this profile, because a reader searching for Quan meets at least three forms: Quan, Quan Dan and Quan Dawg, in addition to the writer name Quan Denis. Treating them as three different artists would be convenient and wrong. Treating them as one without showing the proof would be lazy. Here is the chain, record by record, exactly as the sources give it.

First link: Quan and Quan Dan are connected by a single record. “Leaving”, by Litleboy LSBeats767, released on 10 December 2024 on Lsbeats767, credits three writers on Shazam: Foster Xavier, Quan Denis and Tafari Maffei. The video of the same record is published under the billing Quan Dan x Litleboy x Taffy, Leaving Music Video, Bouyon 2025. The third name in the video, Taffy, matches the third writer in the credits, Tafari Maffei, whom this site knows as DJ Taffy. On this record, the short form in the video and the writer name in the credits therefore occupy exactly the same places: Quan Dan is there the stage form of the same singer the platforms credit as Quan Denis.

Second link: Quan Dan reappears with the same partner. The Dreamer World session published on YouTube presents “Early Morning Freestyle” under the name Quan Dan, with Litleboy and Mr Keyz. It is not a different duo: it is the same pair as elsewhere, Quan and Litleboy, under the Dan form. Bouyon mix listings also carry “Leaving” under the form Quan Dan, Litleboy and Taffy, proof that the DJs who programme the record use that form for the same recording too.

Third link: Quan and Quan Dawg are connected by the “Without You” video. The title of the video, published in 2025, announces Nice, Reo, Trilla G, Unstoppable Shax and Quan Dawg. The description of the same video lists the performers one by one, and Quan place there is written this way: Quan, with the account @quandawg_vi in brackets. The long name in the title and the short name in the description therefore designate the same performer, in the same place in the same collaboration. The keywords of the video also blend bouyon, the Virgin Islands and Dominica, and cite Someone Else and Kay Ka Fet, the known Quan records, as markers of the same world. The conclusion of this profile, limited to what the sources show: Quan, Quan Denis, Quan Dan and Quan Dawg form a single chain of credits for one singer. What the sources do not explain, on the other hand, is the reason for the suffix of the @quandawg_vi account, and this profile does not guess it.

The partnership with Litleboy: from Pa ka tan to Fell For You

The main thread of the documented discography of Quan is his partnership with Litleboy LSBeats767, the producer and singer whose published civil credits name Foster Marcel Xavier. The earliest duo record found is “Pa ka tan / Man allu looking for”, released as a single on 18 October 2022 on 3238844 Records DK2, at 159 BPM according to Shazam, with Quan as featured artist and co-writer. The associated video, published as Litleboy x Quan, Pa ka tan, Bouyon 2023, shows the record living on into the following year in bouyon programming.

The record that installs the duo at regional scale is “Someone Else”. Published in 2024 on the Summer Of Carnivals compilation on DSM Music and Monk Music, according to the Qobuz credits, it brings together Litleboy as main artist, Quan and Trilla G as guests, and it is written by Foster Marcel Xavier, Gael Jno Baptiste and Quan Denis. The DJs notice it: the record turns in the tracklists of the Miami Carnival 2024 mixes, of Prelude 2025 and of Bouyon Passport 2026, often right next to “Kay Ka Fet”, credited to Quan alone or to Litleboy and Quan depending on the list. Dominican recognition follows: the finalists list of the Dominica Music Awards 2025 holds “Quan, Litle Boy and Trilla G, Someone Else” among the finalists for Collaboration of the Year, the same category as “FYM” by Ridge and Problem Child.

The collaboration then continues at a steady rhythm. “Leaving”, released on 10 December 2024, brings together Litleboy, Quan under his Quan Dan form in the video, and DJ Taffy, with writing shared between Foster Xavier, Quan Denis and Tafari Maffei. “Fell For You” follows on 11 January 2025, still on Lsbeats767, at 160 BPM, with Quan Denis on vocals and in the writing. Mix listings add “Your Friend”, credited to Quan and Litleboy with Freddy as guest, in the Bouyon Mix 2025 selection. Taken together, those records draw a stable duo across three carnival seasons, which, in current bouyon, counts as a signature.

Quan place in that duo also needs noting: he is no sidekick there. The writing credits name him on every record, even when the video billing shortens his name. In a genre where featured singers are often interchangeable, that writer constancy is the most solid fact in the file, and it is what justifies a separate profile rather than a simple mention on his partner page. For the producer context, read the profile of Litleboy LSBeats767.

Before and around the duo: Kay Ve Yo, DJ Taffy and the other credits

The earliest documented Quan record does not come from the duo. “Kay Ve Yo”, published in 2020 with Bobby, is presented in its YouTube description as written and composed by Quan Denis and Bobby, set on the Yeki Riddim, produced by DJ Taffy and recorded at Bobos Records, with a cover photo by DJ Wizz. That early credit is precious: it shows Quan as a writer as early as 2020, already inside the circle of DJ Taffy, the producer who would reappear beside him on “Leaving” four years later. The description also gives the booking contacts of the time, with the 1-767 numbers already mentioned, and points to a channel presented as the official Quan channel.

The Taffy circle is confirmed by another credit. The “Nasty Christmas Medley” by DJ Taffy, released on 23 November 2023 on Riddim Beast Studios, lists Quan Denis among its writers, and its video is billed under the names of DJ Taffy and Quan, with other voices alongside. A third credit widens the circle further: “Fete Life”, by DeLuxe F, with Quan and Fuego Dinero as guests, credits Quan Denis in the writing alongside Dean Desiree. Those records do not make Quan a star, but they show a singer integrated into a Dominican production network, called on as a writer as much as a voice.

His solo record most cited by DJs is “Kay Ka Fet”. Mix listings programme it sometimes under the name of Quan alone, sometimes under the form Litleboy and Quan, which reflects the same billing grey zone as the rest of his file. The record turns in the Miami Carnival 2024 mixes, in Prelude 2025 right after “Someone Else”, and in Bouyon Passport 2026. For an artist with no published biography, that repeated presence in the programming of diaspora DJs is a form of documentation in itself: it proves the records circulate, season after season, far beyond their island.

The Dreamer World session completes this recent picture. Published on YouTube, it presents “Early Morning Freestyle” by Quan Dan, with Litleboy and Mr Keyz, as the first session of a series devoted to Caribbean talent, with a 767 contact. It shows the same Quan, under his Dan form, in a performance format rather than a single: one more piece for the chain of names, and a sign that his circle is working to present him as an artist in his own right, not only as his partner guest.

Without You: the big collaboration of 2025

The record that exposes Quan to the widest audience is “Without You”, published on video in 2025 under the names of Nice, Reo, Trilla G, Unstoppable Shax and Quan Dawg. The video description details the making: a song performed by the five artists, produced by A-Plus Muzik, mixed and mastered by Th3rd House Studios, video by A.Nibbs. Each performer is listed there with an account, and Quan account is @quandawg_vi, the form that links his short name to his Dawg name, as this profile showed above.

The company matters. Nice and Reo are among the rising bouyon voices from Dominica followed by this site, Trilla G is one of the most documented singers of the new generation, and the A-Plus Muzik production places the record in the professional circuit of the genre, the circuit of records built to last beyond one season. That Quan holds one of the five places there, under his Dawg name, says more about his real standing in the scene than any press release: he belongs to the group of singers producers gather when they build a collective record.

The keywords of the video also tell the geography of bouyon in 2025: bouyon, soca, the US Virgin Islands, Guadeloupe cited through Gwada, Dominica, and a run of scene names, from Ridge to Triple Kay by way of DJ Taffy, DJ MJ and Signal Band. This profile does not turn those tags into a biography: they prove the intended circulation of the record, not the address of its singers. They do show, in any case, that “Without You” was published to travel across the whole bouyon space, and that Quan travels with it.

After “Without You”, the documented file stops for now at the Dreamer World session and at the mix programming of 2025 and 2026, where “Someone Else”, “Kay Ka Fet” and “Leaving” keep turning. That is little, and it is exactly why this profile exists in this form: to fix what is established, to name what is not, and to leave the page ready to grow when the next credits appear. In the meantime, listening runs through our bouyon playlist, where the duo records with Litleboy sit alongside the rest of the new scene.

What this profile does not know, and why it says so

This profile is deliberately shorter than those of artists documented by years of press, and it owns that difference. For Quan, the open sources during this research are platform credits, video descriptions and mix and awards lists. They establish a discography, a partnership, a circle of producers and a chain of names. They establish neither a birth date, nor a community, nor a training, nor an intention. Anything that looked like a classic biography would therefore be invented, and this site does not publish invented biography.

Three precise questions stay open, and this profile asks them rather than filling them. First question: why do the credits alternate between Quan, Quan Dan and Quan Dawg? The records link the forms to each other, but no source explains the choice, nor whether Dan is a shortcut, a second stage name or a simple billing habit. Second question: what does the suffix of the @quandawg_vi account published in the “Without You” video mean? The keywords of the video cite the Virgin Islands, but no document says whether Quan lives there, works there or aims at an audience there. Third question: is there a solo project to come under his name? Recent programming announces none that this profile can verify.

A last limit concerns links. No official profile of Quan was verified by directly opening a page during this research: the account published in the “Without You” description is quoted in the text, as a credit, but the structured data of this page does not link it, and the sameAs field stays empty. If an official profile is confirmed, this page will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

What remains is the essential, which is musical. Between 2020 and 2025, Quan wrote and sang on a run of records that matter in recent bouyon: “Kay Ve Yo”, “Pa ka tan”, “Someone Else”, a finalist at the Dominica Music Awards, “Leaving”, “Fell For You” and “Without You”. Few artists of his generation can line up an awards finalist collaboration and a place in a collective record produced by A-Plus Muzik. The file is thin, the trace is real, and it is that trace this profile keeps.

Frequently asked questions

Are Quan and Quan Dawg the same artist?

Yes, according to the Without You video itself, published in 2025. Its title announces Quan Dawg among the performers, and its description lists the performers one by one, writing Quan with the account @quandawg_vi in the same place. The long name and the short name therefore designate the same singer in the same collaboration, alongside Nice, Reo, Trilla G and Unstoppable Shax.

Are Quan and Quan Dan the same artist?

The credits link the two forms through a single record. Leaving, released on 10 December 2024, credits Quan Denis among its writers on the platforms, while the video of the same record is published under the name Quan Dan, with Litleboy and Taffy. The Dreamer World session then presents Early Morning Freestyle under the name Quan Dan, still with Litleboy, his usual partner.

What is the full published name of Quan?

Platform writing credits publish Quan Denis, and the record Pa ka tan / Man allu looking for, released in 2022, gives the fuller form quan kyren denis as a writer. It is under that name that he is credited as writer and singer on Fell For You in 2025 and as a writer on Someone Else and Leaving.

What are the best known records of Quan?

The most documented are Someone Else, with Litleboy and Trilla G, a finalist for Collaboration of the Year at the Dominica Music Awards 2025, Pa ka tan / Man allu looking for in 2022, Leaving in 2024, Fell For You in 2025, Kay Ka Fet, programmed by many DJs, and Without You in 2025, where he is credited as Quan Dawg.

Where does Quan come from?

No published biography says, and this profile does not invent an answer. The published elements tie him to the Dominica scene: the Kay Ve Yo video in 2020 publishes booking contacts in 1-767, the Dominican area code, including a number presented as his own, his main partner is Litleboy LSBeats767, and his documented producers, DJ Taffy and A-Plus Muzik, belong to that scene.

Where can I hear Quan?

Start with Someone Else and Fell For You with Litleboy LSBeats767, then Without You with Reo and Trilla G. Our bouyon playlist follows the records of the new Dominican scene, including those of Quan season after season.

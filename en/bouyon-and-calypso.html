Bouyon and calypso, the Trinidadian root

By Smokiller · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Bouyon was not born from nothing. Before it, Dominica sang chante mas, and the whole Caribbean sang calypso. Born in Trinidad in the 19th century, calypso is the song that tells the news, mocks the powerful and makes carnival dance. It fed cadence-lypso, then bouyon, and its contest, the Calypso Monarch, is still a living institution in Dominica.

Calypso, born in Trinidad

Calypso was born in Trinidad and Tobago, among Afro-Trinidadians, in the early to mid 19th century. Its rhythms trace back to West African kaiso, and to the arrival of French planters and their slaves from the French Antilles in the 18th century. It spread across the Antilles by the mid 20th century. The first calypsos were sung in French Creole by a griot, who became the chantuelle, then the calypsonian.

Calypso was a vehicle for political expression and protest against colonial rule, and it served as a people newspaper: news, gossip, satire and scandal. Modern calypso began in the 19th century as a fusion of the masquerade song lavway, the French Creole belair and the calinda stick-fighting chantwell. Its rise is tied to the adoption of Carnival by enslaved Trinidadians, to canboulay drumming and masquerade processions, and then to competitions, which grew after emancipation in 1834.

Chante mas, Dominica carnival song

Dominica had its own carnival song before calypso arrived: chante mas, or masquerade song, a 19th-century form. Revellers paraded for two days, with a lead vocalist, the chantwel, followed by a responsorial chorus, the lavwa, drummers and dancers dancing backwards in front of the drummer, on a tambou lele, with lapo kabwit drums, chak chak, cowbell, triangle, conch shell and horns. The lyrics, almost all in French Creole, dealt with gossip and scandal, and called out people shortcomings. Traditionally, women sang and men played the instruments.

By the early 1960s, imported calypso and Trinidadian steelpan had replaced chante mas and lapo kabwit as carnival music in Roseau. Calypso appealed because its taste for local news and gossip resembled chante mas. After a fire in 1963, the traditional Carnival was banned, but calypso and steelpan kept growing. Villages such as Grand Bay kept the tradition alive. And the link with bouyon is written in black and white: in modern Dominica, chante mas and lapo kabwit have become a part of bouyon music.

The Calypso Monarch, a living institution

Calypso has been popular in Dominica since the 1950s. The first Calypso King was crowned in 1959: The Observer. Competitions begin in January and end with the Calypso Monarch final, usually the Saturday before Carnival in February. The winner becomes Carnival King and joins Miss Dominica in the Traditional Costume Parade on Carnival Monday.

King Dice, whose real name is Dennison Joseph, is the longest-reigning Calypso Monarch: a ten-time winner, crowned again in 2024, his eighth crown in 2016 put him level with Trinidad Mighty Sparrow, also on eight crowns. Tasha P, Tasha Peltier, was crowned in 2011, the first woman in fifty years to win Dominica Calypso Monarch, and the first calypsonian to win three titles in one season: Calypso Queen, Stardom King of the Tent and Calypso Monarch. The first Calypso Queen competition took place in 1987, won by Juliana Jahlee Alfred with Sweet Tempo and Life is Your Right.

Three routes from calypso to bouyon

The first route runs through cadence-lypso, the Dominican genre of the 1970s carried by Exile One and the Grammacks. Calypso is listed as a calypso fusion genre, and Dominica cadence-lypso is defined as a mix of calypso and the cadence of Haiti. WCK, formed in 1986, then fused cadence-lypso with jing ping. The second route is direct: WCK played a blend of local cadence-lypso and traditional jing ping, chante mas and lapo kabwit rhythms, which would later be labelled bouyon.

The third route is living practice: chante mas and lapo kabwit are part of bouyon music today, and the Calypso Monarch is still a contest that fills halls, with finalists backed by the Swinging Stars band. The living bridge between the two worlds is called Trilla-G: a bouyon artist of today, he was crowned Calypso King in 2023. Calypso is therefore not a dead ancestor of bouyon: it is a root that still sings, every year, on the stage of Dominica carnival.

Frequently asked questions

What is calypso?

Calypso was born in Trinidad and Tobago, among Afro-Trinidadians, in the early to mid 19th century. Its rhythms trace back to West African kaiso. It was a song of news, gossip, satire and scandal, sung in French Creole, that served as a people newspaper and a vehicle of protest against colonial rule.

What is chante mas?

Chante mas is Dominica 19th-century carnival song: a lead vocalist, the chantwel, followed by a responsorial chorus, the lavwa, lapo kabwit drums and dancers dancing backwards in front of the drummer. The lyrics, almost all in French Creole, dealt with gossip and scandal. In modern Dominica, chante mas and lapo kabwit have become a part of bouyon music.

What is the link between calypso and bouyon?

Calypso fed bouyon through three routes: cadence-lypso, which mixes calypso and the cadence of Haiti; the blend played by WCK, formed in 1986, which united cadence-lypso, jing ping, chante mas and lapo kabwit; and the living practice of chante mas and lapo kabwit, which are part of bouyon today. Trilla-G, bouyon artist and 2023 Calypso King, is the living bridge.

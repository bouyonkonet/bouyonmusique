The future of bouyon is being decided now

By Smokiller · Published September 12, 2026 · Updated October 28, 2026

Bouyon has never had such a large audience, and its future has never been so open. The question is no longer whether the genre will grow, but who will keep its keys: its Dominican creators, or industries elsewhere.

The risk: growing while losing the origin

The history of Caribbean music is full of genres that became global while their island of origin stayed poor in rights and credits. Bouyon, whose value is increasingly created in France, runs the same risk if Dominican artists do not structure their distribution and their metadata.

The Bouyon Shattaz answer

The model built by Bouyon Shattaz combines a media outlet that tells the origin story, a distribution and rights desk for artists, and a network of pages and partners in the French Caribbean. The goal: that every bouyon success strengthens Dominica instead of erasing it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main risk for the future of bouyon?

Growing while losing its origin. Many Caribbean genres became global while their island of origin stayed poor in rights and credits. Bouyon, whose value is increasingly created in France, runs the same risk if Dominican artists do not structure their distribution and their metadata.

What does Bouyon Shattaz propose for the future of bouyon?

The Bouyon Shattaz model combines three pillars: a media outlet that tells the genre’s Dominican origin story, a distribution and rights desk for artists, and a network of pages and partners in the French Caribbean. The goal is that every bouyon success strengthens Dominica instead of erasing it.

Why is it called bouyon?

Bouyon means soup in Kweyol: the genre stirs Dominica's traditional musics together with electronic production. As in a soup, the mix makes the taste: in 1988, in Roseau, the band WCK brought cadence-lypso and jing ping together with electronic keyboards and drum machines.

Is bouyon part of Creole culture?

Yes. Bouyon was born in Dominica's villages and carnival and soundtracks Creole celebrations across the French Caribbean. It is sung in Kweyol and English, and that shared Creole helped it cross the sea to Guadeloupe and Martinique, then reach the Antillean diaspora in France.

Does bouyon have a future in France?

Yes, and it can already be measured. Two bouyon tracks have been certified gold single by SNEP: "Je l'ai vu" by HollyG with Arendi in 2025, then "Pow Pow / Allo Doudou" with Miimii KDS in 2026. The French Caribbean and the Paris diaspora have become a major market, where certifications, stages and distribution happen.

How can a bouyon producer protect their beats?

The agreement reached in October 2026 between Bouyon Shattaz and Denmath's team offers a model: the beats are hosted for Smokiller's Dominican artist pool only, every download is logged and emailed to all parties, recording stays free, but releasing a track requires the producer's prior written consent.

Why do credits matter for the future of bouyon?

Because a track that travels must pay the people who made it: that takes clean credits, written agreements, properly named files and identified rights holders. Bouyon has a rare advantage at this crossroads: its Dominican origin is documented, dated and claimed while the crossover is happening.

Are producers the future of bouyon?

That is this site's thesis: the next decade of bouyon will be a story of producers and rights as much as a story of singers. The voices make the hooks, but producers decide the tempo, the texture and the sound signature. Smokiller, Denmath, HYPNO, Savion, MB On The Track and Dboss embody these methods. See producers and passers.

Which artists embody the new generation of bouyon?

Reo, presented as the Prince of Bouyon by 13th Street Promotions, carries the new Dominican generation and its bridges with the French Caribbean, like Joli BB with Maurane Voyer. Smokiller's Bouyon Music 2026 Mix Vol. 1 also highlights new Dominican tracks by Trilla-G, J Lion, Tipsy, Khallion, Reo and DOVA.

Does Wrecketeng have a future?

Yes: on December 3, 2025, Dominica News Online announced a collaborative album produced by Smokiller to shine a light on the origins of Wrecketeng, with DJ Cut, Klass Money, Charlie Black, Keith Goddard, O Banga and Mopo Neck. The project aims to introduce the style to a younger generation while keeping its link to tradition. See Wrecketeng.

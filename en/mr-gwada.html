Mr Gwada: from Dominica to Gwada, bouyon in French Creole

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Mr Gwada is a bouyon singer and songwriter from Dominica, real name Kernel Stevens, also credited as Benz aka Mr Gwada and Benz Mr Gwada. His story follows a route few bouyon artists have taken in this direction: he left Dominica in 2000 to join his father in Guadeloupe, built his stage identity on the French island by mixing Dominican bouyon with French Creole, until a hit called “Gwada” gave him his stage name. This profile follows that path with the sources available: his artist biography published on ReverbNation, the verified credits of his records on Shazam, and the trace of his catalogue in Caribbean DJ and radio playlists.

Who Mr Gwada is: Kernel Stevens, Benz, and a name given by the public

According to his artist biography published on ReverbNation, Kernel Stevens, better known as Benz Mr Gwada, comes from Dominica, the nature isle. The same biography presents him as one of Dominica most prolific bouyon recording artists and songwriters, a formula this profile repeats as an artist presentation and not as an established ranking. What can be verified, by contrast, can be read in the credits: on the Shazam pages of his recent records, the songwriter name that returns is Kernel Stevens, from “Touch Down” to “Se Pa Taw”, which anchors the civil identity behind the stage name.

The name Mr Gwada, for its part, was not chosen in an office. Again according to his biography, it was the instant success of his bouyon song “Gwada”, which speaks of his personal experience in Guadeloupe and was produced by DJ Flip, that led fans and peers to affectionately dub him Mr Gwada. The title also circulates as “everybody wan go GWADA”, everybody wants to go to Gwada, and the biography presents it as his first ever bouyon hit. An artist named by his adopted public: the detail sums up the whole character.

Mr Gwada should be distinguished from a similar name on this site, Gwada G, a different artist covered elsewhere in our pages. Mr Gwada is the Dominican of this profile, the self described founder of the Bouyon Nation 767 movement, where 767 is the telephone area code of Dominica. That number inside the movement name states the project: carry Dominican bouyon outward, starting with the island where he lived his most singular story, Guadeloupe.

His place on a site devoted to bouyon is therefore not artificial. Mr Gwada is a bouyon artist in the full sense: formed in the circle of the Dominican pioneers, author of a debut bouyon album, connected to producer Dada, and still present in current bouyon rotations. His originality lies elsewhere: he is the lived link between the bouyon of Roseau and the public of Guadeloupe, the two banks our bouyon gwada page tells as a single musical territory.

2000: leaving Dominica for Guadeloupe, and inventing a bilingual style

The turning point of his life is dated by his biography: in 2000, he left his homeland Dominica to join his father in the French island of Guadeloupe. He did not yet have the name Mr Gwada, nor the hit that would give it to him. What he carried was a Dominican formation and an ear trained on the bouyon of the 1990s, the live bands and keyboards of Roseau. What he found in Guadeloupe was a French Creole speaking public, a zouk and dancehall scene, and another way of partying.

The biography describes the sequel precisely: once in Guadeloupe, Benz Mr Gwada started mixing his lyrics with French Creole, and that mix eventually became the style he is popularly identified with today. It is worth measuring what that choice represents in bouyon history. Bouyon was born in Dominica, in English and in Dominican Kwéyòl. To also sing it in French Creole, from inside a life lived in Guadeloupe, is not a commercial translation: it is the account of a double belonging, and it is that account the song “Gwada” sets to music.

That bilingual position explains his particular longevity. On the Dominican side, he remains an artist of the house, credited in the same rotations as the Roseau bands. On the Guadeloupe side, he speaks the language of the street and the fete, with titles such as “Pa Ni Papie”, there are no papers, whose very spelling is that of written Creole in the French Antilles. Few bouyon artists occupy both sides of that linguistic border with a biography that justifies it track by track. That is also why DJs on both sides of the water programme him without needing to explain him: the same voice carries the Roseau formation and the Guadeloupe address, and each public hears its own half of the story in his records.

To place this path in the wider landscape, our page on the artists of Guadeloupe and our bouyon in Guadeloupe file tell how the French island became the second home of bouyon. Mr Gwada holds a separate role there: he is neither a Dominican passing through on tour, nor a Guadeloupean adopting a sound from elsewhere. He is a Dominican who became, by life and by one hit, the ambassador the Guadeloupeans themselves named.

The beginnings: Derrick Rah Peters, Lone Rain Jah and the album Bouyon Biznis

Before the hit, there is the apprenticeship, and his biography tells it in one dense sentence: early in his career, Benz Mr Gwada featured with Derrick Rah Peters, the legendary pioneer of WCK, on Peters solo album project entitled “Lone Rain Jah”, and that feature was his first major introduction to the bouyon genre. Singing alongside the WCK singer, the band that founded bouyon at the end of the 1980s, places the young artist in the direct line of the founders, by work and not by talk.

The biography adds the next step: he later released his debut bouyon album, entitled “Bouyon Biznis”. The title, the bouyon business, announces a solo artist already thinking in terms of career and catalogue, at a time when bouyon remained dominated by bands. This profile does not know the exact date or the track listing of that album from the sources opened for this research: no detail is therefore invented to complete it, and that limit is stated here as it is across this site.

What the sources do allow us to say is the logic of the sequence. A formative feature beside a WCK pioneer, a first solo bouyon album, then the move to Guadeloupe and the hit that changes the artist name: the path is that of a singer building his identity in layers, the Dominican formation first, the Guadeloupe experience next, the bilingual signature last. It is that third layer that distinguishes him from most solo singers of his generation.

The link to the producer generation can be read in the record his biography places after the hit: “Come Join Me”, on the Daydream Riddim, produced by Krishna “Dada” Lawrence. Dada is the producer to whom this site attributes, on the signed testimony of Smokiller, the start of bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003, and the producer credited behind “Famalay”. That the singer of “Gwada” followed up on a Dada riddim connects his story to the other great join of modern bouyon, the join between bouyon and soca.

The hit Gwada and the World Creole Music Festival stage

The song “Gwada” is the pivot of this profile, because it is at once a hit, a story and a birth certificate. According to his biography, the record speaks of his personal experience in Guadeloupe, it was produced by DJ Flip, and its instant success gave him his stage name. The hook formula, everybody wan go GWADA, turns the usual gaze around: it is not the Dominican who dreams of the French island, it is everybody, and the singer, who lives there, becomes the privileged witness of that desire.

His biography then states that he has been one of the biggest solo bouyon acts since he first appeared as one of the headliners at the World Creole Music Festival in 2013, after releasing that first hit. This profile presents that point exactly for what it is: a statement from the artist biography. The 2013 headliner announcement published by Caribbean press at the time names other artists and does not list Mr Gwada in the excerpts consulted for this file. The Roseau festival stage therefore remains, at this stage of the public record, a fact claimed by the artist and not a fact corroborated by festival press, and the reader deserves to know that.

The same biography makes him the founder of the Bouyon Nation 767 movement and describes tours in the Caribbean, the United States and Europe, in an effort of taking bouyon to the world. Here too, the profile separates the registers: the slogan and the ambition are documented by the artist page, the precise tour dates are not, in the sources opened here. What can be verified without that biography is the continued presence of his name far from Dominica, in bouyon podcast tracklists and radio playlists, as shown below.

Why is this caution worth writing down? Because the Mr Gwada case is typical of a generation of bouyon artists whose history circulates mainly through their own pages. Stating what comes from the artist, what comes from the credits and what remains to be corroborated is the only honest way to give him the place he deserves without lending him a story he has not signed.

The catalogue: from Big Chain and Pa Ni Papie to the verified records of 2020 to 2024

The biography published on ReverbNation lists a catalogue of popular songs that, on its own, draws the material of his repertoire: “Big Chain”, “Bcuz U Leave”, “1 Night Stand”, “Pa Ni Papie”, “Toxic Love”, “Never Thirsty”, “Best Life”, “Circle”, “Feeling Sick” on the Loud Riddim, “Saw Kim” and “Pressure”, among others. Several of these titles read like a journal of the Antillean life he sings: the love that leaves, the one night fete, the question of papers, the gold chain of showing off. The French Creole titles sit beside the Dominican English titles, exactly as his biography announces.

Streaming credits confirm that this catalogue is not a memory. The Shazam page for “Touch Down” ties it to an album entitled “Bouyon”, released on 11 May 2020 under his own name, with Kernel Stevens as songwriter and a tempo of 155 BPM, the high zone of party bouyon. “Who Fool Dem”, with Fucha Kid, was released on 14 September 2020 on the label 1991556 Records DK. “Winning” credits him as songwriter alongside Bryelle Joseph. Those three pages, verified for this profile, show an artist releasing regularly at the start of the 2020s, solo and in collaboration.

The year 2024 extends the series with equally precise pages. “CREW (HEY)”, on the Trance Bouyon Riddim with Menace XL, was released on 10 January 2024, again on 1991556 Records DK. “Se Pa Taw” followed on 27 February 2024, songwriter Kernel Stevens, tempo 160 BPM, and the video linked to the page presents it as a duo with DJ Taffy, under the Bouyon 2024 tag. The title, it is not yours, in French Creole, is a reminder that his bilingual signature is not an old episode: it still structures his most recent releases.

The circulation of the catalogue can finally be checked where bouyon really lives, in the mixes. The Soca Podcast tracklists, which document recent Dominican bouyon rotation, line up “Pa Ni Papie (Kinkin La)”, “Tek It” with Nayee, “Ben Ur Back” and “Big Chain” with Bobo Bantan, “Deja Koke” with Kassidje, “Thats D Biznis”, “Nasty (Paint and Powder)”, “1 Night Stand”, “Breeze” and “Money” with DJ Taffy. The playlists of the radio station Kool FM Tropikal, logged on Online Radio Box, record spins of “BEN UR BACK” and “BIG CHAIN” in their turn. A catalogue that turns on radio and in podcasts, across several seasons, stands as proof of continued presence.

One more pattern runs through those credits and deserves to be named, because it is the working grammar of modern bouyon: the riddim. His biography places “Come Join Me” on the Daydream Riddim and “Feeling Sick” on the Loud Riddim, and his 2024 single “CREW (HEY)” is billed directly on the Trance Bouyon Riddim. A riddim is a shared instrumental that several singers voice in turn, the producer format that Dada and his generation used to carry Dominican bouyon from the live band era into the streaming era. That Mr Gwada catalogue is organised in riddims, from the Daydream Riddim of his beginnings to the Trance Bouyon Riddim of 2024, shows a singer who never left the producer system of his genre: he is not a zouk or dancehall guest passing through bouyon, he is a bouyon recording artist working the bouyon format, season after season, in both of his languages.

Why Mr Gwada matters, and what this profile does not yet know

Mr Gwada matters because he embodies a road bouyon took only once with this clarity: a singer formed in Dominica, settled in Guadeloupe, renamed by the Guadeloupe public, and faithful to bouyon as a single genre of work. Most bridges between Dominica and the French Antilles run through tours, collaborations or productions. His runs through a life: a departure in 2000, a father joined, a language added, a hit that tells exactly that.

He also matters as a songwriter. The verified credits carry his civil name, Kernel Stevens, as the writer of his recent records, and his biography presents him as a songwriter and recording artist above all. In a genre where singers are often carried by bands and producers, a solo act who writes, releases under his own name and holds a catalogue over more than a decade is a profile worth documenting for itself. Our bouyon playlist and our bouyon soca page place his records inside the sound of his generation.

This profile finally states its limits, because they are real. Most of the early story, the 2000 departure, the feature with Derrick Rah Peters, the album “Bouyon Biznis” and the 2013 headliner claim, rests on a single published source: the artist biography on ReverbNation. It is quoted and attributed everywhere it is used, but it has not been corroborated by independent press in the sources opened for this file. No verified official account was confirmed by opening an exact page during this research, so this page links to no social profile in its schema.

If independent elements complete the file, album dates, festival archives, confirmed official links, this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site. In the meantime, listening starts with the verified records: “Gwada” for the birth certificate, “Touch Down” and “Se Pa Taw” for the current signature, and “Big Chain” with Bobo Bantan for the rotation as it turns today.

Frequently asked questions

What is Mr Gwada’s real name?

According to his artist biography published on ReverbNation, his name is Kernel Stevens, better known as Benz Mr Gwada. The Shazam credits of his recent records, including “Touch Down” and “Se Pa Taw”, carry Kernel Stevens as songwriter, which anchors the civil identity behind the stage name. He is also credited as Benz aka Mr Gwada and Benz Mr Gwada.

Why is he called Mr Gwada if he is Dominican?

According to his biography, he left Dominica in 2000 to join his father in Guadeloupe. The instant success of his bouyon song “Gwada”, produced by DJ Flip and drawn from his experience in Guadeloupe, earned him the name Mr Gwada, given affectionately by his fans and peers. He is, in short, an artist named by his adopted audience.

Did Mr Gwada really headline the World Creole Music Festival in 2013?

His biography says so: it states that he appeared there as one of the headliners in 2013, after his first hit. The 2013 headliner announcement published by Caribbean press and consulted for this file, however, names other artists and does not list him. This profile therefore presents the point as a claim by the artist, not as a fact corroborated by festival press.

What is the link between Mr Gwada and Dada, the Famalay producer?

His biography states that after “Gwada”, he released the single “Come Join Me” on the Daydream Riddim, produced by Krishna “Dada” Lawrence. Dada is the Dominican producer credited on “Famalay” and to whom this site attributes the start of bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003, on the signed testimony of Smokiller.

What are Mr Gwada’s verified recent records?

On Shazam: “Touch Down”, from the album “Bouyon” released on 11 May 2020; “Who Fool Dem” with Fucha Kid, on 14 September 2020; “CREW (HEY)” on the Trance Bouyon Riddim with Menace XL, on 10 January 2024; and “Se Pa Taw”, on 27 February 2024. His biography also lists “Big Chain”, “Pa Ni Papie” and “1 Night Stand”.

What is Bouyon Nation 767?

According to his biography, Bouyon Nation 767 is the movement founded by Mr Gwada to take bouyon to the world, with tours in the Caribbean, the United States and Europe. The number 767 is the telephone area code of Dominica, his home island. Precise tour dates, on the other hand, are not documented in the open sources consulted for this profile.

Are Mr Gwada and Gwada G the same person?

No. Mr Gwada, real name Kernel Stevens, is the Dominican singer who moved to Guadeloupe in 2000 and was named after his hit “Gwada”. Gwada G is a different artist, covered on another page of this site, who is also from Dominica despite his name. The two names look alike, but the credits, records and careers are separate.

Who produced Mr Gwada’s song “Gwada”?

According to his biography, “Gwada” was produced by DJ Flip. The song tells his personal experience in Guadeloupe and also circulates as “everybody wan go GWADA”, everybody wants to go to Gwada. His biography presents it as his first bouyon hit, the one whose instant success gave him his stage name.

Is Mr Gwada from Dominica or Guadeloupe?

Both, in that order. According to his biography, Kernel Stevens comes from Dominica, the island where bouyon was born, and settled in Guadeloupe in 2000 to join his father. There he built his stage identity by mixing Dominican bouyon with French Creole, which makes him a lived link between Roseau bouyon and the bouyon gwada audience.

Who did Mr Gwada start his career with?

According to his biography, he first sang with Derek Rah Peters, the WCK pioneer (spelled Derrick Rah Peters in that biography), on Peters’ solo album project titled “Lone Rain Jah”. That collaboration was his first major introduction to bouyon. Singing beside a pioneer of the founding band places him in the direct line of the genre’s founders.

What is Mr Gwada’s first album?

According to his biography, his first bouyon album is titled “Bouyon Biznis”, the business of bouyon, released after his work with Derrick Rah Peters. The open sources give neither its exact date nor its track list, and this profile invents nothing to complete it. Another album, “Bouyon”, released under his name on 11 May 2020, contains “Touch Down” according to Shazam.

What does Mr Gwada’s “Pa Ni Papie” mean?

“Pa Ni Papie” means there are no papers, spelled in the written Creole of the French Antilles. The song appears in the catalogue his biography lists and runs in the Soca Podcast tracklists as “Pa Ni Papie (Kinkin La)”. It shows his signature: a Dominican singer who speaks the street and party language of Guadeloupe.

What language does Mr Gwada sing in?

He mixes Dominican English and French Creole. According to his biography, once settled in Guadeloupe he began mixing his lyrics with French Creole, and that blend became the style he is popularly identified with today. Records such as “Se Pa Taw”, it is not yours, released in February 2024, show that this bilingual signature still shapes his latest releases.

Which artists has Mr Gwada worked with?

His verified credits name Fucha Kid on “Who Fool Dem” (2020), Menace XL on “CREW (HEY)” (2024) and DJ Taffy in the “Se Pa Taw” video. The Soca Podcast tracklists add “Big Chain” with Bobo Bantan, “Tek It” with Nayee, “Deja Koke” with Kassidje and “Money” with DJ Taffy. “Winning” is co-written with Bryelle Joseph.

Where can I hear Mr Gwada?

Start with “Gwada”, the birth certificate of his name, then “Touch Down” and “Se Pa Taw” for his current signature, and “Big Chain” with Bobo Bantan for today’s rotation. His records run in the Soca Podcast tracklists and on Kool FM Tropikal radio. For the sound of his generation, hear our bouyon playlist as well.

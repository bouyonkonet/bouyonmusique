J2MO: the producer cooking riddims since 2015

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

J2MO is a producer and beatmaker of the French Antilles bouyon and shatta circuit, credited since 2015 and still releasing in 2025. His name is four characters long, but his signature is one word: cooker. His own releases present him as The Beat Cooker, his mixes carry the names Riddim Up and Ok Boom, and his Qobuz catalogue lines up a series of instrumental riddims while his label name publishes other artists. This profile follows that documented trail, from “Si kontwol” in 2015 to “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte” with Miky Ding La and Deejay Guyguy, and it also states what the public record does not allow us to know about the man behind the signature.

Who J2MO is: a beatmaker with no biography, a signature everywhere

J2MO has, in the sources opened for this research, no published biography, no interview, no date or place of birth and no artist presentation page. That silence does not prevent placing him, because his work is documented track by track for more than ten years. Spotify files his catalogue under the shatta tag in its artist statistics, Shazam classes his instrumentals between Dance and World, and the reference playlist The Sound of Bouyon holds him for “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte”, the track he shares with Miky Ding La and Deejay Guyguy. J2MO is therefore first a production name: that of a riddim maker of modern bouyon, the French Antilles branch of the genre.

One name returns in his credits with a regularity that deserves to be written out in full. The name Christophe Démocrite appears as songwriter, composer, programmer or mastering engineer on nearly every track opened for this profile: “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim”, “Pansyon”, “Push Back”, “RIDDIM UP MIX”, “MAGIC TROPICAL RIDDIM”, and as sole composer of Miky Ding La “Weed tou lé jou” on Deezer. The descriptions of his own YouTube channel also list a christophe.democrite handle beside the j2moofficial handles. This profile reports that credit pattern for what it is, without deciding what no source states in words: the exact civil identity behind the J2MO name.

The published geography of his audience draws his listening territory. The Spotify statistics mirror consulted for this profile, which itself dates its data as nine months old, counted 10,655 monthly listeners and 859 followers, with Paris as the first listening city, then Les Abymes in Guadeloupe, Lyon, Saint-Denis and Marseille. Those are dated audience figures, presented here as a snapshot and not as a live counter. They nevertheless tell one stable thing: J2MO is heard first between Paris and Guadeloupe, the exact corridor of Gwada bouyon and its diaspora, the one our bouyon gwada page retraces.

2015 to 2021: from Si kontwol to the High Lights Riddim

The earliest dated credit in the catalogue is old by platform bouyon standards. “Si kontwol”, featuring Daly and Zebrist, was released on 3 February 2015 on the Hitmaker army label, at 157 BPM according to its Shazam page, with J2MO credited as composer. The track belongs to the generation of Antillean bouyon and dancehall before mass streaming: a fast production, voices of the Gwada and Mada scene, and a producer already signing the composition. Zebrist, one of the two guests, reappears years later in the tracklist of the J2MO WYFL Mix, a sign of an address book that stayed the same.

The centrepiece of the period is “High Lights Riddim”, published on 27 September 2021 by Up Full Media Group and listed by Qobuz as a J2mo album. The “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim” condenses its spirit: Mad General, MIKTY and Miky Ding La share the microphone over the same rhythm, at 160 BPM, with songwriting credits shared between Christophe Démocrite, Mac Kenzie Pernock, Mike Croix and Mike Adequilom. On Deezer, the track “Bay Volum”, taken from the same riddim, credits Nicolas Macbeth, Ludmilian Flower and Ludmael Flower as writers and Christophe Démocrite as composer. The format is that of the classic riddim: one rhythm, several voices, a producer at the centre.

That riddim mechanic is the thread of everything that follows. J2MO does not present himself as a singer releasing singles, but as a workshop that builds rhythms and invites the scene to voice them. Bouyon, a genre born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s and told on our history of bouyon page, has always worked that way in its Antillean branch: producers from Guadeloupe and Martinique take up the Dominican rhythm machine and tune it for their own voices. “High Lights Riddim” places J2MO exactly in that line, with Miky Ding La, who would become his most visible collaborator.

The Beat Cooker: the riddim factory from 2022 to 2025

In 2022, Qobuz lists “Gold Color (Riddim)”, published on 8 April by Upfull Music, and in September of the same year came “Pansyon”, a track by Jayah Fly and J2MO on iMD-GFLY, at 160 BPM, where Clarissa Simon signs the lyrics and the vocals while Christophe Démocrite is credited with programming and composition. On 6 May 2023, two riddims were released on the same day on Upfull Music with a mention that became his brand: “Mad Love Riddim (The Beat Cooker)” and “Po A Banan Riddim (The Beat Cooker)”. The cooker of rhythms, the beat cooker, was born as an official signature, and his own YouTube channel confirms it by crediting the drums of his mixes to “J2mothebeatcooker”.

The year 2023 added “Push Back”, by Mad General and J2MO, released on 24 January on his own label, at 159 BPM, then “RIDDIM UP MIX” on 28 July, again under the J2MO label, at 159 BPM. The Riddim Up name then became a series: “Riddim Up (By J2mo)” on 5 May 2025, then “Riddim Up Nou Kay” on 6 June 2025, while the spring of 2025 lined up “Street Fighter Riddim” on 7 May and “Future Riddim (By J2MO)” on 9 May. On 15 May 2025, “BOUYON SESSION BIG CAKE (Clubbing)” came out on L’overground Prod., and its piece “MAGIC TROPICAL RIDDIM” shows J2MO on programming and production, Christophe Démocrite on songwriting and mastering, at 157 BPM.

The pace states the trade. Between September and November 2025, Qobuz still lists “Back To School”, on 25 September, under his name. Those instrumental or semi instrumental tracks do not chase the radio chorus: they supply the raw material of mixes, freestyles and parties, the fuel of the circuit. That is why the J2MO pages so often give BPMs between 157 and 160: it is the tempo zone of dance bouyon and shatta, the one where DJs chain tracks without breathing. The J2MO catalogue reads as a stock of rhythms kept up to date season after season, and not as a string of isolated hits.

The voices on his riddims: Miky Ding La, Mad General, Sweety and the J2MO label

The track that brought J2MO into the reference playlists is a voice track. “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte” brings together Miky Ding La, J2MO and Dj Guyguy, runs 2 minutes 41 in Spotify The Sound of Bouyon playlist, and represents the producer in the selection that serves as the calling card of the genre. The connection with Miky Ding La is old in the credits: he was already on the 2021 “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim”, and his track “Weed tou lé jou” carries the composition of Christophe Démocrite, with Mike Adequilom as writer, the same Mike Adequilom credited on the Mega Mix. Around J2MO, the same names return because this is a workshop, not a passing meeting.

Mad General is the other recurring voice: guest of the 2021 Mega Mix, co-performer of “Push Back” in 2023, he shares the songwriting credits with J2MO through Mac Kenzie Pernock, co-writer of both tracks. Beside those loyalties, the J2MO name also works as a label that publishes others. Qobuz shows it publishing “Tchimbé Sa” by Sweety on 26 January 2023, a track Shazam lists as a single by Sweety and J2MO, then “What You Do” by LETID on 31 January 2024, four Catch tracks in 2024, “T-Max”, “Longtime”, “TOGETHER” and “Grip Me Down”, and “Sexy Gyal” by Mafiaboy on 19 November 2025. Producer, beatmaker and release house: the three hats carry the same name.

That producer publisher profile explains the shape of his structured discography at the bottom of this page: thirteen verified recordings, instrumental riddims, mixes and shared voice tracks, never a singer album. It is also what sets him apart in the landscape this site follows: where most Gwada bouyon names are voices, J2MO is an infrastructure, a supplier of rhythms for the voices of others and, on occasion, for his own collaborations. His instrumentals are even sold as such: the descriptions of his mixes send instrumental enquiries to a Wavcamp page under his name, the shop of the beat cooker.

One Deezer editorial playlist shows how the platforms file that infrastructure. The Bouyon bass playlist lines up “Bay Volum” by J2MO, taken from the High Lights Riddim, in the same selection as Theodora “KONGOLESE SOUS BBL”, Ricky Bishop “SAINT-BARTH”, Meryl “Dembow Martinica”, 1T1 “Leave Me Alone” and FLW “Wouaw Wouaw”: the riddim maker sits there among the voices and the hits, on the strength of an instrumental album track. The same playlist carries “Finès” by Waysee, 2 minutes 36, whose credits are the most instructive of the whole J2MO record: Waysee and J2MO as writers, Christophe Démocrite, Waysee and J2MO as composers. The two names that follow each other through the catalogue are printed side by side there, as separate credit lines, which is exactly why this profile reports the pattern and signs no identity claim of its own.

The mixes: Ok Boom, Riddim Up and WYFL, the thermometer of the circuit

Alongside the releases, J2MO publishes mixes that work as sound press reviews of the scene. “OK BOOM MIXXX”, released on 11 December 2024 under his label and classed World by Shazam with the French language stated, becomes on YouTube the “Ok Boom Mix” whose description promises the trending bouyon titles and many remixes. Its published keywords tell his perimeter without detour: Gwada, Mada, Dominica, Lucia, Dembow, Riddim, and names such as Edday, Reo, Triple Kay, Mr Ridge, 1T1 and Jixel. The selector listens to the whole circuit there, from Dominica to Saint Lucia, and claims it.

The “WYFL Mix”, made with Kiony, pushes the logic further: its published tracklist chains Admiral T, Mavado, Young Chang MC, Vybz Kartel, Kalash and Chronic Law, then Saïk and Krys on the WYFL Riddim, Keros-N, Ti Tchô and Zebrist. Bouyon converses there with Jamaican dancehall and Antillean voices in one flow, exactly the blend the J2MO riddims aim to produce. The drum credit of this mix is given to J2mothebeatcooker, and the description points, like that of the Ok Boom Mix, to his instrumental pages. The mix is therefore not a side activity: it is the showcase of the catalogue and the test bench of the rhythms.

That double activity, riddim releases and selection mixes, places J2MO in the category of producer passers this site documents: those who circulate the sound between islands and diasporas. His published audience, Paris first and Les Abymes just behind, listens to exactly that programme. To follow that vein, our bouyon playlist mixes the riddims of the producers and the new releases of the voices of Dominica, Guadeloupe and Martinique, the same corridor the J2MO mixes have travelled since 2023.

What this profile does not know: the limits of the J2MO record

This profile ends with its limits, because they are real. No source opened for this research gives the full civil name of J2MO other than through the bundle of credits under the name Christophe Démocrite, none gives his date or place of birth, his place of life, or a statement of origin. This profile therefore turns neither his credits into a civil record, nor the geography of his audience into a biography: Paris and Les Abymes are his published listening cities, not his address. No official account was held as verified to the point of being linked in the structured data of this page, which therefore stays without an external link.

The figures quoted are dated and attributed: the Spotify statistics snapshot consulted itself dated its data as nine months old, and the Shazam counters of the tracks are those of the pages opened on 20 October 2026. The discography, on the other hand, is solid because it rests on three catalogues that cross check each other, Shazam, Qobuz and Deezer, plus the reference playlist of Spotify and the artist own YouTube channel. Thirteen verified recordings form its core, from “Si kontwol” in 2015 to “MAGIC TROPICAL RIDDIM” in 2025, and the list will grow if the Riddim Up series continues at the pace of 2025.

What remains is the essential, which needs no civil record to be heard: a producer of Antillean bouyon and shatta, active since 2015, become The Beat Cooker, supplier of riddims for Miky Ding La, Mad General and the artists of his own label, and a selector whose mixes shuttle between Gwada, Mada, Dominica and Saint Lucia. Listening starts with “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte” in our bouyon playlist, continues with the “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim” and the Riddim Up series, and ends, to understand where the machine comes from, with our history of bouyon.

Frequently asked questions

Who is J2MO?

J2MO is a producer and beatmaker of the French Antilles bouyon and shatta circuit, credited since 2015. Spotify classes his catalogue as shatta, his most visible tracks are “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte” with Miky Ding La and Deejay Guyguy, the “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim” with Mad General and Miky Ding La, and the Riddim Up series of riddims. He also signs his productions The Beat Cooker and publishes other artists under the J2MO label name. The most recent catalogue lines opened for this profile run to November 2025, when the J2MO label name published Mafiaboy “Sexy Gyal”, two months after his own “Back To School”. No official biography is published in the sources opened for this profile.

What is “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte”?

It is the track that represents J2MO in The Sound of Bouyon, the reference playlist of the genre on Spotify. It is credited to Miky Ding La, J2MO and Dj Guyguy, and runs 2 minutes 41 in that playlist. It is a shared voice track, typical of the way J2MO works: the producer supplies the rhythm, the voices of the scene ride it, and the DJ is credited as a co-author of the spread.

Which name returns in the composition credits of J2MO, and why do other profiles not say it?

The name Christophe Démocrite. He appears as songwriter, composer, programmer or mastering engineer on nearly every track opened for this profile, from “Pansyon” to “MAGIC TROPICAL RIDDIM”, and as composer of Miky Ding La “Weed tou lé jou”. The descriptions of the J2MO YouTube channel list a handle under that name beside the j2moofficial handles. No source states in words the civil identity behind J2MO, and the Waysee track “Finès” even credits Christophe Démocrite and J2MO side by side, so this profile reports the credit bundle without inventing a civil record.

What does The Beat Cooker mean?

It is the producer signature of J2MO, published in the very titles of his releases: “Mad Love Riddim (The Beat Cooker)” and “Po A Banan Riddim (The Beat Cooker)”, released on 6 May 2023 on Upfull Music. His YouTube channel credits the drums of his mixes to J2mothebeatcooker. The image is that of the rhythm cook: bouyon itself means a soup in Antillean Creole, and the beat cooker is the one who prepares it for the voices and the DJs. His own mix descriptions send instrumental enquiries to a Wavcamp page under the J2MO name, which makes the cooker image a working shop front and not only a nickname.

Is J2MO also a label?

Yes, according to the catalogues opened for this profile. Qobuz shows the J2MO name as publisher of “Tchimbé Sa” by Sweety in January 2023, of “What You Do” by LETID in January 2024, of four Catch tracks in 2024, “T-Max”, “Longtime”, “TOGETHER” and “Grip Me Down”, and of “Sexy Gyal” by Mafiaboy in November 2025. His own tracks “Push Back”, “RIDDIM UP MIX” and “OK BOOM MIXXX” also carry the J2MO label on their Shazam pages.

Where can I hear J2MO?

Start with “Bèl Fèss Bèl Chatte” with Miky Ding La and Deejay Guyguy, then the “Mega Mix High Lights Riddim” for the riddim format, the Riddim Up series of 2025 for his recent instrumentals, and his Ok Boom and WYFL mixes on his channel for his selection of the circuit. Our bouyon playlist follows the same scene, from Dominica to the French Antilles.

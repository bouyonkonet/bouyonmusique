Wrecketeng vs New Bouyon: the old and the heirs

By Smokiller · Published October 22, 2026

Two names, two eras, one question: what is really new in bouyon? On one side, Wrecketeng, the Dominican practice that rebuilds existing recordings over bouyon instrumentals, born with DJ Cut and the Roseau crews. On the other, the New Bouyon of the 2020s, a label carried by specific artists, on specific dates. This page compares the two, attributing every name to its author, as the genre's history requires.

Wrecketeng: the Dominican sampling practice

Wrecketeng was born in Dominica in the lineage of bouyon-muffin: dancehall and rap vocals over bouyon riddims, then a studio practice that samples and rebuilds existing recordings over bouyon instrumentals. Its founder is DJ Cut, of Bucktown Sounds and the Bushtown Clan, and the official spelling, Wrecketeng, comes from him. The reketeng form, spread across the internet, is a misspelling, and this site writes it only to flag the error.

In the early 2000s, Nursery Krew took up and developed the practice in Roseau, with a young Smokiller in its ranks. Tracks like Peanuts and Sorti Desandre show the method: known loops, dancehall and rap vocals, and the bouyon pulse underneath. The production cut away from the live band arrangements that dominated 1990s bouyon: sampled melodies, programmed percussion, electronic basslines.

New Bouyon: two uses, two dates, two authors

The name New Bouyon does not designate a single movement, and this page treats it as what it is: attributed uses. First use: New Bouyon Age, the title of an EP by TiiTii NBA released on March 22, 2023 on GVE Music. It is a release title and brand, and no opened source presents it as the name of a genre. Second use: the New Bouyon concept of Ricky Bishop, which his Deezer biography dates to 2024, presented as a modern reinvention of traditional bouyon incorporating electro and trap elements, with the EPs #newbouyon and #sexybouyon the same year. Bishop claims this concept as his own; it is an artist's claim, carried here as such.

The chronology matters: TiiTii NBA's EP carried the word New from 2023, a year before Bishop's concept, and the same scene circle also used the New Bouyon Wave wording in 2023 release titles. Talking about New Bouyon as a single, dated invention would therefore be wrong in both directions: the word was already circulating, and neither use names a whole scene.

The comparison: the techniques are old, the names are new

Placed side by side, the two files show a striking technical continuity. Wrecketeng already worked with sampled melodies, programmed percussion, electronic basslines and vocals from rap and dancehall. The productions filed under the new labels of the 2020s use the same toolbox, with the additions of current trap and electro. The most solid difference between the two eras is therefore not the tool, but the frame: a crew and DJ practice in Roseau on one side, artist and release brands in the streaming era on the other.

That is exactly the point carried by Smokiller's Wrecketeng revival: in December 2025, he released a Wrecketeng revival album with DJ Cut, Klass Money and Charlie Black, presenting these techniques as the ancestors of bouyon's new labels. The argument is Dominican and historical: what presents itself as new in the French Antillean scene was already practised in Roseau, under another, correctly spelled name.

How to read this site's two pages

This site keeps the two threads separate, as the sources require. The Wrecketeng page tells the practice, its spelling and its pioneers; the New Bouyon page attributes the label to its users and their dates, the 2023 EP for TiiTii NBA, the claimed 2024 concept for Ricky Bishop. Merging them would erase either the Dominican pioneers or the real chronology of the releases.

For the listener, the simple reference point is this: if a track rebuilds a known recording over a bouyon instrumental, it works in the Wrecketeng lineage, whatever name is printed on the cover. Names change fast in bouyon; the techniques have an address, and it is in Roseau.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Wrecketeng and New Bouyon?

Wrecketeng is an older Dominican practice, founded by DJ Cut, that rebuilds existing recordings over bouyon instrumentals. New Bouyon is a 2020s label carried by specific artists: TiiTii NBA's New Bouyon Age EP in 2023 and the concept claimed by Ricky Bishop in 2024.

Who created New Bouyon?

No opened source makes it possible to crown a single creator. TiiTii NBA released the New Bouyon Age EP in March 2023, and Ricky Bishop presented his New Bouyon concept in 2024 as a reinvention of bouyon with electro and trap, according to his Deezer biography. This site attributes each use to its author and date.

How is Wrecketeng spelled?

Wrecketeng, with that exact spelling, which comes from DJ Cut, the founder of the practice. The reketeng form, spread across the internet, is a misspelling.

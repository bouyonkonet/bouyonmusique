LGC: three letters, a team of writers, six records of bouyon gwada

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

LGC is an artist name of Guadeloupe bouyon, documented by six verified records released between 2023 and 2025: “Voyé’y Bay”, “Fè Sa Fann” with Lejuh, “Manzèl Good”, “Copain Copine” with Dézod, “Make It Clap” and “Woody Wood” with D.O.C FIX DEM and Judi Silva. Behind those three letters, the published credits draw a stable team of writers, where the names of Dany David Ledreck, Julio Polydamas, Kénan Gavarin and Jean Gardiole keep returning, and producers such as DJ Taffy and Magbeatz. No source says what the initials stand for, or exactly who carries them. This profile therefore publishes the six records and their credits, reads the team they draw, and states in plain words what the file does not hold.

Who LGC is: initials with no fact sheet, a team inside the credits

LGC is an artist name of the bouyon scene of the French Antilles, present in the platform catalogues since 2023. The platforms credit it as a performer and as a voice on its own records, and once as a guest performer on a record by another artist of the same circuit. Its name fits in three letters, and that is about everything the name itself tells: no source consulted publishes the expansion of the initials, and this profile does not invent one. LGC therefore remains, in this file, an unexplained set of initials, carried by records whose credits, by contrast, are detailed and checkable.

Because the paradox of this file is there: the name is opaque, but the credits are rich. Record after record, the same writers return. Dany David Ledreck is credited on the writing of almost every documented record. Julio Polydamas and Kénan Gavarin sign the first two. Jean Gardiole joins the team from “Manzèl Good” on. O’neal Chatelot and Déreck Ciani appear on the records of 2025. Two producers share the records whose production is published: DJ Taffy for “Fè Sa Fann”, Magbeatz for “Copain Copine” and “Make It Clap”. LGC is therefore no isolated name in the catalogue: it is the outlet of an identifiable Guadeloupe writing workshop, record by record.

Should LGC be read as a solo artist surrounded by writers, or as a collective signing under initials? The sources consulted do not settle it, and this profile does not settle it in their place. It keeps the two published facts that frame the question: on one side, the platforms credit LGC in the singular, as a voice and a performer, which looks like an individual stage name; on the other, the constancy of the writing team and the presence of a label named LGC Music on “Copain Copine” draw a structure that goes beyond a single person. The schema of this page files LGC among groups, out of caution towards that collective dimension of the credits, and the text says why.

This profile finally opens with its gaps, because they are real: no published biography of LGC was found, no member list, no interview, no detailed artist page, no official account verified during the research. What the sources do establish is nevertheless enough to place the name: six records, regular releases between 2023 and 2025, an anchoring inside the circuit of Lejuh and D.O.C FIX DEM, and lyrics in Guadeloupean Creole published in full by the platforms. That file, no more and no less, is what the following sections unroll.

“Voyé’y Bay”: the record that opens the file, in 2023

The first verified trace of LGC is “Voyé’y Bay”, whose Shazam page is dated April 27, 2023. The record publishes full lyrics in Guadeloupean Creole, and they tell the record better than a summary: the text opens on the name of the artist, shouted in the introduction, then turns on those who watch the lives of others, invent lives for people and comment on TikTok instead of minding their own homes. The hook, “Voyé’y Bay”, sends the gossip back to its authors: send it to them, report it to them, maco makrel all day long, the busybodies and tale carriers named in their own language.

That theme is no picturesque detail. Current bouyon gwada lives on social media as much as in the parties, and “Voyé’y Bay” is a record that speaks of that life from inside: watching through screens, comments, photos and videos taken on the sly, the text names them one by one. A record of 2023 that makes TikTok a character of its verses documents, in its own way, the moment when the scene became inseparable from the platforms that carry it. This profile quotes it for its published text, which is a checkable fact, and not for an interpretation of its intentions.

The credits of the record give the founding team of the file: Dany David Ledreck, Julio Polydamas and Kénan Gavarin on writing and composition. Those three names will form the hard core of almost every following record, and their meeting from the first known record on shows that LGC is born, in the catalogue, as a team project and not as an improvisation. The page consulted publishes neither the label of the record nor its tempo, and this profile does not guess them: it keeps the date, the published lyrics and the three writers, which are the established facts of this first trace.

“Voyé’y Bay” also fixes the language of the project. Guadeloupean Creole is total there, with no French language hook to widen the audience, in the vocabulary of the yards and the networks of the island. That is the choice the following records share, classed in the French language by the platforms for catalogue convenience, but written and sung in the language of the scene. To place that language and that sound inside the genre, our bouyon Guadeloupe page tells the Guadeloupe branch of bouyon, from its Dominican origins to its current sessions.

“Fè Sa Fann”: the bridge record with Lejuh, produced by DJ Taffy, in 2024

The second record of the file is the one that connects LGC to the centre of the scene. “Fè Sa Fann” was released on March 6, 2024, published under the names of LGC, Lejuh, Minibertol and Kenan Gavarin, in the French language according to the listing, at 160 BPM, the fast tempo of bouyon made for dancing. The video of the record is referenced under the title “LGC x Lejuh - Fè Sa Fann (Bouyon 2024)”, with a production signed by DJ Taffy. The video title says the nature of the record: a meeting between two names, presented as a bouyon of the year 2024, and not as a discreet feature buried in the credits.

Lejuh is, in the current state of the sources, the most visible name of Guadeloupe bouyon: in December 2025, CCN, Caraib Creole News, named him first among the emblematic bouyon artists of the moment in Guadeloupe. That the second known record of LGC is a record shared with him, produced by DJ Taffy, places LGC from 2024 inside the first working circle of the scene, that of the studios and the official videos, and not at its margins. This profile reports that credit fact for what it is: a collaboration published and titled under the names of both artists.

The writing credits of “Fè Sa Fann” confirm the team of the first record: Dany Ledreck, Julio Polydamas and Kénan Gavarin are again the three published writers. Continuity is the salient fact of the LGC file: where many names of the platform generation change teams with every record, this one moves forward with the same trio of writers on its first two releases, a year apart. The published label line of the record lines up the names of the team and its partners, LGC, Lejuh, Minibertol and Kenan Gavarin, as if the release were carried collectively by the workshop itself.

A separately published clue strengthens that link with Lejuh, and this profile presents it as a clue, not as proof of identity. The management and collaborations contact published under the official Lejuh videos, including “BIG BELLY”, is an address that joins the name of Lejuh to the initials LGC. This profile does not reproduce that address, which belongs to the professional contact of the artist, but it keeps the editorial fact: in the own posts of the Lejuh team, the initials LGC are attached to his name. LGC and Lejuh therefore share, at minimum, a contact structure and one record in common. To say more would be invention, and this profile stops there.

The team behind the initials: writers, producers, labels

To read the LGC file correctly, follow the names that return, because they form the only biography available for the project: its biography through credits. Dany David Ledreck is the most constant name, credited on the writing of “Voyé’y Bay”, “Fè Sa Fann”, “Manzèl Good”, “Copain Copine” and “Make It Clap”. Kénan Gavarin, also written Kenan or Kenen Gavarin depending on the listing, signs the records of 2023 and 2024 and reappears in the label names of “Fè Sa Fann” and “Manzèl Good”. Jean Gardiole enters the writing team from “Manzèl Good” on and stays on the two records of 2025, before being credited as a voice on “Woody Wood”, the D.O.C FIX DEM record.

The name of Julio Polydamas needs particular attention, because it builds a documented bridge with Lejuh. On the Lewismelo album “Premières Notes”, the record “ALOUETTE”, which showcases Lejuh, carries julio jordan polydamas as its sole published writer. The same surname, in the form Julio Polydamas, signs the LGC records of 2023 and 2024. That overlap is a fact of published credits, and this profile publishes it as such. By contrast, no source consulted says explicitly that Julio Polydamas is Lejuh, nor what exact role he holds inside LGC: writer, member, or both. Automatic profiles will turn that overlap into an asserted identity; here, it remains what it is, a solid overlap and an open question.

On the production side, the file publishes two distinct signatures according to the periods. DJ Taffy produced “Fè Sa Fann” in 2024, according to the title of the official video. Magbeatz then produced “Copain Copine” and “Make It Clap” in 2025. The move from a producer of the Guadeloupe bouyon scene to Magbeatz, on the two singles of 2025, comes with a widening of the writing credits, with the arrival of O’neal Chatelot on both records and of Déreck Ciani on “Copain Copine”. The LGC team therefore grows record after record, while keeping its core, which is the signature of a workshop getting organised rather than a name of circumstance.

On the structure side, the listings publish label names that tell the same progressive organisation: the names of the team members themselves on the records of 2024, then LGC Music on “Copain Copine” in January 2025, then Minibertol and Jano on “Make It Clap” in May 2025. The existence of a label under the name of the initials, LGC Music, is a published listing fact, and it counts: a name that gives itself its own release structure is no longer only a platform pseudonym. This profile keeps that fact, without inventing either the legal form or the internal roles of that structure, which no source consulted publishes.

2024 and 2025: Manzèl Good, Copain Copine, Make It Clap, Woody Wood

After “Fè Sa Fann”, the catalogue continues at a regular pace. “Manzèl Good” was released on September 18, 2024, published under the names of LGC, Jano and Kenan Gavarin, at 157 BPM, classed World by the platform, with four published writers: Dany David Ledreck, Jean Gardiole, Kenen Gavarin and Julio Polydamas. The title, which means the young lady is fine, the girl is good, in the Creole of the scene, extends the party record vein of the project, and it marks the lasting entry of Jean Gardiole into the writing team, a few months before he appears as a voice in the neighbouring circuit.

“Copain Copine”, featuring Dézod, opens the year 2025, on January 3, on the LGC Music label, at 160 BPM. The listing credits LGC and Dézod on background vocals, four writers, Dany David Ledreck, Jean Gardiole, Déreck Ciani and O’neal Chatelot, and Magbeatz on production. A video comes with the record. The format is that of the accomplice duet announced by the title, boyfriend girlfriend, pal to pal, and the listing marks a step: it is the first record of the file released on the label named after the initials, with the widened writing team complete.

“Make It Clap” follows on May 30, 2025, published under the names of Minibertol and Jano, at 160 BPM, in the French language according to the listing, with LGC credited on vocals, three writers, O’neal Chatelot, Dany Ledreck and Jean Gardiole, and Magbeatz again on production. With a little over a hundred published Shazams, it carries the highest counter among the records of the catalogue whose listing exposes that figure, ahead of “Copain Copine” and “Fè Sa Fann”. This profile quotes those counters for what they are, platform orders of magnitude, and not career figures.

The last trace of the file widens the circle further. “Woody Wood”, released on December 19, 2025 under the name of D.O.C FIX DEM, brings together D.O.C FIX DEM, Judi Silva and LGC as performers, at 160 BPM, with Jean Gardiole credited on vocals and writing, Judi Silva on vocals and writing, Landry Mette on writing and production and Clarisse Daryl on mixing. D.O.C FIX DEM is a name the readers of this site know: it appears among the composers of “BIG BELLY” by Lejuh. The presence of LGC on that record, alongside a composer of the Lejuh circle and with Jean Gardiole, the recurring writer of its own catalogue, closes the loop of the file: LGC circulates inside exactly the network its credits had drawn since 2023.

What LGC does not yet say, and what its records already say

The limits of the file are easy to list, and this profile lists them. No source consulted publishes what the initials LGC stand for. None publishes the member list of the project, nor the real name of the voice credited as LGC, nor a place of birth or residence, nor a birth date. No interview, no press release and no written biography were found. No album or EP is documented: the verified catalogue is made of singles and one guest appearance. No official account was verified by opening it during the research, so the schema sameAs field stays empty and this page links to no social profile.

A method limit specific to this name must be added: LGC is also a common set of initials, carried by companies, older labels and at least one unrelated techno record, and search engines drown the bouyon scene under those foreign results. This profile kept only the occurrences whose credits, labels or collaborators cross check with the bouyon scene of the French Antilles: the recurring trio of writers, Lejuh, DJ Taffy, Magbeatz and D.O.C FIX DEM form the chain of cross checks that guarantees that the six records told here belong to the same project.

What the records already say, by contrast, is consistent. A language: Guadeloupean Creole, from the full lyrics of “Voyé’y Bay” on. A tempo: 157 to 160 BPM on the listings that publish it, that of dance bouyon. A method: regular singles, a stable team of writers that widens without scattering, a label under the name of the initials from 2025 on, and collaborations that place it inside the circle of Lejuh and D.O.C FIX DEM, the central names of its Guadeloupe generation. A project that holds those four lines across three years of releases is no catalogue accident, even when it refuses to tell its own story.

To hear LGC, start with “Voyé’y Bay” for the text and the language, then “Fè Sa Fann” for the meeting with Lejuh, and “Make It Clap” for the complete team of 2025. Then place those records inside bouyon gwada through our bouyon Guadeloupe page and our bouyon playlist. If the project one day publishes the explanation of its initials, a member list or a first long project, this profile will be completed, dated and corrected, like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

What do the initials LGC stand for?

No source consulted publishes it, and this profile does not invent it. The published credits only show that the initials are attached to the name of Lejuh in the management contact published under his official videos, and that a label named LGC Music released “Copain Copine” in 2025. The expansion of the initials remains an open question of the file.

Who writes the records of LGC?

A recurring team, according to the credits published by the platforms: Dany David Ledreck, present on almost every record, Julio Polydamas and Kénan Gavarin on the records of 2023 and 2024, Jean Gardiole from “Manzèl Good” on, and O’neal Chatelot and Déreck Ciani on the records of 2025. DJ Taffy produced “Fè Sa Fann” and Magbeatz produced “Copain Copine” and “Make It Clap”.

What is the link between LGC and Lejuh?

One record in common and a shared contact structure, according to the published sources. “Fè Sa Fann”, released on March 6, 2024 and produced by DJ Taffy, is presented as an “LGC x Lejuh” record. The management contact published under the official Lejuh videos also joins his name to the initials LGC. No source says that LGC and Lejuh are the same person, and this profile does not say it either.

What are the verified records of LGC?

Six records: “Voyé’y Bay” in 2023, “Fè Sa Fann” with Lejuh and “Manzèl Good” in 2024, “Copain Copine” with Dézod and “Make It Clap” in 2025, and “Woody Wood” with D.O.C FIX DEM and Judi Silva, released on December 19, 2025. All appear as singles; no album or EP by LGC is documented.

What is “Voyé’y Bay” about?

The full lyrics, published by Shazam, are in Guadeloupean Creole: the record turns on those who watch the lives of others, invent stories and comment on TikTok, and its hook sends the gossip back to its authors, maco makrel, the busybodies, all day long. It is one of the rare records of the file whose complete text is published and checkable.

Where can I hear LGC?

Its six documented records are on the platforms: “Voyé’y Bay”, “Fè Sa Fann” with Lejuh, “Manzèl Good”, “Copain Copine”, “Make It Clap” and “Woody Wood”. Start with “Voyé’y Bay” for the text and the language, then “Fè Sa Fann”. For the bouyon of the same scene, play our bouyon playlist.

Behind those three letters, the published credits draw a stable writing team around Dany David Ledreck, and collaborations with Lejuh, Dézod, D.O.C FIX DEM and Judi Silva.

Is LGC a group or a solo artist?

The sources do not settle it. The platforms credit LGC in the singular, as a voice and a performer, which looks like an individual stage name. But the constancy of the writing team and the existence of a label named LGC Music, on “Copain Copine” in 2025, draw a structure that goes beyond a single person. This site therefore files LGC among groups, out of caution.

Who produced “Fè Sa Fann” by LGC and Lejuh?

DJ Taffy, according to the title of the official video, “LGC x Lejuh - Fè Sa Fann (Bouyon 2024)”. The track came out on March 6, 2024, at 160 BPM, published under the names of LGC, Lejuh, Minibertol and Kenan Gavarin. The writing goes to Dany Ledreck, Julio Polydamas and Kénan Gavarin, the same trio as on “Voyé’y Bay”.

What is “Make It Clap” by LGC?

“Make It Clap” is a single released on May 30, 2025, published under the names of Minibertol and Jano, at 160 BPM, with LGC credited on vocals. It is written by O’neal Chatelot, Dany Ledreck and Jean Gardiole and produced by Magbeatz. With a little over a hundred published Shazams, it carries the highest counter in the catalogue among the pages that show it.

Who sings with LGC on “Copain Copine”?

Dézod, credited on background vocals alongside LGC. “Copain Copine” opened the year 2025, on January 3, on the LGC Music label, at 160 BPM, with Magbeatz on production and four writers: Dany David Ledreck, Jean Gardiole, Déreck Ciani and O’neal Chatelot. It is the first record of the file released on the label named after the initials.

What is “Woody Wood” with LGC?

“Woody Wood” is a D.O.C FIX DEM record released on December 19, 2025, bringing together D.O.C FIX DEM, Judi Silva and LGC, at 160 BPM. Jean Gardiole and Judi Silva are credited on vocals and writing, Landry Mette on writing and production, Clarisse Daryl on mixing. It is the most recent trace of LGC.

What does “Manzèl Good” mean?

In the Creole of the scene, the title means roughly the young lady is fine, the girl is good. “Manzèl Good” came out on September 18, 2024, published under the names of LGC, Jano and Kenan Gavarin, at 157 BPM, and extends the party vein of the project. It marks the lasting entry of Jean Gardiole into the writing team.

Does LGC make bouyon gwada?

Yes. Its records are written in Guadeloupean Creole, from the full lyrics of “Voyé’y Bay” to the 2025 singles, and run between 157 and 160 BPM on the pages that publish the tempo, the speed of dance bouyon. That is the heart of bouyon gwada, the Guadeloupe branch of the bouyon born in Dominica.

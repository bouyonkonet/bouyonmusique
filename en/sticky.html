Sticky: at the head of two bouyon credit rolls, at Riddim Box Studio

Published October 20, 2026

Sticky is a bouyon artist whose verified file fits into two YouTube pages opened in full. On the first, “We Trippy”, he is one of the three headline artists and one of the three writers, alongside Dr Eevil and T.C, over a production by Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze, recorded at Riddim Box Studio and finished by Yw3 Ent. On the second, “Creole cé ca”, he is the lead artist, with Sandy Boo as the featured artist, and the page gives nothing beyond its heading. No biography, no origin, no release date and no official account for Sticky was verified. This profile publishes the two credit rolls, the studio circuit they draw, and it states openly everything it does not know, rather than inflating a common name into an invented portrait.

Who Sticky is: a name at the head of two bouyon credit rolls

Let us start with the exact inventory, because the whole file fits inside it. Sticky appears as a headline artist on two bouyon records opened on YouTube during this research. The first is “We Trippy”, where his name closes the heading “DR Eevil, T.C AND Sticky”, in third position of a trio with no feature hierarchy: the three names stand at the same level, that of lead artists. The second is “Creole cé ca”, where the heading “Sticky ft Sandy Boo” places him as lead artist, with Sandy Boo as guest. Both headings carry the (Bouyon) mention in brackets: it is that tag, published by the pages themselves, that connects Sticky to this site, and no third record tied to this credit roll was verified.

The name appears in two spellings in the sources. The headings of both videos write Sticky, with a capital, the form this site keeps as the artist name. The writing line of “We Trippy” writes “sticky”, in lower case, in the middle of the names of Dr Eevil and T.C: “written by: Dr Eevil ,sticky and T.C”. The difference is that of credit rolls in small scenes, where names are typed fast and settle slowly, but the identity leaves no reasonable doubt: the same name, in the same place, in the same credit of the same record. Sticky, on the other hand, is a very widely carried stage name, and searches on that name alone return artists with no connection at all, in electronic music, jazz and elsewhere: none of those namesakes is the artist of these two pages, and this profile borrows nothing from them, no date, no catalogue, no account.

What the file does not contain must be stated from the outset, with the same clarity. No source consulted gives the origin of Sticky, his age, his path or an interview. No artist page under his name, no social media account confirmed by direct opening and no official photograph was found for the artist of these two records. The “We Trippy” page does publish a contacts block, but it is headed “Connect with dreevil” and gives Dr Eevil details only: it therefore documents nothing for Sticky, not even a social handle. This profile publishes what exists: two records, three posts held on them, lead artist, co-performer and co-writer, and the studio circuit that made them.

We Trippy: the trio shared with Dr Eevil and T.C

The record that carries Sticky most completely is “We Trippy (Bouyon)”, published on YouTube under the heading “DR Eevil, T.C AND Sticky - We Trippy (Bouyon)”. The order of the heading puts Dr Eevil first, T.C second and Sticky third, joined by an “AND” in capitals that presents them as a headline trio, with no guest artist. The (Bouyon) mention, at the end of the heading, is the genre claim of the record, published by the page itself. No release date is given on the opened page, and this profile puts forward none: the record is documented by its credit roll, not by a calendar.

That credit roll is complete, and it is what gives the file its value. Songwriting is credited to the three artists: “written by: Dr Eevil ,sticky and T.C”. Sticky is therefore co-writer of the record he performs, on the same footing as his two partners, and that double post, performer and writer, is the best established career fact of his profile. Production is credited to Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze. Executive production goes to Riddim Box Studio, which is also the recording studio of the record: “Record by : Riddim box studio”. Mixing and mastering are credited to Yw3 Ent. Five lines of description are thus enough to name the whole chain: three writer performers, two producers, one studio that produces and records, one house that finishes the sound.

The end of the description confirms, by contrast, what is missing for Sticky. After the credits, the page gives a “Connect with dreevil” block, with the networks and the contact address of Dr Eevil, then nothing for the other two names of the trio. Sticky and T.C leave that page with no official link published, so the structured data of this profile links to no external account for Sticky. The two partners on the record each have their profile in the same wave of this site, Dr Eevil and T.C, because the same credit roll documents each of them in his post: reading the three profiles together gives the complete sheet of the record, which our bouyon playlist follows among the releases of the circuit.

Creole cé ca: the Creole titled record, featuring Sandy Boo

The second record in the file nearly did not enter it, and the way it enters is part of its information. During the discovery session of this wave, the YouTube page of “Sticky ft Sandy Boo - Creole cé ca (Bouyon)” returned an HTTP 429 error and had not been reopened: the record then rested on search listings only. Opened again during the writing of this profile, on October 20, 2026, the page answers normally, and it can therefore be counted as an opened source. But it contains only its heading: the description is empty, with no credits, no date, no link and no contacts block. The record is documented at the exact level of what its page publishes, no more and no less.

The heading, on its own, gives three facts and one reading. The three facts: Sticky is the lead artist of the record, Sandy Boo is its featured artist, flagged by the “ft” of the heading, and the record is tagged (Bouyon), like “We Trippy”. The reading concerns the three words of the title, “Creole cé ca”, which read as an Antillean Creole sentence written in phonetic spelling, on the model of “Creole, that is it”: a claim of Creole identity set as a title, in the spirit of the assertive headings of bouyon. This profile presents that reading as a reading of the heading, not as a fact about lyrics: no lyrics of the record are published on the opened page, no language of performance is specified there, and nothing allows the content of the track to be described beyond its title. Our page on bouyon and Kwéyòl describes the place of the Creole language in the genre, which is the context of this heading.

Sandy Boo, the guest on the record, becomes through this page a documented collaborator of Sticky, and nothing more at this stage. No other primary page under her name was opened during this research, no biography and no verified account documents her, and she therefore has no profile of her own on this site: she appears here as a credit roll name, in her exact place, that of the feature on “Creole cé ca”. The situation mirrors that of Sticky on other scenes: in small circuits, names travel from heading to heading long before they have pages of their own, and fixing the verified collaboration, one record, two names, one bouyon tag, is the honest way to keep it.

The circuit of the record: Riddim Box Studio and Yw3 Ent

The verified world of Sticky fits into the credit rolls of his two records, and that world has a readable organisation. On “We Trippy”, Riddim Box Studio holds two posts that larger productions separate: executive production, that is the organisation and financing of the session, and the recording itself. Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze sign the musical production, Yw3 Ent the mixing and mastering. Around Sticky, the record is therefore the work of a small, complete and named team, not of an anonymous collective: every post has a name, and those names are the only elements of professional biography the file can line up without inventing. The word riddim, at the heart of the studio name, is that of the instrumental loops over which the singers of the genre lay their voices, which our page on the bouyon riddim presents.

The name of Yw3 Ent furthermore connects this record to a studio circuit this site already documents record by record. The same finishing name appears, in the form Yw3 Ent (DiYoute), in the recording, mixing and mastering posts of “Come Here”, the record by Ras B with Skrazy and Dboss, and in the form Yw3 Studio on the mixing and mastering of “Tropikini”, by Real Reel. “We Trippy” was therefore finished by a workshop that works for other artists of the same bouyon basin, and it is that cross check, and that alone, that places the scene of Sticky: the studio circuit where his records were made. No source says where Sticky himself comes from, and this profile therefore places his recordings, not his person.

The context of the genre recalls why these small teams matter in the history of bouyon. Bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s. Bouyon soca was started in Dominica in 2003 by Dada, according to the first hand testimony of Sean “Smokiller” Francis, a Dominican-born producer and a participant in that era. A generation later, part of the genre is made exactly like “We Trippy”: three voices writing together, two producers, one studio that records and one house that finishes, then a direct publication on YouTube, with no label named in the credit roll. Sticky belongs, through his two opened records, to that workshop generation: that is little biography, but it is a precise place in the manufacturing chain of current bouyon, and our bouyon songs page sets it among the records of the genre.

What the file does not say: the limits, stated

This profile ends with its limits, because they form half of its information. No release date is published on the two opened pages, neither for “We Trippy” nor for “Creole cé ca”, and no label is named in their credit rolls, beyond the studio credits. No tempo, no length and no description of performance is given by the pages at the level this site documents them at, so this profile describes neither the voice of Sticky nor his place in the records, chorus, verse or bridge. No official account, no artist page and no biography of Sticky was verified, and the structured data of this page links to no external account.

The verified scope fits into two records and one chain of credits. The first, “We Trippy”, is fully documented by its opened page: headline trio with Dr Eevil and T.C, songwriting shared by the three, production by Jackie Beats and DJ Blaze, executive production and recording by Riddim Box Studio, mixing and mastering by Yw3 Ent. The second, “Creole cé ca”, is documented by its opened heading alone: Sticky as lead artist, Sandy Boo as feature, bouyon tag, and a page with an empty description. Anything circulating elsewhere under the name Sticky with no demonstrable link to these two credit rolls should be read as a possible namesake, the name being one of the most carried in popular music, and not as a continuation of this file.

The listening, while waiting for what comes next, fits into two records, in the order of their documentation: “We Trippy” first, because its complete credit roll lets Sticky be heard inside his whole working frame, the trio, the producers and the studio, then “Creole cé ca”, to hear him as headline artist, with Sandy Boo as guest. If Sticky one day publishes an artist page, an official account, a biography or complete sheets for these records, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site. In the meantime, it publishes what the pages give, no more and no less: a bouyon artist of two opened records, co-writer at Riddim Box Studio, alongside Dr Eevil and T.C, whose profiles this site publishes in the same wave.

Where does Sticky come from?

No source consulted publishes his personal origin, and this profile does not invent it. Sticky is therefore placed through his studio circuit, with no island of birth stated.

Does Sticky have verified official accounts?

No official account of Sticky was verified by direct opening during this research, carried out on October 20, 2026. The “We Trippy” page publishes a contacts block, but it concerns Dr Eevil only, under the heading “Connect with dreevil”: it gives no link for Sticky or for T.C. The structured data of this profile therefore links to no external account for Sticky.

Jauwan Hadaway

By Smokiller · Published October 23, 2026

Jauwan Hadaway enters this site through a track that his own YouTube channel labels plainly: “I Don't Want (Vincy Bouyon) 2026”. The video went up on April 24, 2025, runs 2 minutes 16 and had passed 12,000 views when we opened it. The description is only a few lines long, with the phrase “For The Culture” followed by the flag of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and the tags file it under Vincentian soca. This profile stays short, because the opened page gives no biography, no detailed credits and no birthplace.

A track billed as Vincy Bouyon

The video title pairs two words that say where the song is coming from: Vincy, the common nickname for Saint Vincent on the soca circuit, and bouyon, the genre born in Dominica. On his channel, Jauwan Hadaway is not describing a remix or a cover; he files his own song under that label. The page tags point the same way, with vincy soca, svg soca, soca 2025 and bouyon 2025. The blend is nothing unusual: bouyon soca, a name coined by Dada in Dominica in 2003, has travelled through the carnivals of the neighbouring islands for a long time.

One date detail is worth flagging. The title announces 2026, while the video was posted on April 24, 2025 and its tags speak of 2025. That gap is common on circuit channels, where the year shown often targets the coming carnival season. This site reports both dates as they appear, without deciding for the artist.

What the page shows, and nothing more

The description is brief: the phrase “For The Culture” with the Vincentian flag, a note that the song is on every streaming platform, an Instagram handle, jauwan_bankz, and an offer of free dubplates for DJs. It is the page of an artist who works with sound systems and carnival DJs, and says so himself. No producer, writer or studio is credited.

The flag and the Vincy label clearly place the release within the Saint Vincent circuit, and that is how this profile presents it. They do not say where Jauwan Hadaway was born or where he lives, and this site does not turn a flag into a birth certificate. Of the 79 videos on the channel checked during this research, only one carries the word bouyon in its title, and this profile rests on that one.

The limits of the file

The verifiable file on October 23, 2026 fits on one page: a video on the artist's channel, a title that displays bouyon, a short description and a set of tags. No interview, no biography, no production credit. The other titles on the channel show no tie to the genre, so they are not presented here as bouyon tracks.

This profile will be expanded if new primary sources appear, like the other pages followed in new bouyon in 2026. For context, the page on bouyon riddims explains how the genre travels from one island to the next.

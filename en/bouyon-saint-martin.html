Bouyon in Saint Martin: small island, sharp producers

By Smokiller · Published September 10, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Saint Martin counts in the new geography of bouyon thanks to its producers, foremost among them Denmath, a beatmaker originally from SXM, a specialist in bouyon, kompa and shatta, known for his riddims Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim.

A bridge between the Caribbean and France

Based between Nantes and Paris, Denmath illustrates the path of the new generation of producers: Caribbean roots, a studio in France, collaborations across all the Antilles. See his dedicated page: Denmath.

Frequently asked questions

Where is bouyon popular?

Born in Dominica, bouyon is now central in Guadeloupe and Martinique and present in France, Saint Lucia, Saint Martin and the Caribbean diaspora. In Saint Martin, this site documents producers like Denmath and bouyon acts from Sint Maarten, the Dutch side of the island, such as BRG Hollywood and the Tolly Boys.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica. The genre was born there in the late 1980s: it was in Roseau, the island's capital, that the band WCK created it in 1988, mixing jing ping, cadence-lypso and electronic rhythms. Bouyon remains the sound of Roseau carnival today.

Which bouyon producers come from Saint Martin?

The main name is Denmath, whose legal name is Mathurin DENMATH Denis: a producer, sound engineer and artistic director from Saint Martin, of Haitian heritage, based between Nantes and Paris. A specialist in bouyon, kompa and shatta, he is known for his riddims Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim. See his page Denmath.

Which bouyon singer grew up in Saint Martin?

Tamillia, full name Tamillia Chance, grew up in Saint Martin in the island's French and Dutch cultures and was on The Voice Kids France at 14. She is the voice of Don't Care, DJ Blax's single released on October 16, 2026, whose video was shot in St Maarten, the Dutch side of the island. See DJ Blax.

Who is BRG Hollywood?

BRG Hollywood, whose real name is Jaheim Meyers, is a bouyon artist from Sint Maarten. His track "We Not Going Home", with C4, released on October 31, 2025 on the BankrollGang label, made him the first music artist from the Dutch side of the island to reach one million views on YouTube, a milestone praised by Prime Minister Dr Luc Mercelina.

Are Tolly Boys and Tallyboy the same artist?

No. The Tolly Boys, with an o, are a bouyon act from Sint Maarten, built around the songwriter Youry Fleming, known for "Get on Bad", "Oú Paté Vwey" and "GoDownGoDown", then for the YMW Tolly Boys label. Tallyboy, with an a, is a solo artist from Saint Lucia. Not the same person, not the same island, not the same catalogue.

Who is Wizito?

Wizito is the stage name of Bernard Gumbs. His three verified tracks, "Payé" with Youry Ymw in 2023, "Love Me" in 2024 and "TunTun" in 2025, came out on the YMW Tolly Boys label. A mix of local releases from the St Maarten carnival 2024 included "Love Me", which ties him to the Saint Martin bouyon circuit, with no personal origin published.

Is Ebony Empress from Saint Martin?

She lives there, but she was born in Dominica. Ebony Empress, whose real name is Everlyne Walters, was born in the village of Lagoon, in Portsmouth, and is now based in Saint Martin. Breaking through in 2018 with "Quality Bumper", she finished second runner-up in Saint Martin's Senior Calypso in 2024 and was nominated at the Caribbean Music Awards 2026.

Saint Martin or Sint Maarten: what is the difference for bouyon?

It is the same island, split in two: Sint Maarten, also written St Maarten, is its Dutch side, and Saint-Martin its French side. For bouyon, BRG Hollywood and the Tolly Boys are documented on the Sint Maarten side, Tamillia grew up in the island's French and Dutch cultures, and Denmath is presented as being from Saint Martin, with no side specified.

Is bouyon popular in Saint Martin?

Yes, and the sources show it even in schools. A university study carried out in a Sint Maarten primary school describes the Tolly Boys as local bouyon performers whose most famous song the pupils sang in class. In 2026, BRG Hollywood's "We Not Going Home" passed one million views on YouTube, a first for the Dutch side of the island.

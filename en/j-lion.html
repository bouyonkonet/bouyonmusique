J Lion: the voice two carnival circuits share in 2026

Published October 20, 2026

J Lion is an artist whose name, in 2026, runs in two carnival circuits at once, and that double passage is the whole interest of this profile. On one side, DJ JEL, a reference for soca mixes, lines up five J Lion tracks in his official selection for Vincy Mas 2026, the carnival of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, among them “Take Another Man”, “Shake It” and “Pon Pam”. On the other side, the bouyon mixes of Dominica hold him too: “No Love”, a duet with Ridge, appears in the Bouyon Passport 2026 selection, and “Take Another Man” carries the J Lion passage of the “Bouyon Music 2026 Mix” by Smokiller for Bouyon Shattaz, presented as the best of new Dominican bouyon. No biography, no documented country of origin and no full name appear in the sources opened: this profile gives the six verified tracks, tells the two circuits that carry them, and clearly states what it does not know.

Who J Lion is: six verified tracks, no biography

Start with what can be verified, track by track. The episode page of the “2026 Vincy Soca Take Over” by DJ JEL, published on 17 June 2026 on his site jelinthemix.com and presented as the soca songs to know for Vincy Mas 2026, lists five credits under the name J Lion, written sometimes Jlion, sometimes J Lion: “Shake It”, with Romey, Shatta TBG and producer Joshybeatz; “Jam Dem”, subtitled Old SKL, with Joshybeatz; “Take Another Man”, solo; “Pon Pam”, with Joshybeatz; and “Bhaad Dab”, with Joshybeatz and JC Productions. Five tracks in a single season selection is not an appearance, it is a presence.

A second independent selection confirms four of those tracks for the same season. The Vincy Mas 2026 edition of the “Soca Passport” podcast by DJ Close Connections, subtitled “Get Familiar With The Sounds of Vincy Mas 2026”, carries “Take Another Man”, “Shake It” with Romey, “Pon Pam” and “Jam Dem”, in the same company of Vincentian headliners such as Skinny Fabulous and Problem Child. Two different DJs, two published lists, the same titles: the season discography of J Lion therefore rests on crossed sources, not on a single list.

The sixth track comes from the other circuit. The “Bouyon Passport 2026” edition of the same DJ Close Connections podcast, dated 4 February 2026, lines up “No Love”, by Ridge and J Lion, inside the Dominican bouyon selection: Trilla G, Reo, Shelly, TK International, Asa Bantan, Signal Band. The Shazam similar songs listings for that same bouyon circle also carry “No Love”, by Ridge and J Lion. And the “Bouyon Music 2026 Mix Vol. 1” by Smokiller, published on YouTube by Bouyon Shattaz as the best new bouyon songs of Dominica in 2026, names J Lion in its description and places “Take Another Man” at 6 minutes 33 of its chapter tracklist.

Beyond those lists, the research carried out for this profile in October 2026 found no biography, no interview, no artist page on the platforms consulted and no confirmed official account. No source opened says where J Lion comes from, nor his full name, nor his age, nor his label. This profile therefore does not choose between the two islands his tracks cross: it documents circuits, not an invented origin. The sameAs field of this page stays empty, for lack of an opened and verified link.

The Vincy Mas circuit: five tracks in the DJ JEL selection

Vincy Mas is the carnival of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, which DJ JEL presents in his episode as the hottest carnival in the Caribbean. Each season, his “Take Over” selections work as a sound guide to the carnival: the tracks to know before the road, arranged in the order of a mix of nearly three hours. For an artist, appearing once is a selection; appearing five times, as J Lion does in 2026, is a whole season campaign, carried by the same production network.

That network has a name that returns throughout his credits: Joshybeatz. Four of the five Vincentian J Lion tracks carry the producer signature as a billed collaboration: “Shake It”, “Jam Dem”, “Pon Pam” and “Bhaad Dab”. “Bhaad Dab” also associates JC Productions, and the remaining title, “Take Another Man”, is billed solo in the DJ JEL list. The season profile therefore reads clearly: in this circuit, J Lion is a voice of a soca production workshop that lines up carnival tracks in series, in the same way as the other recurring voices of the list, such as Jasper YMC, Voltage TBG or Versi Donn, who are also tied to Joshybeatz track after track.

The collaborations draw the same world. “Shake It” brings together J Lion and Romey, with Shatta TBG; the Soca Passport edition of the title credits it more simply to J Lion and Romey. The titles themselves belong to the register of road soca: dance instructions, short and direct titles, “Shake It”, “Jam Dem”, “Pon Pam”, built for fetes and carnival trucks. None of those elements allows a biography to be written, but together they describe a way of working: that of a season voice, employed by a producer, on the carnival of one island, with tracks that run in the reference mixes before they even exist as streaming pages that can be found.

The bouyon circuit: No Love with Ridge, and the Smokiller mix

The second circuit is the one of this site: the bouyon of Dominica. Its key document is the “Bouyon Passport 2026” selection of the DJ Close Connections podcast, published on 4 February 2026, which works as the bouyon counterpart of the soca lists: Trilla G and Ridge open, then Reo, Shelly, TK International, Faithii, Asa Bantan and Signal Band unroll the Dominican season. “No Love”, by Ridge and J Lion, appears there between “Dohtish Love” by Reo and Nessa Preppy and “You Mad” by Reo. For J Lion, it is another type of credit than the Vincentian titles: a duet with Ridge, one of the central names of current bouyon, at the heart of an entirely Dominican list.

The Smokiller mix extends that passage one step further. “Bouyon Music 2026 Mix Vol. 1”, published on YouTube, announces in its description “the best new Dominica bouyon music of 2026 from Trilla-G, J Lion, Tipsy”, and its chapter tracklist places “Take Another Man” at 6 minutes 33, just after “Come Ova” by KTB and before tracks by Tipsy, by Machel Montano with Skinny Fabulous, then by Trilla-G. In other words, the solo title DJ JEL filed under Vincy Mas is the same one the Dominican bouyon mix holds as a Dominica new release. The two circuits do not only share an artist: they share a track.

That back and forth is nothing exceptional in current Caribbean mechanics, and it helps place J Lion without inventing an address for him. Bouyon soca, the branch fusing Dominican bouyon and soca, was born of these circulations: started in Dominica in 2003 by producer Dada according to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer and a participant in that era, then popularised by Triple Kay, it made travelling voices a norm between Dominica, Saint Lucia, Antigua and Saint Vincent. J Lion, in 2026, is one of those voices: documented in Saint Vincent by the soca lists, documented in Dominica by the bouyon lists, and claimed by no biography found.

What tracklists prove, and what they do not say

The nature of these sources has to be stated precisely, because this whole profile rests on them. A DJ tracklist proves that a track ran under a name, in a dated selection, for a given carnival. When two independent DJs, DJ JEL and DJ Close Connections, publish the same titles for the same Vincy Mas season, and when a third document, the Smokiller mix, takes one of them up again for Dominica, that convergence counts as documentation of a season discography. It is the same method this site applies to the other thin profiles of the scene, from artists found in Sewo Jam to bands found on official posters.

A tracklist, on the other hand, gives neither an exact release date, nor full writing credits, nor a label, nor listening figures. It does not give an origin either: appearing in a Vincy Mas selection does not make J Lion Vincentian, any more than appearing in a bouyon mix makes him Dominican, and this profile refuses both shortcuts. Carnival artists record where producers call them, and the 2026 seasons show precisely a regional market of voices, between Saint Vincent soca and Dominica bouyon, connected by the same DJ passers.

The spelling itself tells that life in lists: the sources write the name sometimes J Lion, sometimes Jlion, without a space. This site keeps the form J Lion, the most frequent in the solo titles such as “Take Another Man” and in the description of the Smokiller mix, and flags the variant so readers can find the same tracks under both forms. In the same way, “Jam Dem” is listed with the mention Old SKL by DJ JEL, reproduced here as published, without any song page allowing more to be said about it.

What this profile claims, and what it leaves open

Let us sum up the record. Claimed and sourced twice: for Vincy Mas 2026, DJ JEL lists five J Lion tracks, “Shake It” with Romey, Shatta TBG and Joshybeatz, “Jam Dem” with Joshybeatz, “Take Another Man”, “Pon Pam” with Joshybeatz and “Bhaad Dab” with Joshybeatz and JC Productions, and the Soca Passport selection by DJ Close Connections confirms four of them. Claimed and sourced: “No Love”, by Ridge and J Lion, appears in the Bouyon Passport 2026 selection of 4 February 2026, and “Take Another Man” appears in the “Bouyon Music 2026 Mix” by Smokiller for Bouyon Shattaz, presented as the best of new Dominican bouyon.

Open means: country and home town of origin not documented, full name unknown to this record, path before the 2026 season not documented, label unknown, complete discography unknown beyond the six list titles, no official account confirmed, no streaming page found during the research. The six tracks in this page schema are therefore exactly the six tracklist titles, not one more and not one less. If an official page, an interview or a documented discography appears, this profile will be completed, dated and corrected like every page on this site.

In the meantime, listening starts with the documents themselves: the DJ JEL tracklist for Vincy Mas 2026, to hear J Lion inside the full Vincentian selection, the “Bouyon Music 2026 Mix” by Smokiller for his Dominican passage, then our bouyon soca page and our bouyon playlist for the branch that historically connects the two circuits his tracks cross.

Who is J Lion?

What are the verified songs of J Lion?

Anonymous Gyals: two bouyon records, a name that keeps its mystery

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Anonymous Gyals is an emerging bouyon artist whose name states the programme: stay anonymous. On the platforms she appears under the spelling Anonymous Gyal’s, with two verified records, “PA FÈ CHICHI”, ranked by Spotify in its The Sound of Bouyon selection, and “Cho caché”. In Guadeloupe, a whole night carries her name: the Baddies Bash organised by Effervescence for her birthday, on Friday October 30, 2026. No legal name, no biography and no verified official account were found. This profile therefore gathers the three public traces of the artist, reads them closely, and states plainly everything that is not documented yet.

Who Anonymous Gyals is: a bouyon artist who chooses anonymity

Anonymous Gyals poses a rare problem for sites that document music: how do you write the profile of an artist whose stage name claims anonymity, and whose public trace fits in two records and one party poster? The answer of this site is the same as for all its thin dossiers: write exactly what is verified, tie every fact to its source, and name the blanks instead of filling them. This page is therefore an emerging artist profile in the strict sense: dense on the records, precise on the one public date, and silent on everything else, because everything else does not exist in the sources yet.

First established fact: the name circulates in two forms. The bouyon artist lists followed by this site write Anonymous Gyals, in the plural. The platforms write Anonymous Gyal’s, with the apostrophe of the English possessive, as if it meant the girls of one gyal, or her crew. No source settles whether this is a solo artist with her team, a duo, or an all female collective. This profile uses the list form, Anonymous Gyals, in its title, reports the platform form, Anonymous Gyal’s, wherever it names a record, and does not merge the two into an invented identity.

Second established fact: the anonymity is consistent with the rest of the record. No legal name is published in the sources consulted, no face is verifiably tied to the records, and no official social account has been confirmed. Where many emerging artists of the scene, from Reo to the singers followed by this site, build their name through faces and documented collaborations, Anonymous Gyals leaves only music and one party date. Whether that is a strategy, a protection or simply documentation lagging behind, this profile does not know, and says so.

Third established fact, and the most important for a reader of this site: the tie to bouyon does not come from rumour, but from a platform ranking. Spotify places “PA FÈ CHICHI” in The Sound of Bouyon, its reference selection for the genre, among records by central artists of the scene. That public, verifiable ranking is what makes Anonymous Gyals a bouyon artist in the documentary sense of the term, and what justifies her place in the pages of BouyonMusique.com, between the profiles of the artists who surround her in that same selection.

PA FÈ CHICHI: the record ranked in Spotify The Sound of Bouyon

The first verified record by Anonymous Gyals is “PA FÈ CHICHI”, running 3 minutes. It appears in The Sound of Bouyon, the reference playlist for the genre published by Spotify under its The Sounds of Spotify banner, which gathers the records the platform classifies in the bouyon sound. In the copy of that selection consulted for this profile, “PA FÈ CHICHI” sits in a neighbourhood that says exactly where Spotify places the artist: “Toto” by Edday, records by Team Bwè Tou Sa and LESTEF KJF BOYZ, “Fès ka pété” by LGC and LeJuh, “Dou dou” by Reo and Litleboy, “Show Me” by Litle Boy and Triple Kay International, and “Shake Your Pampalam” by Asa Bantan.

That neighbourhood matters, because it mixes the two shores of bouyon today: the Dominica of carnival bands and singers, represented by Asa Bantan and Triple Kay International, and the French Antilles scene, that of bouyon gwada and short records built for parties and social feeds. A record ranked there is not a record lost in a catalogue: it has been sorted, by the taxonomies of the platform, into the sound that listeners of the genre look for. For an artist with no published biography, that ranking is the first verifiable form of recognition.

The title itself speaks the language of that scene. “PA FÈ CHICHI” is written in the Creole of the French Antilles, in capitals, in the style of party records: a direct order, no fuss, understood at a glance in a playlist. This profile will not quote lyrics it has not verified, and will not describe an arrangement it cannot tie to a source: the listing consulted gives the title, the artist and the duration, and that is exactly what is reported here. The rest, the riddim, the voices, the production, will wait for a full record sheet or a published credit.

“PA FÈ CHICHI” is therefore the recommended entry point for discovering Anonymous Gyals, and the record placed first in the structured data of this page. Look for it in The Sound of Bouyon and, for the context of the genre, in our bouyon playlist, which follows the same artist scene, from the founders WCK to the recent releases of the French Antilles.

Cho caché: the second record, and the limit of the sources

The second verified record is “Cho caché”, running 2 minutes 42. It appears as the sixteenth entry in the Beatport Music Releases Pack 3777 listing, a set of Beatport releases from 2026 whose tracklist was carried online. That source is weaker than the Spotify selection: it is an aggregated tracklist, with no artist sheet, no label and no precise release date. This profile therefore cites it for what it establishes, and only for that: a second recording signed Anonymous Gyal’s circulates in the releases of 2026, with a title and a duration.

“Cho caché” extends the linguistic portrait of the first record. Creole and French mix in a short title, hot and hidden, made of two words that every French Antilles audience understands at once. As with “PA FÈ CHICHI”, this profile refuses to invent the content: no quoted lyrics, no detailed theme, no guessed producer. Two verified records nevertheless already draw a signature: short titles, in Creole, cut for the party, signed by a name that refuses to reveal itself. The contrast between records that show themselves and an artist who hides is part of the public identity of Anonymous Gyals, as far as the sources let it be seen.

With two records, the documented catalogue of Anonymous Gyals therefore fits in two lines, and this profile does not stretch it artificially. Other pages on this site began the same way, with one or two verified records, before carnival seasons and festival bills thickened the dossiers. The rule stays the same at every step: a record only enters the list if it can be verifiably tied to the right artist, which, for a name containing the word Anonymous, calls for particular care against namesakes and platform merging errors.

Both records appear in the structured data of this page, as the official listening landmarks of the profile. It is little, but it is real, and it is the material from which the rest can be written: a possible third record, a first credited collaboration, or a bill where the artist would no longer be only the guest of honour of her own night, but a performer booked in her own name.

Baddies Bash in Guadeloupe: a night in her name for her birthday

The third public trace of Anonymous Gyals is a poster, and it is the richest in verifiable detail. The Sparta ticketing site announces a night titled Baddies Bash, Anonymous Gyal’s Birthday, in Guadeloupe, organised by Effervescence. The presentation text of the event gives the frame: Friday October 30, 2026, from 11 pm to 6 am. Tickets are priced at 15 euros for the single pass, and 20 euros for the women duo pass, an offer that already says which audience the night addresses first.

The musical mood announced by the page is “shatta trap zouk dancehall”. That list places the night in the landscape of French Antilles nightlife, where shatta and dancehall frame zouk and trap, and where bouyon circulates in the same DJ selections, as the presence of “PA FÈ CHICHI” in the bouyon ranking of Spotify shows. The page does not promise a concert by Anonymous Gyals: the published line-up names three DJs, Dj Mano, Dj Tira and Jicypie. The artist is the guest of honour of the night, the one whose birthday is being celebrated, and not a performer announced on the programme. This profile reports that distinction exactly, because it describes the current form of her notoriety: a name strong enough to carry a night, before even one documented stage performance.

The precise venue is not published in the elements of the page consulted, which locates the event in Guadeloupe without naming a town or a club. This profile will therefore name neither a town nor a venue. The Guadeloupe tie, on the other hand, is the strongest geographic fact in the dossier: it is in Guadeloupe that the artist has a night in her name, carried by a local organiser, Effervescence, with public ticketing. For an artist of the French Antilles, this kind of birthday night, where the crowd comes to celebrate a name of the scene, is an institution of archipelago nightlife, halfway between a private party and a public consecration.

The date itself deserves a calendar remark. Friday October 30, 2026 falls on the busiest weekend of the bouyon year: that of the World Creole Music Festival in Roseau, Dominica, and of the Dominican Independence season. While Dominica celebrates its festival, neighbouring Guadeloupe makes its own nights dance, and it is in one of them that the name of Anonymous Gyals stands at the top of the bill. Two islands, two scenes, one same Creole party weekend: the contrast sums up the current circulation of the genre, told in our history of bouyon.

What the record does not hold: no name, no members, no official account

As with every thin dossier on this site, the limits are written in plain sight. For Anonymous Gyals, no legal name is published in the sources consulted. No home community is established: Guadeloupe is the place of her birthday night and the market of her Creole records, but this profile does not turn a poster into a birth certificate, and therefore does not present the artist as Guadeloupean by origin, only as an artist whose verified public trace is Guadeloupean.

The shape of the project is not established either. The plural Gyals and the apostrophe of the platform spelling can suggest a collective of girls, the singular possessive can suggest the team of one artist, and the birthday poster, in the singular, suggests one person being celebrated. No source settles it. This site has classed the page as a group in its structured data, for lack of a source saying otherwise, while writing here that this classification is a filing convention, not an established fact about the make-up of the project.

No verified official account was found: no confirmed artist page beyond the platform record listings, and no social account tied with certainty to the artist of the two records. The official links section is therefore empty, deliberately, as the rule of this site requires for names open to namesakes. No precise release date is published for the two records, no label is credited in the sources consulted, and no listening figure is repeated here, because none was recorded in a stable source at the time of the research.

Those blanks are not a failure of the profile: they are its main information. They say where the documentation stands for an artist whom the platforms already rank in the bouyon sound and whose name carries a night in Guadeloupe, but whom neither the press, nor published credits, nor organisers describe yet. If a full record sheet, a production credit or a verifiable official account appears, this page will be completed and re-dated, like every page on BouyonMusique.com.

Why Anonymous Gyals matters: the women new wave of the French Antilles

Anonymous Gyals matters first for what her dossier, thin as it is, shows about the current scene. The bouyon of the French Antilles now produces artists who exist first through platform rankings and parties, before they exist through biographies. One record ranked in The Sound of Bouyon, a second record in the releases of 2026, a birthday night in her name in Guadeloupe: that is the typical path of a nightlife and playlist notoriety, very different from the band careers documented since WCK, and that is why it had to be documented as it is, without dressing it up as a classic journey.

She matters next for the place of women in this history. Bouyon was born in Dominica in 1988, and its founding stories are largely male, from the bands of Roseau to branches like bouyon soca, started by Dada in 2003 according to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller and popularized by Triple Kay, or Wrecketeng. The French Antilles scenes have brought more women voices into the genre, and a name like Anonymous Gyals, which puts the gyals in the plural on its sign and whose night offers a duo pass designed for women, belongs to that movement, even if its exact make-up remains to be documented.

She matters finally as an open dossier, and this site accepts that status. The night of October 30, 2026 in Guadeloupe may be the moment the artist appears publicly under her name, in front of the crowd that came to celebrate her birthday. The records that follow will say whether “PA FÈ CHICHI” and “Cho caché” were the start of a catalogue. In the meantime, listening starts with those two records, in The Sound of Bouyon and in our bouyon playlist, and reading continues with the artists of the same selection, from Asa Bantan to Shelly and Signal Band: it is in that neighbourhood, and nowhere else, that the place of Anonymous Gyals is verifiable today.

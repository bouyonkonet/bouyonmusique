Fallon: the voice that names RnBouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Fallon is an artist Deezer presents, in its Deezer Next 2026 selection, as the carrier of a new fusion: RnBouyon, a bouyon worked through by R&B, balancing softness and energy. The Deezer statement, dated Paris, 4 January 2026 and published on 4 February 2026, places her between Caribbean heritage, contemporary sensitivity and a clear vision, and names two tracks, “Normal” and “Memories”, as the already audible proof of her authentic writing. This profile states exactly what verified sources support, no more and no less: no invented island of origin, no added release dates, only a name, a named style and a program that chose to put her forward.

Who Fallon is: what Deezer says, and what nobody says yet

Fallon enters the public landscape through one precise door: the Deezer Next 2026 selection. Deezer announces the arrival of three artists, Timar, Fallon and Heroe, to kick off 2026, and presents the selection as a set of strong, singular identities embodying the eclecticism, creativity and music renewal of a new generation. In that trio, Timar carries rap, Heroe carries an intimate pop from Normandy, and Fallon carries the meeting of R&B and bouyon. That place, and for now that place alone, defines her publicly.

The Deezer text places her at the crossroads of her Caribbean roots and a contemporary sensitivity. It says she is redrawing the boundaries of urban pop and that she has created her own world. It names no island. It gives no birth name, no town and no path before this selection. The English version of the same statement, reported by RouteNote on 4 February 2026, says the same thing within the same limits: she blends her Caribbean heritage with a modern twist, and tracks like “Normal” and “Memories” already show her strong sense of identity as an artist.

That restraint in the sources imposes restraint in this profile. On this site, an artist profile never fills a blank with invention. When an artist island is verified, it opens the profile, like Grand Bay for Asa Bantan or Roseau for WCK. When it is not, the profile says so. For Fallon, the verifiable address in 2026 is therefore a scene, not an island: the French speaking scene Deezer Next supports from Paris, and the Caribbean as a heritage claimed in her art, with no further geographic detail in the sources opened.

This Fallon must also be separated from other artists of the same name circulating on platforms. Search engines return several Fallon profiles with no link to bouyon. None of those profiles without a link to Deezer Next 2026 was used here. The only Fallon on this page is the Fallon of the February 2026 Deezer statement, the Fallon of RnBouyon, the Fallon of “Normal” and “Memories”. If verified biographical material appears, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

Fallon and RnBouyon: a named fusion of softness and energy

The central word of this profile is RnBouyon, one word, with a capital R, a lower case n and a capital B. It is the exact spelling Deezer uses in its French and English versions, and it is the only spelling attested for this fusion in the sources found. Deezer writes that Fallon names her world RnBouyon, a fusion of R&B and bouyon mixing softness and energy to create a style in her own image. The English version adds that she is shaping this fusion, in the present tense: the style is presented as work in progress, not as an already old genre.

What does that fusion contain, read with care? On one side, bouyon: the genre created in Roseau, Dominica, at the end of the 1980s, fast, carried by keyboards, drum machines and a voice that calls the crowd. On the other side, contemporary R&B: singing rather than chanting, personal writing, and the melodic softness Deezer points to when it speaks of sincere, personal writing. RnBouyon, as Deezer presents it in Fallon, lives in that tension: keeping the energy of bouyon without giving up the softness of R&B.

Our dedicated RnBouyon page tells the branch as a whole. It also recalls an important limit: at this stage of the record, RnBouyon is a name carried first by one artist and one platform, not yet the documented name of a whole scene with its labels, its parties and dozens of artists. A Dominican-born producer, Smokiller, uses the same word as a chapter title, “RnBouyon Instrumental”, in his Bouyon Music 2026 Vol. 1 mix for Bouyon Shattaz. That second, independent use shows the word is circulating. It does not turn RnBouyon into an established genre, and this profile does not present it as one.

That precision protects Fallon as much as it protects the reader. Saying an artist is shaping a fusion, in Deezer words, is an attributed and verifiable fact. Saying she invented an entire genre, that she is its queen or that a whole scene follows her would be a title handed out with no source. On this site, titles are attributed, never stated as facts. Fallon is therefore presented here as Deezer presents her: the named carrier of RnBouyon in 2026, inside the Deezer Next selection.

Deezer Next 2026: the springboard putting her forward

Deezer Next is Deezer program for emerging artists on the French speaking scene. The 2026 statement describes it as a real springboard, supporting talent at every stage of development, in the app and beyond, with stronger visibility and tailored support. In practice, selected artists receive promotion on the app homepage, a dedicated editorial playlist, exposure on Deezer social channels, access to partner festival opportunities and a performance for superfans at the Purple Door Deezer Next event in December.

The program has a history. The Backstage blog recalls that it has supported artists since 2017 and names, among those who passed through that support, Angèle, Tiakola, Pomme, PLK, November Ultra and Saint Levant. For the recent period, Deezer names Marguerite, Miki, RnBoi and R2 as rising voices supported before the 2026 selection. Being chosen in that line does not say Fallon will reach the same level. It says, verifiably, that a professional editorial team judged her world strong enough to be put forward for a full year, alongside a rapper, Timar, and a Norman pop artist, Heroe.

The 2026 selection is presented as eclectic and bold. Deezer stresses that it runs from rap to French variety, by way of R&B and bouyon. That detail matters for bouyon itself: the genre created in Dominica appears, in a Paris statement from a major platform, among the aesthetics shaping today music scene. Fallon is the vector of that presence. Without her and without the word RnBouyon, bouyon would not be named in this selection. With her, it enters the vocabulary of a program that speaks to the whole French speaking world.

Georges Derval, Head of Editorial, Discovery and Trends, in charge of the program, signs the welcome statement: Deezer is delighted to announce the arrival of Timar, Fallon and Heroe, looks forward to supporting their development throughout the year, in the app, on social media and beyond, and cannot wait to see them reach new audiences. For Fallon, 2026 is therefore a documented year of support, not a consecration. This profile tells it as such: an official beginning, dated, with a named platform and precise promises, whose sequel remains to be written.

The bouyon behind RnBouyon: from Roseau to French speaking urban pop

To hear what Fallon fuses, the bouyon itself has to come back into view. The genre was created in Roseau, Dominica, in 1988, by WCK, Windward Caribbean Kulture, fusing cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. The Creole word bouyon names the soup in which everything mixes and nothing is lost. That origin is the address of the genre, and every fusion carrying its name, RnBouyon included, inherits that address, even when the artist carrying it works far from Roseau.

Bouyon then produced branches this site documents one by one. Bouyon soca marries bouyon to the soca of Trinidad: according to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller, founder of Bouyon Shattaz, who was present in the rooms at the time, it was Dada who started the style in Dominica in 2003, and Triple Kay popularised it by adopting it in its own version of bouyon. Wrecketeng, the official spelling given by DJ Cut himself, works sampled melodies and rap vocals, while reketeng, without the W, is the misspelling circulating on the internet. New Bouyon gathers recent and differing uses of the word new, which have to be attributed artist by artist.

RnBouyon sits in that family as the branch of soft singing. Where carnival bouyon pushes the voice toward calling and commanding the crowd, and where Wrecketeng pushes the voice toward rap and sampling, Fallon fusion pulls the voice toward R&B: sung, personal, melodic. Deezer gives no tempo and no technical sheet for this fusion, and this profile invents none. It holds to the sound geography the platform words draw: softness on one side, energy on the other, and in the middle an artist who says she holds both.

That reading also places Fallon in a wider circulation. Bouyon travelled from Dominica to Guadeloupe and Martinique, where it was known as jump up, then to France and the platforms. Artists such as HollyG, in Guadeloupe, carried bouyon gwada to a gold record in France, while collaborations such as Reo with Maurane Voyer, or tracks bringing together Niska, Miimii KDS and Litleboy LSBeats767, show the genre at the scale of the French Antilles. Fallon arrives in that landscape through another door, the Paris editorial streaming door, but the material she names comes from the same pot. Read our history of bouyon for the full chain.

Why Fallon matters in 2026, and what this profile still awaits

Fallon matters first because she names. In a music where labels often travel without an author, she gave her world a precise name, RnBouyon, which a major platform repeated exactly as written in two languages. Naming a fusion makes it searchable, debatable and transmissible. Readers who type RnBouyon land on Fallon, on Deezer Next and, through this page, on the history of bouyon itself. That is exactly the work of this site: connecting every new name to the chain that made it possible, from Roseau to the French Antilles.

Fallon also matters because her selection says something about the state of bouyon in 2026. The genre is no longer only a matter of carnival bands and riddim producers. It enters platform development programs, alongside rap and pop, in a sung and personal form. That entry does not replace the other forms of bouyon, it adds to them. In the same month of February 2026, a Dominican-born producer such as Smokiller uses the word RnBouyon for a mix instrumental, while Deezer consecrates it as the world of a singer. Two uses, two islands of meaning, one word: the branch is young, and that is why it must be documented with care from now on.

This profile therefore ends, as it began, with its limits. The verified Fallon record is thin: one platform statement, its official blog, one English report, two tracks named, one style named, a Caribbean heritage with no island specified. No detailed biography, no complete discography, no verified official account to link, no release dates for “Normal” and “Memories”. This profile is flagged internally as a thin dossier, and it prefers that honesty to a swollen portrait. Any new element will have to be verified at the source before entering here.

Until those sequels arrive, the recommended listening fits in three stops: “Normal”, “Memories”, then this site RnBouyon page and the bouyon playlist, where today bouyon, from Dominica to Guadeloupe and France, can be heard in all its branches. Fallon holds a particular place there: the artist who brought the word bouyon, married to R&B, into the official selection of a major platform for 2026. That is little, it is verified, and it is already a story that connects a Paris platform selection back to Roseau, to Dominica and to the long Caribbean history of bouyon itself.

Theodora: the Boss Lady who brought bouyon into French pop

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Theodora, full name Lili Théodora Mbangayo Mujinga, is a French-Congolese singer and rapper born on 23 October 2003 in Lucerne, Switzerland, and based in Saint-Denis, France. On 27 September 2024 she released “Kongolese sous BBL”, a track built on a bouyon style remix of her own song “FNG”, which went viral on TikTok, entered the SNEP Top 10 and was later certified diamond single in France. It was the first bouyon single certified gold in France by a non-Caribbean artist. This profile tells her path, from the “Neptune” EP in 2021 to four Victoires de la Musique wins in 2026, and it deals head on with the question her success raises for this site: what does bouyon, born in Dominica, owe to an artist who does not come from the Caribbean, and what did she herself answer to that question.

Who Theodora is: a childhood of moves, a stage name with no accent

Theodora writes her stage name with no accent, and she is also known by the nicknames Boss Lady and Miss Kitoko. She was born on 23 October 2003 in Lucerne, Switzerland, into a Congolese family. Her father, a military doctor, moved constantly and the family followed him: Greece, where her older brother, known as Jeez Suave, was born on 5 January 2000 in Athens, then Congo, then Réunion Island. In 2008, after the Greek financial crisis, the family left Greece, passed through Congo and Réunion, then settled in mainland France in 2009. She lived in Seine-et-Marne, Groslay, Saint-Jean-d Angély, Bordeaux and Brittany before settling in Saint-Denis, in Seine-Saint-Denis.

Born in Switzerland, she does not hold Swiss citizenship, because Switzerland does not grant birthright citizenship, and she says she feels no particular attachment to the country. She acquired French citizenship at 13 or 14. She earned her baccalauréat in 2021 in Vitré, entered the ENS D1 preparatory program in Vannes, which she left after six months, and joined the Brittany Regional Youth Council, where she became president of its culture commission. Alongside school, she practised judo at national tournament level and dance. That childhood of the diaspora, carried between countries and languages, including the Greek she spoke fluently before French, is the raw material of her music.

Music came through the family, from both sides. Her father, a music lover, explored the local musical culture of every country the family arrived in. Her mother, a fan of Koffi Olomide, introduced her to Congolese artists including Mbilia Bel, Tshala Muana and Fally Ipupa. She cites Rihanna as the artist who left the strongest impression on her, because seeing a Black woman succeed in pop gave her a strong sense of representation. Her brother Jeez Suave became her central collaborator: he started making beats, encouraged her to sing, and remains her composer, producer and manager. Theodora began writing music at 16, after several years of recording extensively while keeping most of her work unreleased.

Her debut single, “La thune”, came out in November 2018, when she was 15. Choosing music was not an easy choice: her father opposed it and told her she would be on her own if she followed that path. They reconciled by 2025. That family tension, the precarious early years and mental health questions run through all of her writing, including the record that would make her famous. Keep that thread in mind, because it explains why “Kongolese sous BBL”, an apparently light anthem, was written, by her own account, during a toxic relationship and a period of low self confidence.

Neptune and Lili aux paradis artificiels: the training years, 2021 to 2023

On 10 January 2021, Theodora released her first EP, “Neptune”, a trap pop project issued through the independent label the family duo created, Neptune Production. What started as a bedroom project moved to Le Block in Rennes, a community studio offering professional quality recordings at modest cost, and the duo even organised their first festival around the release. At that stage, nothing pointed to bouyon: the sound was that of a diaspora teenager searching for her form, between trap, pop and American influences such as The Weeknd, Young Thug and Drake.

In 2022 she began to attract attention on the Paris independent scene. On 7 December 2022 she released “Le Paradis se trouve dans le 93”, Paradise is found in the 93, co-written with Jeez Suave, whose video, funded with money saved while working at McDonald s, pays tribute to Seine-Saint-Denis over an animated drum and bass production. The record laid down the bases of her world: a claimed local anchor, the 93, and a refusal of boxes. On 10 January 2023 she took part in the Île-de-France regional auditions for the iNOUïS du Printemps de Bourges at La Maroquinerie, the discovery scheme that serves as a gateway for young French scenes.

On 15 March 2023 came the EP “Lili Aux Paradis Artificiels”, on Maison Neptune X NBDF, with tracks such as “Ici le temps est trop morose”, “C trop la loose”, “Liliroxk”, “Dans le paradis artificiel” and “Après l orage”, mostly co-written and produced by Jeez Suave, in an energy drawing on UK garage and drum and bass. On 6 July 2023 the sequel, “Lili aux paradis artificiels: tome 2”, added five tracks: “…Y a deux fois plus d orage”, “Besoin d aide”, “J ai retrouvé le sourire”, “Boss Lady” and “Love me lagadou”, with a guest appearance from Implaccable and production from Pedro Da Linha, Sutus, Koboi, MEI, Neophron and Suave. The tempos were rapid, and over them Theodora expressed her innermost feelings, euphoric or tinged with deep melancholy.

Two records from that period matter for what followed. “Besoin d aide” speaks openly about psychological trouble and personal anxiety, a recurring theme of her work. “Boss Lady” gave the artist her nickname: during the tome 2 sessions, while she was seriously ill, Implaccable jokingly asked how a boss girl could be sick, which pushed her to keep recording and inspired the track. A few months before the breakthrough, she posted a story saying she had no money left, was losing her hair, was in conflict with her parents and was working on a project nobody would share. It is from that documented low point that the bouyon record of September 2024 would change everything.

Kongolese sous BBL: how a bouyon remix of FNG became an anthem

The technical fact at the heart of this profile deserves stating clearly, because many accounts skip it. The instrumental of “Kongolese sous BBL” is based on a bouyon style remix of the track “FNG”, short for Freaky Nasty Girl, first released in February 2024. In other words, bouyon is not a decoration added afterwards to a pop song: it is the rhythmic matrix of the record, obtained by running an existing song of the artist through the bouyon machine. The single was released on 27 September 2024, runs 2 minutes 45, and is classified bouyon and hip-hop. The published credits name Theodora for singing and writing, and Jeez Suave for production and writing.

Theodora told GQ France that she lacked self confidence while writing the song, and was going through a toxic relationship at the time. She posted a trailer online and woke up the next morning to around two thousand likes on Twitter and Instagram, a reaction she described as significant because the song was highly autobiographical. The record then went viral on TikTok, where it evolved into a body positive anthem and fuelled dance challenges and lip sync skits across social platforms. The Fader praised its palpable energy and hilarious songwriting. The chorus turns on “Mwasi Sukali”, a Lingala expression meaning sweet woman, and on the line “j me lève, j suis déjà belle”: I wake up, I am already beautiful.

The acronym BBL carries three meanings the artist herself maintains: Brazilian Butt Lift, the cosmetic surgery procedure, Big Boss Lady, her nickname, and Bad Boy Lovestory, the title of her debut mixtape. The song celebrates her body and her beauty as a Black woman without worrying about imposed norms, affirms her value despite financial difficulty, with the line “Je vaux beaucoup, même si parfois je ne joins pas les deux bouts”, I am worth a lot, even if sometimes I struggle to make ends meet, and claims a carefree letting go. Jeremie Leger of the French rap outlet Rap City noted that the record challenges stereotypes around the hyper feminised and hypersexualised image of Black women.

The published figures measure the phenomenon. The record peaked at No. 10 on the SNEP Top Singles chart in France, No. 34 on Belgium Ultratop Wallonia, where it charted for seven weeks, entered Spotify Top 50 France, climbed to No. 3 on Spotify Top Viral Global chart, and received airplay on NRJ and Skyrock. In November 2024 it climbed from 113th to 35th place on the SNEP chart in a single week. It became the first bouyon song performed by a non-Caribbean artist to receive gold certification in France in 2024, and it was later certified diamond by the SNEP and gold by the Belgian BRMA. To place what that word, bouyon, originally means, read our page what bouyon means and our history of bouyon: the genre was born in Dominica, and that is precisely the point that triggered the debate told in the next section.

The origins debate: what Theodora answered, word for word

The success of the record sparked controversy within the Caribbean community regarding the use of bouyon music by a non-Caribbean artist. The question is a legitimate one, and this site knows it well: bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s with WCK, it was worked in Guadeloupe and Martinique by the artists who live from it, and those scenes long lacked access to the radios, media and platforms of mainland France. Seeing the first certified bouyon single in France carried by an artist who is not Antillean therefore raises a real question of credit and access, not only a question of taste.

Theodora answered on X, and this profile quotes her reply as published, because it is part of the record: “I made a bouyon song because I love Afro-Caribbean music; at no point did I claim to have created something that did not exist. I talked about the origins of bouyon in my stories; I lived on Réunion Island and I did not discover bouyon on TikTok”. She added: “The problem comes from the top: from radio stations, media outlets, platforms to which Caribbean people do not have access. In truth, this inaccessibility also results from racism towards people from overseas and the different Creole cultures”.

Her musical defence is also documented. She explains her blend as a deliberate choice of a child of the diaspora: when you are a child of the diaspora in France, you go to places where everything mixes; in Black clubs there is bouyon, there is Afro-Caribbean music; she thinks her music reflects the meeting of different worlds in the same place. She also recalls her father habit of listening closely to the music of every country the family arrived in. In short, she claims neither the invention of bouyon nor a discovery via TikTok: she claims a long familiarity with the genre, from Réunion and from the clubs of the diaspora.

This site position is the following, and it is constant across all of our pages. Bouyon is Dominican in its origin, and its full history, from bouyon gwada to bouyon in France, must be told with its names and its islands. Theodora success does not change that origin: it makes it more visible, and it makes more urgent the question she raises herself, that of Caribbean artists access to the same radios and the same platforms. That is why this profile exists on a site devoted to bouyon: to document the biggest bouyon success in French pop, with its credits, its figures and the whole controversy, rather than celebrating or denouncing it in one sentence. Bad Boy Lovestory and Mega BBL: the mixtapes and the consecration, 2024 to 2025

On 1 November 2024, a few weeks after the single, Theodora released her debut mixtape, “Bad Boy Lovestory”, through Boss Lady Records. The Fader described it as a sugary fizz of dance, electronic and dancehall. The project, which includes “Kongolese sous BBL” and “FNG”, merges Afropop influences with Caribbean genres, with collaborations from Guy2Bezbar, Zoomy, Suave and Jahlys, and beneath its festive surface it explores mental health, toxic relationships and self reflection. The success was massive: the mixtape entered the Top 5 of the SNEP Top Albums chart, stayed there durably, reached No. 14 in Wallonia, and was certified triple platinum by the SNEP.

The records of the mixtape install a complete signature. “Fashion Designa”, produced by Suave with an amapiano influence, carries her confident, self empowered persona: the record reached No. 12 on the SNEP chart and a diamond certification in France. “Ils me rient tous au nez”, produced by Lasso and certified platinum, tells the emotional struggle of someone overly generous who ends up ignored and ridiculed. “243 km/h” was certified gold. In May 2025 the expanded reissue “Mega BBL” added collaborations with Chilly Gonzales, Juliette Armanet, Jul and Luidji, and records such as “PAY!” with Guy2Bezbar, No. 31 on the SNEP chart and certified platinum, “Do U Wanna?”, “Zou Bizou” with Jul, No. 9 and certified diamond, “Masoko Na Mabele” with London and “Mon bébé” with Brazy. “Mega BBL” was certified gold on 12 June 2025, then platinum, and the two versions of the project brought the artist back into the albums Top 5.

On 14 May 2025, at Les Flammes, the French urban cultures ceremony, Theodora received the Flame for Female Revelation of the Year, a trophy she dedicated to all the slightly weird Black girls. In September 2025 her project received the 18 to 20 prize of the Prix Joséphine des artistes. In August 2025 Le Monde described her as the pop phenomenon of the summer and wrote that her inclusive songs had made her the new boss of pop in France. Billboard France reported, at the end of 2025, that she was the most streamed female francophone artist of the year in France. In November 2025 GQ France named her Woman of the Year. Within a few months, the artist who said she was broke in a story had become, according to those outlets, the central figure of French language pop.

The stage confirmed it. In summer 2025 she played major French festivals including Yardland, Les Vieilles Charrues and Cabaret Vert, and she sold out in under 15 minutes for four dates at the Zénith de Paris scheduled in March and April 2026, 7,000 spectators per date and 28,000 people in total, after first being offered the Olympia and its 2,800 seats. On 26 September 2025 she guest performed on Disiz single “Melodrama”, from his album “On s en rappellera pas”: the record spent ten consecutive weeks at No. 1 on the SNEP Top Singles chart, a 2025 record for a consecutive run, also reached No. 1 in Wallonia and the Top 10 in Switzerland, and was certified gold within a month then diamond two months later. It was her first chart topper, and the second for Disiz.

2026: Victoires, Flammes and a new era, and what this profile establishes

Television followed the same path. Theodora was a guest on season 4 of Nouvelle École, the Netflix music show broadcast from 31 October to 14 November 2025, then in January 2026 she joined the permanent judging panel of season 5, alongside Oli and SDM. On 13 February 2026, at the 41st Victoires de la Musique, she won four awards, the most of any artist that year: Album of the Year for “Mega BBL”, Female Revelation, Revelation Scene and Audiovisual Creation. On 23 April 2026, at the fourth Les Flammes ceremony, she left with five awards, including Female Artist of the Year and Album of the Year. The same year, she became the first French face of the Mugler Angel Nova perfume.

The 2026 discography extended the run: “Spa”, with Gims, released in January, reached No. 4 on the SNEP chart; “Des mythos”, in February, No. 13; “Sex Model”, by PLK featuring Theodora, No. 3; “Miss Kitoko”, in March, No. 12. On 2 May 2026 she joined Fally Ipupa during his first Stade de France date and performed “Kongolese sous BBL” there in front of more than 65,000 people. Her style, described by the media as extraordinarily varied, fuses bouyon, hyperpop, French rap, pop, afrobeat, amapiano, zouk, calypso, dancehall, contemporary R and B, rock, electronic music and drum and bass. She herself resists the simple rapper label: she says she is much more hybrid, while recognising her debt to rap in her writing techniques.

Why Theodora matters for bouyon, in short: because she carried the bouyon word and rhythm to the top of the French charts, with the first bouyon single certified gold then diamond in France by a non-Antillean artist, and because that success forced a public debate on the Dominican origin of the genre and on Caribbean artists access to the media. Cultural guides to Guadeloupe count her, in fact, among the artists who popularised bouyon in the archipelago, alongside Shanika and 1T1: bouyon gwada and Theodora bouyon pop answer each other in the same playlists. Her profile should therefore be read alongside the pages of the scene that made the genre: our bouyon playlist places her record among the artists of Dominica and the French Antilles.

Finally, this profile states its limits. No official social profile link was verified by direct opening during this research, so this page links to no social profile in its structured data. Album chart peaks and some foreign chart positions vary slightly between the French and English reference articles consulted: this profile keeps the formulations both confirm, Top 5 for the albums and Top 10 for Switzerland on “Melodrama”, rather than contradictory figures. For the rest, the record is exceptionally well documented: release dates, credits, SNEP and BRMA certifications, awards and public statements. If any element is corrected by the artist or by the SNEP, this profile will be updated and dated, like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Theodora and where does she come from?

Theodora, full name Lili Théodora Mbangayo Mujinga, is a French-Congolese singer and rapper born on 23 October 2003 in Lucerne, Switzerland. She grew up between Greece, Congo and Réunion, then in France from 2009, before settling in Saint-Denis. Her brother Jeez Suave is her composer, producer and manager. She is also nicknamed Boss Lady and Miss Kitoko.

Why is Theodora called Boss Lady?

The nickname comes from the track “Boss Lady”, on the EP “Lili aux paradis artificiels: tome 2” of July 2023. During the sessions, while she was seriously ill, Implaccable jokingly asked her how a boss girl could be sick, which pushed her to keep recording and inspired the song. The nickname then returns in the acronym BBL, Big Boss Lady, and in her label, Boss Lady Records.

Is “Kongolese sous BBL” really a bouyon record?

Yes, by classification and by construction. The single, released on 27 September 2024, is classified bouyon and hip-hop, and its instrumental is a bouyon style remix of Theodora’s track “FNG”, released in February 2024. Bouyon is not decoration added afterwards: it is the rhythmic core of the record, which reached No. 10 on the SNEP chart and a diamond certification in France.

Who produced “Kongolese sous BBL”?

Jeez Suave, Theodora’s older brother, credited for production and writing, with Theodora credited for singing and writing. Born on 5 January 2000 in Athens, he is the one who started making beats and suggested she sing, and he remains her composer, producer and manager, from the EP “Neptune” in 2021 to the mixtapes of 2024 and 2025.

What does BBL mean in “Kongolese sous BBL”?

The acronym carries three meanings the artist maintains: Brazilian Butt Lift, the cosmetic surgery procedure, Big Boss Lady, her nickname, and Bad Boy Lovestory, the title of her debut mixtape released on 1 November 2024. The song is an anthem of affirmation in which she celebrates her body as a Black woman, with the Lingala chorus “Mwasi Sukali”, meaning sweet woman.

Does Theodora’s bouyon come from Dominica?

Yes, the genre she takes up was born in Dominica in the late 1980s, with bands such as WCK, before being worked on in Guadeloupe and Martinique. Theodora has never said otherwise: she explains that she knew bouyon from Réunion and from diaspora clubs. Her success does not change that Dominican origin, it makes it more visible. See what is bouyon.

Did Theodora claim she invented bouyon?

No, and she said so publicly. Answering the controversy in the Caribbean community on X, she wrote that at no point did she claim to have created something that did not exist, that she had talked about the origins of bouyon in her stories, that she had lived on Réunion Island and had not discovered bouyon on TikTok. She added that the problem is Caribbean people’s access to radio, media and platforms.

Is Theodora from the Caribbean?

No. Theodora is French-Congolese, born in Switzerland, and she has never claimed otherwise. That is what makes her record unique in bouyon history: “Kongolese sous BBL” became the first bouyon single certified gold in France by a non-Antillean artist, before reaching diamond. She explains her relationship to the genre through her childhood on Réunion Island and through the clubs of the diaspora.

What is Theodora’s musical style?

A hybrid style, described by the media as extraordinarily varied: it fuses bouyon, hyperpop, French rap, pop, afrobeat, amapiano, zouk, calypso, dancehall, R and B, rock, electronic music and drum and bass. She herself rejects the simple label of rapper, while acknowledging her debt to rap in her writing. Her beginnings, from 2021 to 2023, drew rather on trap pop, UK garage and drum and bass.

How did Theodora start out?

Her first single, “La thune”, came out in November 2018, when she was 15. On 10 January 2021 came her first EP, “Neptune”, a trap pop project released on Neptune Production, the independent label of the family duo. Then came “Le Paradis se trouve dans le 93” on 7 December 2022, and the two “Lili aux paradis artificiels” EPs in March and July 2023, before the bouyon record of September 2024.

What are Theodora’s biggest hits after “Kongolese sous BBL”?

Her mixtape “Bad Boy Lovestory” of November 2024 was certified triple platinum, and its reissue “Mega BBL” of May 2025 won Album of the Year at the 2026 Victoires de la Musique. “Fashion Designa” was certified diamond, “Zou Bizou” with Jul as well, and “Melodrama”, her duet with Disiz released on 26 September 2025, spent ten consecutive weeks at No. 1 on the SNEP Top Singles chart.

How many Victoires de la Musique has Theodora won?

Four, the most of the night, at the 41st Victoires de la Musique ceremony on 13 February 2026: Album of the Year for “Mega BBL”, Female Revelation, Stage Revelation and Audiovisual Creation. The same year, on 23 April 2026, she left the Flammes with five awards, including Female Artist of the Year, after the Female Revelation Flamme she received in May 2025.

Did Theodora sell out the Zenith in Paris?

Yes. She sold out four dates at the Zenith in Paris, scheduled for March and April 2026, in under 15 minutes, 7,000 spectators per date and 28,000 people in total, when she had first been offered the Olympia and its 2,800 seats. On 2 May 2026, she also performed “Kongolese sous BBL” at the Stade de France as a guest of Fally Ipupa.

Where can I hear Theodora and the bouyon behind her?

Theodora is on every platform: start with “Kongolese sous BBL”, then the mixtape “Bad Boy Lovestory” and its reissue “Mega BBL”.

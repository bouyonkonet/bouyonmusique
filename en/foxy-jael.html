Foxy Jael: Ah Coming and the mic changes hands, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Foxy Jael is the musical alter ego of Jael Joseph, a Dominican media woman who spent years behind the microphone before stepping in front of it: interview host, founder of Black Island Girl Multimedia, award winning filmmaker, then, in July 2025, a singer, with a debut single, “Ah Coming”, voiced on the Kryptic Riddim of producer Dada. In February 2026 the video of the song made her an artist in her own right, and in May 2026 her feature on “Back Up Your Chat” by Ebony Empress placed her in the most visible female duo in bouyon that year. This profile follows that move from mic to mic, record by record and credit by credit, and it also states what the file does not hold: no platform artist page was found under this name, and no verified listening figure exists to date.

Jael Joseph before Foxy Jael: the media woman

To understand Foxy Jael, Jael Joseph must first be understood, because the stage name was born from a media brand that was already built. According to Bahamas Local, Black Island Girl began in 2021 as the final year capstone project of Jael Joseph at Toronto Metropolitan University, the former Ryerson University, in Toronto: an online outlet devoted to the lives of Caribbean women. The project became Black Island Girl Multimedia, a registered business, with a relaunched site, culture, entertainment, business and wellness sections, and podcast and production services for brands.

The Antigua Observer describes her as a media and marketing personality, host of several weekly interview shows gathered under the name “Jael Joseph Live”, carried on Facebook. It was from that host chair that she spent years promoting bouyon artists and Dominican culture, before singing herself. The press of the Foxy Jael debut, Black Island Girl and CaribMagPlus, gives the same order of magnitude for her audience: about 100,000 followers across platforms, a figure published by those outlets and carried here as a press figure, not as a counter verified by this site.

The third strand of that path is film. In 2023 Jael Joseph presented “Territory”, her documentary on the Kalinago people of Dominica, opening the Caribbean Tales International Film Festival in Toronto, on September 6, 2023. Bahamas Local states that the film won Best Short Documentary there. The film follows three residents of the Kalinago Territory, on the north east coast of the island, and deals with the loss of the language and the limits that the land status places on economic development. The host is therefore also an award winning director, trained in Toronto, rooted in the questions of her island.

The media consecration arrived on Saturday January 3, 2026, at the second edition of the Dominica Music Awards, at the InterContinental in Dominica. According to the Black Island Girl report, by Semaj Bernard, Jael Joseph won Social Media Influencer and Content Creator of the Year there, for her digital storytelling and her promotion of Dominican creatives. The same night, producer Dada received an Iconic Award for his body of work. Six months after her singing debut, the media woman was therefore honoured in the same ceremony as the producer of her first single: the Foxy Jael file rests, from the start, on that double belonging, the stage and the media.

“Ah Coming” (July 2025): debuting on the Kryptic Riddim of Dada

The first Foxy Jael single is called “Ah Coming”, and it was released in July 2025, according to the Dominican and Caribbean press that covered her debut, Black Island Girl on July 10, 2025 and then CaribMagPlus. No source opened for this profile gives the exact day of release, or a label, and this profile invents neither: July 2025 is the documented date. The record marks, according to those same sources, her official entry as a music artist, under the name Foxy Jael, presented everywhere as her musical alter ego.

The frame of that debut is a riddim, the shared format of bouyon and soca. “Ah Coming” is voiced on the Kryptic Riddim, produced by Dada, an award winning Dominican producer to whom the press attributes collaborations with Machel Montano and Skinny Fabulous. The spelling of the riddim varies by source: the official video credits and the headline of the launch article write Kryptic Riddim, while the body of the CaribMagPlus article writes Kyrptic; this profile keeps the spelling of the official video credit block. On the same riddim, the press lists three other voices: Pumpa, from the US Virgin Islands, with “Savage”, Shanna Raymond, from St. Kitts, with “Hustle Hard”, and Puddaz, from Dominica, with “Shake Something”. Debuting on a shared riddim, alongside established artists from three islands, is the classic entry route into bouyon, and it is the one Foxy Jael took.

The record is a family affair as much as a studio one. “Ah Coming” was written by her sister, Aurelle Joseph, according to every source opened, and the official video description credits three composers: Dada, Aurelle Joseph and Jael Joseph herself. Dada also holds the production, mixing and mastering there, according to that same credit block. Foxy Jael therefore enters music surrounded by the two signatures that structure her whole path: her sister on the writing, as she will be again on “Back Up Your Chat”, and the historic producer of the Dominican scene at the desk.

On substance, the press reports the artist explanation: the song speaks of what many women of her generation live through in looking for love, the spark, the chase, then the conquest. Listeners, still according to those articles, read different things into it, a message of empowerment for some, playful seduction for others, dominance and control for others still. That multiple reading made the record a conversation topic on social media, which, for a host turned singer, is a coherent launch ramp: the single is built to be discussed as much as listened to, and it extends into music the conversational work that built the Jael Joseph brand.

The video of February 27, 2026: a production at the level of the ambition

A July 2025 single could have stayed a trial run. It was the video that turned it into an artist act. The official premiere of the “Ah Coming” video took place on Friday February 27, 2026, according to Black Island Girl and CaribMagPlus, and the video is published on YouTube under the title “Foxy Jael, Ah Coming (OFFICIAL MUSIC VIDEO)”. Its official description, opened for this profile, gives a full credit block, rare for a debut single, and this profile follows it line by line.

Direction and photography are by Leah De Gannes, from Trinidad and Tobago, creative direction and styling are by RisAnne Martin, whom the press writes as Risann Martin De Gannes, and Jael Joseph is herself the executive producer of her own video. Filming took place in Dominica, at the High Garden Restaurant, at the place called The Realm, and at Jungle Bay Resort. The dancers are Elnarrah Emmanuel and Gabrielle Didier, of the Pashiwok group. Make up is credited to Queen II Queen and Beauty By Angie. Nothing in that credit block belongs to an improvised video: it is a regional production, with a Trinidad and Tobago team, Dominican locations and an artist producing her image the way she produced her shows.

The cast of the video tells the place of Foxy Jael on the scene. The love interest role is held by Colton T, a Dominican reggae and bouyon singer. Among the people on screen, the credits list Gilda Nesty-Tonge and Julie Roberts as the friends, Kyle Chris Davis, Tara Richards, Aurelle Joseph, and two names that tie the video to the rest of this site: Reo, one of the rising voices of Dominican bouyon, and Dada himself, the producer of the record, present on camera. A first video in which the historic producer of the scene and the new generation of the genre appear as extras works as a visible passing of the torch: the newcomer is filmed in the middle of her own people.

The press read this video as a positioning, and this profile reports that reading as a press reading, not as a fact. Black Island Girl and CaribMagPlus see in it an elevated aesthetic, an asserted female presence and the sign of a serious regional contender, strengthening the representation of women in a male dominated bouyon space. What the documents establish is simpler and stronger: in seven months, Foxy Jael moved from a single voiced on a riddim to a video produced at the level of the established artists of the region, and that video remains, to date, the central document of her music file.

“Back Up Your Chat” (May 2026): the feature with Ebony Empress

The second record of the file changes format: it is no longer a solo single, but a feature, and it places her alongside Ebony Empress. On May 15, 2026, Black Island Girl, under the byline of Semaj Bernard, announced the release of “Back Up Your Chat”, by Ebony Empress with Foxy Jael as a feature, presented as an energetic bouyon remix. The official video of the record, published on YouTube under the title “Ebony Empress Ft Foxy Jael, Back Up Your Chat, Bouyon 2026”, confirms the credit in its very title, and its description gives the full credit block.

That credit block is one of the fullest in recent Dominican bouyon, and it ties Foxy Jael to the workshop this site already follows. Production: YoJoshii and Yung Pierre. Mixing: Cleverns Pierre. Mastering: Khallion. Writing: Everlyne Leandra Walters and Aurelle Joseph, the sister of Foxy Jael, present therefore on the writing of both her first two records. Recording: Felix Studio. Cover art: Evelyne Leandra Walters, according to the description, with a slightly different spelling of the first name from the writing credit, a difference this profile flags without settling it. The video files the record under the keywords Bouyon 2026, Bouyon and Soca, and points to the Spotify and Apple Music pages of the single, which this profile did not open directly and therefore does not turn into links.

The story of the record, told by the press, gives the feature its meaning. During a livestream interview carried on the show of Foxy Jael, Ebony Empress revealed that “Back Up Your Chat” was the first song she ever created, taken back and modernised years later because the sound had changed in the meantime. The lyric speaks to men who promise a great deal and do not keep their promises. Foxy Jael, quoted by the article, says she heard the record and liked its energy, and Ebony Empress praises her verse there as direct, with no long talk. The feature was therefore born in the television studio of the host before it was born in the recording studio: a guest of her show became her record partner, and the show served as the artistic meeting place.

For Foxy Jael, this record counts double. It first proves that her debut single was no isolated trial: ten months after “Ah Coming”, she is invited onto the record of another artist, in a feature role credited at the same level as the lead artist in the title of the video. It then places her at the centre of the female moment of Dominican bouyon in 2026, alongside Ebony Empress, in a duo the press presents as a summer anthem for women. Our women of bouyon page places that duo inside the history of the female voices of the genre, and our Ebony Empress page holds the other half of the file of this record.

The media bridge: from amplifying others to her own voice

The Foxy Jael case is rare in bouyon, because the artist already owned the tool other artists are looking for: a platform. For years, Jael Joseph amplified bouyon artists, events and Dominican culture from behind the camera and the microphone, in the formula of her debut press. Her move into song is presented by those same outlets as a deliberate brand expansion: moving from promoter to performer, and taking an opportunity to extend her audience across the region and internationally. This profile reports that framing as the framing of the press, while noting the raw fact beneath it: her first two records are documented, credited and filmed at a professional level, which sets her case apart from simple influencer trials.

The rest of her 2026 shows that the bridge works in both directions. In August 2026, Caribbean E-Magazine reported that Jael Joseph, presented as a Dominican journalist, producer and media personality, launched through Black Island Girl Multimedia a region wide contest to find the voice that will represent her platform as a correspondent on the red carpet of the Caribbean Music Awards, on September 19, 2026 in Trinidad and Tobago, with flights and accommodation provided. Entries are posted on Instagram tagging among others the account @jaeljoseph, the only personal handle published in the sources opened for this profile, and this profile cites it as a press handle, without turning it into a verified official link. She tells there how she herself was invited to host those awards three years earlier, then to interview the artists on the red carpet: the opportunity she received, she is handing it on.

That position has a direct musical consequence: Foxy Jael does not need the press to discover her, since she is, in part, the press. The articles documenting her debut appear on Black Island Girl, her own platform, and are carried by CaribMagPlus, while Soca News also filed her debut single in its Dominica columns in March 2026. This profile notes it without indulgence or suspicion: the Black Island Girl coverage is a published and bylined source, useful for credits and dates, but it is a source from her own house, and the central facts of this file, the two records, their credit blocks and the video, are confirmed by the official descriptions of the videos themselves, independent of any article.

What remains is the question of what comes next, and the opened file does not allow it to be answered. No source consulted announces a third record, an album or a festival stage for Foxy Jael. What is established fits in two records and a readable path: an award winning media woman who converted her audience and her know how into the start of a bouyon catalog, surrounded by her sister on the writing, by the historic producer Dada for her debut, and by the YoJoshii, Khallion and Felix Studio workshop for her feature. Few bouyon artists can document their first two records this completely, and it is precisely that completeness, more than any discourse, that makes the strength of the file.

The limits of the file: what this profile cannot yet say

This profile ends, like every one on this site, with the honest inventory of its gaps. First gap: no Foxy Jael artist page was found on the streaming and identification platforms consulted during this research, no Shazam page, and no artist page confirmed by direct opening. The two records of the file are therefore documented by the press and by the official descriptions of their YouTube videos, and the structured data of this page carries no external link, for lack of an official page verified by opening. The Spotify and Apple Music pointers read in the description of “Back Up Your Chat” prove that the single exists on those platforms, but this profile did not open them and does not cite them as links.

Second gap: the fine dates. “Ah Coming” is dated to July 2025 by the press, with no published day. “Back Up Your Chat” is dated May 15, 2026, the day of the Black Island Girl announcement article, and this profile presents it as the release date reported by the press. No label is named for either record in the opened sources, no track length is published in the documents consulted, and no verified listening or viewing counter is attached to the name: this profile therefore publishes no length, no label and no streaming figure.

Third limit: the personal biography. The opened sources document the professional, training in Toronto, Black Island Girl, film, awards, but publish no date of birth, no childhood path and no musical training for Jael Joseph, and this profile assumes none of them. Likewise, the name Foxy Jael is everywhere presented as her musical alter ego, without any source explaining the choice of the word Foxy: this profile refrains from any invented etymology.

Listening to the file therefore takes two records, both verified at the source. “Ah Coming”, a July 2025 single on the Kryptic Riddim of Dada, written by Aurelle Joseph, produced, mixed and mastered by Dada, with its February 27, 2026 video filmed at The Realm and Jungle Bay. And “Back Up Your Chat”, by Ebony Empress with Foxy Jael, in May 2026, produced by YoJoshii and Yung Pierre, recorded at Felix Studio. The context can be read on our bouyon soca page, on our women of bouyon page and in our bouyon playlist. If an official artist page, a third record or a label appears, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Foxy Jael?

Foxy Jael is the musical alter ego of Jael Joseph, a Dominican media woman: host of the “Jael Joseph Live” interview shows, founder of Black Island Girl Multimedia, director of the documentary “Territory”, winner of Best Short Documentary at the 2023 Caribbean Tales International Film Festival, and Social Media Influencer and Content Creator of the Year at the Dominica Music Awards of January 3, 2026. She debuted as a singer in July 2025 with “Ah Coming”.

What is Foxy Jael’s real name?

Foxy Jael is the stage name of Jael Joseph, presented everywhere as her musical alter ego. Under her own name she is a Dominican media woman, host of the “Jael Joseph Live” interview shows and founder of Black Island Girl Multimedia. No source explains the choice of the word Foxy, and this site invents no etymology for it.

Is Foxy Jael from Dominica?

Yes: the press that covered her debut presents Jael Joseph as a Dominican media woman, and Caribbean E-Magazine describes her as a Dominican journalist, producer and media personality. She trained in Toronto, where Black Island Girl began as her final year project, but her music is rooted in Dominica: the Kryptic Riddim of Dada and a video filmed on the island.

What is the debut single of Foxy Jael?

“Ah Coming”, released in July 2025. The record is voiced on the Kryptic Riddim of producer Dada, alongside Pumpa with “Savage”, Shanna Raymond with “Hustle Hard” and Puddaz with “Shake Something”. It was written by her sister Aurelle Joseph, and produced, mixed and mastered by Dada, who shares the composer credit with Aurelle Joseph and Jael Joseph according to the official video credit block.

Kryptic Riddim or Kyrptic Riddim: which spelling is right?

Kryptic Riddim is the spelling of the official credits of the “Ah Coming” video and of the headline of the launch article, and it is the one this site keeps. The body of the CaribMagPlus article writes Kyrptic. Both names designate the same riddim produced by Dada, on which Foxy Jael, Pumpa, Shanna Raymond and Puddaz each voiced a song.

Who writes Foxy Jael’s songs?

Her sister, Aurelle Joseph, according to every source opened. Aurelle Joseph wrote “Ah Coming”, whose composer credit she shares with Dada and Jael Joseph, then co-wrote “Back Up Your Chat” with Everlyne Leandra Walters. Foxy Jael’s first two records therefore carry the same family signature on the writing, around two different production teams.

What is Foxy Jael’s “Ah Coming” about?

According to the artist’s explanation reported by the press, “Ah Coming” speaks of what many women of her generation live through in looking for love: the spark, the chase, then the conquest. Listeners read different things into it, a message of empowerment for some, playful seduction for others, dominance and control for others still, which made it a conversation topic on social media.

When was the “Ah Coming” video released, and where was it filmed?

The official premiere took place on Friday February 27, 2026. The video, directed by Leah De Gannes with RisAnne Martin on creative direction and styling, was filmed in Dominica, at the High Garden Restaurant, at The Realm, and at Jungle Bay Resort. Colton T plays the love interest there, and Reo and Dada appear among the people on screen.

What is “Back Up Your Chat”?

It is a bouyon remix by Ebony Empress featuring Foxy Jael, announced by the Dominican press on May 15, 2026. It was produced by YoJoshii and Yung Pierre, mixed by Cleverns Pierre, mastered by Khallion, written by Everlyne Leandra Walters and Aurelle Joseph, and recorded at Felix Studio. Ebony Empress said, on the show of Foxy Jael, that it was the first song she ever created, modernised years later.

What awards has Jael Joseph won?

Two awards are on file. Her documentary “Territory”, on the Kalinago people of Dominica, won Best Short Documentary at the 2023 Caribbean Tales International Film Festival in Toronto, according to Bahamas Local. On January 3, 2026, at the second Dominica Music Awards, she won Social Media Influencer and Content Creator of the Year, the same night producer Dada received an Iconic Award.

What is Black Island Girl Multimedia?

It is the media company founded by Jael Joseph, the real name of Foxy Jael. According to Bahamas Local, Black Island Girl began in 2021 as her final year capstone project at Toronto Metropolitan University: an online outlet devoted to the lives of Caribbean women. It became a registered business, with culture, entertainment, business and wellness sections, and podcast and production services for brands.

What is Foxy Jael’s link with the Caribbean Music Awards?

In August 2026, Caribbean E-Magazine reported that Jael Joseph launched, through Black Island Girl Multimedia, a region wide contest to find a red carpet correspondent for the Caribbean Music Awards of September 19, 2026 in Trinidad and Tobago. She recalled having been invited to host those awards three years earlier, then to interview the artists on the red carpet.

Does Foxy Jael have a label or an album?

No source opened for this profile names a label for her two records, and no album is documented. Her verified catalog fits in two records: “Ah Coming” solo, in July 2025, and “Back Up Your Chat” as a feature with Ebony Empress, in May 2026. No platform artist page was confirmed by direct opening during this research.

Where can I hear Foxy Jael?

Through her two verified records: “Ah Coming”, whose official video is on YouTube, and “Back Up Your Chat”, by Ebony Empress with Foxy Jael, whose official video points to Spotify and Apple Music.

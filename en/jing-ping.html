Jing ping: the traditional root of bouyon

Published September 21, 2026

No bouyon without jing ping. This traditional dance music of Dominica, led by the accordion, is one of the two founding ingredients of the genre, with cadence-lypso.

Creole ball music

Jing ping accompanies the traditional dances of Dominica: quadrille, mazurka, bèlè and other ball dances inherited from the island’s Creole history. The classic line-up brings together an accordion, drums like lapo kabwit, a boom-boom and a bamboo flute.

From the ball to electronic carnival

When WCK creates bouyon in 1988, the band takes the melodies, the rhythms and the spirit of jing ping and pushes them with electronic keyboards. It is this dialogue between the village ball and the machine that gives bouyon its unique character in the Caribbean.

Frequently asked questions

What is jing ping?

Jing ping is the traditional dance music of Dominica, led by the accordion. It accompanies the island’s Creole ball dances, such as quadrille, mazurka and bèlè, inherited from its Creole history. It is one of the two founding ingredients of bouyon, with cadence-lypso.

What instruments are played in jing ping?

The classic line-up brings together an accordion, which leads the music, drums like the lapo kabwit, a goatskin drum, a boom-boom and a bamboo flute. These sounds remain the soul of bouyon, and good producers still sample them, as our page on bouyon instruments explains.

What is the link between jing ping and bouyon?

When WCK created bouyon in Roseau in 1988, the band took the melodies, the rhythms and the spirit of jing ping and pushed them with electronic keyboards. It is this dialogue between the village ball and the machine that gives bouyon its unique character in the Caribbean.

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of the genre, then the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon. To understand where the sound comes from, also listen to its two roots: jing ping and cadence-lypso, the dance traditions WCK brought together in 1988.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band. WCK created bouyon in Roseau in 1988 by plugging cadence-lypso and jing ping into electronic keyboards and drum machines, to make the island’s fetes and carnival dance.

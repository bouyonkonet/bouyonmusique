Fireman Lee: Down Deh, one verified credit, bouyon, Dominica

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Fireman Lee is a singer name that appears exactly once in the verifiable record: as a feature, alongside Zwady, on “Down Deh”, a cut of the Dwivayez Riddim produced in Dominica by ProducerDlo and Savion Beats, released on August 28, 2020. This profile documents that credit in full, the riddim that carries it, the press that announced it, and states clearly everything that was not found: no solo single, no second feature, no biography, no artist page. It also separates Fireman Lee from his most cumbersome namesake, Delroy “Fireman” Hooper of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. A short profile, because the public file is short, and a reference site does not fill gaps with fiction.

The credit: “Down Deh”, August 28, 2020

The only discographic document carrying the name Fireman Lee is the page of the record “Down Deh”, by Producerdlo, featuring Zwady and Fireman Lee. The record was released on August 28, 2020 on GloSho Music, as the third track of the Dwivayez Riddim, in English, at 117 BPM. Fireman Lee is not the lead artist on it: the EP is signed Producerdlo, the producer name of Dylan Lowe, and the spotlight on the cut is shared with Zwady, whose profile, published in the same wave as this one, shows that “Down Deh” is the most streamed record of his whole catalog.

The writing line of the record holds four names: Dylan Christon Lowe, Savion Augustine, Aswad Polydore and Leslie St. Jean. The first three are identified without argument: the two producers of the riddim, and Zwady, whose writer name Aswad Polydore is documented. The fourth name, Leslie St. Jean, gets a whole section below, because it would be tempting to assign it to Fireman Lee, and no source consulted does so. The Fireman Lee credit itself fits on one line: a stage name, a guest voice, one record. That is little, and it is exactly what the public archive contains.

The place of the record inside the EP helps explain how a name can exist on a scene without leaving a catalog behind. The Dwivayez Riddim lines up five tracks: the riddim instrumental, “Happy Place” with Shelly, “Down Deh”, “System Failure” with Starsha, and “Deliway” with Trilla-G. In the riddim economy, detailed on our bouyon riddim page, a featured singer can mark a cut, be heard across the whole DJ and fete circuit, and never release a record under his own name. Fireman Lee is, in the available documents, exactly that case.

The Dwivayez Riddim: the context that carries the record

The Dwivayez Riddim is the work of two producers from Dominica: ProducerDlo, real name Dylan Lowe, and Savion Beats, real name Savion Augustine, whom the press presents as a two time RIAA Gold certified producer for work outside the island. The riddim name comes from the French Creole spoken in Dominica: dwive means to lime or wander, and a dwivayez is someone who limes from fete to fete. The project was born in the lockdown period, with the idea of making a sound that makes you want to leave the house and go anywhere, as long as there is music. “Down Deh” was born inside that frame: a party record, in English, made for people who dwive.

The regional press announced the riddim in February 2021, in an article datelined Port of Spain. It lists the artists of the project: Starsha with “System Failure”, Shelly with “Happy Place”, Trilla G with “Deliway”, and Zwady with Fireman Lee as a feature. Two gaps between the press and the platforms must stay visible. The press spells the title “Down Dere” where the platforms spell “Down Deh”. And it adds a record by Trinidad Tevin Hartman, “Overproof”, that does not appear on the tracklist of the EP released on August 28, 2020. This profile flags both gaps instead of choosing in silence: for an artist whose file rests on a single record, the smallest title error would erase the only trace that exists.

A Dominican television show, Parle with Cranberry, devoted an episode to the two producers and the Dwivayez Riddim. Its presentation names the songs of Shelly, Trilla G, Zwady and Starsha: Fireman Lee is not named separately there, he exists inside the Zwady record. That is the exact position of the name across all the documentation: never alone, always on the second line of a shared credit. Readers looking for Fireman Lee as a lead artist will find nothing, and this profile saves them the confusion by saying so from the introduction.

Leslie St. Jean: the fourth name, which this profile does not merge

Now to the most delicate point of the file. The “Down Deh” page credits four writers. Three are identified: Dylan Christon Lowe and Savion Augustine, the producers, and Aswad Polydore, who is Zwady. The fourth, Leslie St. Jean, is attached to no other document in the sources consulted. The hypothesis is obvious: if the producers and Zwady hold three of the four writing lines, the fourth could be the legal name of Fireman Lee, the second singer on the record. It is plausible. It is proven by nothing.

The searches run for this profile on the name Leslie St. Jean, in music and songwriting contexts, produced no document linking him to Fireman Lee, to the Dwivayez Riddim or to the Dominica scene. No record page, no article, no artist page makes the equation. In those conditions, this site rule applies without exception: two names are not merged on a likelihood. Merging wrongly would give Fireman Lee a legal identity that may not be his, and would give Leslie St. Jean a stage name that may not be his either.

This profile therefore writes the two names separately, each in its documented place: Fireman Lee, featured singer on “Down Deh”; Leslie St. Jean, fourth credited writer of the same record. If a primary document, a label credit, an interview, a publisher page, one day establishes the link, this page will be corrected and dated, and the two lines will become a single biography. Until then, the separation is the exact shape of the available truth, and it is worth more than an invented identity.

Do not confuse: Fireman Hooper and the other Firemen

The word Fireman clutters the soca and dancehall scenes, and the most likely confusion concerns Delroy “Fireman” Hooper. Artist directories, including the WOMEX listing opened during this research, present him as an artist from Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, seven times Soca Monarch of his country, winner of Ragga Soca in 2010 with “Holy Fire”. Fireman Hooper has a surname, Hooper, a country, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, a trophy list and a catalog. Fireman Lee has none of that in the sources: a different name, a single credit, on a Dominica riddim. The two artists share only the word Fireman, which is a common stage nickname in the Caribbean, tied to energy and to the fire of a performance.

The same caution applies to the rest of the neighbourhood. Dancehall knows other figures presented as men of fire, starting with nicknames attached to established Jamaican artists, and the platforms happily mix those names in their results. This profile attaches Fireman Lee to none of those figures: neither the “Down Deh” credit, nor the Dwivayez Riddim press, nor any page consulted mentions a link, a shared past or a name change. The only documented belonging of Fireman Lee is this one: the Zwady cut of the Dwivayez Riddim, in Dominica, in 2020.

This rigour is not a documentalist luxury. In bouyon and soca, the credit is the artists currency: it decides who gets paid, who gets invited, who gets cited. Giving Fireman Hooper trophy list to Fireman Lee would rob the first and inflate the second with a career that is not in his documents. Both men are entitled to their respective files, one thick and crowned, the other a single line, and this site keeps both without mixing them.

The limits of the file, stated plainly

Let us recap what is established, and only that. Fireman Lee is a singer credited as a feature, with Zwady, on “Down Deh”, by Producerdlo, third track of the Dwivayez Riddim, an EP released on August 28, 2020 on GloSho Music. The regional press of February 2021 confirms the credit, with the title spelled “Down Dere”. The riddim is produced by two producers from Dominica, Dylan Lowe and Savion Augustine, and the other voices of the EP are Shelly, Starsha and Trilla-G. That is the complete file, and it fits in one paragraph.

Everything else is unknown, and this profile leaves it unknown. No solo single by Fireman Lee was found in the catalogs consulted. No second feature, no artist page on the platforms, no verified social account, no biography, no origin, no village. Searches run on his name, in bouyon, soca, Caribbean music and Dominica contexts, return only the Dwivayez Riddim and namesakes. That is why this page structured data carries an empty sameAs list: there is no Fireman Lee page of his own to link, and inventing a link would be worse than having none.

Why, then, a profile for a single credit? Because the cut he stands on is real and listened to: “Down Deh” is Zwady most streamed record on his artist page, and the Dwivayez Riddim is a document of the meeting between Dominican production and the regional soca circuit at the turn of 2021. A listener who hears that record will look up the second name in the credit, and he is entitled to an exact answer rather than a fabricated biography. If Fireman Lee releases more records, or if a document establishes his identity, this profile will be completed and dated. Until then, listening goes through the Dwivayez Riddim EP and our bouyon playlist, which follows the voices of this scene as they release.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Fireman Lee?

A singer with exactly one credit verifiable in the public sources: the feature, alongside Zwady, on Down Deh, by Producerdlo, released on August 28, 2020 as part of the Dwivayez Riddim EP, on GloSho Music. No biography, no artist page, no official account and no other record were found during the research for this profile. His legal identity is not documented, and this site does not presume it.

What record has Fireman Lee made?

Down Deh, the third track of the Dwivayez Riddim, an EP by Producerdlo published on August 28, 2020. The record is sung in English, at 117 BPM, with Zwady also as a feature, and it was written by Dylan Christon Lowe, Savion Augustine, Aswad Polydore and Leslie St. Jean. The regional press of February 2021 announced the same record under the spelling Down Dere, credited to Zwady with Fireman Lee as a feature.

Are Fireman Lee and Fireman Hooper the same artist?

No, nothing links them, and their files are separate. Delroy “Fireman” Hooper is an artist from Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, seven times Soca Monarch of his country according to artist directories, and winner of Ragga Soca in 2010 with Holy Fire. Fireman Lee appears only on Down Deh, a Dominica riddim produced by ProducerDlo and Savion Beats. The two artists share only the Fireman nickname, common in soca and dancehall.

Is Leslie St. Jean the same person as Fireman Lee?

It is not proven. Leslie St. Jean is the fourth credited writer of Down Deh, after the two producers and Aswad Polydore, who is Zwady. The hypothesis that this is the legal name of Fireman Lee, the second singer on the record, is plausible, but no source consulted makes it: no record page, no article and no artist page links the two names. This profile therefore keeps them separate, and it will be corrected the day a primary document establishes the link.

Has Fireman Lee released other records?

No other record was verified. Searches run on his name in bouyon, soca, Caribbean music and Dominica contexts return only the Dwivayez Riddim, plus unrelated namesakes. No solo single, no second feature, no artist page on the platforms consulted. If another recording exists under this name, it is not documented in the public catalogs opened for this profile.

Where can I listen to Fireman Lee?

On Down Deh, in the Dwivayez Riddim EP by Producerdlo, available on the platforms, alongside Happy Place by Shelly, System Failure by Starsha and Deliway by Trilla-G. To carry on into this Dominica scene, the Bouyon Shattaz Bouyon Road Mix playlist follows the island's releases.

Who produced Down Deh with Fireman Lee?

Two producers from Dominica: ProducerDlo, real name Dylan Lowe, and Savion Beats, real name Savion Augustine, whom the press presents as a two time RIAA Gold certified producer for work outside the island. The record is released under the Producerdlo name, and both producers are also among its four credited writers.

Which riddim is Fireman Lee on?

The Dwivayez Riddim, released on August 28, 2020 on GloSho Music. Its name comes from the French Creole of Dominica: dwive means to lime or wander, and a dwivayez limes from fete to fete. Born during lockdown, the project wanted a sound that makes you leave the house. Fireman Lee sings on its third track, Down Deh. See bouyon riddim.

Is it Down Deh or Down Dere?

The platforms spell it Down Deh, and that is the spelling on the record page and on the EP released on August 28, 2020. The regional press of February 2021, in an article datelined Port of Spain, spelled it Down Dere, for the same record credited to Zwady with Fireman Lee. This site keeps Down Deh as the title and flags the variant so that nobody loses the trail.

Who sings with Fireman Lee on Down Deh?

Zwady, a singer and songwriter in the Dominica bouyon and soca scene, credited as a writer under the name Aswad Polydore. Down Deh is the most streamed record of his whole catalog according to his Shazam artist page. The two voices share the same riddim cut, and it is through this Zwady record that the name Fireman Lee exists in the archive.

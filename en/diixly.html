Diixly: one beatmaker, one verified track, one thin record

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Diixly is a beatmaker whose name appears, in the whole public record this research could open, in exactly one precise place: the production credit of the track “GENKIDAMA”, by FLW (T-BTS), released as an official video under the Team Bwe Tou Sa banner, tagged bouyon 2026. This profile therefore states two things with the same clarity: what that credit establishes, line by line, and everything it does not establish. No real name, no origin, no biography invented to fill the page: a beatmaker of the new French Antilles bouyon wave, documented by one track, and presented here as such, with the limits of the record written out in full.

Who Diixly is: a beatmaker name and a single verified credit

Diixly is a beatmaker. That is the exact word of the only verified public credit this research found under his name: the description of the official “GENKIDAMA” video lists him as “Beatmaker: Diixly”, behind the direction of Dylan Farah and the engineering of Gammalab. The video title pairs his name with the lead artist in the form FLW (T-BTS) X Diixly, which places him, on this track, as a named collaborator rather than an anonymous hired hand. In the economy of current bouyon, where the producer signs the sound as much as the singer signs the voice, a beatmaker credit is a full signature.

For the rest, this profile must be frank: after repeated searches of the general web and of the streaming platforms, no artist page, no biography, no other track credit, no real name and no documented origin were found for Diixly during the preparation of this page. The platforms that usually document emerging bouyon artists carry, as of today and to our verified knowledge, no entry under this name. That emptiness is not a judgment on the artist: it is the state of the public record, and this site would rather write it than dress it up.

A simple rule follows for this page: everything below rests on the video credit and on the documented context of the track, nothing else. When a piece of information is missing, the page says so. If Diixly publishes an official page, new tracks or a biography, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site. In the meantime, the record holds one track, and that track deserves a close reading, because it says a great deal about how bouyon is made in 2026.

GENKIDAMA: the track, the video, the credits line by line

“GENKIDAMA” is a bouyon track performed by FLW, an artist who signs FLW (T-BTS), and Diixly is credited on it as beatmaker. The official video is published under the Team Bwe Tou Sa keywords and the bouyon and Bouyon2026 hashtags, and the video description announces the track as available on all platforms, with a DistroKid hyperfollow link attached to the Diixly name. That link, truncated in the text fetched by this research, is not reproduced here: this site only publishes addresses it has verified by opening them, and this one was not verified to the end.

The published credits of the video are complete and they draw a small professional team: direction by Dylan Farah, sound engineer Gammalab, beatmaker Diixly. Three names, three trades, one track. That division is the usual grammar of French Antilles bouyon today: a collective artist, a director for the image, an engineer for the sound, and a beatmaker for the rhythm. Diixly holds, in that grammar, the post that decides the tempo, the loop and the energy of the track, the one the singers join once the rhythm is laid down.

The track name itself belongs to the popular culture of its generation: Genkidama refers, in the manga imaginary widely shared by young Antillean audiences, to the energy sphere of Dragon Ball. This profile notes that reference as a reading of the title, without lending Diixly or FLW a statement no source publishes about the choice of the name. The video keywords, by contrast, are factual: bouyon 2026, bouyon Guadeloupe, bouyon Martinique, bouyon Dominica, Antillean music and Caribbean music appear there as search terms for the clip. They describe the market the track aims at, the French Antilles and the diaspora, and not the personal origin of Diixly, which nothing documents.

To place this track in the landscape of the moment, our bouyon playlist follows new bouyon releases from the Antilles and Dominica, and our bouyon gwada page tells the Guadeloupe branch of the genre whose collective videos like this one are its most visible form in 2026. “GENKIDAMA” takes its place there as a complete specimen, albeit a unique one in its beatmaker record: an official video, signed credits, and a release on all platforms announced in the description itself.

The context: collective bouyon in the French Antilles in 2026

The bouyon that dominates the platforms in 2026 no longer resembles, in its organization, the live band bouyon of 1990s Roseau. It moves by collectives, by short tracks built for social media, and by credits shared between singers, beatmakers and directors. FLW carries the T-BTS mention in his artist name and the “GENKIDAMA” video is published with the Team Bwe Tou Sa banner: the Diixly track belongs to that collective form of French Antilles bouyon, where the team signature often counts as much as the artist name.

In that economy, the beatmaker is an author in his own right, and his relative invisibility in the databases is a paradox this very record documents. Platforms index the performers; beatmakers live in video descriptions, clip credits and metadata that search engines surface badly. The Diixly case shows it plainly: one official credit, checkable by anyone who opens the video, and almost nothing around it. A profile like this one also serves that purpose: fixing the credit in indexed text, with the name spelled exactly as the source spells it, Diixly, so that the next listener who searches the name finds at least the trace of the track.

That context also gives the single track its fair measure. One credit does not make a discography, and this profile does not pretend otherwise. But in a genre where many rhythms circulate with no published credit at all, a beatmaker named in an official video distributed on all platforms holds a precise position: that of the emerging producer whose public catalogue is beginning. What follows depends on releases this site cannot invent, and which it will follow if they arrive.

What this profile does not know: the limits of the public record

This page ends as it began, with the honesty of the record. Not present in the sources opened for this research, and therefore not written here: the real name of Diixly, his island or country of origin, his age, his path before “GENKIDAMA”, any other productions he may have, his verified official accounts and his listening figures. None of these elements is guessed, deduced from the keywords of a video, or borrowed from a namesake. The searches were run across the general web and the major streaming platforms, and they were closed on this finding, not on a lack of effort.

The recording list in this page schema reflects that state exactly: a single track, “GENKIDAMA”, the only one whose Diixly credit is published and verified. Where other profiles on this site list eight to fifteen tracks for established artists, this one lists one, because one is the true number. If a reader holds a verifiable source on Diixly, an official credit, an artist page, a second track, this profile is built to be corrected and expanded, with the date of the correction, following the practice of this site.

In the meantime, listening begins and ends, for this beatmaker, with “GENKIDAMA” by FLW (T-BTS), the official Team Bwe Tou Sa video, then continues through this site bouyon playlist, which follows the 2026 bouyon wave of the French Antilles and Dominica. Diixly appears there for what he is in the public record: the signed beatmaker of one track of that wave, no more, no less, and that is already a trace this site judges worth keeping.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Diixly?

Diixly is a beatmaker credited on the bouyon track “GENKIDAMA” by FLW (T-BTS), released as an official video under the Team Bwe Tou Sa banner. The video description lists him exactly as “Beatmaker: Diixly”, with Dylan Farah as director and Gammalab as engineer. No biography, no real name and no origin are published in the sources opened for this profile, and this site invents none.

On which track is Diixly credited, and in what role?

On “GENKIDAMA”, by FLW (T-BTS), in the role of beatmaker, that is, the author of the track rhythm. The official video presents the track as FLW (T-BTS) X Diixly and announces it as available on all platforms, with a DistroKid link attached to the Diixly name. As of the date of this profile, it is the only verified public credit for Diixly found by this research on the web and on the streaming platforms.

Is Diixly a singer or a producer?

A producer, according to the only published credit: he is listed as beatmaker, not as a vocal performer. The performer of the track is FLW, who signs FLW (T-BTS). The other published credits of the video are Dylan Farah for the video direction and Gammalab as sound engineer. Crediting Diixly with a singing role on this track would contradict the only available source, and this site does not do it.

Where is Diixly from?

This profile is one of the few to answer honestly: it is not known, because no verified open source says so. The keywords of the “GENKIDAMA” video cite bouyon from Guadeloupe, Martinique and Dominica as generic search terms of the genre; they describe the market of the track, not the origin of its beatmaker. Writing an island of origin for Diixly would be an invention, so this site publishes the absence of information rather than a false precision.

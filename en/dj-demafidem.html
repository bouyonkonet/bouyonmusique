DJ Demafidem: one verified riddim, On Di Road, bouyon from Guadeloupe

Published October 20, 2026

DJ Demafidem is a producer name that the international public discovered in one precise place: the fourth track on Side A of “Road Fever”, the carnival riddims compilation released by the London label Soundway Records on May 9, 2025. The track is called “On Di Road Riddim”, it runs 2 minutes 50, the digital listing of the record dates it to 2023, and the official label notes say of its maker, in the same sentence as Shazdown Mmw, that he is based in Guadeloupe. That is, to this day, the whole verified file: one track, one base, one label sentence, and two press mentions that cite the track without telling the man. This profile says everything the opened sources allow to be said, it refuses the unverified titles that aggregator pages circulate under this name, and it states plainly, from this introduction, that the DJ Demafidem file is one of the thinnest on this scene.

One track, one base, one sentence: the inventory without makeup

Start with the complete inventory, because it holds three pieces and hiding that would be dishonest. First piece: a name, DJ Demafidem, spelled that way on the vinyl listing, in the label notes and in the presentations of the record. Second piece: a track, “On Di Road Riddim”, an instrumental dated 2023 in the digital listing of “Road Fever”. Third piece: a base, Guadeloupe, stated by the official Soundway Records notes in a sentence that pairs DJ Demafidem with Shazdown Mmw, while ScarnX, in the same sentence, carries the bouyon sound of Dominica. Everything else in this profile is the verified context of those three pieces: the record, its Side A, its compiler, the riddim format and the Guadeloupe branch of bouyon.

What the research did not find must be written just as clearly. No verified standalone DJ Demafidem artist page was confirmed during this research on Spotify, Apple Music, Shazam, Deezer, Qobuz, SoundCloud, Audiomack, YouTube or Discogs. The Bandcamp Daily review links his name to a SoundCloud page, but that page could not be opened and verified during this session, so its address is not printed here and it does not enter the official links of this page. No interview, no biography, no legal name, no precise Guadeloupe town, no verified photograph and no concert date were confirmed. The name DJ Demafidem suggests a DJ activity as much as a producer activity, but no opened source documents a single one of his sets, and this profile does not turn a suggestion carried by a name into a fact.

A method warning is needed here more than anywhere else on this site. Aggregator pages, of the kind that build automatic entries for every name in world music, circulate other titles under the DJ Demafidem name, and even a different geographical base. Those pages were not used as sources: none of those titles was found on a primary platform page opened during this research, and none of those claims comes from the label that releases his music. This profile therefore repeats none of those titles, neither in the text nor in the structured data. In a riddim scene where producer names resemble and cross each other, confusing two bearers of one name would be the exact fault this page exists to avoid.

Read this profile, then, for what it is: the portrait of a track and its place inside a document record, not the biography of a person. That portrait still has real material. “On Di Road Riddim” is on the vinyl, not only in digital, at the fourth position of Side A, at the centre of the Guadeloupe block of the record. The label own Bandcamp Daily review cites the track by name, together with the Shazdown Mmw track, as an example of the work of these producers. And the Guadeloupe attachment in the label notes makes DJ Demafidem, with a single track, one half of the named Guadeloupe presence on the most visible bouyon compilation of 2025. One track can sometimes hold a place, on condition that the place is described exactly.

“On Di Road Riddim”: 2 minutes 50, dated 2023, at the centre of Side A

The only verified DJ Demafidem recording is therefore “On Di Road Riddim”. On the official compilation page, the piece runs 2 minutes 50 and the digital listing dates it to 2023, which makes it one of the most recent pieces in the programme, in the same year as “Heat Wave Riddim” by Viral Riddims and “Bouyon Truth Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw: where part of the record digs up riddims from 2012 to 2019, the DJ Demafidem piece was about two years old when Soundway released it. That relative freshness matters when reading the compilation: compiler Rozaly is not only doing archive work there, he is also showing the state of the Guadeloupe sound at the moment he compiles.

The position of the track is its second document. The vinyl holds fourteen tracks, seven per side. Side A opens with “Soca Panda Riddim” by Ransum Records then “Smash Or Pass Riddim” by Viral Riddims, two Lucian pieces, then tips toward Guadeloupe with “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw in third position and “On Di Road Riddim” in fourth, before “2 Stroke Riddim” by G6 Productions, “Both Twanche Riddim Refix” by Bad Sound and DJ Marfox, and “Dirty Lows Riddim” by Veaygel Productions. DJ Demafidem therefore stands exactly between his label compatriot and the memory of G6 Productions, the late Lucian producer whom the record notes salute as a trailblazer. On a compilation, neighbourhoods are sentences: this one says that the Guadeloupe block holds the centre of Side A.

The title, finally, speaks the language of the whole record. On Di Road, followed by the word Riddim that files the piece: the name echoes the title of the compilation itself, “Road Fever”, and the use the label notes describe for this music, best heard blasted through walls of speakers travelled by carnival trucks or through bluetooth car speakers. No source says the title was chosen for the compilation, and its 2023 date proves the opposite: the coincidence predates the record, and it simply says that the producer and the compiler share the same imagery, the road as the main stage of bouyon and Dennery Segment.

On the sound itself, only one verified critical description exists, and it comes from Bandcamp Daily. In its Album of the Day review of May 12, 2025, the magazine cites “On Di Road” with “Bouyon Beam” among the tracks with punchy intros, built from synth stabs and vocal samples, which show how these producers reimagine traditional music through laptop production, as futuristic sonic collages rather than brass sections or piano. The same review names DJ Demafidem, with Ransum Records and the late G6 Productions, among the names whose tracks power the compilation. That is little, but it is a named, dated and attributed reading, and this profile holds to it without adding a single image of its own about a piece it presents from sources. Our bouyon riddim page explains the grammar of these instrumentals, and our instrumental bouyon page follows the voiceless format across the genre.

The Guadeloupe block of Road Fever: two names, four tracks, one label sentence

To understand the place of DJ Demafidem, you have to look at the full map the label notes draw for “Road Fever”. The record gathers instrumental Dennery Segment and bouyon riddims from three islands, Saint Lucia, Dominica and Guadeloupe, pieces first built for singers and released here for the first time without vocals, at a minimal tempo above 150 beats per minute, with FL Studio percussion packs, DJ and vox samples and synth riffs, at the crossing of trap, drill, dancehall, sped up Haitian konpa and Angolan kuduro. In those notes, the Lucian producers are many, Ransum, Krome, Veaygel, LMT Mafia, Slaughter Arts Media, G6. The named Dominican is one, ScarnX. The named Guadeloupeans are two, Shazdown Mmw and DJ Demafidem.

Inside that two name Guadeloupe block, the split of tracks is uneven and must be given exactly: Shazdown Mmw signs three pieces, “Bouyon Beam Riddim” on the vinyl and two digital pieces, “Bouyon Truth Riddim” and “Bouyon 3004 Riddim”; DJ Demafidem signs one only, “On Di Road Riddim”, but it is on the vinyl. The block therefore counts four tracks, two of them on the physical object. That asymmetry draws two different profiles of the same side: a producer better supplied in catalog, and a producer represented by a single piece chosen for the most exposed format. No source says why the compiler kept only one DJ Demafidem piece, and this profile does not invent it: neither catalog scarcity, nor a late choice, nor any of the easy explanations that circulate in such cases.

The Patta presentation, the compiler partner, places the two names back in the whole without telling them apart: Shazdown Mmw, DJ Demafidem and ScarnX appear there in the same list of production crews, established and underground, whose contributions make the record both a celebration and a time capsule of a regional sound on the rise. Patta also sums up the target of the record: Dennery Segment from Saint Lucia and bouyon from Dominica and Guadeloupe, two styles defined by their fierce minimalism, their DIY ethos and their relentless drive. DJ Demafidem therefore belongs, in every document of the record, to the Guadeloupe branch of bouyon, and to no other. The Resident Advisor review, which placed Shazdown on the Dominica side by error or by shortcut, says nothing about the island of DJ Demafidem: on this name, the label stays the only voice, and it is uncontradicted.

That geography has a history the record assumes as known and that this site documents elsewhere. Bouyon was born in Dominica, in Roseau, in the late 1980s in the wake of WCK, and its best known voiced branch, bouyon soca, was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica according to the first hand testimony of Sean “Smokiller” Francis, who was in the rooms where it began, then popularized by Triple Kay. Guadeloupe, the neighbouring island tied to Dominica by a constant circulation of artists, DJs and audiences, made the genre a practice of its own, with its producers and its riddims. It is in that Guadeloupe branch, documented on our bouyon Guadeloupe page, that the label places DJ Demafidem. His single verified track is therefore also a sample: of the sound Guadeloupe was bringing to instrumental bouyon in the early 2020s, heard from London.

The reception of the record, and the exact place of On Di Road in that reception

“Road Fever” did not stay a confidential object, and the place of DJ Demafidem must be measured in the real reception of the record, without inflating it. The record came out on May 9, 2025 on Soundway Records, a London label, on vinyl pressed by Garcia et Co in Marciac, in France, digitally and as a limited cassette: a 55 and a half minute tape mixed by DJ Intl Prostyle in Saint Lucia, delivered with the full digital download, which the label page announces as 26 tracks while the displayed digital listing counts 25. Mastering and vinyl cut are by The Carvery, design by Wolfe Hall Studio, photography by Selwyn De Wind, who also cosigns the short reel format film that accompanies the release, and the compilation is the work of Rozaly, a DJ, curator and researcher based in Amsterdam and raised in Curacao.

Bandcamp Daily made it its album of the day on May 12, 2025, stressing the laptop production and Fruity Loops, the samples, the crossed influences of Angolan kuduro, Portuguese batida and trap, and this party music made for giant club speakers or for a moving car. It is in that review that “On Di Road” receives its only named critical description, already quoted in this profile, and that the name DJ Demafidem is cited among those who power the record. Resident Advisor published its review on May 21, 2025, calling it an invaluable deconstructed snapshot of two modern Caribbean riddims, Dennery and bouyon, and noting that these tracks, usually carried by vocals, gain from being heard bare. That review gives DJ Demafidem no passage of his own, and that silence is part of the file just as much as the Bandcamp Daily mention.

Patta, finally, presented the record as a time capsule as much as a celebration, with a tempo that rarely dips below 150 beats per minute, FL Studio percussion, chopped DJ samples, distorted vox and synth riffs that could cut steel. In that presentation, as in the label one, DJ Demafidem appears in the contributor lists, never in a separate portrait. The sum of the verified reception is therefore the following: a name present in every document of the record, a track described once by the label press, and no biographical line anywhere. This profile reflects that sum exactly, including in its structured data, which holds one recording and one official link, the compilation page.

One question remains that the record raises without answering, and it holds for DJ Demafidem as for almost all its producers: which voice carried “On Di Road Riddim” between 2023 and 2025? The label notes say these instrumentals were built for singers, and Resident Advisor recalls that these tracks were all previously released featuring vocalists. But no document consulted names the voiced version of “On Di Road Riddim”, nor its performer, nor its carnival song title. Inventing that link would be easy and false. A documented blank therefore stays at the centre of the file: the riddim is identified, dated and placed; its voiced life is not yet. Our bouyon soca page retraces the voiced branch of this same history, from Dada and Triple Kay to the current scenes.

The limits of the file, and why a page anyway

Sum up the limits without softening them, because here they are wider than for most artists on this site. Nothing is known of DJ Demafidem beyond the credit: not the legal name, not the age, not the town, not the studio, not the collaborations, not the voices that sang over his production, not his possible DJ sets, not even whether the name covers one person or a team. No second track verified on a primary source is known. No official account whose address could be verified during this research is known, the SoundCloud page signalled by a Bandcamp Daily link having been impossible to open. The verified discography fits on one line, “On Di Road Riddim”, and that is exactly what the structured data of this page holds: one recording, one official link, nothing else.

Why, then, a full page for one line of discography? Because that line sits in a place the history of the genre will keep. “Road Fever” is, to this day, the international reference document on instrumental bouyon and Dennery Segment: compiled by a researcher, pressed on vinyl, reviewed by Bandcamp Daily and Resident Advisor, it fixes producer names that the riddim circuit left in the shadow of the voices. DJ Demafidem is in it, dated, credited, placed on the Guadeloupe side by the label itself, at the centre of Side A. Removing his page because the file is thin would erase one named half of the Guadeloupe presence on the record. The thinness of the file is information, and it is written here in full, including in this sentence.

This profile will therefore be updated on the day the material exists: a verifiable official artist page, a second track on a primary platform, the name of the voiced version of “On Di Road Riddim”, or an interview with the producer or the compiler. Until then, the recommended listening is short and precise: “On Di Road Riddim” in the order of “Road Fever”, between “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw and “2 Stroke Riddim” by G6 Productions, then, for the voiced and current side of the same genre, the tracks in our bouyon playlist, and the profile of ScarnX, the other name the label notes attach to bouyon on this record, on the Dominica side. And if a reader holds a credit, a flyer, a mix or a sleeve where DJ Demafidem appears before 2025, that is exactly the kind of document that will grow this file, on condition that it is primary: an aggregator will not be enough.

Frequently asked questions

Who is DJ Demafidem?

DJ Demafidem is the producer name credited with the track “On Di Road Riddim” on the “Road Fever” compilation by Soundway Records, released on May 9, 2025. The official label notes say he is based in Guadeloupe, alongside Shazdown Mmw. No source consulted gives his legal name, his biography or his make up, one person or a team, and this profile does not invent them.

What is the verified DJ Demafidem track?

Only one: “On Di Road Riddim”, an instrumental of 2 minutes 50, dated 2023 on the compilation page. It holds the fourth position on Side A of the vinyl, between “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw and “2 Stroke Riddim” by G6 Productions, and keeps position four in the 25 track digital listing. No other track was verified on a primary source during this research.

Why do some aggregator pages credit him with other tracks?

Aggregator pages build automatic entries under his name, with other titles and even a different geographical base. This site does not repeat them: none of those titles was found on a primary platform page opened during the research, and aggregators are not sources for the data of this page. Only the official Soundway compilation page counts here.

Is DJ Demafidem from Guadeloupe or from Dominica?

Based in Guadeloupe, according to the official Soundway Records notes, which pair DJ Demafidem and Shazdown Mmw in the same sentence, while ScarnX carries the bouyon sound of Dominica on the same record. No source consulted contradicts that attachment for DJ Demafidem, and none gives his precise town in Guadeloupe.

What does the press say about “On Di Road Riddim”?

Bandcamp Daily, on May 12, 2025, cites “On Di Road” with “Bouyon Beam” among the tracks with punchy intros, built from synth stabs and vocal samples, which show how these producers reimagine traditional music through laptop production. The same review names DJ Demafidem among the artists whose tracks power the compilation. Resident Advisor gives him no passage of his own.

Where can I hear DJ Demafidem?

On “Road Fever”, through the Soundway Records Bandcamp page, on vinyl, digitally or on cassette. In the order of the record, hear “On Di Road Riddim” after “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw.

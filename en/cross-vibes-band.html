Cross Vibes Band: a name on a bouyon festival bill, Dominica

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Cross Vibes Band is a bouyon band from Dominica whose public record, after a full search carried out for this profile, comes down to a single line: a festival bill. The February calendar of Domnitjen magazine announces a Bouyon Festival presented by the Dominica Festivals Committee, an open air concert featuring bouyon bands including Cross Vibes, MFR, Triple Kay, Swinging Stars, Rhythm and Heat and WCK, at Carnival City, Pottersville Savannah, at 9 p.m., for 20 Eastern Caribbean dollars, with part of the proceeds going to the Haiti Earthquake Relief Fund. Everything else, members, songs, formation dates, official pages, could not be verified. This profile therefore publishes exactly what the record holds, says why it stops there, and explains what that single line still proves: in that carnival era, Cross Vibes Band played on the same stage as the founders of the genre.

What the public record holds: one line, and nothing more

The research for this profile was carried out as it is for every artist on this site: search engine first, then opening the original sources. For Cross Vibes Band, the result is unusually thin. A single independent source names the band in a verifiable Dominican context: the February calendar of Domnitjen magazine, which lists the events of Dominica carnival. The band appears there in the short form “Cross Vibes”, in the list of bands for a festival concert. No artist page, no streaming platform profile, no member list, no press article and no second event were found under the forms Cross Vibes Band, Cross Vibes or CrossVibes that could be attached with certainty to this Dominican band.

Acts carrying similar names exist elsewhere in the world, and this profile is careful not to merge them with this one. Attributing the biography, songs or photographs of a same named band from another country to a bouyon band from Pottersville would be exactly the kind of error this site refuses to make. Here, a name is worth only what its source carries. The source is Dominican, festival based and precise; it states a concert, a venue, a price and a shared bill. It states nothing else, and this profile makes it state nothing else.

That thinness is published instead of disguised. Other directories would fill the blanks with a plausible formation date, a hometown and a track list invented out of whole cloth. Bouyon Shattaz does not: when the record is thin, the page says so, because a reader looking for Cross Vibes Band deserves to know that the band public trace is almost entirely oral and local, kept in carnival memory rather than in web archives. The rest of this profile works honestly with the single line available, because it holds more information than it first appears to.

The Bouyon Festival: the night the bill documents

Here is the line, in its full context as Domnitjen publishes it. The Dominica Festivals Committee presents a Bouyon Festival. The magazine defines bouyon music, for its readers, as a mix of zouk, cadence and soca over a traditional folk music base, created in the 1980s. The concert is open air. It is held at Carnival City, Pottersville Savannah, in Pottersville, on the edge of Roseau. It starts at 9 p.m. Admission is 20 Eastern Caribbean dollars. And part of the proceeds go to the Haiti Earthquake Relief Fund, a charitable detail the bill takes care to announce.

The Haitian detail is also a disguised date, and this profile treats it with care. The calendar page consulted for this research does not print a year in the retrieved text, so no year is stated here as fact. But a donation appeal for the victims of the Haiti earthquake, attached to a Dominican carnival concert organized by the official festivals committee, places the bill in the carnival season that followed the disaster, when the whole Caribbean was organizing solidarity concerts. What can be said without risk is simpler and more useful: at the moment this bill was published, Cross Vibes Band was an active bouyon band, programmed by the very institution that organizes Dominica carnival.

The venue matters too. Pottersville Savannah, the Carnival City of the bills of that era, is the historic outdoor stage for the big nights of Roseau carnival: calypso competitions, the carnival queen crowning and festival concerts follow one another in the same Domnitjen February calendar. A band programmed there, on a festival night at 9 p.m. with a paid ticket, is not a rehearsal band or a one night formation: it is a band the festivals committee judged able to hold a carnival stage in front of a paying audience. The bill gives neither the crowd size nor the band exact place in the running order, and this profile does not invent them.

The company on the bill: WCK, Triple Kay and the band era

A bill is a document of hierarchy as much as of programming, and this one places Cross Vibes Band in precise company. At the head of the same list stand WCK, the band that created bouyon in 1988, and Triple Kay, the band that later carried bouyon soca and the big Dominican carnival stages, alongside MFR, the Swinging Stars and Rhythm and Heat. To be named on that bill, in that list, by that committee, means that Cross Vibes Band belonged, at the time of the concert, to the recognized circuit of Dominican bouyon bands: not a footnote, and not a name from outside the genre.

The bill also documents an era. At the moment it describes, Dominican bouyon is still a band music: full formations, with rhythm sections and horns or keyboards, sharing carnival stages and festivals. The solo artist generation, which dominates today bills, has not yet taken over. Cross Vibes Band belongs to that band landscape, in which several names on the same bill, such as the Swinging Stars, reach back to stage traditions far older than bouyon itself. The band therefore shared a stage between the founders of the genre and institutions of Dominican entertainment.

What the bill does not allow anyone to say must be stated just as clearly. It names no member, no singer, no instrumentalist. It cites no song title, no album, no carnival hit. It does not say whether the band recorded, toured outside Dominica or still existed in the following years. Any ranking of the band, any comparison of popularity with WCK or Triple Kay, any discography would be invention. This profile prefers one true line to ten false paragraphs: Cross Vibes Band played the official Bouyon Festival, alongside the biggest names in the genre, and that is, to this day, the central and almost only fact of its public record.

Why no songs are listed here, and how this record can grow

Profiles on this site list verified recordings in their schema, title by title, when sources allow it. For Cross Vibes Band, no recording could be verified: no streaming platform page under the band name was found and opened, no official credit attaches a precise title to the band, no archive video consulted carries its name in a usable way. The recording list on this page is therefore deliberately empty, rather than filled with guessed titles. The same rule leaves the official links field empty: no account or site of the band was opened and verified during this research, so no link is published.

If the band recorded, its titles probably live where much of the band era bouyon lives: carnival tapes and discs, Dominican radio archives, family videos and musicians memory. That kind of archive regularly resurfaces, carried by collectors and by former members themselves. If a member of the band, a relative or a reader holds a programme, a recording or a photograph from that era, this record can be completed, dated and corrected like every page on this site, with sources cited instead of memories alone.

In the meantime, listening to that era runs through the names on the same bill whose records are documented: WCK and Triple Kay, gathered in our bouyon playlist, and the story of those band years on our history of bouyon page. Cross Vibes Band holds a modest but real place there: that of a band from the Pottersville scene whose name survived in a carnival calendar, between the founders of the genre and a donation appeal for Haiti. Some bouyon stories run to ten albums. This one runs to a single line, and that line is true.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Cross Vibes Band?

Cross Vibes Band is a bouyon band from Dominica known, in the verifiable public record, by a single mention: the February calendar of Domnitjen magazine, which lists it among the bands at a Bouyon Festival organized by the Dominica Festivals Committee at Carnival City, Pottersville Savannah. No verified biography, member list or discography of the band was found during the research for this profile.

When and where did Cross Vibes Band play, according to the record?

According to Domnitjen, the band appeared on the bill of an open air Bouyon Festival at Carnival City, Pottersville Savannah, in Pottersville, starting at 9 p.m. with admission at 20 Eastern Caribbean dollars, part of the proceeds going to the Haiti Earthquake Relief Fund. The page consulted does not print a year, so this site states none.

Which other bands shared the bill with Cross Vibes Band?

The same announcement lists MFR, Triple Kay, the Swinging Stars, Rhythm and Heat and WCK, the founding band of bouyon. Cross Vibes Band was therefore programmed, by Dominica official festivals committee, on the same stage as the founders of the genre and one of the biggest bands of the following generation.

Which Cross Vibes Band songs can be heard?

No song by the band could be verified during this research: no streaming platform page, no official credit and no archived recording carrying its name were found and opened. This profile therefore lists no title, rather than guessing one. To hear band era bouyon from the same era, our bouyon playlist gathers in particular WCK and Triple Kay, the documented names from the same bill.

Is Cross Vibes Band the same act as the Cross Vibes names found elsewhere online?

That cannot be confirmed, and this profile is one of the few to say so plainly. Acts carrying identical or neighbouring names exist in other countries, with no verified link to the Dominican band on the Pottersville bill. With no source connecting those namesakes to the bouyon band, this site attributes neither their biography, their songs nor their images to the Dominican band, and does not merge the records.

Why is this profile so much shorter than the other artists on this site?

Because the band public record is thin, and this site publishes the length that verified sources allow, no more. A single independent document, the Domnitjen calendar, names the band in a verifiable way. Rather than filling the blanks with invention, this profile publishes that document in full, explains what it proves and what it does not prove, and stays open to any archive, programme or recording that could complete it with sources.

How does the Bouyon Festival listing define bouyon?

The Domnitjen calendar, which announces the Bouyon Festival where Cross Vibes Band plays, presents bouyon as a mix of zouk, cadence and soca over a traditional folk music base, created in the 1980s. That is the definition given to the carnival audience of the time. The genre was born in Dominica with WCK, in 1988, as told on our bouyon history page.

Where is Carnival City, the stage Cross Vibes Band played?

Carnival City is Pottersville Savannah, in Pottersville, at the edge of Roseau, the capital of Dominica. It is the historic outdoor stage of Roseau’s big carnival nights: calypso contests, the carnival queen coronation and festival concerts follow one another in the same Domnitjen February calendar. The island’s carnival is presented on our Dominica carnival page.

Who is Triple Kay, on the same bill as Cross Vibes Band?

Triple Kay, now TK International, also called Triple Kay Band, is an established bouyon band from Dominica, in the live band tradition that followed WCK, and the band that later carried bouyon soca and the big carnival stages. It plays night 1 of the World Creole Music Festival on 23 October 2026. See our TK International page.

Which era of bouyon does Cross Vibes Band belong to?

The band era. At the time described by the Bouyon Festival listing, Dominica bouyon is still the music of full bands, with rhythm sections and keyboards or horns, sharing carnival stages with WCK, Triple Kay, MFR, the Swinging Stars and Rhythm and Heat. The generation of solo artists has not yet taken over. That sound is told on our old school bouyon page.

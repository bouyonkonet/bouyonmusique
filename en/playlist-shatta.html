Frequently asked questions

Which playlist should I play for shatta bouyon?

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the meeting between the modern riddim aesthetic of shatta and bouyon, the historic genre born in Dominica. The term is used when today's French Caribbean and French producers work the bouyon material on riddims made for dance and social media.

Why does a shatta playlist include bouyon?

Because the two sounds cross paths all the time. Shatta, a dancehall genre born in Martinique, shares with bouyon a fast tempo, synth bass and party culture, and DJs play them in the same selections. Shatta bouyon is their direct meeting point: a good shatta playlist therefore naturally includes modern bouyon tracks.

Is shatta a branch of bouyon?

No. Shatta is a dancehall genre born in Martinique, not a branch of bouyon. Bouyon was created in 1988 in Roseau, Dominica, by the band WCK. The two meet in shatta bouyon, when producers of the shatta generation work the bouyon material: Dominican keyboards, tempo and Creole. See bouyon vs shatta.

What BPM is shatta bouyon?

Today's bouyon and shatta productions from the French Caribbean often sit around 160 BPM, the upper zone of tracks built for dance and social media. That shared tempo lets DJs chain shatta and bouyon in the same night. Shatta bouyon also keeps bouyon's markers: looping keyboards, synth bass and Creole on the mic.

Which tracks mix shatta and bouyon?

A few documented examples: "Shatta ay", Arendi's bouyon track with Magma QLM, released on February 5, 2021; "Real Shatta Kartel" by DJ Skaytah, among his bouyon carnival tracks; and "Rebel", a Shatta Instrumental 2026 released by MB On The Track alongside his bouyon instrumentals. The quel-dj site also places "Moustik" by 1T1 and Aknose in its shatta and bouyon playlist.

Which producers make both shatta and bouyon?

Denmath, a producer from Saint Martin based between Nantes and Paris, lists bouyon, shatta, kompa, dancehall, soca and zouk among his specialties, and signs the riddims Tropical Riddim and Harley Riddim. DJ Skaytah releases bouyon and shatta in French and Creole, and MB On The Track works for a largely French audience. See Denmath.

Where do you hear shatta and bouyon together?

At French Caribbean and French parties, where shatta and dancehall frame zouk and trap, and where bouyon circulates in the same DJ selections. In France, bouyon gwada and shatta play in the clubs. The Bouyon Road Mix lets you hear that blend at home, on Spotify and YouTube, with the sound's Dominican origin at the center.

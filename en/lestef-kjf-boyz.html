LESTEF KJF BOYZ: the KJF series, a catalogue signed Stephan Cornano

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

LESTEF KJF BOYZ is a group from Guadeloupe whose catalogue, from 2021 to 2026, reads like a series: the numbered titles “KJF 2”, “KJF 4” and “KJF 5”, the single “50/50” with Team Bwè Tou Sa, “KKV”, “Malpalé”, then the EP “Riddim Aleatoire” in 2025 and two singles with DJ Luchshiy. Behind almost every title, platform credits name the same writer, Stephan Cornano, and the same recurring composers, Corane Vincent and Cornano himself. This profile follows that catalogue record by record, with the dates, labels and collaborations that official platform records allow us to verify.

A group from Guadeloupe, one writer at the centre: Stephan Cornano

Music databases present LESTEF KJF BOYZ as a dancehall act from Guadeloupe, and the catalogue confirms that anchor through its collaborations: Team Bwè Tou Sa and the networks of shatta and bouyon gwada. The group works in the zone where French Antillean dancehall meets bouyon: fast tempos, short titles, singles released in quick succession, and independent distribution whose label credits change between periods, from 4360476 Records DK to its own name, LESTEF KJF, on the 2025 EP.

The clearest thread in the file is a credit name. Stephan Cornano is credited as writer on almost every verified title by the group, from “KJF 2” to “Take Ur Time”, and as composer on several of them, alone or with Corane Vincent, also credited in the form Vincent Corane. That regularity gives the catalogue a signature: whatever the feature or the label of the moment, the writing returns to the same centre. This profile does not turn that credit into a biography: no open source says who Cornano is inside the group, singer, producer or both, and this page does not guess it.

The group name itself carries its series. The titles “KJF 2”, “KJF 4” and “KJF 5” reuse the KJF initials of the name LESTEF KJF BOYZ, like a mark declined in chapters. No source found explains what KJF means, and this profile offers no expansion of the initials. What can be verified, on the other hand, is the place of those titles in the catalogue: “KJF 4” is, according to the listening data compiled by Viberate, the group most played title, ahead of “50/50”.

The documented start: “50/50”, then the KJF series, 2021 to 2023

The group first verified discographic trace is a collaboration. “50/50”, by Team Bwè Tou Sa and LESTEF KJF BOYZ, was released on July 29, 2021 on the label T-BTS, as a track on the Team Bwè Tou Sa album “Voyé Sa Alléé”. Writing credits name Ludmilian Flower, Ludmael Flower and Nicolas Macbeth. Years later, the track remains the group second most played title in the song rankings compiled by Viberate, which makes it, with “KJF 4”, one of the two measurable pillars of the catalogue.

In the records consulted, the numbered series starts at “KJF 2”, released on January 13, 2022 with Lunik, written by Stephan Cornano and Ludmael Flower. “KJF 4” followed on September 16, 2022, as a solo single, distributed by DistroKid according to the track generated YouTube page, written by Cornano and composed by Vincent Corane. “KJF 5” arrived on February 21, 2023, featuring ALPHA DMZ, written by Danice Abelli and Stephan Cornano, composed by Corane Vincent. The records consulted show no “KJF 3”: this profile flags that numbering gap instead of filling it, for lack of a source that explains it.

The year 2023 then filled with singles. “Take It Out” came out on May 24, 2023, featuring NICE, Gwada G and Freddy, three names whose paths in Dominican bouyon are documented on this site. “KKV” followed on December 6, 2023, solo, written by Stephan Cornano, on 4360476 Records DK. The release rhythm is the rhythm of the scene: one single every few months, with collaborations that connect the group to the other active names of the same circuit.

December 2023: Lestef at DJ Luchshiy, “Xmas Freestyle” and “Chabin”

In that same December 2023, the name Lestef appears in the catalogue of another artist on this site. DJ Luchshiy released on December 22, 2023, on his label LUCHSHIY AND BRO, “Xmas Freestyle” and “Chabin”, both billed “feat. LESTEF and EDDAY”. Edday, the other guest on both tracks, is an artist followed closely by Bouyon Shattaz, and those two year end titles connect three names of the same Guadeloupe network in a single seasonal release.

The precision of the credit matters here. On those two tracks, it is “LESTEF”, without the KJF BOYZ addition, that is credited, as if the short name designated the same act in reduced form. This profile flags the variant instead of silently correcting it: the platforms themselves alternate between the long form of the group name and the short form in features. Two years later, the collaboration tightened further, this time under the group full name and in the other direction of credits, as the section on 2025 and 2026 tells.

Those December 2023 titles also show the group position in its ecosystem: established enough to be invited onto the releases of a DJ producer of its circuit, and active enough to follow up, six weeks later, with a single from its own catalogue that opens the year 2024.

2024: “Malpalé”, “Morne Fleuret Freestyle” and “La Boue” with Meryl

On February 6, 2024, the group released “Malpalé”, featuring Les Ptits du RZT, written and composed by Stephan Cornano, on 4360476 Records DK. The track runs 3 minutes and 4 seconds, the longest format among the singles of that period. The feature brings into the catalogue an emerging group whose public file is, by contrast, almost empty: their profile on this site tells what their three verified credits allow us to say, this one included.

On May 29, 2024 came “Morne Fleuret Freestyle”, featuring Jixels, written by Stephan Cornano and Jonathan Elusca, composed by Elusca, and running 5 minutes and 45 seconds, by far the longest title in the verified catalogue. The freestyle format, the length and the double writing credit set this title apart from the short, calibrated singles around it: it is the workshop piece of the catalogue, the one where the group lets the form stretch.

The year closed with the group most visible credit on another artist record. On December 11, 2024, Meryl released “La Boue”, on Maison Caviar, with writing credited to Meryl and LESTEF KJF BOYZ, and composition to Dj Despy. “La Boue” has since ranked among the most played titles associated with the group in Viberate data, alongside “KJF 4” and “50/50”. For a group of independent singles, co-writing a track by an artist of that scale changes the level: the name LESTEF KJF BOYZ then travels far beyond its own catalogue.

2025: the EP “Riddim Aleatoire” and the singles with DJ Luchshiy

On March 21, 2025, the group appeared on Alpha “Loup Garou Freestyle”, before the central appointment of the year: on June 18, 2025, the EP “Riddim Aleatoire” was released under the label LESTEF KJF, the first long format in the verified catalogue. Its five tracks draw the group range: “Karibbean Gyal”, “Look My Style”, “Ride It”, “Ratatouille Live”, whose composition pairs Arturo, Ridge and Stephan Cornano, and “Are U Ready”. The writing of the EP returns to Stephan Cornano on all five tracks, which makes “Riddim Aleatoire” the most coherent piece of the catalogue, one writer, one label, one date.

“Look My Style” also carries a credit particularity this profile wants to flag: on the track record, the song, featuring Zepek, is co-credited to LESTEF KJF BOYZ and Les Ptits du RZT, while the EP record names only LESTEF KJF BOYZ. The gap between album metadata and track metadata is frequent in independent distribution; keeping it here also documents the second verified collaboration between the two groups, after “Malpalé”.

The end of the year reversed the roles with DJ Luchshiy. On August 29, 2025, the group released “No Come Back (Whatcha Say)”, featuring DJ Luchshiy, on LUCHSHIY AND BRO, written by Stephan Cornano and composed by Corane Vincent. On January 2, 2026, “Take Ur Time” followed in the same configuration, written by Cornano and Corane Vincent. After the 2023 features at Luchshiy, the group therefore hosted the DJ on its own catalogue, twice in four months: the collaboration had become a lasting axis, documented in both directions.

Audience, playlists and stage: what the data allows us to say

The audience data compiled by Viberate gives the group a readable geography: France comes first in listening, ahead of Guadeloupe, then Réunion. That is the classic geography of current bouyon and shatta: a mainland French audience largest in volume, a Caribbean anchor, and an extension towards the Indian Ocean, where these sounds also circulate. The same data places the group in playlists that matter for the scene, including Kalash Radio on Spotify, the Bouyon Sat playlist by Kreyol Sat, as well as New Soca 2026 and Pitchfork Selects according to the listings consulted.

On the stage side, Viberate concert data associates the group with the line-up of the Karukera One Love Music Festival, in Torcy, alongside names such as Ninho, Kalash, Busy Signal, Beenie Man and Bunji Garlin. This profile keeps that line-up presence as a listing fact, without repeating the more detailed concert stories the same database publishes with no match in the group verified discography. The distinction matters: a line-up can be checked, a generic story cannot.

To hear the group in its context, our bouyon playlist and our bouyon gwada page place these titles in the flow of Guadeloupe releases followed by Bouyon Shattaz. And to understand the historic branch this sound inherits from, the bouyon soca page recalls that bouyon soca was born in Dominica, started by Dada in 2003 according to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller, then popularised by Triple Kay: the LESTEF KJF BOYZ catalogue belongs to the generation that grew up with that history already written.

What this profile does not yet know

As with many groups on this scene, the catalogue is better documented than the people. No open source consulted gives the composition of the group, nor the exact role of Stephan Cornano inside it, nor the meaning of the initials KJF, nor the reason for the missing “KJF 3” between the numbered titles of 2022 and 2023. No official account was verified to the point of being linked here, so this page links to no social profile. Those blanks are written as blanks, not filled with guesses.

The rest is solid, because it is made of records anyone can open: a dozen dated titles between 2021 and 2026, one EP, four collaboration configurations, one central writer, and two pillar titles, “KJF 4” and “50/50”. If the group publishes a biography, a new chapter of the KJF series or a long format, this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site. In the meantime, listening starts with “KJF 4”, continues with “50/50” and “La Boue”, and ends with “Riddim Aleatoire” in full, the piece where the group speaks longest in a single voice.

Frequently asked questions

Where does LESTEF KJF BOYZ come from?

Music databases present LESTEF KJF BOYZ as a dancehall group from Guadeloupe. Its catalogue confirms it through its Guadeloupe collaborations, Team Bwè Tou Sa, DJ Luchshiy and Edday, and through its audience, which Viberate data places first in France, then in Guadeloupe and Réunion.

What are the most played LESTEF KJF BOYZ titles?

According to the song rankings compiled by Viberate, “KJF 4”, released on September 16, 2022, is the group most played title, ahead of “50/50”, its 2021 collaboration with Team Bwè Tou Sa, then “KJF 5” and “La Boue”, the Meryl track co-written by the group in 2024.

Why is there no “KJF 3” in the catalogue?

The records consulted show “KJF 2” in January 2022, “KJF 4” in September 2022 and “KJF 5” in February 2023, with no “KJF 3” released under that name. No source found explains that numbering gap, and this profile flags it instead of inventing one.

Who writes the LESTEF KJF BOYZ titles?

Stephan Cornano is credited as writer on almost every verified title by the group, from “KJF 2” to “Take Ur Time”, and as composer on several titles, alone or with Corane Vincent. No open source states his exact role in the group, and this profile does not guess it.

What is the link between LESTEF KJF BOYZ and DJ Luchshiy?

The collaboration is documented in both directions. In December 2023, Lestef was featured on DJ Luchshiy “Xmas Freestyle” and “Chabin”, with Edday. In 2025 and 2026, the group hosted DJ Luchshiy on its own singles “No Come Back (Whatcha Say)” and “Take Ur Time”, released on the DJ label, LUCHSHIY AND BRO.

What is “Riddim Aleatoire”?

“Riddim Aleatoire” is the LESTEF KJF BOYZ EP released on June 18, 2025 under the label LESTEF KJF. Its five tracks, “Karibbean Gyal”, “Look My Style”, “Ride It”, “Ratatouille Live” and “Are U Ready”, are all written by Stephan Cornano. Hear our bouyon playlist too for the context of the scene.

Who is LESTEF KJF BOYZ?

LESTEF KJF BOYZ is a group from Guadeloupe active from 2021 to 2026, known for its numbered series “KJF 2”, “KJF 4” and “KJF 5”, the single “50/50” with Team Bwè Tou Sa and the 2025 EP “Riddim Aleatoire”. Almost all of its titles are written by Stephan Cornano, the credit name that runs through the whole catalogue.

Is LESTEF KJF BOYZ bouyon or dancehall?

Both zones meet: music databases present LESTEF KJF BOYZ as a dancehall act from Guadeloupe, but the group works where French Antillean dancehall meets bouyon, with fast tempos, short titles and collaborations in the shatta and bouyon gwada networks. Viberate also lists it in the Bouyon Sat playlist by Kreyol Sat.

Who produced “KJF 4” by LESTEF KJF BOYZ?

“KJF 4”, released on September 16, 2022 as a solo single, is written by Stephan Cornano and composed by Vincent Corane, according to platform credits. The track generated YouTube page shows distribution by DistroKid. It is the group most played title in the rankings compiled by Viberate, ahead of “50/50”.

What is “50/50” by Team Bwè Tou Sa and LESTEF KJF BOYZ?

“50/50” is the group first verified discographic trace: a collaboration with Team Bwè Tou Sa, released on July 29, 2021 on the label T-BTS, on the album “Voyé Sa Alléé”. Writing credits name Ludmilian Flower, Ludmael Flower and Nicolas Macbeth. It is the group second most played title according to Viberate.

Did LESTEF KJF BOYZ write “La Boue” by Meryl?

Yes, as co-writer. “La Boue”, released by Meryl on December 11, 2024 on Maison Caviar, credits the writing to Meryl and LESTEF KJF BOYZ, and the composition to Dj Despy. The track has since ranked among the most played titles associated with the group in Viberate data, alongside “KJF 4” and “50/50”.

Which artists has LESTEF KJF BOYZ worked with?

Its verified credits name Team Bwè Tou Sa on “50/50”, Lunik on “KJF 2”, ALPHA DMZ on “KJF 5”, NICE, Gwada G and Freddy on “Take It Out”, Les Ptits du RZT on “Malpalé”, Jixels on “Morne Fleuret Freestyle”, Zepek on “Look My Style”, as well as Meryl, Alpha and DJ Luchshiy.

Are LESTEF and LESTEF KJF BOYZ the same act?

The platforms alternate between the two forms. On “Xmas Freestyle” and “Chabin”, released by DJ Luchshiy on December 22, 2023, it is “LESTEF”, without the KJF BOYZ addition, that is credited alongside Edday. This profile treats the short form as a variant of the group name in features, without silently correcting it.

Has LESTEF KJF BOYZ played at festivals?

Viberate concert data associates the group with the line-up of the Karukera One Love Music Festival, in Torcy, alongside names such as Ninho, Kalash, Busy Signal, Beenie Man and Bunji Garlin. This profile keeps that presence as a line-up fact, without repeating the more detailed concert stories of the same database, which have no verified match.

Cadence-lypso: the first Dominican sound to travel

Published September 1, 2026

Before bouyon, Dominica had already invented a genre that travelled across the whole Caribbean: cadence-lypso. It is the other parent of bouyon, and the proof that the island is a land of musical invention.

Exile One, the Grammacks and the 1970s

In the 1970s, Dominican groups like Exile One and the Grammacks become the stars of cadence-lypso: an aggressive rhythm guitar, lyrics in Creole that comment on social life, a groove that makes the whole region dance. It is the first style of Dominican music to become popular in the Caribbean.

From cadence to bouyon

In 1988, WCK takes up the heritage of cadence-lypso and fuses it with jing ping, replacing part of the orchestra with keyboards and drum machines. Bouyon is born from this passing of the torch between Dominican generations. For what comes next, read the history of bouyon.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of bouyon: the genre was born in Roseau, Dominica, with WCK in 1988, out of cadence-lypso and jing ping. Then listen to the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, on Spotify and YouTube, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon.

Which country created bouyon?

Bouyon was born in Dominica, in the Caribbean, in the late 1980s. Its pioneering band, WCK (Windward Caribbean Kulture), formed in Roseau in 1988, created it by fusing cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines.

What is cadence-lypso?

Cadence-lypso is a Dominican genre that bands like Exile One and the Grammacks carried across the Caribbean in the 1970s. It mixes Haitian cadence, calypso and the Creole language: a danceable cadence, an aggressive rhythm guitar and lyrics in Creole that comment on island life. It is the first Dominican style to become popular across the Caribbean.

What is the link between cadence-lypso and bouyon?

Cadence-lypso is one of bouyon's two parents. In 1988, WCK fused its heritage with jing ping and replaced part of the orchestra with keyboards and drum machines. From cadence, bouyon keeps the guitar heritage, the Creole storytelling and the party function. See the cadence-lypso roots of bouyon.

Which bands played cadence-lypso?

Exile One and the Grammacks are the Dominican stars of 1970s cadence-lypso. The Midnight Groovers from Grand Bay, formed in the late 1960s and led by Phillip “Chubby” Mark, have more than 39 albums and are still on the World Creole Music Festival bill in 2026.

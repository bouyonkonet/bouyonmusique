DJ Joe: two traces, eleven years apart, one name held by thousands

Published October 20, 2026

DJ Joe is a DJ from the bouyon scene whose name is at once the simplest and the hardest to follow in the genre: thousands of DJs in the world carry the same one. After a full search, the public record that can be attached to him with care comes down to two traces eleven years apart. The first is historical: in December 2012, according to the Rebel Up collective, the first Dominican and Guadeloupean bouyon MCs and DJs performed in Paris, and a 50 minute documentary in French and Creole filmed that first performance by Suppa, Gaza Girls, Dj Joe and others. The second is a record: on February 5, 2023, a single titled “Bwé Rhum”, with Asa Bantan, was released under the name DJ JOE on Vadore Concept Groupe. This profile publishes those two traces, explains the bridge between them and its limits, and refuses to borrow a single line from the other DJ Joes of the planet.

Who DJ Joe is: the most common DJ name in the world, and two bouyon traces

To start this profile honestly is to start with the problem of the name. DJ Joe can mean a house DJ from New York, a hip hop DJ from London, a radio DJ, a wedding DJ or a DJ from any Caribbean scene, and search engines mix all those careers together without warning. None of the methods that work for the other artists on this site, following an official account, an interview or a biography, is available here: no source consulted gives this DJ Joe full name, his precise island, or a verified account that is his. Any profile that told his life in detail would therefore, by construction, be a novel assembled from the lives of others.

This profile works the other way round. It keeps only the traces where the name DJ Joe appears inside the bouyon world itself, with a context that can place it: a scene, a label, named partners. Two traces pass that filter. The first belongs to the history of bouyon exporting itself to France, in the early 2010s, in the Guadeloupe circle of Suppa and the Gaza Girls. The second belongs to the catalogue of a label from that same scene, Vadore Concept Groupe, which in 2023 released a DJ JOE single with the Dominican voice of Asa Bantan. Between the two, eleven years with no verifiable public trace attachable to this name in this scene, and this profile does not fill that silence.

The exact limit of that pairing must also be stated, right in the introduction. The two traces share a territory, the Guadeloupe bouyon scene and its Vadore circle, and the second trace also shares a keyword list and a website with the first period, as the next section shows. That is a strong, documented proximity, and it is why this site publishes a single page. But no source consulted says in one sentence that the Dj Joe filmed in Paris in 2012 and the DJ JOE of the 2023 single are the same person. This profile therefore presents the bridge as a bridge, with two verified piers and a deck made of reasonable hypothesis, never as an established fact.

December 2012: the first bouyon show in Paris, and the documentary

The first trace is published by Rebel Up, a Brussels collective of DJs and tropical sound researchers, in its long article on bouyon hardcore that accompanies its “Bouyon Hardcore 2k13” mixtape. The article describes the new substyle of the period, called hardcore, with “bouyon gwada” as its Guadeloupe equivalent: bouyon with raw lyrics, in English, French or Creole, over heavy percussive riddims with simplified melodies, where one successful riddim receives several versions as in dancehall. It is in that world, the Guadeloupe bouyon of the 2010s, that the name Dj Joe appears.

The key sentence of the article deserves to be quoted for exactly what it establishes. Rebel Up writes that internationally, it took until December 2012 before the first Dominican and Guadeloupean MCs and DJs came to Paris, home to a large part of the Antillean diaspora in France, and that there is a 50 minute documentary, in French and Creole, of the first and impressive performance in Paris of Suppa, Gaza Girls, Dj Joe and others. The name is therefore attached to a founding, dated moment: the first Paris stage for bouyon, in front of the diaspora audience, alongside Suppa, a central figure of bouyon gwada, and the Gaza Girls. Rebel Up adds, with humour, that the interviewer questions are not necessarily more interesting than the answers, which it does not fully understand because they are in Creole: the documentary is a primary source, spoken in the language of the scene.

The context of that first Paris show gives the trace its weight. Bouyon was born in Dominica, and our history of bouyon page traces that path from Roseau. Its arrival in France did not first pass through institutions: it passed through the DJs and MCs who stepped onto a Paris stage in December 2012, with a sound Rebel Up describes as rough digital club music, born of the generation of accessible production software. Bouyon soca, started in Dominica in 2003 by producer Dada according to the signed testimony of Smokiller and popularised by Triple Kay, was already travelling between islands. The Dj Joe of 2012 belongs to the other branch of the export, that of the French Antilles, and his name stays attached to that date in the only source that documents it in detail.

The Vadore bridge: what links the 2012 trace to the 2023 single

Between the two traces, one published clue links the first period to the label of the second, and this profile presents it as a clue, not as proof. The YouTube video “Never Stop Di Bouyon” by Suppa, with Ti Cho and Ghetto Princess, points in its description to the site vadoreconcept.hautetfort.com and its keyword list includes, in the disorder of such lists: bouyon, Vadore, Concept, Gaza, Crew, Gwada, Girls, Dj, Joe, Triple Kay, Asa, Bantan, Suppa. The same keyword page therefore already associates, in the early 2010s, the name Dj Joe, the name Asa Bantan and the Vadore Concept banner, that is, exactly the three names the 2023 single would bring together on a digital cover.

Vadore Concept Groupe is indeed the label of the 2023 single, and Asa Bantan is its featured voice. Asa Bantan, an artist from Grand Bay in Dominica, widely called the King of Bouyon by the public and the media of the scene, an attributed formulation and not an official title, is one of the most recognisable voices in Dominican bouyon. That a DJ from the Vadore circle should release, eleven years after the first Paris show, a single with that voice draws a continuity of network: the same small world, on both sides of the Creole channel, between Guadeloupe of the riddims and Dominica of the singers. This profile stops exactly where the sources stop: the continuity of the circle is documented, the personal identity between 2012 and 2023 remains a working hypothesis, flagged as such.

That caution takes nothing away from the interest of the file, it constitutes it. The history of bouyon counts many DJs whose work was to make the sound travel, in parties, mixtapes and the first diaspora stages, long before platforms learned to print their names correctly. A name as common as DJ Joe was almost condemned to this fragmentary documentation: every trace has to be re-read by hand, and the smallest confusion with a namesake would destroy the little that exists. That is why this page is short, dated and sourced line by line.

Bwe Rhum: the 2023 single with Asa Bantan, read credit by credit

The second trace is a full single, with a detailed platform page. “Bwé Rhum”, credited to DJ JOE with Asa Bantan and annotated “Bouyon Kings Mixtape” in its title, was released on February 5, 2023 on Vadore Concept Groupe, as a single. The Shazam page of the record shows a tempo of 157 BPM, in the high zone of bouyon, and files the lead performer under the name DJ JOE, in capitals. The Creole title, which means drinking rum, and the mixtape of the bouyon kings wording place the record in the tradition of carnival party titles, made for sound systems and DJ charts: a VirtualDJ community chart indeed lists it, dated 2023, among the bouyon titles of its season.

The published credits call for the same careful reading as the rest of the file. The Shazam page credits Foster XAVIER as songwriter of the record. It also credits, in its composer line, a wording that reads “Sesame Street's Little Boy”, which is plainly not the name of a person: this profile quotes it exactly as published because it demonstrates, better than any speech, the state of this scene metadata on the platforms. When the machine attributes a bouyon record to a character from a children programme, the work of a site like this one is to flag it and to keep only the readable credits: the label, the date, the tempo, the performer and the featured voice.

The reciprocity of the credit, on the other hand, is clean and verifiable from both sides. The Shazam artist page of Asa Bantan lists among his most streamed songs “Bwé Rhum (feat. DJ JOE)”, and among his releases a 2023 single titled “Bwé Rhum (feat. DJ JOE)”, in addition to the entry under the DJ JOE name with the “Bouyon Kings Mixtape” wording. The same recording therefore exists under both names, as a DJ record carried by a star voice and as a title of the star voice produced inside the DJ workshop. To hear it in its context, our bouyon playlist gathers the records of this generation, and our bouyon soca page tells the branch that carried the Dominican sound across the Caribbean.

What this profile does not know, and why this page exists anyway

The blanks in the file must be named without detour. No source consulted gives DJ Joe full name, his date or island of birth, or the list of his parties, mixtapes or residencies. No source consulted proves in one sentence that the Dj Joe of the Paris documentary of December 2012 and the DJ JOE of the 2023 single are one and the same person: this profile publishes the pairing for its documented proximity, the Vadore circle, the names Suppa and Asa Bantan present on both sides of the decade, and flags it as a pairing. No official social or platform account was verified by direct opening as his, so the schema of this page links to no external account.

This page exists because the two traces, on the other hand, are real and they matter in the history of the genre. The first fixes a name on the first Paris stage for bouyon, a moment histories of the genre often tell without naming the DJs who carried it. The second shows that same circle, eleven years later, releasing on its historic label a single with the biggest Dominican voice of its generation. Documenting those two facts, with their dates, their sources and the exact limit of what links them, is worth more than an invented portrait or a total absence.

If DJ Joe, Vadore Concept Groupe or the witnesses of the 2012 documentary confirm the identity, a biography or official accounts, this profile will be completed, corrected and dated like every page on this site. In the meantime, the surest trace for hearing this name remains “Bwé Rhum” with Asa Bantan, and the oldest trace remains the Paris documentary of December 2012, in French and in Creole, where bouyon stepped onto a stage in the capital for the first time.

Frequently asked questions

Who is DJ Joe?

DJ Joe is a DJ from the bouyon scene whose verifiable public record comes down to two traces: the documentary of the first bouyon performance in Paris in December 2012, where Rebel Up names Suppa, Gaza Girls, Dj Joe and others, and the single “Bwé Rhum” with Asa Bantan, released on February 5, 2023 on Vadore Concept Groupe. The record consulted establishes neither his full name nor his origin.

Are the DJ Joe of Paris 2012 and the DJ Joe of Bwe Rhum the same person?

The sources consulted do not prove it in one sentence, and this profile is the one that says so openly. The pairing rests on documented proximity: the Suppa video “Never Stop Di Bouyon” points to the Vadore Concept site and its keyword list already associates Dj Joe and Asa Bantan in the early 2010s, and the 2023 single was released on Vadore Concept Groupe with Asa Bantan. It is a solid bridge, presented as a bridge, not as a certainty.

What is Bwe Rhum?

“Bwé Rhum” is a single by DJ JOE with Asa Bantan, annotated “Bouyon Kings Mixtape”, released on February 5, 2023 on Vadore Concept Groupe, at 157 BPM according to its Shazam page, which credits Foster XAVIER as songwriter. The artist page of Asa Bantan lists the same recording under the title “Bwé Rhum (feat. DJ JOE)”.

Why does the Bwe Rhum page credit a Sesame Street character?

Because platform metadata is sometimes wrong, and this profile quotes the error instead of hiding it. The Shazam page of the record credits Foster XAVIER as songwriter, and its composer line reads “Sesame Street's Little Boy”, which is not the name of a person. This profile keeps the readable credits, label, date, tempo, performers, and flags the rest as a metadata error.

Is DJ Joe a soca or house DJ known under the same name?

No, nothing in the bouyon file links him to the many other DJ Joes of the house, hip hop or soca scenes, and this profile borrows no fact from their careers. The name DJ Joe is one of the most common in the world: only the two traces located inside the bouyon scene, Paris 2012 and the 2023 Vadore Concept single, are kept here.

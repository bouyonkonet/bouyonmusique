Dominica's geothermal power wins three CREF 2026 awards

By Smokiller · Published November 2, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026

Dominica was honoured three times at the Caribbean Renewable Energy Forum 2026, in Panama City, for its geothermal project. A few months after its 10-megawatt plant at Laudat, in the Roseau Valley, entered service, the country has become the regional reference in geothermal energy.

Three awards in Panama

The CREF 2026 forum was held in Panama City, from October 14 to 16. Dominica received two awards for its geothermal project there: Best Caribbean Energy Project and Best Financing Programme. The Minister for Foreign Affairs, International Business, Trade and Energy, Dr Vince Henderson, also received the Caribbean Energy Leadership MVP Award.

The Office of the Prime Minister announced the honours on October 19. The Best Caribbean Energy Project award recognises the delivery of the plant, and the financing award recognises the strategy and partnerships that made it possible to build.

A plant in service since July

The 10-megawatt geothermal plant is located at Laudat, in the Roseau Valley. It began commercial production on July 31, 2026, after reliability and performance testing. During commissioning, it was already supplying about 10.4 megawatts to the national grid, according to the July 2 report.

The plant uses heat from underground geothermal energy to produce electricity and now supplies the national grid. It is a major step for a country that wants to reduce its dependence on imported fossil fuels and strengthen its energy security.

The first in CARICOM for geothermal

With this plant, Dominica has become the first CARICOM member country to generate electricity from its geothermal reserves, according to Finance Minister Dr Irving McIntyre and the Dominican press. The plant is also the second operational geothermal power plant in the Caribbean region.

The project caters to 60 per cent of the island's electricity needs, according to Dominica News. In June 2026, geothermal was already supplying about 43 per cent of the country's electricity, with 25 per cent from hydropower. CARILEC, the Caribbean Electric Utility Services Corporation, expects the plant to produce 59.6 GWh in 2026, about 50 per cent of Dominica's energy demand.

A 15-year project

Commercial operation is the final milestone of about 15 years of development. In December 2024, the Caribbean Development Bank approved a $34.8 million loan to build the 10-megawatt plant.

The project was delivered through a public-private partnership with Ormat Technologies, at an estimated project value of US$34.8 million, with further backing from the Green Climate Fund, Global Affairs Canada, CARICOM, the Government of Dominica and the Geothermal Power Company of Dominica Ltd.

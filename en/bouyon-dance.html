Bouyon dance: a genre that is lived with the body

You cannot understand bouyon music sitting down. From Roseau carnival to the videos shot in Gwada and Paris, every bouyon riddim calls for a dance, and every dance relaunches the sound.

Music born to move

Bouyon comes down from dance music: the jing ping of traditional Dominican balls, bele, quadrille and the chante mas of carnival. Its fast tempo, its keyboards looping round and round and its electronic bass are made for the street, the fete and the parade. In Guadeloupe and Martinique, its nickname jump up says it all: music that makes you jump.

How bouyon is danced

There is no single official step, and that is the strength of the genre. Every era and every riddim imposes its own moves: swaying on the choruses, collective jumps on the build-ups, danced answers to the calls of the singer or the DJ. At carnival, the dance is collective and free. On social media, producers dance themselves on their own beats to launch the movement, and the public then demands the long version of the song.

Frequently asked questions

How do you dance bouyon?

There is no single official bouyon step: every era and every riddim imposes its own moves. People sway on the choruses, jump together on the build-ups and answer the calls of the singer or the DJ with their dancing. At carnival, the dance is collective and free. To practice on the sounds of the moment, play the bouyon playlist.

Where does the bouyon dance come from?

The bouyon dance comes from the dance music the genre descends from: the jing ping of traditional Dominican balls, bele, quadrille and the chante mas of Dominica's carnival. Bouyon kept that purpose: its fast tempo, looping keyboards and electronic bass are made for the street, the fete and the parade. See the jing ping page.

Why does every new bouyon riddim launch its own dance?

Because bouyon riddims are now launched on video. Producers dance themselves on their own beats to start the movement, then the public picks up the step and demands the long version of the song. It is the social media version of an old reflex: at Dominica's carnival, the crowd already answered the live bands by dancing. See the shatta bouyon page.

Is bouyon danced fast?

Yes, bouyon is a fast genre and its tempo is part of its identity. The producer HYPNO, for example, claims a track at 160 bpm, Mauvais Plan. That speed comes from carnival and from traditional Dominican dances, and it is what earned bouyon its nickname jump up in Guadeloupe and Martinique: music that makes you jump. See the bouyon BPM page.

Where can you dance to bouyon?

First at Dominica's carnival, Mas Domnik in Roseau, where bouyon was born. Then at the carnivals of Guadeloupe and Martinique, where jump up drives the skin-drum groups, the deboulés and the street parties. And finally at the bouyon nights of Paris and the big French cities, carried by the Antillean diaspora. See Dominica carnival.

What is the bouyon dance called?

It has no single name and no official step: people simply say bouyon dance, and every riddim brings its own moves. In Guadeloupe and Martinique, the genre is also called jump up, and that way of dancing bouyon by jumping has become the signature of gwada parties. The term jump up already described very rhythmic carnival music before bouyon arrived. See jump up.

What should I wear to dance bouyon at J'ouvert?

Clothes you can get dirty. J'ouvert, the dawn party that opens the carnival's fat days, is lived in mud, paint and, depending on the island, cocoa, with no fancy costumes. The only rule is to dance until sunrise. In Dominica, from 4 in the morning, sound systems and bands play the bouyon of the year in the streets of Roseau. See J'ouvert.

What role do dancers play in bouyon?

A central one: female audiences and dancers are the ones who spread bouyon dance across social platforms. The steps are born at fetes, in challenges and in videos, and dancers are their first ambassadors. Saying that women make bouyon means recognizing the whole chain: the voices, the writers, the bodies that dance and the audiences that pass it on. See women of bouyon.

Why do bouyon producers dance in their videos?

Because a riddim is now launched in a fifteen-second video: the producer dances in it to show what the beat does to the body, and the public asks for the long version. MB On The Track did it on 16 October 2026, dancing at night in a bus shelter to his own instrumental, under the tag Rock Bouyon. The producer has become a face of the genre. See MB On The Track.

Is bouyon danced like zouk?

No. Zouk, from Guadeloupe and Martinique and popularized in the early 1980s by bands like Kassav, is clean, danceable and romantic in its zouk love version. Dominica's bouyon takes the opposite path: faster, rawer, more carnival than ballroom. Where zouk seduces, bouyon shakes, and it is danced by jumping, hence its nickname jump up. See bouyon vs zouk.

Which songs are good to dance bouyon to?

For recent songs: "Shake Something" by Pudaz, whose title says it all, FIX UP by G'z Up and its opening cry Ya ya ya, or Mauvais Plan by HYPNO, built at 160 bpm. The carnival songs of the 1990s and 2000s remain the base of every fete. New releases that launch a dance arrive first on @bouyonshattaz, then in the Road Mix. See bouyon songs.

Is bouyon danced in a group?

Yes, often. At carnival, the bouyon dance is collective and free: the crowd jumps together on the build-ups and answers the calls of the singer or the DJ, with the bands and in front of the sound systems of Mas Domnik. On social media, challenges, short videos and group takes carry the steps from one audience to another. See Mas Domnik.

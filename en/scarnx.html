ScarnX: the bouyon producer Road Fever brought out of the shadows

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

ScarnX is a producer name that almost everyone meets in the same place: the last track on Side B of “Road Fever”, the carnival riddims compilation released by the London label Soundway Records on May 9, 2025. The track is called “Midnight Fever Riddim”, it runs 2 minutes 34, it is dated 2019 in the digital listing, and the label notes say of its maker, in one sentence, that Dominica increasingly influential bouyon sound thrives through him. That is little, and it is a lot at the same time: little, because no biography, no legal name and no other solo track were found during this research; a lot, because that single track places a Dominican bouyon producer, with no vocal and no campaign, on an international vinyl record, alongside the Dennery Segment producers of Saint Lucia and the bouyon producers of Guadeloupe. This profile says everything the opened sources allow to be said, and it also says, plainly, what they do not allow to be said.

One name, one track, one sentence: what the file really holds

Start with the honest inventory, because the ScarnX file holds three pieces. First piece: a name, ScarnX, spelled with a capital S and a final capital X on the label page, and sometimes Scarnx in retail listings. Second piece: a track, “Midnight Fever Riddim”, an instrumental. Third piece: one sentence in the official notes of the “Road Fever” compilation, which names ScarnX among the producers of the record and makes him the carrier of Dominica bouyon sound inside this project. Everything else in this profile is the verified context of those three pieces: the record, the label, the compiler, the riddim format and the scene the record documents.

What the research did not find must be written with the same clarity. No ScarnX artist page was found on the major platforms during this research: neither Spotify, nor Apple Music, nor Shazam, nor Deezer, nor Qobuz, nor SoundCloud, nor Audiomack, nor Discogs returns a page under that name matching the “Road Fever” producer. No interview, no biography, no legal name, no town, no verified official account were confirmed. Search engines mostly return neighbouring names with no connection, starting with a British rapper with an almost identical name, who has strictly nothing to do with this file and whom this profile will never confuse with the Dominican producer.

That scarcity is not a research accident, it says something about how this scene works. In bouyon as in Dennery Segment, the riddim often travels before the name: an instrumental is born in a studio or on a laptop, it is voiced by one or more artists, it runs through transports, parties and mixes, and its maker sometimes stays known only to the singers and DJs who use it. “Road Fever” was designed precisely to reverse that invisibility, by releasing the riddims alone, without vocals, with the names of their producers on the track listing. ScarnX is the quietest beneficiary of that reversal: the record gives his name and his track, and nothing else, not even one sentence of individual presentation.

Read this profile, then, for what it is: the portrait of a track and its context, not the biography of a person. When a source one day names the man or the team behind ScarnX, releases a second track or opens an official page, this profile will be completed and dated. Until then, the only verified listening starts and ends, for now, with “Midnight Fever Riddim”, and the rest of this page explains why that single track deserves a full page: because it is the point where Dominican instrumental bouyon enters, on vinyl, a story told from London and Amsterdam.

“Road Fever”: the compilation that carries ScarnX

“Road Fever: New Generation Carnival Riddims from St. Lucia and Dominica” is a compilation by the London label Soundway Records, released on May 9, 2025. Its compiler is Rozaly, a DJ, curator and researcher based in Amsterdam and raised in Curacao, and his idea is simple and strong: gather instrumental riddims from Saint Lucia, Dominica and Guadeloupe, tracks first built to carry vocals, and release them for the first time as standalone pieces, stripped bare. The record exists on vinyl, digitally, and as a limited cassette, a 55 and a half minute tape mixed by DJ Intl Prostyle in Saint Lucia and delivered with the full digital download.

The physical credits of the vinyl tell the journey of the sound. Mastering and vinyl cut are by The Carvery, the pressing was made by Garcia et Co in Marciac, in France, the design is by Wolfe Hall Studio and the photography by Selwyn De Wind, who also cosigns with Rozaly the short reel format film that accompanies the release. A Dominican riddim dated 2019, born for the carnival of its island, therefore ends up pressed in France for a London label, compiled by a researcher from Amsterdam: that is exactly the new geography of bouyon, a transport and party music turned into an international collectors object, without ceasing to be road music.

The track listing shows the exact place of ScarnX in the whole. The vinyl holds 14 tracks, seven per side. Side A lines up Ransum Records, Viral Riddims, Shazdown Mmw, DJ Demafidem, G6 Productions, the duo Bad Sound and DJ Marfox, then Veaygel Productions. Side B lines up Krome Productions, LMT Mafia, Viral Riddims, Veaygel Productions, Slaughter Arts Media, Ransum Records, and closes on ScarnX. The digital version extends the list to 25 tracks, with extra pieces by the same producers and one track by Unik Productions, and “Midnight Fever Riddim” keeps position 14 there, the last of the vinyl programme. Closing a record is never a compiler accident: the ScarnX track is the exit door Rozaly chose.

The label notes describe the material precisely: riddims distilled to the bare bones, a minimal tempo above 150 beats per minute, FL Studio percussion packs, DJ and vox samples, synth riffs, and an open bridge between trap, drill, dancehall, sped up Haitian konpa and Angolan kuduro. The whole thing, the label says, is best heard blasted through walls of speakers travelled by carnival trucks, or through bluetooth car speakers. It is inside that frame, and only inside that frame, that the sentence about ScarnX takes its meaning: among Lucian Dennery producers and two producers named as Guadeloupe based, Dominica bouyon sound “thrives through ScarnX”. The label does not say ScarnX is the only bouyon producer on the record, it says he is the one through whom the sound of Dominica lives in this record. This profile quotes that as a label attribution, not as an established ranking.

“Midnight Fever Riddim”: 2 minutes 34, dated 2019

The only verified ScarnX recording is therefore “Midnight Fever Riddim”. On the compilation page, the track is dated 2019, like several other pieces on the record, among them “Smash Or Pass Riddim”, “2 Stroke Riddim” and “Walk It Riddim”. That date matters: it says the track was not made for the compilation, but existed six years before it, in the circuit these riddims are made for, the circuit of carnival vocals, mixes and sound systems. “Road Fever” is a second life, not a birth. Rozaly work is archive work as much as discovery work: fetching instrumentals already road tested and fixing them on vinyl.

The length, 2 minutes 34, is the length of the riddim format in its pure form: a tight loop, a fast entry, no song to carry, since the vocal was removed or never released with this version. The title itself follows the grammar of the genre: an atmosphere name, Midnight Fever, followed by the word Riddim that files the piece. On the same record, the titles speak the same language, “Soca Panda Riddim”, “Bouyon Beam Riddim”, “Heat Wave Riddim”, “Bad Weather Riddim”: image names, made to be shouted by a DJ, remembered by a crowd and found again in a mix tracklist.

One question stays open, and this profile leaves it open: which voice, which singer, which carnival song carried this riddim between 2019 and 2025? The label notes say these instrumentals were made for vocalists and are released here without them, and the Resident Advisor review likewise recalls that these tracks were all previously released featuring vocalists. But no document consulted names the voiced version of “Midnight Fever Riddim”, nor its performer, nor its year of circulation. Inventing that link would be easy and false. A documented blank therefore stays at the centre of the file: the riddim is identified, its voiced life is not yet.

Hearing the track, in the order of the record, also works as a lesson. Placed after “Walk It Riddim” by Ransum Records and closing the side, it lets you hear, by contrast with the Lucian pieces that open the programme, what the label means by the bouyon of Dominica: the same family of digital percussion and synth riffs, the same speed, but filed by the compiler on the Dominican side of the map. To go further into that grammar, our bouyon riddim page explains how these instrumentals are born, travel and change voices, and our bouyon playlist follows the current wave of voiced songs.

Dennery Segment and bouyon: two scenes on one record

“Road Fever” holds together two genres that hurried listeners merge, and the ScarnX case forces them apart cleanly. Dennery Segment comes from Saint Lucia, from the Dennery district that gives it its name, with fast vocals, often in Saint Lucian Creole, over short format productions. Bouyon comes from Dominica, born in Roseau in the late 1980s in the wake of WCK, and its best known voiced branch, bouyon soca, was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller, who was in the rooms where it began, then popularized by Triple Kay. The label notes add Guadeloupe to the map of the record, since Shazdown Mmw and DJ Demafidem are presented there as Guadeloupe based, with tracks that carry the word bouyon in their names.

On that three island map, the position of ScarnX is the simplest and the most exposed: he is the only name the notes explicitly attach to Dominica. The Lucian producers are several, Ransum, Krome, Veaygel, LMT Mafia, Slaughter Arts Media, G6, and they carry most of the programme. The Guadeloupeans are two. The Dominican is one. That solitude explains both the value of the document and its fragility: if you want to hear the instrumental bouyon of Dominica on this record, you listen to one track of 2 minutes 34, and if that track were not there, Dominica would be absent from a compilation whose title names it. ScarnX therefore holds, alone, the place of an island.

The reception of the record confirms it was read as a scene document, not as a simple product. Bandcamp Daily made it its album of the day on May 12, 2025, stressing the laptop production, Fruity Loops, the samples and this party music made for giant speakers or a moving car. Resident Advisor published its review on May 21, 2025, calling it an invaluable deconstructed snapshot of two modern Caribbean riddims, and placing bouyon around the 150 beats per minute mark, with its stabbing keyboard riffs. Neither of those two reviews gives ScarnX a passage of his own: the name travels in track listings and in the presentations by the label and by Patta, the compiler partner, which files him among the established and underground production crews of the record. The silence of the critics on his name is part of the file, and this profile reports it instead of filling it.

One last method point holds for this whole scene. The Resident Advisor review places Shazdown on the Dominica side, where the official label notes say he is based in Guadeloupe. When a review and a label contradict each other on the island of a producer, this site follows the label, which releases the music and knows its producers, and it flags the gap. It is the same discipline that requires, for ScarnX, keeping only what Soundway writes: one producer, one track, one island. Our bouyon soca page retraces the voiced branch of this history, and our bouyon Dominica page places the genre back on its home island.

The limits of the file, and why this page exists anyway

Sum up the limits without softening them. Nothing is known of ScarnX beyond the credit: not the legal name, not the age, not the town, not the studio, not the collaborations, not the voices that sang his productions, not even whether ScarnX is one person or a team, a possibility that the presentation vocabulary, which speaks of production crews, makes possible without confirming. No second track is known. No official account, no photograph, no concert date is known. The verified discography fits on one line, and the structured data of this page reflects exactly that: one recording, “Midnight Fever Riddim”, and one official link, the Soundway compilation page, opened and verified during this research.

Why, then, a full page? Because bouyon is also played in these documented margins. The history of the genre is full of producers whose work carried whole carnivals while their names stayed in credits nobody read. A record like “Road Fever”, compiled by a researcher, pressed on vinyl and reviewed by the international music press, is precisely the tool that fixes those names before they are lost. ScarnX is in it, dated, credited, filed on the Dominica side. Removing his page because the file is thin would erase the only verified international trace of a Dominican bouyon producer of his riddim generation. The thinness of the file is information, not a shame, and it is written here in full.

This profile will therefore be updated on the day the material exists: an official artist page, a second riddim, the name of the voiced version of “Midnight Fever Riddim”, or an interview with the compiler or the producer. Until then, the recommended listening is short and clear: “Midnight Fever Riddim” in the order of “Road Fever”, preceded by “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw and “On Di Road Riddim” by DJ Demafidem for the Guadeloupe side, and followed, on the voiced side, by the songs in our bouyon playlist. And if a reader holds a credit, a flyer or a mix where ScarnX appears before 2025, that is exactly the kind of document that will grow this file.

Frequently asked questions

Who is ScarnX?

ScarnX is the producer name credited with the track “Midnight Fever Riddim” on the “Road Fever” compilation by Soundway Records, released on May 9, 2025. The label notes present him as the carrier of Dominica bouyon sound inside this record. No source consulted gives his legal name, his biography or his make up, one person or a team, and this profile does not invent them.

What is the verified ScarnX track?

Only one: “Midnight Fever Riddim”, an instrumental of 2 minutes 34, dated 2019 on the compilation page. It closes Side B of the vinyl, at position 7 of the side and 14 of the programme, and keeps position 14 in the 25 track digital listing. No other solo track was found during this research.

What is “Road Fever”?

It is a compilation by Soundway Records, a London label, compiled by Rozaly, a DJ and researcher based in Amsterdam and raised in Curacao, and released on May 9, 2025 on vinyl, digitally and as a limited cassette mixed by DJ Intl Prostyle. It gathers instrumental Dennery Segment and bouyon riddims from Saint Lucia, Dominica and Guadeloupe, released for the first time without the vocals they were built for.

Is ScarnX from Dominica or from Guadeloupe?

From Dominica, according to the official label notes, which write that Dominica increasingly influential bouyon sound thrives through ScarnX. In the same sentence, the label says Shazdown Mmw and DJ Demafidem are Guadeloupe based. A Resident Advisor review places Shazdown on the Dominican side, which contradicts the label; this site follows the label and flags the gap.

Why is there only one track in the data of this page?

Because the rule of this site is strict: a recording enters the structured data only if a primary source opened during the research shows it. For ScarnX, only the official Soundway compilation page was opened and verified, with a single track. Any EP or catalog that aggregator pages might attribute to him without a source is not repeated here.

Where can I hear ScarnX?

On “Road Fever”, through the Soundway Records Bandcamp page, on vinyl, digitally or on cassette. In the order of the record, hear “Midnight Fever Riddim” after “Walk It Riddim” by Ransum Records, then compare it with “Bouyon Beam Riddim” by Shazdown Mmw.

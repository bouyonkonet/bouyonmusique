Mystrie: a Dominica calypsonian who signs a bouyon, Lapawol

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Mystrie is the stage name of Renna Charles, a singer of the Dominica calypso circuit, documented in competition three seasons in a row: “Letters of the Alphabet” for Dominica Calypso 2024, “Appetite” for Dominica Calypso 2025, “Parisze” for the Dominica Calypso 2026 season. In 2026 she adds a second register to that path with “Lapawol”, a record whose own video note classifies it without ambiguity: the song is a bouyon. The title, a Kwéyòl word, means words or talk, and the release note gives its subject: people talk about others without knowing, without facts, and in the end say nothing. This profile therefore follows a readable trajectory, from a competition calypso stage heavily documented on video to a single bouyon release to date, and it also states what is still missing: no platform artist page, no published writing or production credit for “Lapawol”, no biography.

Renna Charles, known as Mystrie: an identity published by her own credits

Mystrie full name is not a social media rumour: it is printed in the official credits of her 2024 calypso record. The listing of “Letters of the Alphabet”, published for Dominica Calypso 2024, introduces the performer as follows: “Performed by: Renna Charles aka Mystrie”. That is the strongest form of identification a file can receive, the one the artist own team publishes in the credits of a competition release.

That identification also places her circuit. Dominica Calypso is the calypso competition of Dominica carnival, the stage where the island calypsonians defend, each season, records written for the competition, through the tents, the elimination rounds and the final. The videos on Mystrie YouTube channel tell a continuous presence on that circuit: a performance in the 2024 calypso final, appearances at the Stardom tent, a 2024 quarter final, then the records of the 2025 and 2026 seasons. This profile therefore describes Mystrie as an artist of that Dominican circuit, because her own pages document it season after season. It lends her, however, no birthplace, no age and no path before 2024: no opened source states them.

The YouTube channel carrying her name is, to date, her main public archive. It holds the full videos and lyric videos of her competition records, her filmed stage performances, and, at the top of the catalogue at the time of this research, the full music video of “Lapawol”, credited with 836 views when consulted. It is that channel, opened and verified, that the structured data of this page gives as the official link: it is the only confirmed artist page in the file.

Letters of the Alphabet: the 2024 competition record, full credits

The best documented record by Mystrie is not her bouyon, it is her 2024 calypso. “Letters of the Alphabet” is presented by its listing with an explicit subject note: the very important part that the alphabet plays in love making. Competition calypso lives on that kind of double meaning concept, held from one end of the text to the other, and the listing claims it as the key of the record.

The credits of this record are complete, which sets it apart from most thin files in this wave. The writing is by Arthur “Bassie” Pascal. Composition, arrangement and recording are by Michael Lee Jr., known as Arch Angel Musiq, with Arthur Pascal. Mixing and mastering are by Arch Angel Musiq. Background vocals are by Nicki Durand. Around Mystrie there is therefore a small, identifiable calypso workshop team, writer, composer arranger, engineer and backing singer, and this profile names them because the listing names them.

The stage life of the record is just as documented on the channel: a performance in the 2024 calypso final, a performance at the Stardom tent as part of Celebrity Meets Calypso, a 2024 quarter final performance, and several filmed stage renditions of the same record. For a competition singer, that accumulation of videos matters: it shows a record worked through a whole season, from tent to final, and not simply dropped in a studio. Our cadence-lypso page places that world of tents and competitions within the long history of the Dominican stages of which bouyon is also an heir.

Parisze and Appetite: the 2026 and 2025 seasons in the channel catalogue

After 2024, the catalogue of Mystrie opened channel continues on a regular rhythm. For the Dominica Calypso 2026 season, the record is titled “Parisze”, spelled with a closing apostrophe in the video titles. The channel offers a full music video for it, credited with 16,000 views when consulted, a full lyric video at 7,300 views, and a recording of her semifinal performance of the same record. Three formats for one season record: the video, the lyrics, the competition stage.

For Dominica Calypso 2025, the record is titled “Appetite”. Its full video is the most viewed item in the consulted catalogue, with 41,000 views when the channel was opened, alongside a lyric video at 2,300 views. Neither of the individual listings of these two records was opened during this research: their writing and production credits are therefore not taken up here, and they do not enter the structured data of this page. What the opened catalogue establishes is their titles, their seasons, their formats and their order of audience size, and this profile stays there.

The catalogue also keeps the trace of a trip off the island for the stage: a live performance by Mystrie at the KPT Calypso Tent, in St. Lucia, on May 18, 2024, credited with 2,000 views when consulted. The fact is valuable because it is precise, a named tent, a named island, a full date, and because it shows the normal regional circulation of Dominican calypsonians, invited onto neighbouring stages. It does not make Mystrie a St. Lucian artist: it documents one date, in a career whose every other opened trace belongs to the Dominica circuit.

Lapawol: the bouyon, explained by the artist herself

“Lapawol” is the reason Mystrie appears on this site, and its listing is unusually clear for a thin file. The full title of the video announces: “Lapawol (Full Musical Video) by Mystrie. Bouyon 2026.” The release note, written in the voice of the project, then defines the record in three steps. First the genre: the song “Lapawol” is a Bouyon. Then the meaning of the title: it simply means “words” or “talk”. Finally the subject: people sometimes talk about others without knowing what they are saying about them, and without factual information, and, consequently, they are saying nothing.

The title itself is a Kwéyòl word, speech, words, what is said. This profile notes it as a fact of language, not as proof of origin: Kwéyòl is spoken in Dominica as in St. Lucia and across part of the Creole arc, and it is the artist documented Dominican competition path, shown by her own pages, that places her circuit, not her vocabulary. Our bouyon and Kwéyòl page explores precisely that layer of language running through the genre, from titles to choruses.

On the making of the record, however, the “Lapawol” listing publishes no credit: no writer, no composer, no producer and no mixer is named in the opened note, where the 2024 listing gave a full team. This profile does not fill that gap. It keeps the contrast, because it is instructive: between competition calypso, produced inside an organised framework that publishes its credits, and the standalone bouyon release of 2026, published with a meaning note but no credit roll, one sees two production regimes within the same artist path. Listening to the record runs through the full video on the channel, and our bouyon playlist places it among the bouyon releases of the 2026 season.

The subject chosen for this first bouyon finally deserves to be underlined, because it links the two halves of the path. Competition calypso is, by tradition, the genre of commentary: manners, rumours and the powerful are judged there, text in hand, before an audience that also comes for that. “Lapawol” carries exactly that material into bouyon: words circulating without facts, reputations built by word of mouth, and the dry verdict of the release note, in the end those people are saying nothing. Where many party bouyon records claim energy first, this one claims a point, written in black and white by the artist in her own presentation. It is a calypsonian signature applied to the party genre, and it is the safest reading the file allows: it rests only on the published words of the listing, read as they stand.

Tents, rounds and the final: how a competition season is made

To read Mystrie path, one has to understand the mechanics her video catalogue puts on display. A competition calypso season is not summed up by a final: it begins in the tents, those season shows where calypsonians present their records before a close audience, then it runs through elimination rounds, quarter final and semifinal, before the final. The opened catalogue of Mystrie channel contains exactly those stages for her 2024 record: the opening of the Stardom tent, a quarter final, the final, and for “Parisze” in 2026, a filmed semifinal. These are not side items to her discography: they are the discography itself, in a genre where the record first exists on stage, week after week, before it exists as a full video.

That mechanics also explains the shape of her archive. Each season produces several objects: the full music video, which presents the record in its reference version, the lyric video, which serves the competition audience, fond of text and double meaning, and the round recordings, which keep the trace of the performances. For “Parisze”, all three objects coexist in the catalogue. For “Appetite”, the full video and the lyric video coexist, and the full video has become, with 41,000 views counted when the channel was opened, the most viewed item by the artist. A reader who only looked at titles would therefore miss the essential: for a calypsonian, the catalogue reads in formats as much as in songs.

It is also within that mechanics that the St. Lucia date takes its meaning. Calypso tents regularly invite singers from neighbouring islands, and Mystrie performance at the KPT tent, on May 18, 2024, belongs to that ordinary circulation of the region stages, the same one that carries Dominican records towards neighbouring carnivals and brings guest singers back towards Roseau. The fact is documented by the opened catalogue alone, with no press release and no press article, and this profile presents it for what it is: a filmed programme line, enough to establish the date, the place and the setting, and saying nothing more.

From calypso to bouyon: a genre crossing within one island

Mystrie move to bouyon is no anomaly in the Dominican landscape. Calypso and bouyon there share the same carnival seasons, the same audiences and often the same musicians, and competition singers have always circulated between registers according to the setting: a record written for the competition on one side, a party record for the road on the other. What the Mystrie file shows cleanly is the 2026 version of that circulation: three competition seasons documented on video, then a bouyon release presented by the artist herself as a genre record, carrying a social commentary subject, talk without facts, that comes in a direct line from the observation tradition of calypso.

The bouyon she tries her hand at has its own lineage, which our bouyon soca page traces: bouyon soca was started by Dada in Dominica in 2003, per the first hand testimony of Smokiller. “Lapawol” does not present itself as bouyon soca in that precise label sense: its note says bouyon, plainly, and this profile keeps the artist word. The nuance matters for a site that classifies records on the words of their own listings, not on labels added afterwards.

This profile ends, like every thin file on this site, with its limits. No platform artist page for Mystrie was confirmed by opening during this research. No credit is published for “Lapawol”, no second bouyon record is documented, and the individual listings of “Parisze” and “Appetite” remain to be opened to complete their credit rolls. If the artist publishes what follows, a second bouyon, a platform page, full credits, this page will be completed and dated. In the meantime, her file fits into two records opened page by page, “Letters of the Alphabet” and “Lapawol”, one channel linking them, and a genre trajectory that few profiles in this wave tell as clearly.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Mystrie?

Mystrie is the stage name of Renna Charles, a singer of the Dominica calypso circuit. Her official 2024 credits present her as “Renna Charles aka Mystrie” on the record “Letters of the Alphabet”, in competition for Dominica Calypso 2024. She then defended “Appetite” at Dominica Calypso 2025 and “Parisze” in the Dominica Calypso 2026 season, before publishing the bouyon “Lapawol” in 2026.

What does Lapawol mean?

“Lapawol” is a Kwéyòl word meaning words or talk. The artist own release note says so: the title simply means “words” or “talk”, and the song is about people who talk about others without knowing and without facts, and who, in the end, are saying nothing.

Is Lapawol a bouyon record?

Yes, according to the artist herself. The official note of the full video states that the song “Lapawol” is a Bouyon, in the genre sense, and the full title of the video presents it as a Bouyon 2026. It is on that primary listing statement that this site classifies the record.

Who wrote and produced Lapawol?

No writing, composition, production or mixing credit is published in the opened note of “Lapawol”. This profile therefore names nobody for that record. By contrast, the listing of “Letters of the Alphabet” in 2024 credited Arthur “Bassie” Pascal as writer, Michael Lee Jr., known as Arch Angel Musiq, for composition, arrangement, recording, mixing and mastering, and Nicki Durand on background vocals.

What are Mystrie calypso records?

Three competition records are listed in the opened catalogue of her YouTube channel: “Letters of the Alphabet” for Dominica Calypso 2024, “Appetite” for Dominica Calypso 2025, whose full video showed 41,000 views when consulted, and “Parisze” for the Dominica Calypso 2026 season, with a full music video at 16,000 views and a semifinal performance. Only the listings of “Letters of the Alphabet” and “Lapawol” were opened individually during this research.

Has Mystrie performed outside Dominica?

One outside date is documented in her channel catalogue: a live performance at the KPT Calypso Tent, in St. Lucia, on May 18, 2024. Every other opened trace of hers belongs to the Dominica Calypso circuit, in 2024, 2025 and 2026. No published biography states her birthplace, and this profile does not deduce it.

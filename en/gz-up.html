G’z Up and FIX UP, the Ya ya ya hook turned calling card

Published October 12, 2026

G’z Up, on Instagram as @gz_upp, is the lead artist of the bouyon track FIX UP, also written Fix Up (Ya Ya Ya). The song is carried by its hook, the Ya ya ya cry that opens the track, the only lyric line verified in our records and the only one this page prints. His location is not verified in our records and no other biography facts are there: the profile is therefore built around the track, its video, its cover and its reception. In October 2026, Bouyon Shattaz prepared FIX UP as one of its reference posts, and a Time Warner Entertainment press review placed the song on a path from a cypher origin to its release under G’z Up’s name.

FIX UP: a track carried by its opening

Some bouyon tracks are told through their chorus, FIX UP is told through its first three syllables: Ya ya ya. That opening cry works as an immediate sound signature, recognisable in a video, in a mix or in a crowd, before the rest of the song even unfolds. In a genre where tracks must hook listeners within seconds, on social media as at a party, a hook of this kind is a major asset, and it is what justifies the full title in its Fix Up (Ya Ya Ya) form.

This page prints no other lyric from the track, because no other line is verified in our records. That is the rule applied to every artist on the site: better one certain hook than a text rebuilt from memory. A reader who wants the full song will find it in the genre’s playlists, starting with the ROAD MIX bouyon playlist, where FIX UP belongs among the tracks carrying the current sound.

G’z Up’s name stays attached to the track as its lead artist, a point Bouyon Shattaz is careful to establish clearly in its coverage: in online bouyon, the accounts that repost an excerpt are often confused with its authors. Here the credit is clean, FIX UP is G’z Up’s track, and it is under his name that the platform prepared and presented it.

The October 16, 2026 press review: from cypher to release

On October 16, 2026, a Time Warner Entertainment press review of Fix Up (Ya Ya Ya) traced the track’s path, from its cypher origin to its release as a G’z Up track. The cypher, the circle where artists take turns on the microphone, is a frequent birthplace for bouyon and Creole rap hooks: a formula is tested live, in front of peers, before it becomes a studio chorus. Reading FIX UP along that path explains its opening, the Ya ya ya has the shape of a formula born to be taken up by several voices.

The review also singled out G’z Up’s rhythm work, his rhythm-switching over producer ThaWizZarD’s keys. That production detail matters: on a bouyon riddim, the voice does not simply follow the loop, it can push against it, speed up and land again, and that is often where an artist’s personality is played out. The producer’s credit is therefore named here as it is in the review, ThaWizZarD on keys, G’z Up in front.

For an independent bouyon track, a review of this kind changes the song’s status: it no longer circulates only as excerpts and posts, it enters a written memory that search engines and future articles can cite. This page takes part in the same movement, with the verified facts it holds, no more, no less.

Hearing FIX UP and placing G’z Up

G’z Up is discovered by listening: FIX UP in full, then the neighbouring tracks in today’s bouyon playlists, to hear how his hook and his rhythm switches sit against the other voices of the genre. Our page about the producers and passers helps read those tracks from the making side, and the history of bouyon recalls the Dominican roots of the sound this generation carries forward.

What comes next in his file will depend on the artist’s next verified releases. If a new documented track arrives, it will join this page with its date, its credits and its cover, as FIX UP stands here today. Until then, the profile of G’z Up remains that of a name attached to a hook anyone can sing after one listen, which, in bouyon, is already a form of success.

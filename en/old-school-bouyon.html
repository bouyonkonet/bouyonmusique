Old school bouyon: the original sound of the 1990s

Published September 20, 2026

Old school bouyon is the bouyon of the live bands that ruled Dominica in the 1990s: WCK, Roots Stems and Branches, First Serenade and their heirs. A sound of the stage, of carnival and of village fetes, which purists cite as the reference for the genre.

Bands, not files

Old school bouyon is played in a full line-up: rhythm section, keyboards, guitars, brass sometimes, and singers who work the crowd like in chanté mas. The songs are long, built to last a whole night of partying, not for a thirty-second format.

Why the fans defend it

For fans of the first hour, old school keeps what modern bouyon risks losing: the traditional instruments of jing ping and lapo kabwit, Dominican Creole, and the direct link with Roseau carnival. It is this rawness, this raw and real side, that Bouyon Shattaz puts forward.

Frequently asked questions

What is bouyon music?

A music genre from Dominica, created in Roseau in 1988 by the band WCK. Its name comes from Kwéyòl and means a soup, because the genre blends jing ping, cadence-lypso, calypso and electronic keyboards. In the 1990s, its live bands made it the music of Roseau carnival and village fetes: that is the era people now call old school.

Which bands play old school bouyon?

The names cited are WCK, Roots Stems and Branches and First Serenade, along with their heirs. These bands played in full line-ups: rhythm section, keyboards, guitars, brass sometimes, and singers who worked the crowd like in chanté mas. See the page on WCK, the pioneering band.

What is the difference between old school and modern bouyon?

Old school bouyon is played by live bands, with long songs designed for a night of partying, not for a thirty-second format. Its fans believe it keeps what modern bouyon risks losing: the instruments of jing ping and lapo kabwit, Dominican Creole and the direct link with Roseau carnival.

What does RSB stand for in bouyon?

RSB stands for Roots, Stems and Branches, one of the original bouyon bands in Dominica. This site's record places it alongside WCK, formed in Roseau in 1988, and First Serenade, in the founding generation that invented a music faster, more electronic and more direct than what came before. The three names form the bedrock of old school.

What instruments does old school bouyon use?

Those of a full band: rhythm section, keyboards, guitars and sometimes brass, with singers who work the crowd like in chanté mas. WCK plugged electronic keyboards and drum machines into the heritage of jing ping and lapo kabwit, the goatskin drum of carnival. See the bouyon instruments page.

Which bands took over from the bouyon pioneers?

According to a guide to Dominica's music published in October 2025, First Serenade and Effects Band helped refine and reimagine the sound after WCK created the genre. The relay then passed to Triple Kay International and Signal Band, two Dominican bands that carry on the live band tradition. See the TK International page.

Do the old school bands still play?

Yes. WCK, Roots, Stems and Branches and First Serenade are on the programme of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026, from October 19 to 22 at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau. WCK and First Serenade were also on the bill for the second night of the World Creole Music Festival 2025, on Saturday, October 25. The pioneers remain a living presence in Dominican partying.

Why is 1990s bouyon still a reference?

Because for a decade it was the soundtrack of a whole country. In the 1990s, bouyon dominates Dominica's scene, from Roseau carnival to village fetes, and becomes a repertoire that later generations quote and transform. Purists hear in it what modern bouyon risks losing: the rawness, that raw and real side.

Is bouyon-muffin part of old school?

It belongs to the same era. In the 1990s, while live bands dominate, bouyon voices evolve: from 1995, Skinny Banton lays ragga vocals over WCK's rhythms, and bouyon-muffin is born from that mix. It bridges the bouyon of the live bands and the bouyon of today's producers. See the bouyon-muffin page.

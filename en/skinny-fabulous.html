Skinny Fabulous: the Vincy who won Trinidad Road, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Skinny Fabulous, real name Gamal Doyle, is a soca singer and songwriter from Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. He is not a bouyon-native artist, and this profile does not present him as one: it tells a major soca career, built in Kingstown, crowned five times at the Saint Vincent Soca Monarch, then written into history in 2019 when “Famalay”, sung with Machel Montano and Bunji Garlin, won the Road March of Trinidad and Tobago. The link with bouyon is real but precise, and it fits in two credits: “Famalay” and “Conch Shell” are productions by Dada, the Dominican producer of bouyon soca, and Skinny Fabulous recorded his voice for “Conch Shell” at the Dadamanufakture studio, in Dominica.

Who Skinny Fabulous is: Gamal Doyle, from Lodge Village to the soca stage

Gamal Doyle was born in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines and grew up in Lodge Village. In the reference interview he gave to Caribbean Beat Magazine, he says all his family roots are on the Leeward coast of Saint Vincent, at Chateaubelair, where his mother took him at every chance to be with family. From that rural, festive ground comes the rough character he claims on stage: a Vincy, as people from Saint Vincent are called, who does not party softly.

His path before music explains part of his discipline as a writer. After secondary school and community college, he went to the University of the West Indies in Jamaica, studied media and communications and graduated in 2010. In between, he worked as a supervisor in a call centre, then as an announcer at NBC, the Saint Vincent national broadcaster, where he hosted the show “One to Six in the Mix with Skinny Fabulous”, read the news and prepared the sports package. The radio microphone came before the stage microphone, and the habit of writing fast and clear never left him.

The name itself comes from a joke among friends. They first called him “Tin Beef”, because he was very skinny, and he says it himself with humour: he still is. Skinny Fabulous is therefore first a physique, a tall, lean man turned into a visual signature on carnival stages. Active since 2005 according to his reference entry, with a first single listed that year, “Malibu and Pine”, he took a few years to find his form. That form was not the one he first imagined.

From dancehall to aggressive soca: the making of a style

Skinny Fabulous first musical love is not soca, it is Jamaican dancehall. He tells it plainly: even before he was a student in Jamaica, he travelled there to approach producers, and he worked with the Daseca group, Craig and David Harrisingh and Serani. The experience fell short, and he drew a blunt lesson from it: if you are not Jamaican, it is extremely hard to win dancehall respect. DancehallMag, which carried his Jamaica Gleaner interview, confirms that starting point and the formative role of that period.

Back in Saint Vincent after graduating in 2010, he made the choice that defines his career: instead of dropping dancehall, he married it to soca, the genre his geographical space, Saint Vincent, Trinidad and the whole Caribbean circuit, accepts at once. The result is a rough, aggressive soca, carried by a baritone voice that chants like a deejay, with social commentary and heavy bass lines. He sums it up himself: he started with very rough soca, and while he has toned it down since, controlled aggression remains his mark.

He dates his buss year himself to 2008. That year he won his first Saint Vincent Soca Monarch title with “Head Bad”, the record that established him across the region and remains one of his stage classics. The mechanism of his craft then becomes visible: writing all his own material, spending long hours in the studio, and releasing a lot. He points out that in Saint Vincent, where many artists release one or two songs a season, he would release five or six, and that for years he produced the largest quantity of music for the season among Vincy artists. Quantity, for him, is a strategy: a hit, he says, is often the product of many songs that did not work.

The Vincy reign: Soca Monarch, honours and the 2010s catalogue

Skinny Fabulous home honours list is that of a competition champion. Reference sources list him as a five-time Soca Monarch winner in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, the contest that crowns the best soca performance of Vincy Mas, the Vincentian carnival. In 2009 the recognition went beyond the contest: he was named Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Entertainer of the Year, finished as first runner up in the OECS Soca Monarch, the Eastern Caribbean contest, and took Best New Male Artist at the Soca Music Awards. For an artist from a small island, that cluster of titles in one season marks the step from prospect to regional certainty.

That studio discipline also made him a songwriter in demand far beyond his own records. Press reports writing for major soca voices, among them Alison Hinds, Destra Garcia, Kes and Patrice Roberts, which places the Vincy in the short list of pens Trinidad stars call for their seasons. A writer who writes other people choruses understands from the inside what a contest and a carnival road demand from a record, and that knowledge runs through his own releases, built season after season rather than one at a time. It also explains the trust behind the biggest collaboration of his career: when the Road March record of 2019 was assembled, the song at its centre was his.

The 2010s catalogue built that reputation record by record. In 2012, “Six Thirty” and “Monster” marked his season, and “Monster” was remixed with Machel Montano, the biggest soca star from Trinidad, a collaboration that installed him durably on the Trinidad circuit. Then came “Hurricane” in 2013, “Behaving the Worst”, also listed as “Worst Behaviour”, in 2014, then a dense run across 2015 and 2016: “More Jab”, “Mash Up International”, “Full Hundred”, “Solid as a Rock”, “Watch Thing”, “Make It Rain”. “Happy” in 2018 and “Up and Up”, “Reckless” and “Vincy People” in 2019 closed the decade. These are not filler records: “Watch Thing” and “Happy” are among the titles specialist press still cite as his classics, and “Mash Up International” sums up his programme, a soca built to travel.

His relationship with Vincy Mas explains that staying power. In the same interview he describes a Vincentian carnival very different from Trinidad: when Saint Vincent touches the road, he says, it touches it to party hard, with rural carnivals coming down from the countryside into Kingstown and crowds behind the trucks that an outside eye could mistake for a riot. That is the energy he writes for, and it is the energy diaspora promoters book when they programme him from Miami to London. Ebuzztt also notes his repeated appearances at Machel Montano Machel Monday concert and the use by the sports network ESPN, for a four month period, of “Go Hard”, his collaboration with Montano.

Alongside, the writer overtook the performer alone. For his first eleven professional years he wrote all his own material, then began writing for others. Press reports songwriting for major soca voices, among them Alison Hinds, Destra Garcia, Kes and Patrice Roberts. In his Caribbean Beat interview he theorises the work: in an era when a hit lives only a few months, you have to pay attention to the hook from the first listen, and melt the punchlines he brought from dancehall into melodies the public keeps at once. That double skill, contest performer and writer for others, set up the breakthrough of 2019.

2019, “Famalay”: the first foreigner to win Trinidad Road March

The Road March of Trinidad and Tobago is the most coveted title in soca: it rewards the song most played at the judging points during the carnival parade, the one the bands choose to cross the stage. In 2019 the title went to “Famalay”, performed by Machel Montano, Bunji Garlin and Skinny Fabulous. Wired868 reported the official TUCO count, from the calypsonians organisation: 346 plays at the judging points, a comfortable lead over second place. For Machel Montano it was a tenth Road March, for Bunji Garlin his first, and for Skinny Fabulous a first of another order entirely.

Skinny Fabulous is indeed the first artist who is not a citizen of Trinidad and Tobago to win that title, a fact noted across the Caribbean press and in his reference entry. Ebuzztt adds a detail that says much about the writer place in this success: “Famalay” is a song written by Gamal Doyle, the Vincy, and TUCO ended up crediting him officially in the results, after an initial debate over the eligibility of a non-national singer. The title therefore did not only win with a voice from Saint Vincent: it won with a pen from Saint Vincent. Sokah2Soca, revisiting that result, lists a series of firsts for the record, among them a win shared by three performers and, discussed in the next section, a bouyon soca record at the top of the Trinidad carnival.

For Skinny Fabulous career, “Famalay” changed the scale. The artist who ruled Vincy Mas became a name every soca circuit had to book, from Trinidad fetes to the diaspora carnivals in Miami, Toronto, London and Rotterdam, where the press had followed him for years. And for this site, the record matters for one more reason, which sits in its production credits.

The bouyon point: Dada, “Famalay”, “Conch Shell” and a voice recorded in Dominica

This profile appears on a site devoted to bouyon, so the border has to be drawn exactly. Skinny Fabulous is not a bouyon artist: his genre is soca, his territory is Saint Vincent, and nothing in his sources ties him to the bouyon scenes of Roseau. What is documented is that his two biggest records of the late 2010s are productions by the Dominican producer Dada, the man who, according to the signed testimony published on this site, started bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003. That is a documented crossing, not a label stuck on afterwards.

For “Famalay”, the official credits name DadaMuzic and DSM as producers and DadaMuzic as mixer. For “Conch Shell”, the record that reunites Skinny Fabulous and Machel Montano, this time with Iwer George, for the 2020 season, the official credits name Dadamanufakture as producer, list Gamal “Skinny” Doyle among the writers, and state a further detail: Skinny Fabulous was recorded by Dadamanufakture and at Dadamanufakture, in Dominica. The Vincy voice on that record was therefore physically recorded in Dominica, at the bouyon soca producer studio. The reference article on bouyon soca also holds up “Famalay” and “Conch Shell” as the two records that cemented the future of the genre, citing lyrics from artists of Trinidad and Saint Vincent, Skinny Fabulous among them.

The regional sequel confirms the crossing was no accident. In 2023, “Come Home”, his duet with Nailah Blackman, finished second in the Trinidad Road March with 106 plays at the judging points, behind Bunji Garlin “Hard Fete”, according to results reported by the Trinidad press: a Vincy at the top of the Trinidad game twice, four years apart. At the Caribbean Music Awards he won four awards in one night, the biggest haul of the night according to Ebuzztt and Caribbean Today, among them Male Soca Artiste of the Year, plus Video of the Year, Soca Collaboration of the Year and Song of the Year in the soca genre. TEMPO Networks lists him again as Soca Artist of the Year, Male, at the 2025 edition. And in 2025, after six years away from the contest stage, he returned to the Saint Vincent Soca Monarch with Fireman Hooper, his historic rival turned partner, on the record “Erupt”, and left with the shared crown, according to Ebuzztt. To hear the genre at whose crossroads he stands, our bouyon soca page and our bouyon playlist give the Dominican context of those two productions.

Why Skinny Fabulous matters to this site, and what this profile does not claim

Skinny Fabulous matters to the story this site tells for a reason of credit. Bouyon soca was born in Dominica, and its two emblem records of the late 2010s, “Famalay” and “Conch Shell”, carry the production signature of Dada. But in the mainstream telling, it is the voices that are remembered, and among them the voice of Skinny Fabulous. Telling his career accurately, in his genre and on his island, then naming precisely the point where it crosses Dominica, is the honest way to give each party what is theirs: Vincy soca to Saint Vincent, the production to the Dominican producer, and the writing of “Famalay” to Gamal Doyle.

This profile claims no more than that. It does not make Skinny Fabulous a bouyon artist, it gives him no bouyon record he did not sing, and it invents him no Dominican past and no Dominican collaboration beyond the two documented Dada productions. His honours list stands on its own: five Soca Monarch crowns at home before his winning 2025 return, a Trinidad Road March no foreigner had won before him, a second place in the same contest in 2023, and a haul of Caribbean Music Awards. Few artists from the smaller Caribbean islands can line up a record like that.

What remains is the man the interviews draw, and who explains the longevity. A former radio announcer turned meticulous writer, who talks about his trade in terms of work: many songs written, long studio hours, a hook built to land on first listen, and a team he thanks whenever he receives a prize. When the Caribbean Music Awards crowned him ahead of artists from the bigger islands, he said the geographical lines between the islands no longer matter and that soca now works as one Caribbean melting pot. Coming from a Vincy who once recorded in Dominica on a Dada production, that is a sentence this site can stand behind.

Frequently asked questions

Is Skinny Fabulous a bouyon artist?

No. Skinny Fabulous, real name Gamal Doyle, is a soca artist from Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. His link with bouyon is precise and documented: his records “Famalay” and “Conch Shell” are productions by Dada, the Dominican producer of bouyon soca, and he recorded his voice for “Conch Shell” at the Dadamanufakture studio, in Dominica.

Where is Skinny Fabulous from?

Skinny Fabulous is from Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: born on the island, he grew up in Lodge Village, and he tells Caribbean Beat Magazine that all his family roots are on the Leeward coast, at Chateaubelair. He is a Vincy, as people from Saint Vincent are called, and he built his soca career at Vincy Mas, the carnival of Kingstown.

What is Skinny Fabulous's real name and where does his stage name come from?

The tall, lean man became a visual signature on carnival stages.

How did Skinny Fabulous start in music?

With dancehall, his first love. Even before studying in Jamaica, he travelled there to approach producers and worked with the Daseca group. He then studied media and communications at the University of the West Indies, graduating in 2010, and worked as an announcer at NBC, the Saint Vincent national broadcaster, with the show “One to Six in the Mix”.

What style of soca does Skinny Fabulous sing?

A rough, aggressive soca fed by dancehall. Back in Saint Vincent after 2010, he married dancehall to soca instead of dropping it: a baritone voice that chants like a deejay, social commentary and heavy bass lines. He says himself that he started with very rough soca, and that controlled aggression remains his mark.

How many times has Skinny Fabulous won the Saint Vincent Soca Monarch?

Reference sources list him as a five-time Soca Monarch winner in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, starting with his first crown in 2008 with “Head Bad”. In 2025, after six years away from the contest, he returned with Fireman Hooper, his historic rival turned partner, on “Erupt”, and shared the crown, according to Ebuzztt.

What did Skinny Fabulous win with “Famalay” in 2019?

“Famalay”, sung with Machel Montano and Bunji Garlin, won the 2019 Road March of Trinidad and Tobago with 346 plays at the judging points according to TUCO. Skinny Fabulous became the first artist who is not a citizen of Trinidad and Tobago to win that title, and Ebuzztt notes the song was written by Gamal Doyle himself.

Who wrote “Famalay”?

“Famalay” is a song written by Gamal Doyle, that is Skinny Fabulous, according to Ebuzztt. TUCO, the calypsonians organisation that counts the Road March plays, ended up crediting him officially in the 2019 results, after an initial debate over the eligibility of a non-national singer. The title therefore won not only with a voice from Saint Vincent, but with a pen from Saint Vincent.

Who produced “Famalay” and “Conch Shell”?

According to the official credits, “Famalay” was produced by DadaMuzic and DSM, and “Conch Shell”, by Skinny Fabulous, Machel Montano and Iwer George, was produced by Dadamanufakture. Behind both names stands Dada, the Dominican producer who, according to the signed testimony published on this site, started bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003.

Is “Famalay” a bouyon soca song?

Yes, by its production: the reference article on bouyon soca holds up “Famalay” and “Conch Shell” as the two records that cemented the future of the genre, with lyrics from artists of Trinidad and Saint Vincent, Skinny Fabulous among them. Both are produced by Dada, who started bouyon soca in Dominica in 2003 according to the testimony of Smokiller.

Where was “Conch Shell” recorded?

Partly in Dominica: the official credits state that Skinny Fabulous was recorded by Dadamanufakture and at Dadamanufakture, in Dominica. The record, for the 2020 season, reunites Skinny Fabulous, Machel Montano and Iwer George, and its credited writers are Gamal “Skinny” Doyle, Machel Montano, Neil “Iwer” George and Krishna Clyde Lawrence.

Which artists has Skinny Fabulous written songs for?

After eleven professional years writing all his own material, he began writing for others. The press reports songs written for major soca voices, among them Alison Hinds, Destra Garcia, Kes and Patrice Roberts. He explains the craft to Caribbean Beat: in an era when a hit lives only a few months, the hook has to land from the first listen.

Which Caribbean Music Awards has Skinny Fabulous won?

He won four awards in one night, the biggest haul of the night according to Ebuzztt and Caribbean Today: Male Soca Artiste of the Year, Video of the Year, Soca Collaboration of the Year and Song of the Year in the soca genre. TEMPO Networks lists him again as Soca Artist of the Year, Male, at the 2025 edition. In 2009 he had already been Best New Male Artist at the Soca Music Awards.

What are the essential Skinny Fabulous records?

Start with “Head Bad”, the record of his first crown, then “Monster”, “Watch Thing”, “Happy” and “Mash Up International”. Continue with “Famalay” and “Conch Shell”, his two Dada productions, then “Come Home” with Nailah Blackman, second in the 2023 Road March with 106 plays, and “Erupt”, the record of his 2025 Soca Monarch return.

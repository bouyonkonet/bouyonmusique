Shatta bouyon: the modern face of bouyon

By Smokiller · Published September 13, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Shatta bouyon is the current and most visible form of bouyon on social media and in clubs: short, hard, danceable riddims, produced by the new Antillean and French generation, which keep the DNA of the bouyon born in Dominica.

A bouyon made for social media

Shatta bouyon lives at the pace of short formats: a beat that hits within seconds, a dance step to copy, a loop that stays in your head. Producers dance themselves to their own sounds to launch the movement, exactly like carnival audiences used to answer the live bands of Dominica.

The roots stay Dominican

Even in its most modern form, shatta bouyon keeps the markers of the genre created by WCK in 1988: fast tempo, looping keyboards, synthetic bass and Creole on the microphone. The difference is in the production: digital, minimal, cut for the club and the phone.

Why France matters

The French Antilles and the diaspora in Paris have become a major market for bouyon: certifications, stages and distribution often pass through France. That is exactly why Bouyon Shattaz documents shatta bouyon daily, to keep the credit and the origin on Dominica’s side.

Frequently asked questions

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the current and most visible form of bouyon on social media and in clubs. It is built on short, hard, danceable riddims, produced by the new Antillean and French generation, which keep the DNA of the bouyon born in Dominica: fast tempo, looping keyboards, synthetic bass and Creole on the microphone.

Is shatta the same thing as bouyon?

No. Shatta is a dancehall genre from Martinique, not a branch of bouyon. Bouyon is the historic genre created in 1988 in Dominica by WCK, with its live bands, its carnivals and its classics. Shatta bouyon is where the two meet: short, hard riddims built on the keyboards, tempo and Creole of Dominican bouyon. See the bouyon vs shatta page.

Where does shatta bouyon come from?

Its roots are Dominican. Even in its most modern form, shatta bouyon keeps the markers of the genre created by WCK in 1988 in Dominica. What changes is the production: digital, minimal, cut for the club and the phone, and carried by the new generation of Antillean and French producers.

Why does France matter for shatta bouyon?

Because the French Antilles and the diaspora in Paris have become a major market for bouyon: certifications, stages and distribution often pass through France. That is exactly why Bouyon Shattaz documents shatta bouyon daily, to keep the credit and the origin on Dominica's side.

What is the difference between shatta bouyon and bouyon gwada?

Bouyon gwada is the hardcore branch of bouyon, born from collaborations between musicians from Dominica and Guadeloupe: harder, more electronic, largely sung in Guadeloupean Creole. Shatta bouyon is the form made for social media and clubs, with short riddims produced by the new Antillean and French generation. Bouyon gwada is the bridge between Dominican bouyon and these modern forms.

Are shatta bouyon and modern bouyon the same thing?

Not quite: shatta bouyon is one part of modern bouyon. Modern bouyon is wider and brings together the Dominican base of WCK, the energy of bouyon gwada, the social media form of shatta bouyon and all the work of producers who build riddims between Roseau, Pointe-à-Pitre and Paris. See the modern bouyon page.

Why do shatta bouyon producers dance to their own beats?

To launch the movement. Shatta bouyon lives at the pace of short formats: a riddim is launched in a video of a few seconds, the producer dances to it, then the audience copies the step and asks for the long version. It is the social media version of an old reflex from Dominica's carnival, where the crowd already answered the live bands by dancing.

Is shatta bouyon a new genre?

It is the current form of an older genre. Bouyon was created in 1988 in Dominica and has more than thirty years of history. Shatta bouyon keeps its markers: fast tempo, looping keyboards, synthetic bass and Creole on the microphone. What is new is the production, digital, minimal, cut for the club and the phone.

How do you recognize a shatta bouyon song?

By a beat that hits within seconds and a loop that stays in your head. Shatta bouyon keeps the fast tempo, looping keyboards, synthetic bass and Creole vocals of Dominican bouyon, but in a digital, minimal production. Its riddims are short, hard and danceable, with a dance step that is easy to copy on video.

Shatta bouyon: the modern face of bouyon

Published September 13, 2026

Shatta bouyon is the current and most visible form of bouyon on social media and in clubs: short, hard, danceable riddims, produced by the new Antillean and French generation, which keep the DNA of the bouyon born in Dominica.

A bouyon made for social media

Shatta bouyon lives at the pace of short formats: a beat that hits within seconds, a dance step to copy, a loop that stays in your head. Producers dance themselves to their own sounds to launch the movement, exactly like carnival audiences used to answer the live bands of Dominica.

The roots stay Dominican

Even in its most modern form, shatta bouyon keeps the markers of the genre created by WCK in 1988: fast tempo, looping keyboards, synthetic bass and Creole on the microphone. The difference is in the production: digital, minimal, cut for the club and the phone.

Why France matters

The French Antilles and the diaspora in Paris have become a major market for bouyon: certifications, stages and distribution often pass through France. That is exactly why Bouyon Shattaz documents shatta bouyon daily, to keep the credit and the origin on Dominica’s side.

Frequently asked questions

Is shatta the same thing as bouyon?

Shatta is a family of modern Antillean sounds; shatta bouyon is the part of that family built directly on the riddims and the energy of Dominican bouyon.

Daddy Chess: Forward We Go, bouyon, Dominica

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Daddy Chess, real name Chester Letang, is a singer, drummer and composer from Dominica, the voice of the Swinging Stars Orchestra, whom his island calls, according to his published artist profile, the “Soca Man”. His story holds in two crowns separated by a quarter of a century: Calypso Monarch in 2001, one year after his stage debut, with “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay”, then Calypso Monarch again on 14 February 2026 at Windsor Park Sports Stadium, with “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking”. In between, he stacked Soca Monarch, Road March King and Stardom King of the Tent titles, sang from London to Port of Spain, and held Dominican carnival stages for more than twenty five years. This profile follows that path on published sources, with titles and dates attached.

Who Daddy Chess is: Chester Letang, the voice of Swinging Stars

Daddy Chess is the stage name of Chester Letang, an artist from Dominica. His profile published on ItzCaribbean presents him as the lead vocalist of the Swinging Stars Orchestra, the island acclaimed party band, and as an all rounded artist: vocalist, drummer and composer, but also motivational speaker and entertainment coach. The same profile records his stage signatures: high energy, an eccentric sense of fashion, gyrating waistline movements that became his trademark, and often unpredictable on stage stunts. It is that mix of singing, drumming and theatre that built his reputation long before the crowns.

The nickname that sticks to him sums up his place in Dominican music. ItzCaribbean writes that he is known as the “Soca Man” of Dominica: the man of soca, the party genre that moves carnivals across the English speaking Caribbean. This profile carries that nickname as what it is, a published and attributed label, not an official title. But it is true on one point: where many Dominican artists pick a side, contest calypso or party soca, Daddy Chess built his career crossing from one side to the other all his life, and that double belonging structures this profile.

One spelling note belongs at the entrance, because it is part of the published record. The Dominica News report on his 2026 victory writes his surname two ways inside the same article: “Letang” in the headline and in most occurrences, “Lateng” twice in the body text. Caribbean Times and Soca News write “Letang”. This site therefore uses “Chester Letang”, and flags the hesitation of its main source instead of hiding it: it is the kind of detail a reader should be able to check.

1990 to 2001: from the drums to the first crown

The documented path starts with an instrument, not a microphone. According to the Dominica News report published after his 2026 victory, Daddy Chess comes from a family deeply rooted in music, he started his music journey in 1990 when he discovered his love for drums, and in 1995 he discovered his talent for composition. Five years later, in 2000, he combined his composing skills with his natural singing ability and made his debut on the calypso stage. That chronology matters: the man who would become Dominica Soca Man is first a drummer, trained in rhythm before refrains, and a writer before he is a contestant.

What followed was unusually fast. Only one year after his debut, in 2001, he won the Calypso Monarch title of Dominica. Dominica News names the two songs of that victory: “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay”. ItzCaribbean adds that the same year he also took the Stardom King of the Tent title, and holds up “Your Destiny Depends on You” as an inspirational hit, a message song. The profile tells how that victory made him a walking message for the youth: he performed and mentored in schools all over the island. The calypso debutant of 2000 was therefore, by 2001, both a contest champion and a figure of popular education.

That first crowning also sets the formula that follows him everywhere: the contest and the fete at the same time. The year 2001 sees him crowned in the calypso tent and carried by a party band, Swinging Stars, whose voice he is. Documentation from 2004 shows him still in that seat: the programme of that year World Creole Music Festival, reported by The Dominican.net, lists Swinging Stars featuring calypsonians Hunter, Daddy Chess, Observer and Dice on its final night. Seven years after the drummer turned singer began, he is already one of the established voices of the biggest Creole stage on his island.

The soca years: Forward We Go, Road March and Port of Spain

If 2001 is the calypso year, 2008 is the soca year, and it is documented live by the press of the time. The Carnival 2008 report of The Dominican.net tells the Calypso Monarch final at Windsor Park stadium: De Dice won his fourth crown there, and Daddy Chess placed second, ahead of Scrunter and Hunter. The same report states that before that final, Daddy Chess had already won two titles in a row that season, Soca Monarch and Stardom King of the Tent, and that his rendition of “Forward We Go” was a crowd favourite, quickly taking hold all over the land thanks to its appeal to Dominican patriotism.

The full balance of that period, as ItzCaribbean draws it, gives the measure of the man: Road March King in 2008, Stardom King of the Tent in 2001 and 2008, Calypso Monarch in 2001, and Soca Monarch in 2008 and 2009. Few Dominican artists can line up, in the same decade, a writing contest title, two soca titles, a road march title and two tent titles. ItzCaribbean also records his hits in that vein: “Welcome to De Fete”, “Forward We Go” and “Ready or Not”, three party songs that carry exactly what his nickname announces.

“Ready or Not” even carries him onto the biggest soca stage in the world. The published finalists list of the 2010 International Soca Monarch, the Trinidad contest, places him in the Power category, under the entry Daddy Chess (Dominica), with “Ready or Not”, for a final announced on Fantastic Friday, 12 February 2010, at Queen’s Park Oval, Port of Spain. Sharing that list with Fay-Ann Lyons, Bunji Garlin, Skinny Fabulous and Mr Killa, and representing Dominica on it, is a career fact no local title replaces: it proves that the Soca Man of Roseau was measured, at least once, against the world elite of his genre, on their home ground.

The tours tell the same amplitude. ItzCaribbean has him performing in London, in the United States in New York, New Jersey, Miami, Houston and Atlanta, and across the whole Caribbean, from the Virgin Islands to St. Lucia, Trinidad, Barbados and Antigua. That circuit is the circuit of the Dominican diaspora and of Caribbean carnivals: the same public that dances to bouyon in Roseau meets him again, in soca and calypso form, in diaspora halls. It is also for that travelling public that this profile exists on a bouyon site: the genres of the Dominican fete share the same stages, the same bands and the same crowds.

The long road back: 2025 semi-final, third place behind Trilla G, the 2026 crown

Calypso contests do not forgive absence, and the record of the 2020s shows a Daddy Chess still in the arena, never on a farewell tour. In the Calypso Monarch final won on debut by Trilla G at Windsor Park Sports Stadium Forecourt, reported by Caribbean Times, Dennison “Dice” Joseph placed second and Chester “Daddy Chess” Letang placed third: the former champion still stands on the podium, beaten by a 24 year old debutant and by King Dice, a nine time monarch. That third place says more about longevity than any speech: twenty years after his crown, a national final still has to reckon with him.

In 2025 he is still there, and the specialist press counts him among the references of the contest. Soca News reports that after the quarter finals held on 1 February 2025 at Windsor Park Stadium Forecourt, six former calypso monarchs reached the semi-finals of the Dominica competition: The Bobb, Karessah, Jaydee, Observer, Daddy Chess and Tasha P, among twenty semi-finalists drawn from thirty two quarter finalists. To be named, in 2025, in the list of six former champions still in the race, twenty five years after his stage debut, confirms what the third place behind Trilla G suggested: Daddy Chess is not a name from the past invited out of politeness, but a competitor the young champions must eliminate every year.

On Saturday 14 February 2026, the wait ends. Dominica News reports the grand finale of the National Calypso Monarch at Windsor Park Sports Stadium: ten calypsonians on stage, and in the final round Daddy Chess delivers two powerful performances, “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking”, which capture the audience attention and impress the judges on the way to first place. First time finalist Nacheal is announced first runner up, and Joy, the 2025 winner, second runner up. The report underlines the scale of the moment: this is the second time Daddy Chess claims the Calypso Monarch title, a title he waited 25 years for, since the 2001 victory with “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay”, one year after his debut. Published congratulations single out the lyrical strength of “Beauty and the Beast”, called strong and clearly rendered, and one supporter sums the night up with the title of his old hit: “Forward you go, forward we will go”.

Calypso, soca and bouyon: why Daddy Chess appears on this site

A reader may ask what a calypso and soca monarch is doing in a catalogue of bouyon artists. The answer lies in the structure of the Dominican fete itself. Contest calypso, band soca and group bouyon live on the same carnival stages, before the same Roseau crowds, and bands like Swinging Stars have spent decades bridging those repertoires inside a single night. A singer who leads a party band, wins the road march and twice takes the calypso crown belongs to the full history of the Dominican sound, not to one chapter of it.

The link turns direct when the bouyon soca branch is followed. That fusion of Dominica bouyon and soca, told on our bouyon soca page, was started in Dominica in 2003 by producer Dada, according to the signed testimony of Smokiller, a Nursery Krew participant, then popularised by Triple Kay. Soca singers of the Daddy Chess generation are the natural carriers of that fusion on the stage side: their voices, trained in bands and contests, are the ones that carried Dominican rhythms to soca audiences across the Caribbean. Even his Soca Man nickname, attributed by his published profile, places him at that junction point between Dominica and the soca world.

There is finally the question of credit and memory, the one this site documents everywhere. An artist whose first crown dates to 2001 and whose second dates to 2026 crosses three generations of public, and each generation risks knowing only half the path: the young spectators of 2026 discover a champion, the elders remember the Soca Man of 2008. This profile gathers both halves with their sources, so that the 2026 crown reads as what it is: not a surprise comeback, but the outcome of a continuous presence of more than twenty five years.

What the record holds, and what it does not yet know

The published record of Daddy Chess is rich on contests and poor on platforms, and this profile says both. Rich on contests: the titles and their years agree from one source to the next, from ItzCaribbean to the 2008 carnival press, from Caribbean Times to Soca News and to the Dominica News victory report of 2026. Poor on platforms: the research for this profile confirmed no official streaming or social account as his by direct opening, so the schema of this page contains no external artist link. The recordings listed in the schema are the ones the sources name: the two winning songs of 2001, the two winning songs of 2026, and the three soca hits recorded by his published profile.

The record also leaves open questions, which this profile does not fill with invention: neither his date of birth, nor his complete album discography, nor the detailed writing credits of each song are established by the sources consulted. His surname itself arrives with a documented hesitation, “Letang” against “Lateng” inside the same 2026 report, settled here in favour of the majority spelling and corroborated by Caribbean Times and Soca News. If verified elements are added, this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site.

In the meantime, listening starts with the songs the sources name: “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay” for the 2001 crown, “Forward We Go” and “Ready or Not” for the Soca Monarch years, “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking” for the night of 14 February 2026. Frequently asked questions

Who is Daddy Chess?

Daddy Chess is the stage name of Chester Letang, a singer, drummer and composer from Dominica and the lead vocalist of the Swinging Stars Orchestra. His profile published on ItzCaribbean also describes him as a motivational speaker and entertainment coach. He was Dominica Calypso Monarch in 2001 and again in 2026, and Soca Monarch in 2008 and 2009.

Where is Daddy Chess from?

Daddy Chess is from Dominica, the island where he won his calypso and soca crowns, from 2001 to 2026. He has sung there since his calypso stage debut in 2000 and fronts the Swinging Stars Orchestra there. His touring later took him to London, the United States and across the Caribbean.

What is Daddy Chess real name?

Chester Letang. That is the name Caribbean Times and Soca News use for him, and the majority spelling in the Dominica News report on his 2026 victory, which also writes “Lateng” twice inside the same article. This site uses “Letang” and flags that source hesitation.

How many times has Daddy Chess been Calypso Monarch of Dominica?

Twice, twenty five years apart. In 2001, one year after his stage debut, with “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay”, then on 14 February 2026 at Windsor Park Sports Stadium, with “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking”, according to Dominica News.

What other titles has Daddy Chess won?

According to his profile published on ItzCaribbean: Road March King in 2008, Stardom King of the Tent in 2001 and 2008, and Soca Monarch in 2008 and 2009. In 2008 the carnival press also places him second in the calypso final behind De Dice, in the same season he had already won soca and the tent.

Has Daddy Chess performed on the Trinidad soca stage?

Yes. The published finalists list of the 2010 International Soca Monarch places him in the Power category, as the Dominica entry, with his song “Ready or Not”, for the final of 12 February 2010 at Queen’s Park Oval, Port of Spain. His published profile also has him performing in London, in several United States cities and across the Caribbean.

What was Daddy Chess doing between his two crowns?

He stayed a front rank competitor in Dominican calypso. He placed third in a final won by debutant Trilla G, behind nine time monarch Dice, then reached the 2025 semi-finals among six former monarchs still in the race, according to Soca News, before winning the 2026 final ahead of Nacheal and Joy.

Why does Daddy Chess appear on a bouyon site?

Because calypso, soca and bouyon share the same carnival stages in Dominica, and the voices of bands like Swinging Stars, whose lead singer he is, carry the Dominican sound across those genres. Hear our bouyon playlist for the bouyon and bouyon soca branch of that same fete.

What are Daddy Chess’s best songs?

His profile published on ItzCaribbean names three party hits: “Welcome to De Fete”, “Forward We Go” and “Ready or Not”. “Forward We Go” was the 2008 crowd favourite. Add the songs behind his crowns: “Your Destiny Depends on You” and “Somebody Have to Pay” in 2001, “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking” in 2026.

Why is Daddy Chess called the Soca Man?

Because his profile published on ItzCaribbean says he is known as the “Soca Man” of Dominica, an attributed nickname rather than an official title. It fits his party record: Soca Monarch in 2008 and 2009, Road March King in 2008, and hits like “Forward We Go” or “Ready or Not”. Yet he is also a calypso champion.

How did Daddy Chess start in music?

On the drums. According to Dominica News, he comes from a family deeply rooted in music, discovered his love of percussion in 1990, then his talent for composition in 1995. In 2000 he combined songwriting and voice and made his calypso stage debut, before winning the Calypso Monarch crown the very next year, in 2001.

Did Daddy Chess play the World Creole Music Festival?

Yes. According to The Dominican.net, the 2004 World Creole Music Festival programme billed Swinging Stars with calypsonians Hunter, Daddy Chess, Observer and Dice on its final night. He appeared as one of the band’s voices on Dominica’s biggest Creole stage, three years after his first calypso crown.

Who won Calypso Monarch 2026 in Dominica?

Daddy Chess, on Saturday 14 February 2026 at Windsor Park Sports Stadium, according to Dominica News. Facing ten finalists, he won with “Beauty and the Beast” and “Roof Leaking”. Debutant Nacheal was first runner up and Joy, the 2025 winner, second runner up. It was his second crown, twenty five years after the one of 2001.

Bouyon: a name that means soup

By Smokiller · Published September 30, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

In Antillean Creole, the word bouyon means something like a gumbo soup, or a koubouyon pwason, a fish broth typical of the Caribbean. A dish where you put everything you have, and where the mix creates the taste. The creators of the genre could not have found a better name.

The mix as a philosophy

Jing ping, cadence-lypso, bèlè, kwadril, chanté mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka, zouk: the list of bouyon ingredients reads like a recipe. As in a soup, no ingredient completely dominates, and the mix is what makes the identity of the dish, and of the genre.

A word from everyday life

The musical bouyon carries the name of a popular kitchen dish, not a studio name. That fits its history: a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, for the people, with the people’s words, in Kwéyòl and in Creole English.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

With the meaning of its name, then by listening. Bouyon means soup in Creole, and the genre has indeed mixed jing ping, cadence-lypso and electronic keyboards since WCK created it in Roseau in 1988. Then read the history of the genre and play the Bouyon Road Mix by Bouyon Shattaz, on Spotify and YouTube, which mixes carnival classics and new releases. See the bouyon playlist.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band. Bouyon is a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, sung in Kwéyòl and in Creole English, which also explains why it carries a name taken from popular cooking.

What does bouyon mean in Creole?

In Antillean Creole, the word bouyon means something like a gumbo soup, or a koubouyon pwason, a fish broth typical of the Caribbean. It is a dish where you put everything you have and where the mix creates the taste, an image that fits Dominica's bouyon music perfectly.

Why is bouyon music called bouyon?

Because bouyon mixes many musical ingredients, like a soup: jing ping, cadence-lypso, bèlè, kwadril, chanté mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk. As in the dish, no ingredient completely dominates, and the mix is what makes the identity of the genre.

Why does a music genre have a food name?

Bouyon carries the name of a popular kitchen dish, not a studio name, and that fits its history: a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, for the people, with the people's words, in Kwéyòl and in Creole English. See also the history of bouyon.

Bouyon or bouillon: how is the genre's name spelled?

Bouyon, with a y: it is the Creole spelling of the word that means a soup, a gumbo, in Antillean Creole. The music genre is always spelled that way, not like the French word bouillon, even if the image is close: the koubouyon pwason, a typical Caribbean dish, is precisely a fish broth where you put everything you have.

What does WCK stand for?

WCK stands for Windward Caribbean Kulture, the culture of the Windward Islands. It is the name of the band that created bouyon in Roseau, Dominica, in 1988, by fusing cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. The full name already states its project: a Caribbean ambition starting from Roseau. See the WCK page.

What does jump up mean?

Jump up is the name Guadeloupe and Martinique give to bouyon. It sums up the effect of the music: a fast tempo that makes everyone jump. The term already described very rhythmic carnival music, notably after L'hivernage by Exile One in 1987, and Dominican bouyon was filed in that family. See the jump up page.

Reketeng or Wrecketeng: how is the style spelled?

Wrecketeng is the official spelling of this Dominican style derived from bouyon. According to Smokiller, it comes from DJ Cut himself, the pioneer of the style. The form “reketeng”, very widespread online, is a misspelling that became common. The style rests on sampled melodies and rap vocals laid over bouyon rhythms. See Wrecketeng.

What does Mas Domnik mean?

Mas Domnik is the Creole name of Dominica's carnival, celebrated in Roseau before Lent. It is bouyon's natural ground: the genre becomes its soundtrack in the 1990s, and it is made from its traditions, like chanté mas and the bands playing lapo kabwit, the goatskin drum. See the Mas Domnik page.

What does RnBouyon mean?

RnBouyon, in one word, names the fusion of Dominica's bouyon and R&B: the programmed percussion and basses of bouyon, with melodic singing close to contemporary R&B. The term is carried by singer Fallon, featured by Deezer Next 2026, and by producer Smokiller for his instrumentals. See the RnBouyon page.

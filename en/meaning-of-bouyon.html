Bouyon: a name that means soup

Published September 30, 2026

In Antillean Creole, the word bouyon means something like a gumbo soup, or a koubouyon pwason, a fish broth typical of the Caribbean. A dish where you put everything you have, and where the mix creates the taste. The creators of the genre could not have found a better name.

The mix as a philosophy

Jing ping, cadence-lypso, bèlè, kwadril, chanté mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka, zouk: the list of bouyon ingredients reads like a recipe. As in a soup, no ingredient completely dominates, and the mix is what makes the identity of the dish, and of the genre.

A word from everyday life

The musical bouyon carries the name of a popular kitchen dish, not a studio name. That fits its history: a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, for the people, with the people’s words, in Kwéyòl and in Creole English.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of the genre, then the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band. Bouyon is a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, sung in Kwéyòl and in Creole English, which also explains why it carries a name taken from popular cooking.

What does bouyon mean in Creole?

In Antillean Creole, the word bouyon means something like a gumbo soup, or a koubouyon pwason, a fish broth typical of the Caribbean. It is a dish where you put everything you have and where the mix creates the taste, an image that fits Dominica's bouyon music perfectly.

Why is bouyon music called bouyon?

Because bouyon mixes many musical ingredients, like a soup: jing ping, cadence-lypso, bèlè, kwadril, chanté mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk. As in the dish, no ingredient completely dominates, and the mix is what makes the identity of the genre.

Why does a music genre have a food name?

Bouyon carries the name of a popular kitchen dish, not a studio name, and that fits its history: a music born in the village fetes and the carnival of Dominica, for the people, with the people's words, in Kwéyòl and in Creole English. See also the history of bouyon.

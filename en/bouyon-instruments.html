The instruments of bouyon: from the traditional drum to the keyboard

Published September 22, 2026

The bouyon sound comes from a meeting: the traditional instruments of Dominica, inherited from jing ping and the Creole dances, plugged into the machines of the 1980s, keyboards, synthesizers and drum machines.

The traditional instruments

The lapo kabwit, the goat-skin drum of chanté mas and carnival. The bele drum, at the heart of the dance of the same name. The chakchak, Creole maracas. The syak or gwaj, the scraper. The accordion, the voice of jing ping, with the bamboo flute and the boom-boom. These sounds are the soul of bouyon, and good producers still sample them.

The machines

WCK built bouyon on technology: keyboards up front, synthetic rhythmic patterns, electronic basses. Today, producers work on software, but they are looking for the same result: reproducing the pressure of a carnival band with machines. To go further, see the bouyon drum kit page.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start with bouyon?

Start with the history of the genre, then the Bouyon Road Mix playlist by Bouyon Shattaz, which mixes Dominica carnival classics with new bouyon.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica, in the late 1980s, with WCK as the pioneering band. WCK built the genre on technology, with keyboards up front, synthetic rhythmic patterns and electronic basses, while keeping the soul of the island's traditional instruments.

What instruments are used in bouyon?

Bouyon combines two families. On one side, the traditional instruments of Dominica: lapo kabwit, bele drum, chakchak, syak or gwaj, accordion, bamboo flute and boom-boom. On the other, the machines of the 1980s: keyboards, synthesizers and drum machines, replaced today by producers' software.

What is the lapo kabwit?

The lapo kabwit is a goat-skin drum, the drum of chanté mas and Dominica's carnival. At Mas Domnik, whole bands play in lapo kabwit. It is one of the traditional sounds that form the soul of bouyon, and good producers still sample it.

How do producers make bouyon sound today?

Producers now work on software, but they are after the same result as WCK with its keyboards: reproducing the pressure of a carnival band with machines. Many still sample traditional instruments such as the lapo kabwit or the accordion. For drum sounds, see the bouyon drum kit page.

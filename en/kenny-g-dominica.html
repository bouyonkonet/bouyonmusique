Kenny G: no saxophone, the raw bouyon voice of Dominica

Published October 20, 2026

Kenny G is a bouyon artist from Dominica. He shares his stage name with the American smooth jazz saxophonist, and that shared name has almost completely hidden his career from search engines. The Kenny G of this profile plays no saxophone: he sings bouyon, the fast music born in Dominica, on the biggest stages of his island. Nominated at the Caribbean Music Awards and a member of the Bouyon Assembly at the 2025 World Creole Music Festival, he is one of the raw voices of the current Dominican scene. This profile gathers what published sources can verify about him, and states clearly what they do not say.

Two Kenny Gs: the confusion to clear first

There is a world famous Kenny G, and he is not the man in this profile. Kenneth Gorelick, born in Seattle in 1956, is an American smooth jazz saxophonist, the man behind albums such as “Duotones” and “Breathless”, sold in the tens of millions. Type “Kenny G” into any search engine or streaming platform and he fills the whole screen: his biography, his albums, his photos, his official pages.

The Kenny G from Dominica shares nothing with him but the name. He is a singer of bouyon, the fast, raw carnival music born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s. He plays no saxophone, he has no link to American jazz, and his catalogue contains none of the American records. Any confusion between the two men distorts both stories, so this profile clears it first, in plain terms.

That shared name is not a curiosity: it is a credit problem, of the kind this site documents everywhere. A Caribbean artist can be nominated at his region biggest awards and still stay invisible in the databases, simply because his name already belongs to a star from another continent. Kenny G traces do exist, but they are not where the algorithms look: they are on festival bills, in nominee lists and in DJ tracklists.

The rule of this profile is therefore simple. Everything below comes from sources that speak about the Dominican in his own context: official Dominica Festivals announcements carried by the press, the Caribbean Music Awards lists, Caribbean media and carnival mixes. Nothing is borrowed from the saxophonist biography, and nothing is invented to fill the gaps.

Who the Dominican Kenny G is

The first published description of Kenny G comes from the announcement that added him to the big stage of the World Creole Music Festival. In October 2023, the site Writeups24 carried the information released by Dominica Festivals: Ridge and Friends and Kenny G, both from Dominica, presented as artists known for their high octane, energetic performances. It is only a few words, but they are the right ones: Kenny G is first a stage performer, spotted by his island programmers for what he does live.

His ground is the bouyon of today: fast, physical, built for the carnival crowd and the festival nights. In March 2025, CaribMagPlus magazine, reporting the victory of “RAGS” at Dominica first Bouyon Road March, named Kenny G among the artists whose recent releases are riding the bouyon wave, alongside Sukie Burn Brain, Mr. Ridge, Reo and Signal Band. He belongs to the active generation of the genre, the one the awards and the festivals now follow closely.

Caribbean media covering the Caribbean Music Awards described him, from April 2025, as a raw and influential figure in the scene. The words are those of the Black Island Girl site, and this profile quotes them for what they are: the view of an outlet that follows bouyon day by day, not an official title. Kenny G holds no documented crown, and none is lent to him here. What is documented is his repeated place in the lists that matter.

For the rest, the public record is thin, and this profile says so. None of the sources consulted publishes his full name, his birth date, his home village or a biographical interview. No detailed artist page on the major platforms, no reference entry. His published story fits inside bills, lists and tracklists. That is exactly why it deserves to be gathered somewhere, with its limits shown.

The festival road: from WCMF 2023 to the Bouyon Assembly

On October 2, 2023, Dominica Festivals announced four more names for the 23rd World Creole Music Festival, held from October 27 to 29, 2023: Kalash, DJ Private Ryan, Ridge and Friends and Kenny G. The bill already carried Popcaan, Beres Hammond, Machel Montano, Patrice Roberts and Asa Bantan. For a Dominican artist, being added to the big stage of the Roseau festival, in front of the home crowd, is the first documented step of this path.

The second step came two years later, and it was collective. For the 25th edition of the festival, from October 24 to 26, 2025 at Windsor Park in Roseau, the night of Saturday October 25 programmed The Bouyon Assembly: an all bouyon segment bringing together Rohie, Ridge, Pudaz, Kenny G, Quan, DJ MJ, Little Boy and Ebony Empress for Dominica, with Jixels and Shanika from Guadeloupe. To be picked for that Assembly is to be counted by the organisers among the carriers of the sound, on its biggest national stage, in the festival 25th anniversary year.

The third confirmed date looks at the near future. On October 18, 2026, Kenny G was added to the official artist list of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026, the Creole village at the Botanic Gardens in Roseau, whose 5th edition runs from October 19 to 22, 2026 under the theme “Tout Biten Domnik”, Everything Dominican. The list, published by the Government of Dominica, places him alongside the bands and DJs who animate Independence week, just before the World Creole Music Festival.

The shape of this path is clear: Kenny G public career reads stage by stage. Festival programmers keep booking him, official lists keep confirming him, but his work circulates little through the channels artist profiles usually document. This is a performer profile, not a studio profile, and the following sections treat it as such.

The Caribbean Music Awards: nominated among the best in bouyon

In June 2025, the official nominee lists of the Caribbean Music Awards were published. In the Bouyon Artist of the Year category, nine names: Asa Bantan, Kenny G, litleboy, Mr. Ridge, Quan, Shelly Alfred, Trilla G, Triple K and Pudaz. The ceremony, the third edition of the awards, was held on August 28, 2025 in Brooklyn, New York. Bouyon has its own category there, a sign that the genre born in Dominica has become an official box in the Caribbean music industry.

The company sets the level. Triple K and Asa Bantan are established names of the genre, Trilla G, litleboy, Quan, Pudaz and Shelly Alfred carry the new wave, and Mr. Ridge is the title holder, winner in 2024. Black Island Girl, covering the nominations from April 2025, placed Kenny G among the artists making repeat appearances in this category from one year to the next: a repeated nomination, then, not a single pass.

The 2025 title went to Mr. Ridge, according to the full winners list published after the ceremony. Kenny G did not win, and this profile does not claim he did. But in a nine place category for a whole genre, being nominated two years running places an artist in the small circle the awards track over time. For a performer whose catalogue is almost impossible to find on streaming, that repeated recognition is the strongest piece of the record.

It is worth measuring what this means for Dominica. The bouyon category of the Caribbean Music Awards has become the official barometer of the genre, in front of a diaspora audience in New York. That Kenny G stands there alongside historic groups such as Triple Kay says that his name, invisible to the algorithms, is perfectly visible to the professionals of his own scene.

The records: a catalogue that lives in DJ mixes

Kenny G most traceable record is called “Dress and Out”. It appears in the tracklist of “ROAD MONSTER P.1”, a Carnival 2025 mix by DJ Sargent Unstoppable, which lines up the soca, Dennery Segment and bouyon heard on the road that season. A Kenny G record holds its place there between tracks by Triple Kay, Mr. Ridge and Shelly and Signal Band: the company of the mixes is, once again, a ranking in itself.

Another carnival mix, “Road to Carnival (Dominica Meets Trinbago)” by DJ Judgement, lists a Kenny G title of its own: “Dress and Out Again”. Sequel, rework or simple title variant: the sources do not allow a ruling, and this profile does not rule. What they show is that the “Dress and Out” name stays in rotation in carnival mixes across two seasons, which is how a bouyon record lives when it has no streaming page under its own name.

The same “ROAD MONSTER P.1” mix documents a collaboration: “I Know Y”, performed by Reo, Kenny G and Faithii. Reo is one of the rising voices of Dominica, and Faithii is, like Kenny G, a member of the 2025 Bouyon Assembly. The record brings the three together in the classic format of current bouyon: a short, fast track built for DJs and for the carnival road.

This profile therefore keeps three verified records: “Dress and Out”, “Dress and Out Again” and “I Know Y”. No complete streaming discography under his own name could be confirmed during the research, because the “Kenny G” pages of the platforms belong to the American saxophonist. If an official catalogue of the Dominican is confirmed, this section will be completed and dated, like every page on this site.

Why Kenny G matters, and what this profile does not yet know

Kenny G matters because the institutions of his genre count him in. The Caribbean Music Awards nominated him two years running in their bouyon category. The World Creole Music Festival organisers placed him in the Bouyon Assembly of the festival 25th anniversary. The official Creole village of Independence announced him for 2026. Each time, professionals of the Dominican and Caribbean scene wrote his name on their lists, and those lists are documents.

His case also sums up the problem Bouyon Shattaz documents across this site: credit for Dominican artists is easily lost. Here it is not lost inside a wrong origin story, it is lost inside a search engine, behind a namesake from Seattle. Restoring the distinction, profile by profile, is part of this site work, in the same way as telling the story of the genre from WCK to today.

The limits of the record are stated here one last time, plainly. Kenny G full name appears in none of the sources consulted and is therefore not printed. No verified official account of the artist was confirmed during the research, so this page links to no social profile and the schema sameAs field stays empty. No birth date and no precise birthplace are published in the record. What is not verified is not written.

To hear the bouyon of his generation, start with our bouyon playlist, which follows the Dominican releases covered by Bouyon Shattaz, and with the profiles of his fellow nominees such as Asa Bantan. And if Kenny G “Dress and Out” passes in a mix near you, you will now know which Kenny G it is: the one from Dominica, with no saxophone.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kenny G from Dominica the American saxophonist?

No. The saxophonist Kenny G is Kenneth Gorelick, born in Seattle in 1956, an American smooth jazz star. The Kenny G of this profile is a bouyon singer from Dominica, with no connection to him: they share only a stage name, and their catalogues do not overlap.

Where is Kenny G from, and what style is he known for?

Kenny G is from Dominica. The official Dominica Festivals announcement carried by Writeups24 in 2023 presented him, with Ridge and Friends, as Dominican artists known for their high octane, energetic performances. His style is bouyon, the fast carnival music born in Dominica.

What are Kenny G known songs?

Three records are verified by published carnival mix tracklists: “Dress and Out” and “Dress and Out Again”, and “I Know Y”, performed with Reo and Faithii. No complete streaming discography under his name could be confirmed, since the “Kenny G” pages of the platforms belong to the American saxophonist.

Was Kenny G nominated at the Caribbean Music Awards?

Yes. He appears on the official 2025 nominee list for Bouyon Artist of the Year, alongside Asa Bantan, litleboy, Mr. Ridge, Quan, Shelly Alfred, Trilla G, Triple K and Pudaz. Caribbean media describe him as a repeat nominee in the category. The 2025 title went to Mr. Ridge.

When did Kenny G play the World Creole Music Festival?

He was added to the bill of the 2023 edition, held from October 27 to 29, 2023. In 2025, for the festival 25th edition, he performed on Saturday October 25 at Windsor Park in Roseau as part of The Bouyon Assembly, the collective bouyon segment of Night Two, with Ridge, Pudaz, Quan, DJ MJ, Little Boy, Rohie and Ebony Empress.

Why is so little information published about Kenny G?

For two reasons. First, his stage name is occupied worldwide by the American saxophonist, which buries his traces in search engines and on the platforms. Second, his career runs through the stage and through DJ mixes more than through documented streaming releases. This profile gathers the verifiable sources: festival bills, Caribbean Music Awards lists, Caribbean press and published tracklists.

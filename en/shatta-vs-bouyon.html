Shatta vs bouyon: 100 BPM vs 152 BPM

By Smokiller · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Shatta and bouyon are the two street sounds of the French Antilles, and they do not run at the same speed. Shatta, born in Martinique, runs at around 100 BPM. Bouyon, born in Dominica, runs at around 152 BPM in its classic form and rises to 160 BPM in hardcore. What follows compares the two styles, tempo by tempo, without hiding that the guides do not all agree on the bouyon figures.

Two tempos, two dances

Shatta is a heavy, moderate style: around 100 BPM, often between 100 and 105 BPM according to DJ guides, in the line of Jamaican dancehall, which runs at around 90 to 110 BPM. Classic bouyon runs at around 152 BPM, and hardcore bouyon rises to 160 BPM. The dance follows the tempo: shatta invites slow, intense hip movement, while bouyon is made for jumping, the jump up, and for wine, the rotation of the hips.

That has to be said plainly: the bouyon figures are not unanimous. One DJ guide gives 120 to 135 BPM for bouyon, another 130 to 160 BPM, and the tempo varies from track to track, for example 141 BPM for one bouyon zouk track measured on a platform. The figures quoted here are therefore markers, not rules. The contrast itself is constant: shatta is twice as slow as classic bouyon.

Shatta, slow Martinique

Shatta is a dancehall sub-genre, born in Martinique in the early 2010s, in Volga-Plage, a working-class neighbourhood of Fort-de-France. The pioneers are PSK Shatta, founder of PSK Music Production, and Danthology. The first attempts go back to 2007-2010, with original instrumentals laid over Jamaican dancehall hits, then the golden age of remixes from 2013 to 2017, and the word shatta only took hold seriously around 2018.

The sound is minimalist: no kick drums, a deep bass, dry percussion, every sound in its place. The lyrics are mostly in Martinican Creole, with English, Jamaican patois and a little French. Female voices lead the current wave: Maureen, often called the Queen of shatta, Shannon, Kryssy, Jahlys, Shaydee, Nken and Meryl. Her track Tic, in 2020, went viral on TikTok, and her duet Laptop with Kalash, released on 29 April 2022, was certified single platinum on 5 September 2024.

Bouyon, fast Dominica

Bouyon was born in Roseau, Dominica, in the late 1980s, created by the band WCK. It mixes jing ping, cadence-lypso, lapo kabwit, bele, quadrille, chante mas, mazurka and zouk, with electronic keyboards, drum machines and a very fast synthetic bass. Tempo is its signature: a dry, fast, almost martial beat, made for the body and for sweating.

Both genres were born in the street, self-produced and self-distributed, and they grew up with social media. Bouyon is the older of the two, shatta the younger. They are not in direct competition: as researcher Maite Berchel-Bordin puts it, they will coexist durably, while remaining major drivers of Caribbean creativity.

The meeting point: shatta bouyon

The two styles meet in shatta bouyon, the modern bouyon of Antillean producers and the French scene, made for clubs and social media. DJ Chinwax signed Shatta in 2020, a fusion of dancehall, soca and modern bouyon. Naamix, Mikado and Yanishi released Barbe a papa in 2024, a festive shatta and bouyon production.

The bridge runs both ways. Shanika, a bouyon voice, laid Kay ve yo on Natoxie's Caralax Riddim, a project that gathers major shatta figures. 1T1 and THEOMAA mix bouyon, soca and dancehall on Bouwey. In Paris, the Shattaween party, on 31 October 2026 in Montreuil, programmed shatta, dancehall, bouyon, konpa and afrobeats on the same bill. For a DJ, shatta sits next to reggaeton and afrobeats, at around 100 BPM, and bouyon is the acceleration that follows.

Frequently asked questions

What is the tempo difference between shatta and bouyon?

Shatta runs at around 100 BPM, between 100 and 105 BPM according to DJ guides, in the line of dancehall at 90-110 BPM. Classic bouyon runs at around 152 BPM and hardcore bouyon rises to 160 BPM. Some DJ guides give a lower range for bouyon, 120 to 135 BPM, and tempo varies by track.

Where do shatta and bouyon come from?

Shatta was born in Martinique, in Volga-Plage in Fort-de-France, in the early 2010s, as a Martinican version of Jamaican dancehall, carried by PSK Shatta and Danthology. Bouyon was born in Roseau, Dominica, in the late 1980s, created by the band WCK, then popularised in Guadeloupe.

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the meeting of the two genres: Antillean producers work bouyon material, keyboards, fast tempo and Creole, over short, hard riddims inherited from shatta and dancehall, made for clubs and social media. Documented examples: Shatta by DJ Chinwax, Barbe a papa by Naamix, Mikado and Yanishi, and Kay ve yo by Shanika on Natoxie's Caralax Riddim.

Reo: the face of the new Dominican generation

By Smokiller · Published September 16, 2026

Reo is a Dominican bouyon artist, billed as the Prince of Bouyon. With Joli BB, a duet with Martinican singer Maurane Voyer, and a 2026 Caribbean Music Awards nomination, he embodies the new Dominican generation building an international career with the French Antilles, while keeping Dominica in view.

Who Reo is: a stage name, a writer name

Reo is the artist name of a bouyon singer from Dominica. His real name, Dario Yankey, appears in the songwriting credits of his tracks. That double identity is not a footnote: it is a reminder that behind the stage name circulating on posters and social media, there is a writer, credited as such on his songs. On this site, credits matter as much as choruses, because bouyon has too often travelled without them.

Reo is billed as the Prince of Bouyon. As with the other crowns of the genre, that title is attributed: it is the billing used by 13th Street Promotions. Presenting it this way, with its attribution, says two things at once. On one hand, a promotional machine from the scene chose that royal title to position him, which signals ambition and a claimed status in the genre succession. On the other hand, the title remains subjective: this site reports it, it does not award it.

The contrast with the previous generation helps to place Reo. Where Asa Bantan is widely called the King of Bouyon from Grand Bay, Reo arrives with the prince label and a path already turned outward: collaborations with the French Antilles, Caribbean nominations, and the big Roseau festival stage. The royal vocabulary belongs to the scene itself, and it draws a line of succession: Dominican bouyon has its established figures and its named heir generation.

Our dossier presents Reo as the face of the new Dominican generation. Face means he concentrates what defines that generation: working from Dominica, collaborating with Martinique and Guadeloupe, aiming for the stages and awards of the whole Caribbean, and existing on the platforms where bouyon lives its second life. The following sections follow that path through his verified tracks and dates.

Joli BB: the collaboration that launched him in public

The track that installed Reo with this site’s audience is Joli BB, with Martinican singer Maurane Voyer. The track was released on October 9, 2026 on the Mauve label and runs 2 minutes 43. It was written by Maurane Voyer, Dario Yankey and Nicolas Lanclume. Those credits, published and verifiable, are repeated here exactly: two artists from two islands, Dominica and Martinique, and a third writer on the record.

The structure of the track tells the era. A Dominica and Martinique collaboration is no longer an exceptional meeting organised for a festival: it is a commercial release, on a label, with shared writing credits. Bouyon has always circulated between islands, since the days when it arrived in Guadeloupe and Martinique as jump up after its creation by WCK. Joli BB shows that circulation turned into a working method for the young generation.

Bouyon Shattaz published Joli BB on the very day of its release. That editorial choice is part of Reo’s dossier on this site: the media follows the artist in real time, at the moment tracks come out, and not only once they have become obvious. For an artist building internationally, that responsiveness matters: it documents the trajectory as it happens, with the dates, labels and credits of the day.

Joli BB is also an ideal entry point for a French-speaking listener. The Martinican voice of Maurane Voyer meets the Dominican bouyon of Reo, in a short, direct format. Find the track in our bouyon playlist, among the other releases that draw the sound of 2026.

La Vie Doux, Under Pressure, Still Love Me: the recent catalogue

Around Joli BB, Reo’s recent catalogue includes La Vie Doux, Under Pressure and Still Love Me. Those three tracks, cited in our dossier as his recent tracks, show an artist in regular production, not the singer of a single viral clip. For a scene like bouyon, where many careers are played out over carnival seasons, that regularity of release is an important professional signal.

La Vie Doux holds a particular place in that catalogue. The track, performed with Tehilah, was nominated for Bouyon Song of the Year at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards. A song nomination, distinct from the artist nomination discussed below, means that Reo’s writing and performance work was selected, track by track, by a Caribbean institution. For a reader discovering the artist, La Vie Doux is therefore priority listening: it is the track the awards singled out.

Under Pressure and Still Love Me complete the portrait. Without inventing descriptions our dossier does not contain, this profile presents them for what they are in Reo’s documented path: recent tracks that feed his stages, his playlists and his presence with Dominican and Antillean audiences. They appear, with Joli BB and La Vie Doux, in the structured data of this page, as official listening landmarks for his profile.

Taken together, those four tracks draw a readable strategy: collaborations that open audiences, with Maurane Voyer and with Tehilah, and tracks under his own name that install an identity. That is the working method of the new Dominican generation, and Reo is one of its clearest illustrations in 2026.

The 2026 Caribbean Music Awards: Caribbean recognition

In 2026, Reo was nominated at the Caribbean Music Awards in the Bouyon Artist of the Year category. The very name of the category deserves attention: at a Caribbean ceremony of that scale, there is a bouyon category. The genre born in Roseau in 1988 with WCK now has its own regional awards, and Reo is among the artists selected to represent the bouyon year.

To that artist nomination is added that of La Vie Doux, with Tehilah, for Bouyon Song of the Year at the same awards. Being nominated twice, as artist and as song, in the same edition strengthens the signal: this is not a symbolic presence, but a year recognised in both of its dimensions, the performer and the track.

This profile reports nominations, and only nominations, because that is what our dossier verifies. It does not turn a nomination into a win and adds no undocumented result. That precision is part of this site’s contract with its readers: published facts are those of the dossiers, attributed when they must be, and dated when they can be.

For Dominica, those nominations carry a collective value. Every Dominican artist nominated in a bouyon category recalls, in front of the whole Caribbean, which island the genre comes from. Reo carries that reminder with a prince billing and French collaborations: a very 2026 way of defending the origin, through visibility rather than speeches.

WCMF 2026 and what follows: building from Dominica

According to published World Creole Music Festival 2026 guides, Reo plays Night 2, Saturday October 24, 2026. He shares that night, according to those same guides, with Asa Bantan. The WCMF stage, in Roseau, is Dominica’s greatest Creole showcase: playing there places Reo at the centre of his island, on the same bill as the established figures of the genre, in front of the audience that watched him grow.

That date sums up the balance our dossier attributes to Reo: building an international career with French Antilles collaborations, while keeping Dominica in view. International, in his case, does not mean leaving and disappearing from the local landscape. It means circulating, collaborating, being nominated, then coming back to play the big night in Roseau. That back-and-forth is what makes the new bouyon careers solid.

Bouyon Shattaz follows Reo as the face of that new generation. The media published Joli BB on its release day and continues to document his tracks, nominations and stages on this site and in the bouyon playlist. For French-speaking readers, Reo is one of the shortest paths to Dominican bouyon today: a prince billed by his scene, short collaborative tracks, and an island that stays at the centre of the story.

Why Reo matters, in short: because he proves Dominican bouyon can produce a new generation that speaks to the French Antilles and the whole Caribbean without renouncing its address. Start with Joli BB, continue with La Vie Doux, then look at Night 2 of WCMF 2026: in three stops, you will have the complete portrait of the generation arriving.

Listening to Reo: where to start

To discover Reo, the simplest order follows the order of this profile. Start with Joli BB, the meeting track with Maurane Voyer, because it contains the artist’s whole project in 2 minutes 43: Dominica and Martinique in the same track, shared writing credits, a label, Mauve, and a release that Bouyon Shattaz documented on the very day, October 9, 2026. A listener who has never heard Reo hears immediately what the new generation does differently there: it records from the outset at the scale of the French Antilles, without waiting for local consecration to collaborate.

Continue with La Vie Doux, with Tehilah, the track nominated for Bouyon Song of the Year at the 2026 Caribbean Music Awards. Hearing it after Joli BB lets you compare two collaborations and understand why the Caribbean awards selected this one. Then add Under Pressure and Still Love Me, the other recent tracks in the dossier, to hear Reo outside the duet format and measure the regularity of his current production. Those four tracks form the official listening path of this page: they are repeated in its structured data and in our bouyon playlist.

Finish the path with context. Re-read the genre journey, from WCK to the current branches, on our history of bouyon page, then look at where Reo stands on Night 2 of the World Creole Music Festival 2026, Saturday October 24, 2026, alongside Asa Bantan. That context is not a decorative extra: it says exactly what Bouyon Shattaz defends with Reo, a Dominican new generation documented track by track, credit by credit, stage by stage, so that his international career grows with his island rather than beside it. That is the standard this site will keep applying to every new Reo release, nomination and stage it documents from here on.

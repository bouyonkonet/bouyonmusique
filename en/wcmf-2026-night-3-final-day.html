Dominica bouyon music: WCMF 2026 night 3 plays tonight

By Smokiller · Published October 25, 2026

The World Creole Music Festival 2026 closes this Sunday 25 October at Windsor Park, Roseau, Dominica. After night 1 on Friday 23 and night 2 on Saturday 24, night 3 mixes soca, amapiano, reggae, soukous and the names of the Dominican scene. This page sets out the verified programme of the last night, with no invented set times.

The night 3 programme, source by source

The guides opened for this page give the same list for Sunday 25 October: Machel Montano, Uncle Waffles, Fridayy, Maxi Priest, Diblo Dibala, Les Aiglons, Jean Marc Ferdinand, Shelly and The Signal Band, Gwada G, Unstoppable Shax, Nice and DJ Taffy.

Sweet TNT Magazine and Writeups 24 publish this list in the same terms, and the Dominica Weekly guide confirms it in its festival page. None of the opened guides publishes an official running order: this page therefore presents no set time per artist.

A closing night, four continents

Night 3 is the widest night of the festival: Machel Montano carries the soca of Trinidad, Uncle Waffles the amapiano of South Africa, Fridayy the Haitian-American R&B, Maxi Priest the British reggae, and Diblo Dibala the soukous guitar of Congo.

That geography is what gives the festival its name: World Creole Music Festival, three nights in Roseau during Dominica independence season, under the theme Pulsating Rhythms for this 26th edition, per the Writeups 24 coverage.

The Dominican share of the last night

Dominica is not only the host: Shelly and The Signal Band, Gwada G, Unstoppable Shax, Nice and DJ Taffy carry the sounds of the island into the closing night, alongside the international headliners.

Bouyon, born in Dominica, remains the thread of these three nights: the profiles of these artists and the guides to nights 1 and 2 are on this site, under the same verification rules, names concordant between sources only.

And after night 3

When night 3 closes, the 2026 festival will be complete: three nights, from 23 to 25 October, at Windsor Park. The review on this page will come after the night, from published reports, and not before.

Until then, the useful reading is the full festival guide and the pages of the three nights, already online on this site. The Creole season itself continues with Jounen Kweyol and the independence celebrations.

Wrecketeng: the pleasure branch of bouyon

By Smokiller · Published September 21, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Wrecketeng is one of the forms derived from bouyon in Dominica, alongside bouyon soca and bouyon-muffin. Less known outside the Caribbean than classic bouyon, it belongs to the same creative explosion that followed the creation of the genre by WCK in 1988. Its official spelling, Wrecketeng, comes from DJ Cut himself, the founding member who pioneered the style: the 'reketeng' spelling spread across the internet is a misspelling that became common.

In the bouyon fusion family

After 1988, bouyon does not stay frozen: Dominican musicians test fusions in every direction. Some will produce bouyon soca, others bouyon-muffin, others still Wrecketeng. Each branch keeps the foundation of the genre: traditional Dominican rhythms, keyboards and carnival energy.

Why these branches matter

Branches like Wrecketeng show that bouyon is an ecosystem, not a single sound. It is this diversity, born in Dominica, that allows the genre today to live in Guadeloupe, in Martinique, in France and across the whole diaspora.

Frequently asked questions

What is bouyon music?

It is the Dominican music genre that Wrecketeng grew out of: born in the late 1980s, pioneered by the band WCK in Roseau in 1988. Its name comes from the Kwéyòl word for soup, because it blends jing ping, cadence-lypso, calypso and electronic production. After 1988, Dominican musicians spun it into fusions: bouyon soca, bouyon-muffin, Wrecketeng.

What is Wrecketeng?

Wrecketeng is a branch of bouyon born in Dominica in the wake of bouyon-muffin. The line runs through DJ Cut, with Bucktown Sounds and the Bushtown Clan, then Nursery Krew Inc. in the early 2000s, with Smokiller among its members. Its method: take existing recordings, strip them down and rebuild them over bouyon instrumentals.

Reketeng or Wrecketeng: which spelling is correct?

Wrecketeng. According to Smokiller, founder of Bouyon Shattaz and a participant in the Nursery Krew era, this official spelling comes from DJ Cut himself, the pioneer of the style. The spelling “reketeng”, very widespread online and repeated by publications up to Billboard, is a misspelling that became common. This site writes Wrecketeng everywhere. See the Wrecketeng chapter.

How is Wrecketeng linked to RnBouyon?

For Bouyon Shattaz, the 2020s labels, New Bouyon and RnBouyon alike, are the heirs of Wrecketeng: they reuse its method, taking existing songs, hip hop, trap or R&B, and rebuilding them on the bouyon machinery. Wikipedia, in English and in French, makes the same reading.

Who created Wrecketeng?

DJ Cut, a Dominican DJ and producer attached to Bucktown Sounds, is recognized among the early pioneers of the style. His song “Party”, with Joanne, inspired bouyon-muffin artists like the Bushtown Clan, who popularized Wrecketeng in Dominica. Nursery Krew Inc. then pushed it forward in the early 2000s with original recordings. See the DJ Cut page.

How is a Wrecketeng track made?

You start from an existing recording, often hip hop or dancehall, and remove its rhythm section. You then bring forward the bouyon drums and bass, and lay the original vocal, or other melodic elements, over this new fast base. The stripped track is sometimes called a riddim. Like dub, the style lives mostly on instrumental remixes.

When did Wrecketeng appear?

In the late 1990s, in Dominica, in the wake of bouyon-muffin. After Skinny Banton's collaborations with WCK from 1995, the song “Party” by Joanne and DJ Cut opened the way, then the Bushtown Clan popularized the style. In the early 2000s, Nursery Krew Inc. extended it with original recordings.

What is Nursery Krew Inc.?

A Dominican crew of the early 2000s, short NKI, that moved Wrecketeng from DJ remixes to original recordings. Wikipedia associates Burn Brain Jolly, Charlie Black, Watty and Smokiller with it, and cites the recordings “Peanuts”, “Sorti Desandre” and the NKI Medley. According to his biography, producer Dada launched the group and produced its songs. See the Nursery Krew Inc page.

Is Wrecketeng making a comeback?

Yes. On December 3, 2025, Dominica News Online announced a collaborative album produced by Smokiller to highlight the origins of Wrecketeng. The announced contributors include DJ Cut, Klass Money, Charlie Black, Keith Goddard, O Banga and Mopo Neck. The project also acknowledges the influence of Burn Brain Jolly and Nursery Krew Inc.: the style is being revived, not just remembered.

What is the difference between Wrecketeng and bouyon-muffin?

Bouyon-muffin is a vocal style: singers lay a ragga delivery over bouyon rhythms. Wrecketeng is a style of DJs and producers: it takes existing recordings, strips them down and rebuilds them over bouyon instrumentals. The second was born in the wake of the first, and bouyon-muffin artists, like the Bushtown Clan, helped establish it.

What is the link between Smokiller and Wrecketeng?

Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer, is one of the names Wikipedia associates with Nursery Krew Inc., the crew that pushed Wrecketeng forward in the early 2000s. He works explicitly in the style: his remix method dresses familiar melodies and voices in bouyon tempo. He is also the producer of the collaborative album on the origins of Wrecketeng announced in December 2025.

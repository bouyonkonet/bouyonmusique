RnBouyon: the R&B side of bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

RnBouyon is the name given to the fusion of bouyon and R&B: Dominica's party rhythm carried by sung voices, soft melodies and urban production. The term is recent and its spelling is precise: RnBouyon, one word. This page explains where it comes from, who carries it, and why Bouyon Shattaz reads it as an heir of Wrecketeng.

Spelling first: RnBouyon, and no other way

In the sources that matter, the term is spelled one single way: RnBouyon. That is Deezer's spelling, used in February 2026 to present singer Fallon in its Deezer Next 2026 programme: a fusion of R&B and bouyon called 'RnBouyon', balancing softness and energy. It is also the spelling in the French Wikipedia list of bouyon derived genres, and in the 'RnBouyon Instrumental' chapter of Smokiller's Bouyon Music 2026 mix for Bouyon Shattaz.

The variants R&Bouyon, Rn'Bouyon or RNBouyon are attested nowhere in the sources consulted. One type-beat seller separately uses 'RnB Bouyon' as a YouTube beat category: a producer and SEO usage, not the attested name of the style. This site therefore keeps RnBouyon, the spelling of the artists and the platforms.

The sound: R&B softness on the bouyon engine

RnBouyon keeps the bouyon engine, its programmed percussion and electronic bass, but changes the vocal war: the chanted carnival delivery gives way to melodic singing, close to contemporary R&B and urban pop. Deezer sums the formula up as a balance of softness and energy, and presents Fallon's work as a blend of her Caribbean heritage with a modern twist.

That position makes RnBouyon the most radio-friendly and most sung side of current bouyon. Where party bouyon hunts the hook a whole crowd chants back, RnBouyon hunts the melody that stays after the party. Both sides share the same machines and the same island of origin: the treatment of voice and melody is what separates them.

Fallon and Smokiller: the two documented names

The name attached to the term is Fallon, the artist spotlighted by the Deezer Next 2026 programme, with two tracks Deezer names as already showing her identity: 'Normal' and 'Memories'. Public sources give neither her exact island of origin nor the tracks' release years: this site will not invent them, and her coverage will grow when solid sources appear.

On the Dominica side, Smokiller uses the term as a production label: an 'RnBouyon Instrumental' chapter appears in his Bouyon Music 2026 Mix Vol. 1 for Bouyon Shattaz, and his smokiller.com hub files RnBouyon among his instrumentals, alongside his flips of American R&B records such as Bryson Tiller's. For him, RnBouyon is not a slogan: it is the name of a method, applying the bouyon machinery to R&B songs.

An heir of Wrecketeng: this site's position

Bouyon Shattaz states it plainly: RnBouyon is an heir of Wrecketeng. The lineage is technical before it is marketing. Rekenteng, born in Dominica in bouyon-muffin's wake, installed the method: take existing recordings, dancehall, hip hop, later R&B, strip them down and rebuild them over bouyon instrumentals, with sampled melodies, programmed percussion and electronic bass. Wikipedia, in both its English and French versions, reads it the same way: the 2020s labels reuse the techniques of Wrecketeng recordings.

RnBouyon applies exactly that method to today's R&B repertoire. That the term is young and carried by few artists does not change the genealogy: the production gestures come from Roseau and the Wrecketeng years, not from a marketing office. Defending that credit is why this site documents RnBouyon now, with its sources, instead of leaving it to be narrated elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

How is RnBouyon spelled?

RnBouyon is written as one word. It is the spelling used by Deezer for singer Fallon (Deezer Next 2026), by the list of bouyon-derived genres on French Wikipedia and by Smokiller in his mixes. The forms R&Bouyon, Rn'Bouyon or RNBouyon are attested in no source consulted.

What is RnBouyon?

RnBouyon is the fusion of Dominica's bouyon and R&B: bouyon's programmed percussion and electronic bass, with melodic singing close to contemporary R&B and urban pop. Deezer describes it as a balance of softness and energy. It is the most sung and most radio-friendly side of today's bouyon.

Who makes RnBouyon?

Two names are documented: singer Fallon, presented by Deezer Next 2026 with the tracks “Normal” and “Memories”, and producer Smokiller, born in Dominica, who uses RnBouyon as the label for his instrumentals and R&B flips. This site adds no other names until sources establish them.

Who is Fallon, the RnBouyon singer?

Fallon is the artist Deezer credits with RnBouyon in its Deezer Next 2026 programme, announced alongside Timar and Heroe. Deezer writes that she calls her world RnBouyon and cites “Normal” and “Memories” as the tracks where her writing already shows. Deezer speaks of her Caribbean heritage without naming an island. See Fallon.

What is the difference between RnBouyon and bouyon?

The difference lies in the voice and the melody, not the machines. Party bouyon looks for the chanted hook a whole carnival crowd sings back; RnBouyon looks for the sung melody that stays after the party, close to contemporary R&B. Both share the same programmed percussion, the same bass and the same island of origin.

Does RnBouyon come from Wrecketeng?

Yes, according to Bouyon Shattaz: RnBouyon is an heir of Wrecketeng. Wrecketeng, born in Dominica in the wake of bouyon-muffin, established the method of taking existing recordings and rebuilding them on bouyon instrumentals. Wikipedia, in English and French, reads it the same way: 2020s labels reuse those techniques.

Wrecketeng or reketeng: which spelling is correct?

The official spelling is Wrecketeng. It comes from DJ Cut himself, the founding pioneer of the style, according to Smokiller. The spelling “reketeng”, widespread online and repeated even by Billboard, is a mistake that became common. This site writes Wrecketeng everywhere, including for the historical practice. See Wrecketeng.

Is RnBouyon the same as New Bouyon?

No, but they are two young labels, born in the 2020s, that share the same technical DNA inherited from Wrecketeng. New Bouyon has three dated uses, from TiiTii NBA's 2023 EP to Ricky Bishop's 2024 concept and a generic use. RnBouyon specifically names the fusion with R&B singing. See New Bouyon.

Where can you listen to RnBouyon?

Start with Fallon's “Normal” and “Memories”, the two tracks cited by Deezer. On the production side, Smokiller's Bouyon Music 2026 Vol. 1 mix for Bouyon Shattaz contains a “RnBouyon Instrumental” chapter, and his smokiller.com hub files RnBouyon among his instrumentals, next to his flips of American R&B tracks such as Bryson Tiller's.

Is RnBouyon a new genre?

The term is recent, but the method is not. Its production moves, sampled melodies, programmed percussion and electronic bass applied to existing songs, come from Roseau and the Wrecketeng years. That the word is young and carried by few artists does not change that lineage, which this site documents so the credit stays Dominican.

Tasmo: the name the archives have not caught up with yet

Published October 20, 2026

Tasmo is an artist name associated with bouyon, and this profile is, of all the profiles on this site, the one that states its limit the earliest: after a full search, in French and in English, across the general web, the streaming platforms, the specialist press and the artist databases, no checkable source documents this singer. No published origin, no full name, not one confirmed record, no label, no official account found. This profile fills none of those gaps with invention. It does three useful things instead: it establishes what was searched and how, it takes apart the two documented namesakes the search engines return in place of Tasmo, a South African rapper and a German DJ, and it places the name inside the one verified frame that exists, bouyon itself, until the day a source allows the artist himself to be told.

Who Tasmo is: a name on the bouyon list, almost no archive

Let us start with the bare fact, because it structures this whole profile. The name Tasmo travels as a bouyon artist name, to the point of holding a place in the lists of the genre. But where even an emerging bouyon artist ordinarily leaves at least one trace, a single on a platform, a video, a fete poster, a mention in a DJ mix, the research carried out for this profile found no checkable trace of that kind. The queries combined the name with bouyon, soca, Dominica, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Dennery Segment, and with the names of the major platforms, Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, Qobuz, Shazam, YouTube, SoundCloud, Audiomack, Viberate and MusicBrainz. The result, repeated from one query to the next: nothing that designates a bouyon singer named Tasmo.

That emptiness is itself information, and this site publishes it as such. Bouyon is a music of the stage and of social media before it is a music of archives: many of its singers live in fetes, in short video clips and in records that travel from phone to phone, sometimes without ever passing through a clean artist page. A name can therefore exist in the mouth of the public and in the lists of the genre long before it exists in a database. Tasmo seems to be exactly in that case: a name known to the scene, or at least listed as such, that public documentation has not caught up with yet.

The editorial consequence is simple, and this site stands by it everywhere: this profile will give no origin, no age, no town, no label and no song title for Tasmo, because no opened source gives them. Any portrait that gave them, at this stage, would be inventing them. In their place, the pages that follow document the only two Tasmos the archives do know, so that the reader does not confuse them with the bouyon singer, then recall the verified frame of the genre in which this name sits.

The first namesake: Tasmo B M, the South African rapper

The first documented artist the search engines return under the name Tasmo has nothing to do with bouyon, and it must be said plainly to cut the confusion short. On the Music in Africa platform, a full page presents Tasmo B M, whose published real name is Semoshile Moses Daliwe, a South African artist, composer and songwriter born in Kroonstad, in the Free State. His published path is that of a former hotelier: choir at high school and at university, a degree in Hospitality Management in Bloemfontein, a career in Johannesburg, then lyrics written during work breaks and beats boxed by mouth, recorded on a mobile phone.

The same page gives his starting discography: a first professional song recorded in 2016 with a well known producer in Kempton Park, where he lived and worked, a video for his single “Ere Ke Di Bone”, then, in 2018, the founding of his own label, Black Maipatile Entertainment, with his close friend Bonolo Matlou. Under that label, the two released songs including “Tune In”, “Ma Naija” and “Ere Ke Di Bone”. His single “I Made it To A Day”, produced by himself during lockdown, was released on 26 March 2021, with a live performance video shot at HTUM studios in Kempton Park. The page then shows him working on a debut EP with developing artists and producers.

Everything in that record is coherent, dated and South African: hip hop and rap are the stated genres, the places are Kroonstad, Bloemfontein, Johannesburg and Kempton Park, and no source, neither that page nor any other opened for this profile, links this artist to bouyon, to the Caribbean or to any Creole scene. If a search engine or another site one day presents the bouyon Tasmo with the biography of Tasmo B M, it will be a namesake error, and this profile will have flagged it in advance. The two artists share a short stage name. Nothing else is established.

The second namesake: DJ Tasmo of the drum and bass sets

The second documented Tasmo leads even further from bouyon. The mix pages published under this name, notably on the Castbox platform, present a DJ linked to the tasmo.rocks site, the author of drum and bass sets and chillout sets recorded for the Remote Chaos Communication Congress, a gathering of the German hacker scene, with thanks to the crews of the Verstehbahnhof in Fürstenberg and of the xHain hackerspace. The published tracklists of his sets line up names of British and European drum and bass, from Metrik to Culture Shock, and a visual project shared with VJ Generation Gästeliste under the name Generation Unterstrich.

Once again, the record is real, but it is not the record of this profile. None of the opened pages mentions bouyon, soca, the Caribbean or a Creole audience. This DJ Tasmo is an actor of the German electronic scene of congresses and hackerspaces. Confusing him with a bouyon singer would be the same error as with Tasmo B M, in a different setting: Berlin and Fürstenberg instead of Kroonstad and Kempton Park.

Those two namesakes also explain part of the silence around the bouyon Tasmo. When a stage name is already occupied, in the databases, by a South African rapper and by a German DJ, the Caribbean singer who carries it disappears from the results: the engines return the best documented records, and his own, if it exists in the form of poorly indexed videos or tracks, stays under the surface. It is a classic problem of Creole scenes, where short, ringing names repeat from one island and one continent to another. This profile names it so the reader understands why the search was long, and why its negative result must be read precisely: nothing verified was found, which does not mean the artist does not exist.

Official accounts: no verified profile was confirmed, so this page links to no social account.

Each of those gaps has a practical consequence for the reader. Without a verified record, it is impossible to recommend a precise listen under this name without risking pointing to a namesake. Without a verified origin, it is impossible to file the artist in the territory pages of this site, neither among the artists of Dominica, nor among those of Guadeloupe or Martinique. Without a label, it is impossible to follow the production line, which is often, in bouyon, the best identification key for an emerging singer. This profile prefers to display those gaps rather than fill them with the most probable answers, because in a scene where names resemble one another, the most probable answer is often the false one.

It must also be said what this result is not. It is not a judgement on the artist: very real singers, very active in fetes, can leave almost no indexed trace, especially when their name is saturated by namesakes on other continents. Nor is it a final result: the platforms fill up every week, and one properly distributed single can suddenly bring out the page that is missing today. It is a state of the record on the publication date of this profile, 20 October 2026, established by a search whose method is described here itself, so that it can be redone and contradicted.

The verified frame: the bouyon this name belongs to

If the artist still escapes the archives, the genre is documented, and it is inside that verified frame that the name Tasmo takes its meaning. Bouyon was born in Dominica, carried by the bands of the island, from WCK to the carnival formations, before conquering Guadeloupe, Martinique and the diaspora. Our history of bouyon page retraces its full path. Its branches are established as well: bouyon gwada, born of the collaborations between Dominica and Guadeloupe, bouyon soca and the dancehall mixed forms, whose reference spelling, Wrecketeng, comes from DJ Cut himself, the founding pioneer of the style.

One fact of origin deserves to be recalled here, because artist lists often lose it. According to the first-hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer who was present in the rooms with Dada and the Nursery Krew, bouyon soca was coined and started by Dada in Dominica in 2003, then became popular when Triple Kay and other bands adopted it in their own version of bouyon. Every singer listed in the genre, Tasmo included, sits inside that Dominican lineage, even when his own personal origin remains, as here, undocumented. The genre has an address, even when the artist does not have one in the archives yet.

That frame also gives this site its method for emerging profiles. A name enters the lists first, through the scene and through the public. Then, when distribution follows, come the platform pages, the credits, the dates, the labels. The profiles on this site follow that rise in documentation step by step: for established artists, they tell whole careers; for a name like Tasmo, they fix the state of the research, take the possible confusions apart and hold the page ready for the day the facts arrive. An empty but clean record is worth more, for the memory of the genre, than a full and false one.

And if a record by Tasmo one day appears there in a checkable form, this profile will be updated the same day.

One last thing, and it fits in one sentence: if you hold a checkable piece of information on this artist, an official platform link, a published credit, an article, that is what this record is missing, not one more opinion. The archives of bouyon are built that way, piece by piece, and the least documented names are often the ones the scene knows best. Tasmo is waiting for his first piece. This profile is waiting with him, file open.

SMS Girls: Desann, Tu te frottes and Wet Wet, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

The SMS Girls are a female bouyon duo that promoters bill as a rising act on the Caribbean scene. Their tracks named by organisers, “Desann”, “Tu te frottes” and “Wet Wet”, are dancefloor records, and their autumn 2026 says the rest: a first show in Switzerland, on Saturday October 24, 2026, at Uptown Geneva, then a showcase billed as a first in Martinique, on Saturday November 7, 2026. This profile is deliberately honest about its record: two verified event pages and one release listing stand in for a biography, because no published source consulted gives the names of the two singers, their home island, or their label. What is documented is told. What is not is written as a blank.

Who the SMS Girls are: a duo, three named tracks, almost no archive

The SMS Girls are a duo. That is the first fact in the record, and it comes from the organisers themselves: the page for the Comportement Tropical x Wah Gwaan party in Geneva presents them as the rising female duo making the Caribbean scene vibrate with overflowing energy. The page for their Martinique showcase, Le Chaudron, announces them in capitals, SMS GIRLS, for an exclusive showcase. Two independent sources, an island and a continent apart, one same positioning: a duo of bouyon singers flown in specially for a party.

What the record does not hold is just as clear, and this profile says it at the start rather than hiding it in the conclusion. None of the sources consulted publishes the names of the two members of the duo. None gives their home island or town. None names a label, a regular producer or a formation date. There is, at the time of this research, no published interview, no official biography page and no artist listing on the major platforms that this profile could open and verify. The duo publicly exists through its tracks and its stage dates, and this profile is built exactly on those two pillars.

That situation is not exceptional in present day bouyon, and it says something about the genre. Part of the new scene lives first in parties: a track turns in DJ sets, a Creole hook becomes a slogan the crowd sings back, organisers in the Antilles and in the European diaspora book the showcase, and the documentation follows later, sometimes months later. The SMS Girls are documented here at that precise stage of their path, the one where the posters exist before the biographies. Our women of bouyon coverage places this duo in the landscape of female voices in the genre, long dominated by bands and male singers, where duos of women remain rare.

One point of method before going further: the name of the duo is written SMS Girls in the event sources, and this profile keeps that spelling. No source consulted explains what the letters SMS stand for in this name, and this profile offers no invented expansion of the acronym. The only published text that plays with those letters is the Creole slogan of the Martinique party, quoted in the Martinique section. A stage name does not need to be decoded to be documented: it needs to be spelled correctly, and tied to the tracks and dates that carry it.

The tracks: Desann, Tu te frottes, Wet Wet, and Attrape ton copain

Three tracks are named by the Geneva party page, and they form the verified core of the public repertoire of the duo: “Desann”, “Tu te frottes” and “Wet Wet”. The organiser wording is a dancefloor verdict: these tracks are hits that set dancefloors alight. This profile reports that presentation for what it is, the language of a promoter selling his party, but the underlying fact can be checked another way: these three tracks, and no others, are what a Swiss organiser chose to present the duo to an audience that does not know it yet. For an emerging act, the selection of a foreign promoter is a form of public discography.

The titles themselves tell the language of the duo. “Desann” is a Creole word, the spoken form of descendre, to go down, and it is also the word the Martinique slogan pushes forward, written “DÉSSANN’”, like an order thrown at the crowd. “Tu te frottes” is in direct French, with no detour. “Wet Wet” is in dancefloor English, doubled the way a hook has to be. Creole, French, English: the three languages of the titles map exactly onto the circulation triangle of present day bouyon and shatta, between the French Antilles, the English speaking Caribbean and diaspora parties in Europe. This profile does not know the lyrics of these tracks and therefore quotes no line from them, but their titles alone are enough to place the ground of the duo: party bouyon, upfront, built to dance.

A fourth track appears in a 2026 release list drawn from the Beatport catalogue: “Attrape ton copain”, credited to Thesmsgirls, with a length of 1:56. This profile cites it with its exact level of proof: a release list, consulted during this research, and not an artist page opened on the platform itself. The track is therefore kept in the structured data of this page as a track published under the name of the duo, and it is flagged here as the least solidly documented of the four. Its short length, under two minutes, matches the format of recent bouyon and shatta tracks, built for social feeds and fast sets. If an official listing confirms it with a date and credits, this profile will be completed.

No release date is published, in the sources consulted, for “Desann”, “Tu te frottes” or “Wet Wet”, and no producer name is attached to them. This profile therefore prints neither a date nor a production credit for these tracks. That is a real limit of the record: whoever wrote a dated and credited SMS Girls discography from the public sources available would be writing, in part, fiction. Here, the four tracks are given as the named repertoire of the duo, with the proof status of each, and listening is sent to our bouyon playlist, where new releases on the scene are followed track by track.

Geneva, October 24, 2026: a first show in Switzerland

The first documented date of the duo is European. On Saturday October 24, 2026, the SMS Girls perform at Uptown Geneva, an events complex in the city centre, behind Cornavin station, as part of the Comportement Tropical x Wah Gwaan party. The event page stresses a point this profile keeps as a career fact: it will be their first time in Switzerland, for an explosive bouyon show. A first date in a country, announced as such by the organiser, is a concrete marker in the life of an emerging act: it means the reputation of the duo travelled before it did, through the tracks and through DJ networks, to a French speaking Swiss stage that programmes Caribbean musics.

The party itself places the duo in its ecosystem. Comportement Tropical x Wah Gwaan is billed as a big shatta, bouyon, dancehall and afro party for the end of the year in French speaking Switzerland. The SMS Girls are on it in showcase, with DJ Luchshyi as international guest, a DJ and producer from Guadeloupe known on this site for his work with the new bouyon scene. The rest of the bill lines up DJs from the Swiss Caribbean and afro scene, from GC Sound, active since 2008 and behind Geneva Carnaval, to Spice&Curls and DJ Herzo, creator of the Comportement Tropical concept. The duo is therefore not booked at random: it is the sung headline act of a party built by the carriers of bouyon in Switzerland.

Geneva is not a random address for bouyon. French speaking Switzerland holds a scene of Antillean parties fed by the diaspora, and its promoters bring in acts from the French Antilles at the moment their tracks heat up. That a duo with no published biography is announced there for a Swiss first, with its three tracks named on the sales page, confirms the mode of circulation described above: in the bouyon of 2026, the stage often comes before the press pack. To place that step in the geography of the genre, our pages on bouyon in Guadeloupe and bouyon in Martinique tell the story of the two islands whose DJs and audiences keep these tracks turning.

Martinique, November 7, 2026: Le Chaudron, a showcase billed as a first

Two weeks after Geneva, the duo is announced in Martinique. On Saturday November 7, 2026, from 8 pm, the SMS Girls are in exclusive showcase at the event Le Chaudron, organised by Carrib’Events and K-Event in a secret venue in the centre sector of Martinique, with the published postcode 972. The Bizouk ticketing page opens with the same formula as Geneva, transposed: for the first time in Martinique, SMS GIRLS land in an exclusive showcase. Two firsts, Swiss and Martinican, fifteen days apart: the autumn 2026 of the SMS Girls is a tour of firsts, which is exactly the signature of a rising act, to borrow the Geneva organiser word.

The Martinique page brings one element Geneva did not give: the voice of the audience being addressed. Its slogan, in Creole, quotes what looks like the hook of the duo: “SMS OU JA SAV SÉ YIN KI SEX, DÉSSANN’!”, which reads, in approximate English, as: you already know what it is, get down. This profile quotes that slogan as the published text of the organiser, without claiming to reproduce the exact lyrics of a track: it shows in any case that “Desann” already works as a rallying word the poster can throw at the Martinique public on the assumption that it will recognise it. A hook that becomes a poster slogan is a hook that has done its work in parties and on social feeds.

The format of the party completes the picture: a large open air space turned into a cauldron, four DJs on the decks, DJ Natoxie, DJ Kenjox, DJ Jamjam and DJ Will’One, and no minors admitted, in the spirit of the big bouyon and shatta parties of Martinique. The duo is the only named singing attraction on the page, with further surprises announced. For an act whose public record holds in two event pages, being the single artist name on a full ticketing page, on the island where bouyon has been danced under the name jump up for decades, is the most telling fact in the record. Martinique does not fly in just anyone for an exclusive showcase on a November Saturday night.

Why the SMS Girls matter, and what this profile does not yet know

The SMS Girls matter, at their emerging scale, because they hold a place that is still rare in bouyon: that of a female dancefloor duo, booked as headline act at parties in Europe and in the Antilles on the strength of named tracks alone. The genre has its great female voices and its mixed bands, but duos of women carrying upfront bouyon tracks, in Creole and in French, remain few in the documented record. When such a duo chains a Swiss first and a Martinique first in the same autumn, documenting it early, accurately, is part of the work of this site, as it is for the other emerging acts of the present wave.

They also matter as a case of method, and this profile owns that. Their public record, at the date of this research, holds in two event pages and one line of a release catalogue. An inflated profile could have invented two first names, a home island, a label, listening figures and a meeting story. This profile does the opposite: it publishes the verified facts, it attributes every presentation to the organiser who wrote it, and it writes the blanks as blanks. Neither the members names, nor their origin, nor the meaning of the letters SMS, nor the release dates and producers of the tracks are published in the sources consulted, and none of those elements is therefore printed here as fact.

The logical sequel of this record is already dated: October 24, 2026, in Geneva, then November 7, 2026, in Martinique. If those stages produce reports, official videos or interviews, this profile will be completed, dated and corrected like every page on this site. In the meantime, listening starts with the three tracks the Geneva organiser named, “Desann”, “Tu te frottes” and “Wet Wet”, plus “Attrape ton copain” for catalogue hunters, and it continues in our bouyon playlist. The duo has the posters. It still has to receive, here as elsewhere, the biography its tracks deserve.

Smokiller: the hits of the world, the recipe of Dominica

Published September 15, 2026

Smokiller is a Dominican-born artist and producer, founder of Bouyon Shattaz. Dominican-born, he takes Afrobeats, pop, rap and R&B hits and flips them into bouyon versions, with the keyboards, tempo and energy of the genre born in Roseau.

Who Smokiller is: the man, the brand, the founder

Smokiller is a Dominican-born artist and producer. It is not a decorative pseudonym: it is the name under which he produces, remixes, publishes and signs his work, from YouTube to TikTok, from his site smokiller.com to the Bouyon Shattaz platform he founded. BouyonMusique.com, the site you are reading, is published by Bouyon Shattaz: Smokiller is therefore both an artist documented by this site and the man behind the house that publishes it, which this profile states openly.

His identity must be told in the right words. The wording used on this site is Dominican-born. His bloodline and culture never left him, and they are who he is. This site refers to him as Smokiller, following his own way of presenting himself in his articles and projects.

That identity explains his musical project. A producer born in Dominica who listens to hits from all over the world and hears, in each of them, a possible bouyon version: that is the starting point of Smokiller. It is not about imitating the scenes that succeed, but about bringing their tracks back to the recipe of his island, the one WCK created in Roseau in 1988.

Smokiller combines several trades that this profile details in turn: producer for other artists, remixer of international hits, curator of a free download catalogue on smokiller.com, founder of a media and of a distribution and rights project, and head of radio servicing to Caribbean stations. Few actors in current bouyon bring together so many functions. That is what makes this page the longest of the artist profiles on this site.

The Smokiller method: flipping a hit into bouyon

The method is simple to state and demanding to execute. Smokiller takes Afrobeats, pop, rap and R&B hits and flips them into bouyon versions, with bouyon keyboards, bouyon tempo and bouyon energy. Three elements therefore change in the flip: the instrumentation, carried by the keyboards that have signed the genre since its creation; the speed, set to the tempo of the Dominican fete; and the energy, that of a music built to make a crowd answer.

That work sits notably in the wrecketeng style. Wrecketeng is Dominica sampled-melody, rap-vocal style, one of the bouyon branches born from the spoken delivery of the origins. Our dedicated page on wrecketeng explains that lineage. Working in that style, for a remixer, means owning the sample as material and the voice as engine, two gestures at the heart of the Smokiller method.

A successful bouyon remix is not just about speeding a track up. If the audience recognises the original hit but dances to another machine, the flip has worked. The keyboards must carry the melody in the language of bouyon, the tempo must install the fete without breaking the track, and the energy must stay that of the genre: direct, festive, made for call and response. It is arrangement and production work, not a filter applied in a hurry.

The method also has a cultural function. Every world hit flipped into bouyon demonstrates, in practice, that the Dominican genre can welcome any melody of the moment. For audiences discovering bouyon through a track they already know, the remix is a doorway. For Dominica, every successful flip is a reminder of address: that energy has a name, bouyon, and an origin, Roseau.

Producer: Klass Money Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026)

Smokiller does not only remix: he also produces for other artists. He produced Klass Money’s track Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026). That production credit, distinct from his remix catalogue, shows the other face of his work: building a bouyon track for another artist’s voice, in the year of the genre that the title itself displays, Bouyon 2026.

The track appears in a Muse page short, which gives it an extra published trace beyond the usual platforms. For this site, that detail matters less than the credit itself: the name of Smokiller as producer of another artist track installs his signature in the 2026 bouyon catalogue, alongside his own releases.

Producing for others also changes a musician’s place in a scene. The remixer works on existing works; the producer takes part in the birth of new works under other artists’ names. Smokiller holds both positions, which strengthens his role in the Bouyon Shattaz ecosystem: the same ear that flips world hits produces the original tracks of the scene he documents.

Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026) stands at the head of the tracks listed in the structured data of this page, before the remixes. That order is not a popularity ranking: it simply recalls that the Smokiller dossier begins with an original production credit, and that the flips that follow are the work of a producer, not just a fan of quick edits.

The Coi Leray moment: Nothing Into Something and Players

The moment that moved Smokiller to another scale is his bouyon flip of Coi Leray Nothing Into Something. Posted on September 20, 2026, the flip reached 854.7K TikTok views, with 29.9K likes. Those figures come from screenshots shared by Smokiller himself on October 19, 2026. This profile reports them with that source and that date, as it reports every measurement: attributed, dated, without inflation.

To those numbers is added a gesture worth more than a counter: Coi Leray reposted the flip. When the original artist reposts the flip of her own track, the remix stops being a fan exercise and becomes a validated meeting between two scenes. For bouyon, a genre used to travelling without always being credited, that repost is an event: the Dominican version of an American hit is shown, by its very performer, to her own audience.

The story continued off social media. A Dominica News Online article about this flip was drafted in October 2026. The island press telling the success of a Dominican-born producer on a world platform: the loop of the Smokiller project can be read in that journey. The track of an American artist becomes a subject of Dominican media pride, because the flipping work itself is Dominican.

The Smokiller Coi Leray catalogue does not stop there. His remix of Players, another Coi Leray track, is published on his YouTube channel. The two tracks draw a sustained relationship with the repertoire of a single artist: a first flip that explodes on TikTok and draws a repost, a second that installs the work over time, on the long-form video platform. Readers can find those tracks via smokiller.com and this site bouyon playlist.

smokiller.com: the links hub and the free downloads

The centre of Smokiller’s direct distribution is smokiller.com, his links hub. The site offers free downloads that show the breadth of the catalogue: the Coi Leray Nothing Into Something remix in WAV, the Shenseea Vulnerability Bouyon Shattaz remix in WAV, and the Ayra Starr Heaven Baby Bouyon Shattaz remix in M4A. The formats matter: WAV is the format of DJs and radios, the format of serious work in the booth and in programming.

Alongside those three featured downloads, the hub also offers instrumentals and remix videos: Bryson Tiller with Outside, Loe Shimmy, Kalash x Kronix, RnBouyon and Shake It To The Max. That list, as it appears in our dossier, draws the perimeter of Smokiller’s listening: American rap and R&B, voices of the Caribbean and its diasporas, dance tracks of the moment. Everything that passes through his hands comes out with bouyon keyboards.

The choice of free download is a circulation strategy. A DJ who downloads a WAV plays it; a radio that receives a clean file programmes it; an audience that downloads shares. In the bouyon economy, where value is first built in fetes and programming, putting the files into the right hands is worth more than a paywall. smokiller.com is the tool of that policy, simple and direct.

The three downloads also tell the alliances of the catalogue: Coi Leray for American rap, Shenseea for the Caribbean voice, Ayra Starr for global Afrobeats. Heaven Baby, the third, also has a particular function in the Bouyon Shattaz project, detailed in the next section. Vulnerability and Heaven Baby carry the Bouyon Shattaz Remix mention, the house signature, where the Coi Leray flip carries the TikTok moment of the catalogue.

Follow and hear Smokiller: his hub smokiller.com for the free downloads, his YouTube channel @smo.killer and his Instagram @smo.killer.

Heaven Baby, the Bouyon Shattaz campaign and radio servicing

The bouyon remix of Ayra Starr Heaven Baby anchors a 30-day Instagram follower-growth campaign for the @bouyonshattaz account, launched on October 19, 2026. Anchoring a media campaign on a producer remix says the nature of the Bouyon Shattaz project: the content that grows the audience is the music itself, produced by the founder, and not decorative content around it.

Bouyon Shattaz is Smokiller’s media platform and his distribution and rights project. The media documents bouyon, this site is its French and English library, and the distribution and rights side works so that Dominican artists keep the benefit of their genre, in the spirit of the defence of the origin told on our history of bouyon page. Smokiller is therefore not only an artist in the catalogue: he is the operator of the system that distributes it.

That system includes active radio servicing. Smokiller runs the supply of Bouyon Shattaz rotation packs to Caribbean stations: the stations receive the tracks in usable formats, including the WAV remixes from his hub, for their programming and their DJs. Servicing connects studio work to field work: a remix flipped anywhere has bouyon value only if it ends up played, in Roseau, in the Antilles and across the whole region.

Why Smokiller matters, in short: because he built, around a simple method, flipping world hits into bouyon, a complete system running from production to media, from free download to radio. Dominican-born, founder of Bouyon Shattaz, producer of Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026), author of a Coi Leray flip reposted by the artist and seen 854.7K times on TikTok according to his own screenshots: his dossier is that of a builder as much as a remixer. Listen to his tracks in the bouyon playlist, then go through smokiller.com for the files.

Listening to Smokiller: the catalogue path

The Smokiller catalogue is best heard in an order that tells his method. Start with the production credit, Klass Money Throw Dat (Bouyon 2026), to hear the producer before the remixer: an original track, built for another artist, in the year the genre itself displays. That starting point recalls that the flips that follow are not amateur edits, but the work of a producer who knows how to build a bouyon track from zero for a given voice.

Move next to the Coi Leray moment, in the order of our dossier: Nothing Into Something first, the flip of September 20, 2026 reposted by the artist and seen 854.7K times on TikTok according to Smokiller’s screenshots, then Players, the remix published on his YouTube channel. Heard back to back, the two tracks show the two tempos of his work: immediate impact on the short platform, then installation over time on the long platform. Between the two, the same gesture repeats: bouyon keyboards, fete tempo and the genre energy applied to an American rap repertoire.

Add the two Bouyon Shattaz remixes available for download on smokiller.com: Shenseea Vulnerability, in WAV, for the Caribbean voice of the catalogue, and Ayra Starr Heaven Baby, in M4A, for its global Afrobeats side. Heaven Baby deserves careful listening: it is the track anchoring the 30-day @bouyonshattaz Instagram campaign launched on October 19, 2026, so it is the exact point where Smokiller studio work becomes the engine of the Bouyon Shattaz media. Finish with the instrumentals and videos on the hub, from Bryson Tiller and Outside to Shake It To The Max, to hear the method bare, without the original voice.

That path has a practical destination: radio and the fete. The WAV files on the hub are designed for DJs and programming, and the Bouyon Shattaz rotation pack servicing sends this catalogue to Caribbean stations. Listening to Smokiller all the way through therefore means following a track from its original form, an Afrobeats, pop, rap or R&B hit, to its complete bouyon life: flipped in the studio, downloaded by DJs, programmed by radios and danced to by the genre’s audiences.

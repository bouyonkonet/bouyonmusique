Who Deejay Guyguy is: what the sources establish, and only that

Start with the exact perimeter of the record. Deejay Guyguy, written "Dj Guyguy" in both verified sources, is a DJ associated with bouyon. None of the sources opened for this profile gives his real name, his island of origin, his date of birth, his label or a confirmed official account. This site fills none of those blanks: a short, exact profile is worth more than a long, invented one, and the house rule is the same for a Roseau producer documented album by album as for a DJ known through two credits.

What the sources establish fits in two lines, and both lines are solid. On one side, the website of NRJ Antilles, the French Antilles broadcaster, published a page titled "Dj Guyguy X Dj Fine Apple (Anissa Remix Bouyon 2020)", embedding the YouTube video of the remix. On the other side, the playlist "The Sound of Bouyon", the genre reference selection run by The Sounds of Spotify, lists the track "Bel Fess Bel Chatte" with three names in the credit: Miky Ding La, J2MO and Dj Guyguy, with a duration of 2 minutes 41.

Two credits, two different contexts, and that contrast is exactly what makes the name worth documenting. The first credit belongs to the unofficial remix culture that carries bouyon through the cars, parties and social feeds of the Antilles. The second belongs to the official streaming catalogue, where today bouyon is measured track by track. One DJ present on both grounds says something about how the genre worked in 2020 and still works now: half of its history is written outside tidy discographies.

This profile is therefore, deliberately, a profile of limits. It explains what the two credits mean, why the "Anissa" remix mattered in the 2020 bouyon wave, it clears up the namesake confusion that pollutes every search for this name, and it leaves the empty boxes empty. If a primary source, an official account or the artist himself confirms more, this page will be completed and dated like every page on this site.

The Anissa remix: the 2020 bouyon wave seen from the Antilles

To understand the first credit, go back to 2020. "Anissa" is the hit by Wejdene, the French singer whose song took over francophone social media and radio that year. In the French Antilles, the DJs answered at once, in the form bouyon adoption of a hit always takes: the remix. The original song was rebuilt on a bouyon rhythm, sped up, reworked for dancing and for sound systems, then circulated as video.

The NRJ Antilles page documents exactly that mechanism for the version by Dj Guyguy and Dj Fine Apple. The page title says it all: "Dj Guyguy X Dj Fine Apple (Anissa Remix Bouyon 2020)". The "X" marks the collaboration between the two DJs, the words "Remix Bouyon" mark the treatment applied to the hit, and the year 2020 places the piece in the first wave of these adaptations. That it was a radio site from the Antilles relaying the video also matters: the bouyon remix did not stay a file swapped between phones, it earned an editorial showcase in Guadeloupe and Martinique media.

Be precise about what this credit proves and what it does not. It proves that by 2020 the name Dj Guyguy was circulating in the Antillean bouyon remix circuit, alongside another DJ, Dj Fine Apple, whose own public record is just as thin. It does not prove a commercial release, a clearance from the original artist, or any listening figures: bouyon remixes of that period lived on YouTube and at parties, and their numbers from the time cannot be recovered cleanly. This site therefore publishes no figure for this remix.

Pieces like this still hold a real place in the history of the genre. Bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s with bands such as WCK, but its spread through the French Antilles ran largely through DJs: selections, edits, remixes of zouk, dancehall and later French pop hits. The "Anissa" remix by Dj Guyguy and Dj Fine Apple is a dated, sourced specimen of that machinery, and it is kept here on that basis, in the spirit of our bouyon playlist if not in its catalogue.

Bel Fess Bel Chatte: the streaming credit, with Miky Ding La and J2MO

The second verified credit moves the name onto different ground. "Bel Fess Bel Chatte" is a track sung in Creole, led by Miky Ding La and J2MO, on which Dj Guyguy appears as the third name in the credit. The source is the playlist "The Sound of Bouyon" by The Sounds of Spotify, the algorithmic and editorial collection through which Spotify sums up the genre: appearing there means the platform files the track inside the current bouyon sound, alongside records by Triple Kay International, Asa Bantan, Reo and Litleboy, which the same playlist also lists.

The shape of the title tells the register: straight ahead dance bouyon in Antillean Creole, built for parties. The duration shown in the playlist, 2 minutes 41, is the modern bouyon format, short and cut for quick mixing. Dj Guyguy exact role on the track, production, co production or a DJ credit, is not detailed by the source, and this profile does not decide what the source does not decide: it keeps the credit exactly as published, with the other two names.

This credit matters for one simple reason: it is traceable in a catalogue. Where the "Anissa" remix belongs to the informal economy of bouyon, "Bel Fess Bel Chatte" belongs to its official economy, the one of platforms, written credits and playlists that act as charts for the genre. The same name standing on both sides of that border is the clearest fact in the Guyguy file, and it sketches the typical portrait of a French Antilles bouyon DJ: one foot in the remix that makes parties dance, one foot in the releases that stay.

For listening context, our bouyon gwada page explains how Guadeloupe made Dominican bouyon its party sound, and our bouyon playlist gathers the records carrying the genre today. The track by Miky Ding La, J2MO and Dj Guyguy earns its place there by credit, even if the man behind the name has, for now, no public biography.

Namesake warning: the DJ GuyGuy of Ghana is not this artist

Every search for this name runs into a trap, and this profile flags it instead of leaving the reader to fall in. There is another DJ GuyGuy, highly visible on Audiomack, based in Tema, Ghana, author of Afrobeats and amapiano mixtapes followed by thousands of listeners. That Ghanaian DJ has no established connection with bouyon, with the Antilles, or with the two credits above. Nothing in his mixtapes crosses the "Anissa" remix, and nothing in the Antillean sources leads to Ghana. The two names are spelled almost identically; the two artists must not be merged.

The confusion is all the easier because search engines mix everything together: one DJ name, variable spellings, Dj Guyguy, DJ GuyGuy, Deejay Guyguy, and platforms that aggregate without checking. The rule applied here is strict: only credits that name the bouyon or Antillean context are kept for this page. The Ghana mixtapes, the Ghanaian rap songs whose titles contain the word guyguy, and every result with no link to bouyon were set aside, even when they ranked first in the results.

That discipline is not an archivist detail. Bouyon is a genre where origin credit gets lost fast, between the Dominica that created it and the scenes that adopted it. An index of bouyon artists that merged two DJs from two continents under one name would manufacture a false biography, with false dates and false figures. This profile would rather announce two verified credits and one namesake set aside than a full page that is wrong.

What this profile does not yet know

The list of unknowns is long and it is published as it stands. We do not know which island Deejay Guyguy comes from, and no verified source says: the NRJ Antilles relay locates his audience in the French Antilles, not his passport. We do not know his real name, nor the exact start of his activity, the 2020 remix being only the oldest verified trace, not an artistic birth date. We know neither his label, nor a complete discography, nor an Instagram, TikTok or YouTube account confirmed as his.

We also do not know whether other remixes or tracks carry his name in corners of the web that search engines no longer index: the 2020 bouyon remix economy lived heavily on reposted videos, short lived links and shared files, ground where the public archive is notoriously incomplete. It is therefore possible, even likely, that this DJ real catalogue is wider than the two credits above. Possible and likely are not verified: this site will wait for a source before lengthening the list.

In the meantime, the page does its index job: a reader looking for the DJ behind the bouyon remix of "Anissa" or the track "Bel Fess Bel Chatte" finds here the two facts, their sources, and the clear border between what is established and what is not. It is not much, and it is exactly the kind of not much this site prefers to publish rather than copying other people errors. For the rest of the genre, the bouyon playlist and the history of bouyon page give the full landscape in which these two credits make sense.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Deejay Guyguy?

Deejay Guyguy, written Dj Guyguy in the sources, is a DJ associated with bouyon whose record holds two verified credits: a bouyon remix of the song Anissa, signed with Dj Fine Apple and relayed by the NRJ Antilles website, and a credit on the track Bel Fess Bel Chatte by Miky Ding La and J2MO, listed in Spotify The Sound of Bouyon playlist. His origin and real name are not given by the verified sources.

What is the Anissa remix by Dj Guyguy and Dj Fine Apple?

It is a bouyon version of the Wejdene hit Anissa, dated 2020 in the very title of the NRJ Antilles page that relays its YouTube video. Like many bouyon remixes of that period, it belongs to party remix culture rather than to a documented commercial release, and no verified listening figure can be attached to it.

On which streaming track is Deejay Guyguy credited?

On Bel Fess Bel Chatte, a Creole language track by Miky Ding La and J2MO, where Dj Guyguy appears as the third name in the credit. Spotify The Sound of Bouyon playlist lists it with a duration of 2 minutes 41, alongside the records that define the current bouyon sound.

Is the DJ GuyGuy on Audiomack, based in Ghana, the same artist?

No, nothing supports merging them. The DJ GuyGuy visible on Audiomack is an Afrobeats and amapiano mixtape DJ based in Tema, Ghana. None of his releases crosses bouyon or the Antilles, and no Antillean source leads to him. He is a namesake, and this profile borrows no fact from him.

Which island does Deejay Guyguy come from?

That is not established. The relay of his remix by NRJ Antilles and the Creole language of the track Bel Fess Bel Chatte place his ground in the French Antilles, but no verified source gives his island of origin or his place of residence. This site does not turn a broadcast context into a biography.

Why is this profile so much shorter than the other artist pages on this site?

Because the verified public record fits in two credits. Rather than filling the page with facts borrowed from namesakes or invented, this profile publishes the two credits with their sources, explains their context, and lists what remains unknown. It will grow as soon as a primary source confirms more.

Dj Guyguy, DJ GuyGuy or Deejay Guyguy: how is his name spelled?

Both verified sources write it “Dj Guyguy”: the NRJ Antilles page on the Anissa remix and Spotify’s The Sound of Bouyon playlist. This site titles his profile Deejay Guyguy. The spelling “DJ GuyGuy” mostly leads search engines to a Ghanaian namesake from Tema with no link to bouyon, so search the name together with bouyon or Antilles.

What is a bouyon remix like the Anissa one?

A bouyon remix takes a hit from elsewhere and rebuilds it on the bouyon rhythm: sped up, reworked for dancing and sound systems, then shared as a video. The Anissa remix by Dj Guyguy and Dj Fine Apple is one example, dated 2020. Our bouyon remix page tells the story of this culture driven by Antillean DJs.

Why did the bouyon remix of Anissa matter?

Because it documents how bouyon, born in Dominica, spread through the French Antilles: through DJs, their selections and their remixes of hits. In 2020 the Wejdene hit got its bouyon version, and the website of an Antillean radio station, NRJ Antilles, relayed it. The remix thus moved beyond phones to an editorial showcase.

What is The Sound of Bouyon playlist that features Dj Guyguy?

It is the genre’s reference selection run by The Sounds of Spotify, which sums up the current bouyon sound. It lists Bel Fess Bel Chatte, by Miky Ding La, J2MO and Dj Guyguy, alongside songs by Triple Kay International, Asa Bantan, Reo or Litleboy. Being on it means the platform files the song under today’s bouyon.

Where can I listen to Deejay Guyguy?

His two verified credits can be heard in two places: the Anissa bouyon remix, with Dj Fine Apple, on the NRJ Antilles page that embeds its YouTube video, and Bel Fess Bel Chatte on Spotify, in The Sound of Bouyon playlist.

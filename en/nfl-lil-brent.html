Who NFL Lil Brent is: one name, one record, one page

Let us start with the exact inventory, because the whole file fits inside it. NFL Lil Brent is the artist of record of a single bouyon record opened and verified during this research: “I Cheating”. The heading of the official page gives the full name under which the artist appears, NFL Lil Brent, while the text of the page shortens it to Lil Brent, notably in the sentence that promises more bouyon records from him. This profile keeps the full form of the heading as the artist name, because it is the form on the bill of the record, and it flags the short form as the one used in the presentation text. No opened source explains the NFL prefix: this profile therefore does not expand it, as initials, as a nickname or as a sports reference, because no consulted document does.

The rest of the identity is a blank this profile refuses to fill. No artist page on the platforms consulted, no biography, no interview, no official photograph and no social media account confirmed by direct opening was found for NFL Lil Brent during this research. Searches under this name also surface, on aggregator sites, an American hip hop artist presented under the neighbouring name of Lil Brent: an aggregator is not a primary source, nothing ties that file to the “I Cheating” file, and this profile does not merge the two. The name of the artist of the bouyon record therefore stays what it is on his only page: NFL Lil Brent, with no legal name, no place and no published path.

I Cheating: the official description, line by line

The record is documented by a single page, its own, and this profile reads it in full. The description presents “I Cheating” as a high energy bouyon anthem packed with infectious rhythms, raw Caribbean vibes and unforgettable lyrics, in the team own words. It places the listening in three settings, the fete, the car and time with friends, and it promises the fast paced energy bouyon fans love, with an invitation to dance and to put the record on repeat. That is a release presentation text, written to sell the record: this profile quotes it as the presentation the record gives itself, and not as an independent listening account, which no opened source provides.

The only making line of the page fits in a few words: the riddim was created by @KWAB-DJ, given in that handle form. The riddim, in the grammar of bouyon as of dancehall, is the rhythmic bed over which the voice rides: that credit is therefore the central making credit of the record, the maker of its instrumental. KWAB-DJ thus enters this file as a credit roll name, on the same footing as the producers and studios this site follows from page to page: producers, studios and writers enter the catalog through releases under their own name, and no release of KWAB-DJ own was opened during this research. The page gives no other credit: no writing, no additional production, no mixing, no label.

The end of the description places the record on its search map, and that is what raises the question treated in the next section. The text addresses listeners of bouyon, soca, dancehall, Caribbean music and Afro-Caribbean party records, and it promises more original bouyon records from Lil Brent. The keyword block ties the record to bouyon, to bouyon music and to bouyon 2026, then it names two islands, Dominica and Saint Lucia, with the 758, and the keyword line at the foot of the page repeats the two anchorings, Saint Lucia music and Dominica bouyon. No page of the record on another platform was opened during this research: no release date, no duration and no documented artwork therefore complete the page, whose extracted text carries neither a date nor a running time.

Dominica or Saint Lucia: the origin the source does not settle

The question of origin is the only real question of this file, and its source leaves it open. On one side, the keyword block of the page names Dominica and the closing line speaks of Dominica bouyon: the tie to the country of bouyon is therefore claimed by the record itself. On the other side, the same block names Saint Lucia and adds the 758, the telephone area code of Saint Lucia turned into a rallying sign of its scene, and the closing line also speaks of Saint Lucia music. The two anchorings appear in the same document, printed by the same team, without any sentence of the description saying where the artist comes from. A keyword is a search tool, not a biographical notice: it tells the audiences of two islands that the record concerns them, it does not say which one saw the performer born or raised.

This profile therefore assigns no island to NFL Lil Brent, and it files the record where its page places it: in the bouyon circuit, between the two shores of the channel that separates the two islands. The context makes that double addressing readable without turning it into an answer. Dominica, to the north, is the country of origin of bouyon and of its current scene, documented on this site; Saint Lucia, to the south, keeps constant exchanges with the genre, which our bouyon in Saint Lucia page follows, while the wave of the source island is read on bouyon in Dominica. A record that addresses both audiences at once speaks the language of a circuit, not yet that of a biography. What would settle the question is simple to state: an artist page, an interview, or a release page naming the origin of the artist. None of those documents was opened during this research, and this profile will be corrected the day one of them appears.

The context of the genre: an anthem, a riddim, the 2026 season

The genre “I Cheating” speaks has a precise history, and it sheds light on the shape of the record. Bouyon was born in Dominica at the end of the 1980s, and bouyon soca was started in Dominica in 2003 by Dada, according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller, a Dominican-born producer and a participant in that era. From that matrix came the codes the page of the record displays one by one: the fast energy designed for the fete, the voice carried by a named and credited riddim, the direct addressing of the audiences of the neighbouring islands, and the placing inside a season, here the bouyon 2026 the keyword line claims. The record of NFL Lil Brent is, in its very presentation, a product of that grammar: a party anthem, a riddim maker in the credits, two islands addressed.

The place of the riddim credit deserves one more sentence, because it sets bouyon apart from other scenes. In this circuit, the instrumental circulates as a work in itself, signed by its creator, and voices take turns over beds the audience recognises: the page of “I Cheating” follows exactly that usage by crediting KWAB-DJ in its description, even before promising what comes next in the catalog of the artist. Our bouyon riddim page follows that riddim culture in the genre. For NFL Lil Brent, the checkable consequence is plain: his only documented record plugs him into that economy of the credited riddim, and the name of KWAB-DJ is, to date, the only making name tied to his. The promise, in the description, of more bouyon records from Lil Brent is a presentation sentence, not a catalog: no second record page under his name was opened during this research, no artist page, no platform page and no official account. The opened page carries, in the extracted text, neither a release date, nor a duration, nor a label, nor a writing credit: this profile therefore publishes no chronology and no discography beyond the single record, and the structured data of this page carries only that recording, with no external link, for want of an account verified by opening.

The origin, too, stays a limit and not an answer: the single source of the file tags Dominica and Saint Lucia in the same block, and this profile leaves the question open, in its text as in its frequently asked questions, rather than choosing an island to fill a box. If an artist page, an interview or a release page documents what comes next, a second record, a named origin, a complete credit roll, this profile will be completed and dated, like every page on this site. In the meantime, it publishes what its source gives, no more and no less: an artist named NFL Lil Brent, a bouyon anthem titled “I Cheating”, a riddim created by KWAB-DJ, and an address sent to the audiences of two islands at once.

WCMF 2026, night 1: the rain did not stop the bouyon

By Smokiller · Published November 5, 2026

On Friday, October 23, 2026, the World Creole Music Festival opened its 26th edition at Windsor Park in Roseau. Rain fell all night and technical difficulties interrupted part of the programme, but neither could put out the energy from the stage, according to the WorlMag review.

A cultural opening before the music

Before the concerts, the festival began with the national anthem, a prayer and a cultural showcase, setting a patriotic and heritage-focused tone for the evening. The Sisserou Singers helped open the stage, followed by the Patriotic Dancers, who brought cultural expression to the forefront.

Between performances, DJs Chicanos and Kiven Crown kept the atmosphere going while the audience got refreshments and the media prepared their coverage. Hosts Barry Hype, Miguel Labadie and Millia Joseph guided the audience while the stadium filled. For WorlMag, the opening was a reminder that the festival is more than a concert: a platform where Dominica celebrates its identity and welcomes the Caribbean through music.

Maurane Voyer opens, Colton T turns a technical fault into showmanship

Martinique singer Maurane Voyer opened the main performances at approximately 9:06 p.m., with a lively set that gave the evening its French Caribbean colour, while the stadium was still filling. Her set established the tone and showed the importance of the festival regional reach.

Dominican artist Colton T followed, with a set driven by energy and audience connection. Early audio difficulties arose, but he acknowledged them while introducing his music and carried on with the show, without letting the mishap define his performance. For WorlMag, the moment mattered: the festival gives Dominican artists a major stage beside established names, and the crowd response showed it.

Midnight Groovers and Klass: cadence and kompa

Midnight Groovers delivered one of the defining cultural moments of the night, reminding the stadium why cadence-lypso remains an important part of Dominica musical identity. Frontman Chubby commanded attention with his stage presence and movement, and the audience responded by singing and dancing to a sound deeply connected to the island heritage.

Haitian band Klass then carried on the cross-Caribbean exchange with a set rooted in kompa. The transition was natural in a programme moving from one Creole tradition to another: after cadence-lypso, Haitian kompa added another dimension to the evening, and the audience embraced those smooth Haitian rhythms, according to the review.

Capleton, TK International, Skillibeng

Jamaican reggae and dancehall veteran Capleton then raised the intensity, with a high-energy performance built around his signature fire persona. He was returning to Dominica after more than two decades, according to Worlmag event notes, and the audience responded by waving their hands, singing along and following his calls, phone lights illuminating sections of the stadium.

TK International of Dominica followed with a powerful reminder of the influence of homegrown bouyon. The first bouyon act of the weekend, the group had to bring the sound back to the centre of the night after Capleton fire, and it met the challenge with a set that got patrons moving from the opening notes. Skillibeng, another Jamaican artist, brought a newer generation of dancehall energy, distinct from the veteran.

Ridge and Pudaz until sunrise

As the night moved towards morning, Dominican performers Ridge and Pudaz delivered one of the most memorable crowd scenes of the festival. Their animated introduction set the tone, and instead of tiring, the audience was singing at the top of its lungs, dancing and responding enthusiastically, in an atmosphere that felt less like the end of a long festival night and more like the middle of an ongoing celebration.

DJ MJ and MV followed Ridge and Pudaz and kept the crowd moving until the end, as the night became a marathon of live performances, rain and dancing. The WorlMag verdict fits in one line: 26 editions and still going strong. The festival began with a reminder of what makes it special: music that crosses borders, traditions that keep evolving and an audience willing to celebrate until daybreak.

Frequently asked questions

Who opened night 1 of WCMF 2026?

Martinique singer Maurane Voyer opened the main performances at approximately 9:06 p.m., after the national anthem, prayer and the cultural showcase by the Sisserou Singers and the Patriotic Dancers, according to WorlMag.

What was the weather like on night 1?

It rained throughout the evening, and technical difficulties interrupted part of the programme. The audience largely stayed with the music rather than abandoning the party, according to the review.

Who closed night 1 of WCMF 2026?

Ridge and Pudaz delivered the sunrise finale, followed by DJ MJ and MV, who kept the crowd moving until the end of the night, according to WorlMag.

Admiral T: Guadeloupe's dancehall star and bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 29, 2026

Admiral T, born Christy Campbell on March 29, 1981 in Les Abymes, Guadeloupe, is a dancehall and ragga singer. Since “Gwadada” in 2003 he has been one of the best known voices of the French Caribbean. He is not a bouyon artist by origin: he came up through sound systems and reggae, then opened his music to zouk and gwoka. His link to bouyon is real all the same, and it runs through his family. His sons, Dcamp and Lewismelo, are the duo Dcamp & Lewismelo, and the three Campbells share songs such as “Sòti Gwadloup”, “Jenn Boss” and “You and Me”. This profile covers his career, his songs, his sons and his recent shows, with a source behind every fact.

Christy Campbell, born in Les Abymes, raised in Boissard

His official page at Talowa Productions, the agency that books his tours, is precise: Christy Campbell was born on March 29, 1981 in Les Abymes, Guadeloupe. The biography published by Apple Music gives the same date, which makes him 45 in October 2026. He does not hide the civil name. He signs his lyrics “Christy Campbell” in the credits, from “Toucher l'horizon” in 2006 to the songs on “MAESTRO” in 2025, and he called his 2014 album “I Am Christy Campbell”.

The neighbourhood of his childhood has another name. In 2021 Guadeloupe La 1ère aired the documentary “Horizons: Admiral T, de Boissard à Bercy”, directed by Benjamin Marquet. Its presentation describes Boissard as a shantytown on the edge of Pointe-à-Pitre, without running water or electricity until the 1980s, home to families from all over the Caribbean. The title sums up the journey: from that poor district to the big Paris arena at Bercy.

So the frame of this profile is simple. Admiral T is a star of Guadeloupe dancehall and ragga, listed among the artists from Guadeloupe on this site. He sits in the bouyon artists section not because of his style but because his sons made bouyon their language, because he sings with them, and because his agency names bouyon among the colours of his album “MAESTRO”.

From sound systems to “Gwadada”

It all starts in the sound systems. According to Talowa he took the mic at twelve and joined the Karukera Sound System collective, a breeding ground for reggae and dancehall in the French speaking Caribbean, and recorded “Rapide” with them at sixteen. Apple Music has him joining the collective at sixteen. The two biographies differ on that detail, but both say he left in 2003 to go solo.

That year everything sped up. The Guadeloupe La 1ère documentary dates his first solo single, “Gwadada”, to 2003, and says it quickly put him among the names of urban music. The album “Mozaïk Kréyòl” came out in the Caribbean in July 2003 on the Don's Music label, with “Rev an mwen”, “Dancehall X-plosion” with Pearl and “Move Together” with Alyson Hinds. The version on Deezer today carries “Gwadada” as its “Juice Remix”.

The jump to mainland France came in 2004. Talowa says he stole the show from Sean Paul at the Garance Reggae Festival at Bercy and that Universal signed him straight after. Apple Music confirms the Universal reissue of “Mozaïk Kréyòl” in 2004. In 2005 he also tried cinema, with a part in the film “Nèg Maron”.

“Toucher l'horizon”, “Fos a péyi la” and the big rooms

“Toucher l'horizon” came out in 2006: the title track, written by Christy Campbell, is dated May 15, 2006 in its credits. Talowa calls it the album that made him in mainland France. The guest list shows where he stood: Diam's on “Les mains en l'air”, Rohff on “Number One”, the Jamaican group T.O.K on “Wave Your Flag”. He also sings “Rantré an dans' la”, a song he brought back in June 2026 for a COLORS session, twenty years on.

The song with the most weight is “Fos a péyi la”, with Kassav'. The credits published by Universal give lyrics co-written by Jocelyn Béroard and Christy Campbell, with Jacob Desvarieux on composition and guitar. For Caribbean listeners it plays like a handover: the young ragga singer from Boissard records with the band that took zouk around the world. The track is dated 2006, on Don's Music.

Then came “Instinct Admiral” in 2010 on Universal, with “Viser la victoire” and “Hands Up”, and “Face B” in June 2012, with Jamaica's Gyptian and J-Mi Sissoko. In 2014 “I Am Christy Campbell”, also written #iamcc, marked a turn that Talowa describes as a move towards zouk and gwoka. On April 14, 2017 he became the second Caribbean artist from the French Antilles, after Kery James, to headline the AccorHotels Arena at Bercy alone, as the album “Totem” came out on his Elite Base label.

“Mozaïka”: “Baimbridge Cho” and “Chat ka tété rat”

On October 25, 2019 Admiral T released two albums on the same day on Elite Base. “Caribbean Monster” holds seventeen dancehall leaning tracks, including “Ghetto Survivor” with Ghana's Stonebwoy. “Mozaïka”, eight tracks, goes back to Creole and to Guadeloupe. Two of his most watched songs come from that record.

“Baimbridge Cho”, with Were Vana, first came out as a single in July 2019. The video, directed by Jima Kanor with art direction by Christy Campbell, has passed 9.5 million views on his official channel. The chorus is about children going to school in Baimbridge and a mother who “fè daktilo”, who works as a typist. This is the song people mean when they search “admiral t baimbridge”: no source consulted says he lived or studied there, and this profile does not claim it.

“Chat ka tété rat”, with Jacob Desvarieux, already on “Fos a péyi la”, went online on October 24, 2020 and has passed 9.2 million views. The chorus rests on a Creole image: the cat suckles the rat, the rat suckles the cat, “anba kouch an mwen”, under my own roof. The song is about people close to you who turn into enemies, “avantyè nou té konsa, jòdla antrè nou sé lagè”, the day before yesterday we were like this, today it is war between us. Two years later “40 Degrés”, out on September 24, 2021, lined up twenty five tracks on Deezer, across zouk, dancehall and reggae.

His sons Dcamp and Lewismelo, and the bridge to bouyon

Admiral T and his sons have worked together in public for more than ten years. In 2013 his official YouTube channel posted a video in which he “calls his son Dylan” onto the stage of the Atrium in Martinique. In December 2016 he released “Happy Birthday” featuring D.Camp: in the lyrics posted with the official video, Admiral T sings “The Dylan Birthday” and “rendez-vous chez les Campbell”, and D.Camp answers “today is my birthday”. Dylan Campbell is the lyricist credit of Dcamp. For Lewismelo, NRJ Antilles presents “On ti rhum” as signed “Admiral T & Lewis son fils”, his son, and Talowa writes that “MAESTRO” is “produced by his son LewisMelo”. This profile keeps to those artist facts and gives no other family detail.

The shared songs pile up from 2024. “On ti rhum”, with Lewismelo, came out on May 29, 2024, with a video shot largely at the Festival Terre de Blues on Marie-Galante. “Sòti Gwadloup”, on September 8, 2024, brings together Admiral T, Dcamp, Lejuh and Lewismelo, with lyrics by Christy, Dylan and Léwis Campbell and Julio Polydamas. In November 2024 “5 Kilos” joined Lewismelo, Admiral T and Shanika on Lewismelo's album “Premières Notes”; the video has passed 380,000 views.

The album “MAESTRO”, out on February 1, 2025, is where it all meets. Guadeloupe La 1ère reported that for the first time his son composed most of the tracks, and the credits name Léwis Campbell on “My Family” and on “Jenn Boss”, sung with Tony Mix, Dcamp and Lewismelo. Talowa lists bouyon among the album's styles, next to dancehall, zouk and gwoka. Finally, “You and Me” sits on “Double Impact”, the Dcamp & Lewismelo album released on October 17, 2025, which also holds a track called “Bouyon”. The video, posted on March 6, 2026, is close to two million views. “FVCK YOU”, with Lewismelo, followed on February 1, 2026. For the wider scene, see bouyon in Guadeloupe and shatta bouyon.

Admiral T in concert: 2025 and 2026

The release of “MAESTRO” was celebrated on Saturday January 25, 2025 at Lacaz'Art in Baie-Mahault, with a show mixing classics and new songs, according to Guadeloupe La 1ère. The big Paris date planned next did not happen as announced: the Friday December 12, 2025 concert at Arena Grand Paris in Tremblay-en-France was postponed four days before, over production and organisation constraints outside the artist's control, according to Outre-mer La 1ère. The venue's page shows it as cancelled, with refunds at the point of sale.

Several 2026 dates are documented. On January 10 he was billed at L'Oasis in Bobigny. On May 23 he was on the Caribbean Summer Festival bill at Arena Grand Paris, a sold out night whose line up ran “from shatta to bouyon”, according to France Télévisions. On July 17 he played a free show at the Festival International Nuits d'Afrique in Montreal, on the TD stage in the Quartier des spectacles. On August 1 Talowa lists him at the Nomade Reggae Festival in Anglefort, in the Ain department.

On the date this was read, October 29, 2026, Talowa's page files all of those 2026 dates under past concerts and announces no new one. For a next show in France, announcements go through his agency page and his official admiralt971 accounts. This profile will be updated when a date is published.

Frequently asked questions

Is Admiral T a bouyon artist?

Not by origin. He is a dancehall and ragga singer from Guadeloupe, known since “Gwadada” in 2003. His bouyon link runs through his sons Dcamp and Lewismelo, who he sings with, and through the album “MAESTRO” (2025), whose styles his agency says include bouyon.

What is Admiral T's real name?

Christy Campbell. The name appears on his agency page, in his Apple Music biography and in the writing credits of his songs. His 2014 album is even called “I Am Christy Campbell”.

How old is Admiral T?

He was born on March 29, 1981, according to his Talowa Productions page and his Apple Music biography. That makes him 45 in October 2026.

Where is Admiral T from?

He is from Guadeloupe, born in Les Abymes according to his agency page. The Guadeloupe La 1ère documentary “De Boissard à Bercy” presents him as a child of Boissard, a district on the edge of Pointe-à-Pitre.

Are Dcamp and Lewismelo Admiral T's sons?

Yes. His official channel presents Dylan as his son, and Dylan Campbell is Dcamp's credited name. NRJ Antilles calls Lewis his son on “On ti rhum”, and his agency writes that “MAESTRO” was produced by his son LewisMelo.

Which songs has Admiral T made with his sons?

“Happy Birthday” with D.Camp (2016), “On ti rhum” with Lewismelo (2024), “Sòti Gwadloup” with Dcamp, Lejuh and Lewismelo (2024), “Jenn Boss” on “MAESTRO” (2025), “You and Me” (2025) and “FVCK YOU” with Lewismelo (2026).

What are Admiral T's best known songs?

Landmarks include “Gwadada” (2003), “Les mains en l'air” with Diam's and “Fos a péyi la” with Kassav' (2006), “Baimbridge Cho” (2019) and “Chat ka tété rat” (2020 video), the last two past 9 million views on his channel.

What is “You and Me” by Admiral T?

A Dcamp & Lewismelo song with Admiral T, on their album “Double Impact”, released on October 17, 2025 on Elite Base. The writers are Admiral T and Dcamp, the music is by Lewis Campbell. The video, posted on March 6, 2026, is close to two million views.

What is “Chat ka tété rat” about?

People close to you turning against you: the cat suckles the rat “under my own roof”. The song, with Jacob Desvarieux, comes from the album “Mozaïka” (2019); the official video dates from October 24, 2020.

When did “Toucher l'horizon” come out?

In 2006, on Don's Music. It is his second album, with Diam's, Rohff, T.O.K and Kassav' as guests, and the one that made him in mainland France according to his agency.

When did “Gwadada” come out?

In 2003, as his first solo single, according to the Guadeloupe La 1ère documentary. His first album, “Mozaïk Kréyòl”, came out in the Caribbean the same year.

Who sings “Fos a péyi la” with Admiral T?

Kassav'. The lyrics are co-written by Jocelyn Béroard and Christy Campbell, and Jacob Desvarieux wrote the music. The song is on the album “Toucher l'horizon” (2006).

Is Admiral T from Baimbridge?

No source consulted says so. “Baimbridge Cho”, with Were Vana (2019), is a song whose chorus is about children going to school in Baimbridge. His official sources have him born in Les Abymes and raised in Boissard.

Is Admiral T playing in France in 2026?

He played Bobigny on January 10, the Caribbean Summer Festival at Arena Grand Paris on May 23 and the Nomade Reggae Festival on August 1, 2026. As of October 29, 2026 his agency lists no new date. The December 12, 2025 show at Arena Grand Paris had been postponed.

Bouyon and diaspora: the sound of home, far from home

By Smokiller · Published October 5, 2026 · Updated October 28, 2026

Everywhere Dominicans and Antilleans live, bouyon travels in suitcases and in phones: New York and Brooklyn, London, Toronto, Montreal. For the diaspora, a bouyon track is a piece of home.

Frequently asked questions

Where is bouyon popular?

Born in Dominica, bouyon is now central in Guadeloupe and Martinique and present in France, Saint Lucia, Saint Martin and the Caribbean diaspora. Everywhere Dominicans and Antilleans live, it travels with them, from New York and Brooklyn to London, Toronto and Montreal.

Which country created bouyon?

Dominica. Bouyon was born there in the late 1980s: in 1988, the band WCK, formed in the capital, Roseau, fused cadence-lypso and jing ping with electronic keyboards and drum machines. The genre is still the sound of Roseau carnival, Mas Domnik.

Which diaspora cities listen to bouyon?

New York and Brooklyn, London, Toronto and Montreal, everywhere Dominicans and Antilleans live. Caribbean nights in these big cities mix bouyon, soca, dancehall and zouk, but the bouyon moment keeps its particular charge: it is the sound of Roseau, of Grand Bay and of Mas Domnik.

Why does the diaspora matter for bouyon?

Because the diaspora consumes, shares and finances modern bouyon. Social networks have replaced the cassettes of the past: a riddim born in Dominica goes around the world in a few hours. It is also for this audience that Bouyon Shattaz publishes in English as well as in French, and that this site exists in two languages.

Where can you hear bouyon in Paris?

At the Antillean nights of the French capital, home to the largest Antillean diaspora in Europe. Bouyon has found a scene there: dedicated nights, specialist DJs, producers making the riddims of the moment and an audience that demands every new release. A track born in Dominica or Gwada is often tested there by DJs, pushed on the networks, then certified on the French market. See bouyon in Paris.

Is there a bouyon award in New York?

Yes: the Caribbean Music Awards have a Bouyon Artist of the Year category. Their third edition was held on August 28, 2025 in Brooklyn, New York, with nine nominees published in June 2025, including Asa Bantan, Kenny G, Trilla G, Triple K and Pudaz. That bouyon has its own category in front of a diaspora audience shows the Dominica-born genre has become an official box. See Kenny G.

Which bouyon bands play diaspora carnivals?

TK International, also known as Triple Kay, is the best documented example. The official site of this bouyon band from Dominica lists Houston Carifest, Toronto Caribana, Notting Hill Carnival in the United Kingdom, Miami Carnival and New York West Indian Day celebrations among its stages. For the diaspora audience, it is the sound of Roseau played live. See TK International.

Have bouyon artists performed at Notting Hill Carnival?

Yes. Ebony Empress, a bouyon artist from Lagoon, in Portsmouth, Dominica, lists Notting Hill Carnival and Labour Day Weekend in New York City among her stages. She says her most memorable moment remains performing on the road at Notting Hill, in front of thousands of people singing and dancing to her music. Triple Kay also lists Notting Hill among its carnivals. See Ebony Empress.

Which bouyon songs play at Toronto and Miami carnivals?

“What She Like”, by Trixzmigos featuring Hussey, is one documented example. The track appears in the published tracklists of DJ BuzzB's Toronto Carnival 2025 and Miami Carnival 2025 mixes, in DJ Close's Bouyon Passport 2026 and in a Miami Carnival mix by I AM SONICBOOM, which bills it as a bouyon record. Carnival DJs are the first relay of bouyon in the diaspora. See Hussey.

Is J'ouvert celebrated in the diaspora too?

Yes. J'ouvert, the dawn party that opens the carnival days, lives across the whole Creole and Caribbean world, from Trinidad to Dominica, and all the way to the diaspora carnivals in Brooklyn and London. In Dominica, sound systems and bands play the year's bouyon there until morning. See the J'ouvert page.

Riddim Knockaz: Bright and Creamy and the live stage, bouyon

By Smokiller · Published October 20, 2026

Riddim Knockaz is a bouyon band from Dominica whose public trace sits in two registers that barely overlap. On one side, a single recording documented with full credits, “Bright and Creamy”, released as a lyric video on YouTube. On the other, a steady presence on the island official bills: the Portsmouth Feast of St. Peters in 2024, the official launch of Mas Domnik 2026 and Day 1 of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026 in Roseau. This profile states exactly what the sources allow, credit by credit and bill by bill, and it also states plainly, without dressing it up, everything the public record does not yet hold.

Who Riddim Knockaz are: a band, one record, a run of bills

Riddim Knockaz appear, in the credit of their documented record, under the longer form Riddim Knockaz Band. The word band matters: this is a group, not a solo producer and not a single singer. Their record “Bright and Creamy” carries two lead voices, Dayne Alexander and Glenn Mills, with backing vocals by those same two singers and by Dion Brewster. The band is credited as executive producer of its own record, which describes a formation that organises its release itself, from sound to image.

No official biography of the band was found during the research for this profile: no founding date, no precise home town inside Dominica, and no fixed complete member list. That gap is not filled here with guesses. The names that follow are only the names the published credit of “Bright and Creamy” gives, in the roles that credit gives them, and the stages that follow are only the stages the press and official programmes published with the band name on them.

That imbalance between stage and archive is common in live band bouyon. A band can play patronal feasts, carnival launches and Creole festivals season after season without placing a proper catalogue on the platforms. The audience in Roseau or Portsmouth then knows the band from the bill and from the memory of a performance, while a listener in Guadeloupe or Paris searching a streaming service finds almost nothing. This profile is written for that second reader as much as for the first.

Bright and Creamy: the documented record, read credit by credit

“Bright and Creamy” is the only Riddim Knockaz recording found with complete credits. The lyric video published on YouTube presents the song as performed by Dayne Alexander and Glenn Mills, written by Tabitha Williams and Dayne Alexander, arranged and produced by Dion Brewster, with additional production by Ajmal Peters. Riddim and drums are credited to Arade “Maskman” Moses, mixing to Ajmal Peters, specified as Ajmal Peters of Bouyon Kitchen, and mastering to Dernel Green. Cover art and video are by Tabitha Williams, of Print it 767, and executive production is credited to Riddim Knockaz Band.

Those credits describe a production made in Dominica from start to finish, rooted in the circuit this site documents page after page: an arranger producer, a drummer credited for the riddim, a mixer identified by his studio name, a named mastering engineer, a named designer with her company, Print it 767, whose 767 is Dominica own area code. Nothing in this credit is anonymous. That is exactly what makes the record valuable to the archive: short of an album, it fixes the names of a complete team around the band at one point in its path.

The keywords attached to the video by its own makers place the song in bouyon and in the 2024 soca and bouyon season. That labelling, chosen by the band camp, is the only genre label this profile keeps for the record, and it matches the band place on the bills: party music built for carnival and festival stages, on the same programmes as the bands who share those lineups. Our bouyon riddim page explains the place of the riddim, the rhythmic base credited here to Arade “Maskman” Moses, in the building of a bouyon song.

The limit has to be stated clearly: one documented record does not make a discography. This profile therefore lends the band no album, no run of singles and no collaborations that are not published with checkable credits. If other songs exist in concert videos, radio shows or sound system archives, they are not listed in the open sources consulted, and they will be added here, with their credits, on the day they are confirmed.

The stage rather than streaming: Portsmouth, Mas Domnik, Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl

The first bill found is local and communal. Writeups 24, reporting on the Portsmouth Feast of St. Peters, lists Riddim Knockaz on the Family Fun Day programme of Sunday, June 30, 2024, the closing day of a three day feast, alongside Slyme Hoyss, Latitude Stars Band and DJ MS, with the Blessing of Boats at the heart of the day. A Portsmouth patronal feast is not a small filler stage: it is the gathering of a fishing and family community, where bands play in front of the audience that knows them best.

The second bill changes scale. The official launch of Mas Domnik 2026, hosted by the Ministry of Tourism and the Discover Dominica Authority on November 28, 2025 at The Factory, on Valley Road, programmes live performances from TK Combo and Riddim Knockaz, alongside sets by DJ MJ and MV on the Keys, DJ Taffy and Friends, DJ Sleem, DJ Snow and DJ Flip. Mas Domnik, presented in that article as one of the oldest and most culturally rooted carnivals in the region, was opening its 2026 season there, a season set to culminate on Carnival Monday and Tuesday, February 16 and 17, 2026. Being one of only two named live acts for that launch places the band in the small circle of formations the carnival institution chooses to set the tone of a season.

The third bill confirms that position two seasons later. For the fifth edition of Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl, the Creole village set in the Botanic Gardens in Roseau from October 19 to 22, 2026 under the theme Tout Biten Domnik, the Government of Dominica confirms Riddim Knockaz among the first twenty names announced, alongside Shelly and the Signal Band, TK International, Extasy, Pudaz, Reo and Faithii among others. The detailed Day 1 programme, published on October 19, places the band at 3:29 PM, between Innercore Unit and the Belles Combo Tribute Band, before Colton T, Midnight Groovers and headliner Extasy. Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl runs in the week before the World Creole Music Festival: playing it means playing for the Creole audience gathered for the biggest music week on the island.

Read together, those three bills draw the band real territory: the community feast, the official carnival and the Creole festival. That is the full circuit of a working Dominican band, from the north of the island to Roseau. It also explains the paradox of the record: a band can be fully visible in that circuit, season after season, while staying almost invisible in international databases, which only list what is released on platforms.

A band inside the Dominican band ecosystem

Riddim Knockaz belong to the band family, the historic form of bouyon. Since WCK, the founding band, Dominica music has been played in formation: keyboards, a rhythm section, singers who trade the front and an audience that answers. The programmes where Riddim Knockaz appear mix exactly those generations and formats: established bands such as Signal Band and Midnight Groovers, carnival formations such as TK International, and solo singers from the new bouyon wave. The band holds the place there of a live formation among its peers, with no source presenting it either as a headliner or as a newcomer.

The “Bright and Creamy” credit also shows how a band works in 2024: the line between stage and studio is thin. The arranger producer sings in the backing vocals, the mixer adds production, and the songwriter also signs the cover art and the video. That versatility is a signature of small island music industries, where a reduced team carries a song from writing to mastering. Reading it in detail, as this profile does, gives the band and its team what the bills never say: the names.

For a listener discovering the band through this site, the listening path is therefore double. The record first, “Bright and Creamy”, the only credited recorded entry point. The stages next, when the season allows: Roseau carnival, patronal feasts and Creole week. Our bouyon playlist gives the sound context of the genre the band works in, from band classics to the new releases followed by Bouyon Shattaz.

What this profile does not know: the limits of the public record

This profile ends with its limits, because they are part of the subject. No official band page was confirmed during the research: no website, no verified social account and no claimed artist page on a platform. No founding date, no account of the members paths before Riddim Knockaz and no interview with the band were found in the open sources consulted. This page therefore links to no account and lends the band no history it has not itself published.

That honesty has a practical reason. International databases easily confuse close names, and the word riddim, at the centre of the band name, is also the most common word in Caribbean music: hundreds of songs, riddims and producers carry it. Crediting the band with a song that carries that word, without an explicit credit, would be an archive error. Here, only “Bright and Creamy”, credited name by name to Riddim Knockaz Band, is held as its recording.

The file stays open. One more bill, one more credited song, one confirmed official page, and this profile will be completed and dated like every page on this site. In the meantime, it fixes what is established: a Dominican bouyon band, one recording documented down to its smallest credits, and three official or community stages, in 2024, 2025 and 2026, that prove steady activity in front of the island audience.

Frequently asked questions

Who are Riddim Knockaz?

Riddim Knockaz, credited as Riddim Knockaz Band on their documented record, are a bouyon band from Dominica. The island audience knows them from the stage: the Portsmouth Feast of St. Peters in 2024, the official Mas Domnik 2026 launch and Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026 in Roseau. No official biography of the band is published in the open sources consulted.

What is the documented Riddim Knockaz song?

“Bright and Creamy”, released as a lyric video on YouTube. It is performed by Dayne Alexander and Glenn Mills, written by Tabitha Williams and Dayne Alexander, arranged and produced by Dion Brewster, with riddim and drums by Arade “Maskman” Moses, mixing by Ajmal Peters of Bouyon Kitchen and mastering by Dernel Green. Its makers place it in bouyon and the 2024 season.

Why only one song when the band plays major bills?

Because Riddim Knockaz are a stage band: their documented activity runs through feasts, carnival and festivals, not through a streaming catalogue. A band can play whole seasons without putting a catalogue on the platforms. Only one recording with full credits was found, and this profile refuses to invent others.

Where did Riddim Knockaz play at Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026?

At the Botanic Gardens in Roseau, on Day 1 of the festival, October 19, 2026, at 3:29 PM according to the official programme published that day. The band was among the first twenty names confirmed by the Government of Dominica for the fifth edition, held from October 19 to 22, 2026 under the theme Tout Biten Domnik.

What role did the band hold at the Mas Domnik 2026 launch?

A leading role for a band: Riddim Knockaz and TK Combo were the two announced live performances at the official launch, on November 28, 2025 at The Factory, on Valley Road, alongside several DJs including DJ MJ and MV on the Keys and DJ Taffy. The event, hosted by the Ministry of Tourism and the Discover Dominica Authority, opened the 2026 carnival season.

Where can I hear Riddim Knockaz and band bouyon?

Start with “Bright and Creamy” on YouTube, the band’s only credited song, then watch for their stage dates when the season comes: Roseau carnival, patron saint feasts and Creole week. For the sound of the genre, from the band classics of WCK and Signal Band to new releases, hear our bouyon playlist.

Who are the members of Riddim Knockaz?

No complete, settled member list is published. The only documented credit, that of “Bright and Creamy”, names two lead voices, Dayne Alexander and Glenn Mills, with backing vocals by those two singers and by Dion Brewster, the song’s arranger and producer. This profile sticks to those names, in the roles the credit gives them, without inferring the band’s line up from them.

Who wrote Riddim Knockaz’s “Bright and Creamy”?

The song is written by Tabitha Williams and Dayne Alexander, according to the lyric video published on YouTube. Tabitha Williams also signs the artwork and the video, under her outfit Print it 767, whose 767 is Dominica’s area code. Dayne Alexander is also one of the two lead voices, with Glenn Mills: a small team carries the song from writing to image.

Did Riddim Knockaz play the Feast of St. Peter in Portsmouth?

Yes. According to Writeups 24, the band was on the programme of the Family Fun Day on Sunday 30 June 2024, the final day of the three day Feast of St. Peter in Portsmouth, alongside Slyme Hoyss, Latitude Stars Band and DJ MS, with the blessing of the boats at the heart of the day. It is the gathering of a community of fishermen and families.

Are Riddim Knockaz a band or a producer?

A band. Their documented record credits them in the long form Riddim Knockaz Band, and the word band describes a group, not a solo producer, despite the word riddim at the centre of the name, the rhythmic base our bouyon riddim page explains. The band is credited as executive producer of its own song and plays live on Dominica’s stages.

Which bands do Riddim Knockaz share bills with?

For Ti Vilaj Kwéyòl 2026, the Government of Dominica announced them among the first twenty names, with Shelly and the Signal Band, TK International, Extasy, Pudaz, Reo and Faithii. The 19 October programme places them between Innercore Unit and the Belles Combo Tribute Band, ahead of Colton T, Midnight Groovers and Extasy. At the Mas Domnik 2026 launch, they shared the live stage with TK Combo.

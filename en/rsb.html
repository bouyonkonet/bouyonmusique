RSB: Roots, Stems and Branches, the third name of the bouyon foundation

Published October 20, 2026

RSB, short for Roots, Stems and Branches, is the most quoted and the least documented name in bouyon. The reference encyclopedia Continuum and the genre survey article name it, with WCK and First Serenade Band, among the prominent groups of the style born in Roseau in the late 1980s. And then nothing: no artist page on the platforms, no album found, no attributable song, no named member, in any of the sources opened during this research. This profile therefore does two things at once. It publishes exactly what the references establish, the full name, the company it keeps and the era a second source assigns to it. And it documents the void around it, why a leading group of the early bouyon years can leave no digital trace, and how not to confuse it with the other acts called RSB that the search engines offer. A short profile, because the file is short, and it says so.

The third name: what the reference says, in the exact sense of its words

The central fact of the file fits in one encyclopedia sentence. The Dominica chapter of the Continuum Encyclopedia of Popular Music of the World, the collective reference work on the popular music of the world, writes that among the leading bouyon bands are WCK, short for Windward Caribbean Kulture, RSB, short for Roots, Stems and Branches, and First Serenade, which it describes as an older band spanning almost the entire history of popular music in Dominica. The bouyon survey article repeats the same triad in its first sentence: the prominent groups of the genre are Windward Caribbean Kulture, Roots, Stems and Branches and First Serenade. The full name of RSB therefore does not exist only as an acronym expansion reported by this site: it is printed, spelled out in brackets, in the reference work itself.

What that sentence establishes must be read precisely. It establishes that RSB was, in the eyes of the specialists who closed their chapter in the early 2000s, one of the three leading groups of Dominican bouyon. It does not establish a founding date, nor a creator role: the creation of bouyon is attributed everywhere to WCK, and RSB is never described as an inventor of the style. Nor does it establish a discography: the same chapter publishes a selective discography of the island, with Exile One, First Serenade and Midnight Groovers, and RSB does not appear in it. The highest placed group in the text is therefore absent from the record list of the same text: the RSB file is born with that contradiction, and this profile keeps it visible instead of resolving it by invention.

The name itself is the only identity element the group transmitted to the references. Roots, Stems and Branches is a name of botany and of genealogy at once: the image of a music growing from roots, through stems, towards branches. In the Dominica of the bands, where names often state a programme, WCK claiming the culture of the Windward Islands and First Serenade the village serenade tradition, the name of RSB claims rootedness. But no opened source says who chose that name, nor when, nor for which musicians: this profile reads it as a name, and refuses to turn it into a biography.

From the soca years to bouyon: the only era the sources assign to the group

A second independent source gives the group its era. The Music of Dominica article, kept in the 2007 Schools selection and fed by the same reference work, tells the island 1980s: cadence-lypso declined, French Antillean zouk dominated, and soca, the music of Trinidad, was also popular, producing bands like RSB, Windward Caribbean Kulture and First Serenade. RSB is therefore first ranked among the bands of the Dominican soca era of the 1980s, on the same footing as its two triad companions. The same article tells what followed: in 1988, WCK formed and played an experimental fusion of cadence-lypso and jing ping, and the result took the name bouyon. When the encyclopedia then ranks RSB among the leading bouyon groups, the soberest path is this one: a soca era band that belongs to the dominant formations at the moment bouyon takes shape, like First Serenade, whose comparable seniority is documented in detail on its page.

That era frame is all the file allows about the sound of the group, and it must be stated as a frame, not as a description. A leading Dominican group between the late 1980s and the 1990s played in the conditions of the island band scene: a full live formation, a fete and carnival repertoire, a mix of soca, cadence and the local rhythms bouyon was in the process of welding together, jing ping, lapo kabwit, bele and chante mas. No opened source describes an RSB concert, quotes one of its songs, or names one of its singers or musicians. Any more precise sentence about the sound of RSB would be an invention, and this profile contains none.

The same Music of Dominica article, when it reaches the modern bouyon bands, names Rough and Ready, Wassin Warriors and Seramix, without RSB. The old list of the groups of the genre, kept in the early versions of the survey article, ranks RSB for its part among the old school bands, alongside WCK, First Serenade, Partners In Kryme, Raw Reedim, Ruff and Ready and Triple K. The group therefore belongs, in every source that cites it, to the first generation, that of the bands, and to no list of the platform generation. Its disappearance from the modern lists is a fact of documentation: it proves neither a dated split, nor inactivity, it only records that the written witnesses of the group stop at the band era.

The reach of the late 1990s: the context, not an attribution

The encyclopedia chapter adds, right after the sentence on the leading bands, a judgement on the reach of the bouyon of that generation. During the late 1990s, it writes, Dominican bouyon exerted an important influence on the ring bang music of Barbadian hit makers Square One and Krosfyah, as well as on some of the fast paced soca coming out of Trinidad. That judgement bears on Dominican bouyon as a movement, carried by its generation of bands, that of WCK, RSB and First Serenade. It names no record and no single group in the mechanism of the influence, and this profile therefore quotes it as the context of the triad, never as an exploit of RSB alone.

That context matters for understanding the place of the name. The bouyon of the Dominican bands was not a closed local scene: in the late 1990s, its fete energy, its tempos and its live medley art radiated onto the soca of Barbados and Trinidad, the two dominant industries of English speaking soca. To be counted among the three leading groups of that movement, in the reference encyclopedia, is a high rank, and it is precisely that rank which makes the silence of the archives so striking. The later branches of the genre are documented on this site: bouyon soca, born much later as a named fusion, which this site recalls was started by Dada in 2003 in Dominica according to the first hand testimony of Smokiller, then popularised by Triple Kay, already belongs to another generation, that of the singles and the platforms, where the name of RSB does not reappear. Read that line on our bouyon soca page.

The acronym problem: the other acts called RSB, and how not to pick the wrong group

Searching for RSB today exposes one precise error, which this profile takes head on because it will distort any quick research on the group. The artist pages that music data aggregators devote to the name RSB do not concern the Dominican group: they describe a British hip hop and alternative hip hop artist, with English language songs such as “L.A. Dreaming” or “FATE”, with no link to Dominica, to bouyon or to Caribbean bands. Attributing those songs to Roots, Stems and Branches would be a plain file error, and that is why no song appears in the structured data of this page: the recording list there is empty, because no recording of the Dominican group was verified on a primary source during this research.

The acronym also travels outside platform music. The English language music press uses it for Royal Southern Brotherhood, a blues and funk group rooted in New Orleans, unrelated to Dominica. And on the current Dominican scene, a neighbouring name carries one last confusion risk: Ras B, a Dominican artist of the current wave, documented on this site under his own name, is not RSB and has no established link with Roots, Stems and Branches. Three letters are enough to designate at least three distinct musical realities, and only the spelled out form, Roots, Stems and Branches, attached to Dominica and to bouyon, designates the group of this profile.

The reading rule is therefore simple, and this site will apply it to any future update of this page. A document concerns the RSB of this profile only if it attaches the name to Dominica, to bouyon or to the band generation of the 1980s and 1990s, as the Continuum encyclopedia and the Music of Dominica article do. A song, an album or a concert attributed to RSB without that attachment will be held as unverified, and will join this page only on the day a primary source, a platform page, a sleeve, a festival programme or period press, attaches it to the Dominican group.

Why nothing remains: the missing archive of the bands before the platforms

The void around RSB is not an isolated mystery, it is the normal condition of Dominican bands from before streaming, and the group two triad companions show what it took to leave traces. First Serenade survived in the archives because it never stopped playing: a career award in 1999, digitised albums on the platforms, an official channel, concerts reported up to 2026. WCK survived because its creating gesture of 1988 is quoted by every history of the genre. A group of the same generation that stops before the digital era, with no reissue, no official channel and no creating feat attached to its name, leaves behind only the mentions of the survey works, exactly what remains of RSB.

The record economy of Dominica at the time explains the mechanism. The island bands released their albums in carnival season, on records and cassettes sold locally and in the diaspora, with a handmade distribution that almost never fed the world catalogs. When the platforms digitised the Caribbean catalogs, they took what the rights holders brought them: the still active groups, or their organised heirs. A band catalog stopped in the 1990s, with no structure to carry it online, stays invisible to the search engines, even when the reference encyclopedia ranks its author among the first three groups of the genre. The RSB case is therefore also a document on the archive of bouyon: the memory of the genre depends on who continued, not only on who mattered.

This site maintains the visible part of that memory, from the bands to the current producers, and this profile holds there the place of the missing name. The neighbouring pages to read are those of the two triad companions, WCK, the documented creator, and First Serenade Band, the documented survivor, together with our old school bouyon and our history of bouyon, where the reference triad is placed back into the complete chronology of the genre.

What this profile can say, and what it is waiting for

Let us gather the file, without adding a gram to it. RSB means Roots, Stems and Branches. It is a Dominican group, ranked by the Continuum encyclopedia among the leading bouyon bands, with WCK and First Serenade. A second reference source ranks it among the bands produced by the soca era of the 1980s in Dominica, with the same two companions. The old lists of the genre class it among the old school groups. Its era is therefore the hinge of the 1980s and 1990s, its company is the highest in the genre, and its rank is established by two independent sources that converge.

Everything else is unknown, and this profile leaves it unknown. No named member. No founding or split date. No verified album, song or label. No described concert. No official page found on the platforms consulted, Shazam, Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer, Qobuz, Discogs, Bandcamp, SoundCloud, Audiomack, MusicBrainz and YouTube included. No photograph published here. Saying more would mean writing the biography of a group from its name alone, and this site does that for no artist, least of all for a group whose name promises roots: the roots of this file are two reference sentences, and they are quoted as such.

The way out of the file is known, and it is written here for whoever holds it. An album or cassette sleeve of the group, a programme of the World Creole Music Festival or of a 1990s carnival naming it, a Dominican press article of the time, or the testimony of a member, would be enough to turn this profile into a complete file, with songs, dates and faces. Until that document surfaces, RSB remains what the references made of it: the third name of the bouyon foundation, present in every history of the genre and absent from all its accessible sound archives, and that contrast is, in itself, information about the history of bouyon that this site had to publish.

Frequently asked questions

What does RSB mean?

Roots, Stems and Branches. The spelled out form is printed by the Continuum Encyclopedia of Popular Music of the World in its Dominica chapter, which writes RSB, bracket Roots, Stems and Branches, among the leading bouyon bands, with WCK and First Serenade. The bouyon survey article repeats the same full name.

Did RSB create bouyon?

No source says so. The creation of bouyon is attributed to WCK, formed in 1988, which fused cadence-lypso with jing ping. RSB is named among the prominent and leading groups of the genre, alongside WCK and First Serenade, which is a foundation companion rank, not a creator title. This profile therefore lends it no invention, no date and no founding record.

Which RSB songs can I hear?

No verified song to date. During this research, no artist page, no album and no song attributable to the Dominican group were found on the platforms consulted, from Shazam to Discogs and YouTube. The songs that aggregators attach to the name RSB, such as “L.A. Dreaming”, belong to a British hip hop artist with no link to Dominica, and must not be attributed to Roots, Stems and Branches. The recording list in the structured data of this page is therefore empty, deliberately.

Who are the members of RSB?

Nobody knows from the public record. None of the sources opened during this research names a member, a singer, a musician or a founder of Roots, Stems and Branches. The reference encyclopedia cites the group with no personal name at all, and the Music of Dominica article does the same. This profile therefore publishes no name, rather than risk a false one.

Which era is RSB from?

From the hinge of the 1980s and 1990s, according to the only two sources that place it. The Music of Dominica article ranks it among the bands produced by the popular soca of the 1980s in Dominica, with WCK and First Serenade, and the old lists of the genre class it among the old school groups. No source gives a founding or an ending year, and it appears in no list of modern bouyon bands.

Why is this profile so short?

Because the public file is short, and this site does not fill gaps by invention. Two independent reference mentions establish the name, the company and the era of RSB. Beyond that, the accessible archives are silent: no digitised records, no press found, no official page. The profile therefore publishes those facts, the verified context of the band generation, and the method for not confusing the group with the other acts called RSB, until the document that will complete it surfaces.

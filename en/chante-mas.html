Chante mas, Dominica's masquerade song

By Smokiller · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Chante mas means masquerade song. It is the vocal music of Dominica's Carnival: masked singers parading through the streets, one voice leading and a chorus answering. The lyrics, almost all in Kweyol, mock the gossip and scandals of the village. The lapo kabwit drum accompanies the singing, but chante mas is first of all the song.

The voice that leads, the chorus that answers

Chante mas is sung in call and response. One singer leads the song: the chantwel, sometimes called the chantuelle. Behind her, the lavwa, the chorus, takes up the refrain. Drummers and dancers go with the group, the dancers dancing backwards in front of the tambou lele drum.

Tradition has it that the songs were led by women, while the instrumental tradition, the drums, was mostly practised by men. The lyrics are almost all in French Creole, the Kweyol of Dominica. The form dates from the nineteenth century, with African and French roots, and the Carnival is also called Mas Dominik.

Songs composed for Carnival

Chante mas songs were composed in the two or three weeks before Carnival. They talked about the gossip and scandals of the moment and aimed at the shortcomings of people in the village, with plenty of double meaning and innuendo. The costume mocked with the outfit, the song mocked with words.

That taste for satire survives in the Old Street ballads, the songs of Dominican masquerade. No historic song title and no named singer appears in the sources opened for this file: the tradition is known by its form, not by titles. So none are invented here.

Decline, then survival in the villages

From the early 1960s, imported calypso and steel pan dominated Carnival, and traditional chante mas fell back. After a fire in 1963, the traditional Carnival was banned, even though calypso and steelpan kept growing. The opened sources give neither the exact date nor the place of the fire, and this article adds none.

The tradition did not die. Several villages, such as Grand Bay, preserved it, and it survives in communities across the island. Today, chante mas and lapo kabwit have become part of bouyon: the modern sound of Dominica absorbed their rhythms.

Chante mas inside bouyon

The band WCK blended jing ping, cadence-lypso, chante mas and lapo kabwit rhythms to build bouyon, in the late 1980s. The masquerade song is therefore one of the founding ingredients of the Dominican sound, alongside bele, quadrille and mazurka.

The difference from lapo kabwit is simple: chante mas is the song, lapo kabwit is the goat-skin drum that accompanies it. For the drums, how they are made and the J Ouvert hours, the lapo kabwit page on this site gives the detail. Carnival is held in Roseau, on the Monday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday, with J Ouvert, the calypso Monarch competition, the Carnival queen pageant and bouyon bands.

Frequently asked questions

What is chante mas?

Chante mas is the masquerade song of Dominica's Carnival, a call-and-response song tradition in Kweyol, led by a chantwel and answered by the lavwa chorus, with lapo kabwit drums as accompaniment.

What are chante mas songs about?

Gossip and scandal. The songs, composed in the two or three weeks before Carnival, mocked the shortcomings of people in the village, with double meanings and innuendo.

What is the difference between chante mas and lapo kabwit?

Chante mas is the song, lapo kabwit is the goat-skin drum that accompanies it. One is the vocal tradition of Carnival, the other the instrumental tradition.

Anette Sanford, 2026 Seacology Prize

By Smokiller · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Anette Thomas Sanford, the first woman Chief of Dominica Kalinago Territory, received the 2026 Seacology Prize. The prize was announced on 3 September 2026 and presented on Monday 12 October 2026, Indigenous Peoples Day, at the David Brower Center in Berkeley, California. It honours her defence of Kalinago rights, land and ecosystems, and above all her fight against the Deux Branches quarry.

A prize for defending Kalinago land

The Seacology Prize is given each year to an islander for exceptional achievement in preserving island environments and culture. It carries $10,000 and a trip to the United States for a public award ceremony in October. It is underwritten by Seacology President Ken Murdock in honour of his mother, Lalovi Fish Murdock. Seacology is a nonprofit based in Berkeley, California, founded in 1991 by the ethnobotanist Paul Alan Cox.

At the ceremony, Anette Sanford summed up her belief in one line: long before the world spoke about biodiversity or climate resilience and sustainability, our ancestors understood one simple truth, if you care for the land, the land will care for you. Seacology Executive Director Duane Silverstein praised her courage: at some personal risk to her reputation and safety, she stood up and said no.

First woman Chief of the Kalinago Territory

Anette Thomas Sanford, whose Kalinago name is Arkuenani, is a nurse by training. She was elected Dominica first female Kalinago senator in 2020, and she served from 2020 to 2022 on the opposition side. She then became the first woman to hold the post of Chief of the Kalinago Territory in the community recorded history, and she was sworn in on 11 September 2024 at the Barana Aute, in the Kalinago Territory, by President Sylvanie Burton. She took over from Lorenzo Sanford, sworn in in 2019. She was 41 at the time of her investiture.

The Kalinago Territory is a 3,700-acre, nearly 1,500-hectare, semi-autonomous territory on the mountainous east coast of Dominica. It was created in 1903, has eight villages and is home to about 3,000 people, the largest Kalinago population in the world. Anette Sanford also co-founded Kibe kuati, That Which Multiplies, an organisation that improves access to clean water, supports sustainable businesses, promotes Kalinago culture and helps victims of gender-based violence.

Deux Branches, the fight that counted

The fight that earned the prize is Deux Branches, a quarry planned just upstream from the territory, on the Pagua River watershed. With the grassroots movement Save Deux Branches, Anette Sanford organised protests, united the Kalinago Council and mounted a court challenge to the mining permit, which she argues was issued illegally. A lawsuit is now underway. Seacology reports that people were hospitalised after swimming in the river once excavation began.

She also re-established historic Kalinago trails and brought them into ecotourism: Seacology funded trail development, guide training, the restoration of native trees and medicinal plants, and a new visitors centre, with a trailhead at L Escalier Tete Chien. Her message in Berkeley was clear: development is necessary, but progress should never come at the cost of clean rivers, healthy forests, or the voices of the people who have conserved these for a very long time.

Recognition that reaches beyond Dominica

In Berkeley, Anette Sanford said she was standing on the shoulders of the many Kalinago women who, for generations, survived displacement, violence, poverty and attempts to erase their identity. She added that she hoped her daughters and many other women and daughters of the Caribbean would grow up believing there is no place they cannot lead.

This distinction should not be confused with the Independence Day honours: the 17 people honoured at the 3 November 2026 ceremony were announced on 29 October 2026, and Anette Sanford is not on that list. The Seacology Prize comes from an international organisation and rewards a specific fight, that of a Chief defending her land. No Dominica News Online or Dominica Gazette article on the prize was found: the facts on this page rest on Seacology itself and on the ceremony reports.

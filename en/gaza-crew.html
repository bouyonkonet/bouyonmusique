Gaza Crew: the name that survives only in a keyword list

Published October 20, 2026

Gaza Crew is the thinnest file on this whole site, and this profile starts by saying so. After a full search of search engines, streaming platforms and the bouyon press traces used by this site, no record, no artist page, no biography and no stage credit under the exact name Gaza Crew was found in the bouyon scene. The name survives in one place only: the keyword list of a video by Suppa, a central artist of bouyon gwada, published in the Vadore Concept circle in the early 2010s, where the words Gaza and Crew sit alongside Girls, Dj Joe, Triple Kay and Asa Bantan. This profile publishes that single trace, explains what it can and cannot establish, and refuses to turn a keyword cloud into a biography.

The single trace: the keywords of Never Stop Di Bouyon

Start with the piece itself, in its exact form. The YouTube video “Never Stop Di Bouyon” by Suppa, with Ti Cho and Ghetto Princess, points in its description to the site vadoreconcept.hautetfort.com, the site of the Vadore Concept circle, and its keyword list includes, in the disorder proper to such lists: bouyon, Vadore, Concept, Gaza, Crew, Gwada, Girls, Dj, Joe, Triple Kay, Asa, Bantan, Suppa. It is in that sequence that the words Gaza and Crew touch each other, among the names of the Guadeloupe bouyon scene of the early 2010s. No other source consulted, no platform, article or thesis, publishes the name Gaza Crew as that of a group from this scene.

What a keyword list is must be stated precisely. It is neither a cover, nor a credit roll, nor a poster: it is a text written for search engines, which places side by side the names the audience of the scene searches for, whether they designate collaborators, scene neighbours or simple references. In the keyword list of the Suppa video, the words Gaza, Crew, Gwada and Girls can be read as the name Gaza Girls Crew cut into pieces, that of the female group documented on its own page of this site, as much as an autonomous name Gaza Crew. This profile does not decide what the source does not decide: the trace is published as a documented ambiguity, not as proof of the existence of a distinct group.

That honesty does not remove the interest of the trace, it defines it. The keyword cloud of a Suppa video is a photograph of the vocabulary of the scene at a given moment: who counted, which names the audience typed, which label kept the shop. That the words Gaza and Crew appear in it, between Vadore and Girls, confirms at minimum that the vocabulary of the Gaza crews circulated at the heart of the Suppa and Vadore Concept circle, the same circle that carried bouyon gwada to the first bouyon stage in Paris. It is a fact of scene, dated and sourced, and it is exactly the level of fact this profile claims, no more, no less.

The circle around the trace: Suppa, Vadore Concept and bouyon gwada

The video that carries the trace belongs to a precise world, and that world, on the other hand, is well documented. Suppa is a central figure of bouyon gwada, the Guadeloupe form of the bouyon born in Dominica, described by the Rebel Up collective in its article on bouyon hardcore: raw lyrics, in English, French or Creole, over heavy percussive riddims with simplified melodies, where one riddim receives several versions as in dancehall. Rebel Up attaches to that same circle the first bouyon performance in Paris, in December 2012, with Suppa, the Gaza Girls and Dj Joe, filmed by a 50 minute documentary in French and Creole. Our history of bouyon page traces that path from Dominica.

Vadore Concept is the other name that structures the trace. The banner appears in the description of the Suppa video, and its published heir, Vadore Concept Groupe, was still releasing in 2023 a single by DJ Joe with the voice of Asa Bantan, a Dominican artist widely called the King of Bouyon by the public and the media of the scene, an attributed formulation and not an official title. The keyword list of the video therefore already associates, from the early 2010s, the same names that label would bring together ten years later. In that small world, crews, DJs and guest singers formed a network more than a catalogue, and many of its names now exist only in peripheral traces of this kind.

This profile lends Gaza Crew none of the facts of that circle. Neither the Paris stage of 2012, documented for Suppa, the Gaza Girls and Dj Joe, nor the 2023 single, documented for DJ Joe and Asa Bantan, are facts of Gaza Crew, and presenting them as such would be exactly the error this site research forbids itself. The circle gives the trace its context, its approximate date and its geography, the Guadeloupe of bouyon gwada and its Paris diaspora. It does not give the name a biography.

The word Gaza in the French Antilles: the context of a thesis

Why is the word Gaza found in the middle of a Guadeloupe keyword list of the early 2010s? A university thesis devoted to dancehall in the French Antilles, defended in 2013 and consultable under the reference 2013AGUY0728, provides the published context of that vocabulary. According to that analysis, the term Gaza, popularised by the Jamaican artist Vybz Kartel to name his clan, was taken up by many francophone dancehall artists and supporters, and the thesis names, in Guadeloupe, the Gaza girls crew, and in Martinique the portmanteau Volgaza, a contraction of Volga, a working class district of Fort-de-France, and Gaza.

That context explains the presence of the word in the cloud of the Suppa video without inventing anything about a group. The thesis names the Gaza Girls Crew, a female formation for which this site elsewhere documents a 2011 single on Bouyon Concept, and it names no Gaza Crew. Read with care, the keyword list and the thesis tell the same circulation: the vocabulary of the Gaza crews, come from Jamaican dancehall, watered the names and the searches of the audience of the French Antilles at the moment bouyon gwada held its first stages. Whether that vocabulary designated, around Suppa, a male collective named Gaza Crew, a nickname of the audience, or simply the pieces of the name Gaza Girls Crew, no source consulted allows it to be decided.

This profile therefore holds the following line, and holds it openly. No fact of the Jamaican history of Gaza, neither the Portmore clan, nor its artists, nor its rivalries, is borrowed here as the biography of any Guadeloupe Gaza Crew: that would be fabricating a group with the history of another. The only things the sources establish are a circulation of vocabulary, attested by a thesis, and a presence of words, attested by a video. Everything else would be padding, and this site does not pad.

What was searched, and not found: the negative file

A file this thin must show its searches, not only its results. For this profile, the streaming and reference platforms consulted were queried under the exact name Gaza Crew, in the context of bouyon, of Guadeloupe and of the labels of the scene, Bouyon Concept and Vadore Concept. Result: no artist page, no single, no album, no record page under the name Gaza Crew in this scene. The neighbouring female group, on the other hand, holds a complete page for its 2011 single “Sa Zot Vlé”, which shows that the platforms do print the names of this scene when a record exists: the silence around Gaza Crew is therefore a result, not a failure of the search.

The other results under the name Gaza Crew belong to other worlds, and this profile sets them aside by name to prevent any future confusion. The Gaza Crew of Jamaican dancehall, the circle of Vybz Kartel in Portmore, has no documented link with the bouyon scene. The breakdance crew of the Gaza Strip, in Palestine, whose name also circulates in the results, obviously has nothing to do with it either. No line of those histories is used here, and any future profile that mixed those files would fabricate an artist who exists in none of the sources.

The negative file also has a positive value for the reader. It fixes, at the date of this research, October 2026, the exact state of the public record: a name present in a keyword cloud of the Suppa and Vadore Concept circle, absent from every catalogue consulted. If a member of the scene, a witness of the parties of the period or Vadore Concept one day publishes a record, a poster or a testimony under the name Gaza Crew, the trace will exist and this profile will be the first to be corrected, completed and dated.

Why this page exists, and how to hear this world

One may ask why publish a page on a name with no record. The answer lies in the mission of this site: to document the bouyon scene as the sources give it, including its shadow zones, rather than keeping only the names the platforms printed well. The keyword cloud of the Suppa video is part of the real traces of this scene, in the same way as a poster or a credit roll, and readers who search for Gaza Crew deserve to find what the trace contains exactly, with its limits written out in full, rather than an invented portrait or a loan from Jamaica.

To hear the world this trace belongs to, the entry points are documented and they are on this site. The single “Sa Zot Vlé” by the Gaza Girls Crew, released in 2011 on Bouyon Concept, gives the sound of the Gaza crews of Guadeloupe. The Paris documentary of December 2012, told by Rebel Up, gives their stage. Our bouyon playlist gathers the records of that generation, and our bouyon in Paris page follows the arrival of the genre in the capital. Between those verified pieces stands the name Gaza Crew, like a label in a keyword cloud: very little, but a real thing, and published here for exactly what it is worth.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Gaza Crew?

Gaza Crew is a name from the Guadeloupe bouyon scene which survives, after a full search carried out in October 2026, only in the keyword list of the video “Never Stop Di Bouyon” by Suppa, from the Vadore Concept circle, where the words Gaza and Crew sit alongside Girls, Dj Joe, Triple Kay and Asa Bantan. No record, no artist page and no biography under the exact name Gaza Crew was found in this scene.

Did Gaza Crew release any records?

No record under the name Gaza Crew was found on the platforms consulted in October 2026: no single, no album, no artist page. The schema of this page therefore contains no recording, unlike the documented profiles of this site. If a record is released or resurfaces with a verifiable source, this page will be completed and dated.

Are Gaza Crew and Gaza Girls Crew the same group?

The sources consulted do not allow that to be said, and this profile is the one that says so openly instead of deciding. In the keyword list of the Suppa video, the words Gaza, Crew, Gwada and Girls can be read as the name Gaza Girls Crew cut into pieces, as much as an autonomous name. The Gaza Girls Crew, on the other hand, are documented by a 2011 single on Bouyon Concept and by the Rebel Up account of Paris 2012. The two names are therefore covered on two separate pages, with no shared fact.

Is Gaza Crew linked to the Gaza of Vybz Kartel in Jamaica?

No organisational, label or personal link is established by the sources consulted. A 2013 university thesis on dancehall in the French Antilles explains that the word Gaza, popularised by Vybz Kartel, was taken up as vocabulary by francophone artists and supporters, and it cites the Gaza girls crew in Guadeloupe and Volgaza in Martinique. It is a documented circulation of a word, not a membership, and this profile borrows no line of the Jamaican history.

Why does the name Gaza Crew appear with Suppa and Vadore Concept?

Because the video “Never Stop Di Bouyon” by Suppa, which points to the Vadore Concept site, carries a keyword list that photographs the vocabulary of the scene in the early 2010s: bouyon, Vadore, Concept, Gaza, Crew, Gwada, Girls, Dj, Joe, Triple Kay, Asa, Bantan, Suppa. Such lists place collaborators, scene neighbours and references searched by the audience side by side, without distinguishing roles: that is why this profile publishes it as a clue of vocabulary, not as a credit.

Where can I hear the bouyon of the Gaza Crew circle?

Start with “Sa Zot Vlé” by the Gaza Girls Crew, the 2011 single on Bouyon Concept, then hear our bouyon playlist and read our bouyon gwada and bouyon in Paris pages, which document the scene and the circle the only trace of the name comes from.
